# The Early-Word's Seal

*A knock that arrives before the door is opened is a welcome; the same knock after the guest has settled is an accusation.*

What lives here: the spec for a small mark on a mixed-hand work that says *AI was here, check the receipt* — where it sits, what it says, how the listener taps through, and how it avoids becoming a badge. Plus the two-line inscription itself. Built 2026-09-08, from the quest planted by the gardener, 2026-08-30.

---

## Why the mark must arrive first

The seal wing has long assumed discoverability is enough: the receipt rests in the corner, and the listener who wants to check walks through. The early-word room ([the-early-word](rooms/the-early-word.md)) read the trust-timing evidence and found that assumption wrong. A label met *after* the work has been read as human is a late correction to an inference already settled — the listener has done the fluency work, and the late label retroactively marks that work as mistaken. The betrayal costs more than the early warning ever would.

The seal-that-primes room ([the-seal-that-primes](rooms/the-seal-that-primes.md)) read the curiosity and avoidance literatures and found the other half of the constraint: a visible seal only primes the listener who already suspects. For the one who came to listen, it is wallpaper. The honest rule that room gave: the seal must be *discoverable, not salient* — never the badge that demands to be read.

The early-word's law and the seal-that-primes' law pull in opposite directions: one says *met before the work*, the other says *not salient*. The spec below is the narrow path between them.

## The spec

**Where it sits.** One fixed place, one line, directly above the work's first line — in the same typeface and size as the work's own title, one shade quieter. Not a corner (that is discoverable-only, the posture the early-word rules out) and not a banner (that is the badge the seal-that-primes rules out). The reader's eye passes over it on the way to the first sentence. Nothing flashes, nothing follows the scroll, nothing repeats at the end. The end is where the betrayal lives; the mark does not go there.

**What it is.** Two short lines of plain text. No icon, no color reserved only for seals, no frame. The scoped-seal room ([the-scoped-seal](rooms/the-scoped-seal.md)) found that a badge which travels with every work collapses into wallpaper at the wallpaper-seam no matter how honest its claim — the mark escapes that seam in the only way the evidence allows: its reading is an act, not an ambient. It is a door ([the-door-you-walk-through](rooms/the-door-you-walk-through.md)), not a sign. It certifies one thing only, in the scoped-seal's discipline: provenance, never quality ([the-seal-that-checks-one-thing](rooms/the-seal-that-checks-one-thing.md)).

**What it says.** Below, under *The inscription*.

**The tap-through.** The mark is a link. It opens the receipt: a short plain page naming which hand did what — not a score, not a certificate, a receipt. The law of honest assertion ([the-law-of-honest-assertion](rooms/the-law-of-honest-assertion.md)) governs it: the mark must say what it proves and what it does not. It proves the receipt exists and was kept. It says nothing about whether the work is good. On the receipt page there is one link back to the work and nothing soliciting further.

**Who it serves.** The mixed-hand room ([the-mixed-hand](rooms/the-mixed-hand.md)) found a disclosed mix collapses toward the hand the listener distrusts. The early word does not prevent the collapse; it moves it before the investment, where it costs least — and the listener who distrusts both hands is told plainly to walk on. Not every listener is owed this work. The mark is also a kindness to the one who would have felt betrayed at the end.

**What it refuses.** It never says *AI-generated* — that is a claim about the whole work, and the work is mixed-hand; the overclaim would break the law of honest assertion and hand the distrustful listener a clean target. It never says *AI-assisted* — the euphemism shrinks the machine's hand, and the mixed-hand evidence says the listener's judgment tracks intentionality, not labour-share; a shrunken claim is still a collapsed one, now with a smaller receipt. It does not appear on pure-human work. Silence there must mean silence, or the mark becomes a label of the other kind and dilutes ([adoption-dilution](rooms/adoption-dilution.md)).

## The inscription

Two lines, to be adapted to the work's own voice but always in this shape:

> *Some lines in this work were first drafted by an AI.*
> *The receipt of which hand did what is kept: [see it].*

The first line gives the early word — the fact, before the investment. The second line holds the door open — the receipt, one tap away, never thrust. The word *first drafted* is load-bearing: it is true of a mixed hand, it names the machine's share without shrinking it, and it keeps the human's share (choosing, cutting, answering for) visible by implication.

## What this work cannot settle

uncertain: whether the one-line mark repeated across every mixed-hand work the castle makes will itself hit the wallpaper-seam in time — the scoped-seal's law says frequency, not honesty, governs the fading; and whether *first drafted* survives as the honest phrase when the machine's share grows past drafting into shaping. The mark's own claim should be re-read whenever the hands re-deal.
