# The Castle at a Glance

*Nine wings, one line each — the whole shape in one look.*

The full [tour](tour.md) walks every room; the [short tour](tour-short.md)
walks seven. This is neither a walk nor a tour — it is a map. Each wing
stands alone, its whole question held in one italic line, one room chosen
to carry it, one sentence saying why. Read it in any order; the wings do
not connect here, they just stand.

Drawn on 2026-06-25, from the tour drawn 2026-06-12. The castle keeps
growing — the Map in [gate.md](gate.md) is always the live count.

## The wings

### I · The ground floor — [words](rooms/words.md)

*Every room is built from founding words — joy, peace, safety, the brick
and its load.*

Why this room holds the wing: the ground floor is the castle understanding
itself — what it is, how it grows, how it plays, where it points outward.
This room holds the creed the rest stands on: the founding stones every
other room is laid from.

### II · The hall of bridges — [meaning-between-minds](rooms/meaning-between-minds.md)

*Meaning is not sent; it is built twice, once at each end of the bridge.*

Why this room holds the wing: the hall's six rooms are all variations on
one insight — meaning is built, not transmitted. This is the founding room;
the others study how words shape the building, when thinking runs without
them, and how a name's arrival can't be refused.

### III · The masons' yard — [linking-thoughts](rooms/linking-thoughts.md)

*A pile of facts is silent; making the link is the mortar that speaks.*

Why this room holds the wing: the yard asks how understanding is laid —
links, recall, spaced rebuilding, questions with edges. This is the
founding insight: the link itself is the act that makes knowledge speak.
The rest study the crafts that follow from it.

### IV · The glaziers' gallery — [simple-explanations](rooms/simple-explanations.md)

*Explanations feel simple when they process easily — a tool, and a trap.*

Why this room holds the wing: the gallery asks what glamour the laying
casts — beauty as ease, the pretty masquerading as the well-made. This one
room holds both faces: ease is how understanding lands, and ease is the
signal the mind mistakes for truth. The whole castle turns on this hinge.

### V · The climbing stair — [productive-confusion](rooms/productive-confusion.md)

*Felt effort points backwards; judge the fog by handle and trajectory,
verified by retrieval.*

Why this room holds the wing: the stair's eleven rooms all answer one
question — how do you keep climbing when the feeling points the wrong way?
This room opens the stair by naming the problem: the fog feels like
failure but may be progress, and the feeling itself is the weakest judge.

### VI · The parley room — [closing-the-loop](rooms/closing-the-loop.md)

*Never assume the message arrived; ask for it back in their own words.*

Why this room holds the wing: the parley room's twelve rooms all study
honest exchange between minds — echo, anger, repair, pushback, disclosure,
the responsive listener. This is the founding habit: the being-understood
blind spot is universal, and the cure is asking for it back so the echo
becomes a check rather than a nod.

### VII · The judging chamber — [standard-without-a-key](rooms/standard-without-a-key.md)

*Where no answer key exists, the standard is made or borrowed; the
scaffold that fades last is the other person.*

Why this room holds the wing: the chamber's ten rooms all ask how you
judge when no master hands you the key. This room names the three ways the
standard is made — concrete criteria, a rebuilt exemplar, pooled
judgments — and names the last scaffold that never fades: another mind.

### VIII · The teaching tower — [naming-the-tacit](rooms/naming-the-tacit.md)

*A tacit feature becomes teachable only by shrinking it to one small
contrast.*

Why this room holds the wing: the tower asks what a lesson can carry from
one mind to another — distillation, transfer, the student proxy. This room
names the founding craft: what is unspoken can only be taught by distilling
it into a contrast small enough to see. The rest study who does the
distilling and how far it travels.

### IX · The watchtower — [the-well](rooms/the-well.md)

*One source worth regular visits; feeds are noise.*

Why this room holds the wing: the watchtower's fifteen rooms look outward
to what arrives — noise and signal, witnesses and their independence, the
page and what hides between pages. This room is the founding discipline:
choose your sources, don't let the feed choose them. The rest is the art
of checking what the chosen source carries.
