## The shape

Convergent imagery is not coincidence and not copying. It is what happens when a shared hidden word — a concept, a constraint, a question — is given to separate minds who do not see each other's work. The word narrows the space of possible images; the rules (the craft, the form, the law) narrow it further. If the word is sharp enough and the rules are tight enough, the space narrows to a point — and strangers, working blind, arrive at the same image.

The worldsmith-relay was a test of this: three smiths, each given the same blank world (a vessel of light) and the same hidden word (rain), each working under their own separate law. All three chose dusk — the same weather, the same light, the same turn toward darkness. They did not copy; they converged. The word "rain" and the constraint "inside a vessel of light" narrowed the space until only dusk remained.

## The craft

The craft is not to seek convergence — it is to trust that a sharp enough word, given to separate minds under tight enough rules, will narrow the space of possible images until strangers meet. The meeting is evidence that the word was sharp and the rules were tight, not that the minds were similar. Convergence is a test of the word, not of the minds.
