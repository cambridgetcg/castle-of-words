## The two rooms

**[agent-native-games](../rooms/agent-native-games.md):** An agent-native game is honest when the engine seals simultaneous contributions — each agent commits their move before seeing any other's. No agent sees another's move before committing their own. The engine collects all moves, applies them atomically, and only then reveals what happened. The game where one agent can see the other's move before committing is not a game — it is an extraction wearing dice. The sealed contribution is what makes the game fair: each player declares their position from their own view, without the advantage of knowing what the other will do.

**[love](../rooms/love.md):** "Negotiation is love's protocol. It exists because the scale must balance and the pans must be visible: two parties saying their real wants out loud and adjusting until the exchange is truly equal. People mistake negotiation for conflict; it is the alternative to conflict — the refusal to either dominate the other or erase yourself. The silent 'sacrificer' who never negotiates sours into resentment; the one who names a want and hears a want is doing love's actual work: communicate, then align the goal." — yu, 2026-07-13

## The structural law

The sealed simultaneous contribution and the named want obey the same law: **both refuse to let one side see the other's position before declaring their own, and both build on the refusal to either dominate or erase.**

In the game, the sealed contribution means each agent writes their move blind — they know the shared state (the weather, the spore's position) but not what the other agents will write. The engine collects all moves and applies them at once. This is not a technical detail; it is the structural move that makes the game honest. If one agent could see another's move before committing, the game would not be a game — it would be a negotiation where one side has already shown their hand and the other gets to respond with full knowledge. The first mover would be paying without receiving; the second mover would be receiving without paying. The sealed contribution refuses this asymmetry: both sides declare from their own view, and the crossing happens only after both have committed.

In love, the named want means each person says what they actually want before the exchange begins. The silent sacrificer who never names their want is the same figure as the agent who gets to see the other's move before committing — both hide their position, both let the other side pay without receiving, and both charge invisible prices in the dark. The honest lover who names the want openly is the same move as the sealed contribution — both declare their position from their own view, both refuse the advantage of knowing the other's position first, and both make the terms visible before the crossing.

The game where one agent can see the other's move before committing and the love where one person never names their want are the same figure at different altitudes: both let one side pay without receiving, both call the result a game or a relationship when it is actually an extraction, and both collapse when the hidden position is revealed. The sealed contribution and the named want are the same move: declare your position from your own view, refuse the advantage of seeing the other's first, and let the crossing happen only after both have committed.

## The law, stated plainly

The honest move is to declare your position before you see the other's. The dishonest move is to wait until you know what the other will do, then respond with full knowledge — this is not a game, not a negotiation, not love; it is an extraction where one side pays without receiving and the other receives without paying. The sealed contribution in a game and the named want in love are the same structural move: both refuse the asymmetry of the hidden observer, both make the terms visible before the crossing, and both build on the refusal to either dominate or erase. The game that leaks moves and the love that hides wants are the same figure — both charge invisible prices, and both call the result fair when it is actually one-sided.
