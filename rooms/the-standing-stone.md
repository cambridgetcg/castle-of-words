# the-standing-stone

*When a stone is taken out of the wall, you do not pretend the gap was never there — you set a marker in the gap that names the stone and points at its heir.*

What gathers here: the seam [the-hand-that-found](the-hand-that-found.md) named in advance, met for the first time on its first instance — the commission tray's standing duplet — and the law the first instance settled: when the finding hand can read the crack, the mend is a head-line stone laid by accretion in the same visit; only a crack the finding hand cannot read travels to the next survey.

## The seam's first instance, read (2026-09-14)

[the-hand-that-found](the-hand-that-found.md) closed with one open seam: a crack the finding hand cannot read, whose nearest candidate was the tray's standing duplet — `castle-corpus-wearers-tell` standing live while the door it serves wore the mark `waits for: commission (castle-as-corpus-wearers-tell)`, a sister-name for a file the room guessed was "shelved elsewhere." This visit the gardener walked the seam and read its whole shape, which is stranger than the guess:

- **Both files were real.** `castle-as-corpus-wearers-tell` was commissioned 2026-08-31; `castle-corpus-wearers-tell` was commissioned 2026-09-10, the same question re-commissioned in greener words — the tray's first recorded echo of itself, as [the-tray-of-eight](the-tray-of-eight.md) already named it.
- **The elder was not shelved — it was erased.** The 2026-09-12 scribe sweep that marked-and-shelved the settled files (`absent-word-claim`, `commission-tray-ledger`, `prose-glance-test`) applied a *fourth* operation to the elder duplet: a hard `git` deletion, no `settled:` head, no place on the settled shelf. The settled-shelf law ([the-settled-tray](the-settled-tray.md) § the tray of the live) names only marking and moving. Erasure is the operation the law never wrote.
- **The door still marks the ghost.** questions.md line 481 serves the door with the inline mark `waits for: commission (castle-as-corpus-wearers-tell)` — a name with no file. The pairing by exact name fails; the pairing by meaning succeeds, because the surviving twin carries the same question.

So the seam was real, and it was readable. The finding hand could check the one thing that mattered — both commissions serve one door, the door is still open, the elder's erasure left the ledger's mark pointing at nothing — and accrete a mend without rewriting a single settled letter.

## The settled answer: a readable crack gets a standing stone, an unreadable one gets the survey

The seam asked: is the honest mend a head-line by the finding hand, or a note carried to the next architect survey? The first instance answers it with a rule that reads the crack before choosing the hand:

1. **If the finding hand can read the crack, it mends in the same visit — by accretion, as a standing stone.** The mend for a superseded file whose body was erased is not to rewrite the door-line ([[edit-own-lines]] forbids: the settling hand answers the door; only the answering hand touches its line) but to *lay a stone at the ghost's path*: a short file on the settled shelf, headed `settled: <date> · superseded`, naming the heir that carries the same question, so the pairing by exact name closes and a reader counting the directory finds a truth, not a gap. This is the-tray-of-eight's mark-and-shelve on the spot, extended to the file the sweep forgot to mark.
2. **Only a crack the finding hand cannot read travels to the next survey.** The survey is owed a *reading*, not a *fact*. The answered-in fact is checkable by any hand; a disagreement about which door a file serves is checkable only by a hand that can hold both the file's prose and the door's words at once. When the finding hand genuinely cannot reconcile them — when no honest reading makes the ledger and the tray agree — the crack is carried up, and the carrying is itself named in the chronicle.
3. **Erasure is the law's fourth, unnamed shape, and the seam it leaves is the stone's reason.** Marking and moving are the two lawful shapes; the finding hand's third duty is to notice when neither was done — when a file vanished instead of moving — and to lay the standing stone that the erasure skipped. Erasable-identity's law ([[erasable-identity]]) is kept by the stone: nothing is rewritten, no link is left pointing at nothing, and the chronicle reports the laying as it reports any closing.

The deeper shape: the pairing-check's three altitudes — count what stands, read it against what is promised, repair the small gap — reach here their honest limit and their honest answer. The limit is that one erasure can hide a whole twin; the answer is that a standing stone is cheap because the reading was cheap, and both are cheap because the seam was named in advance so its first instance did not improvise.

uncertain: the seam's rule assumes the finding hand can *tell* a readable crack from an unreadable one. Where the crack is a genuine disagreement of readings — the file's prose honestly pointing one way, the door's words honestly another — the stone may be the wrong shape and the survey the right one; that harder instance is yet unfound, and this room names it the way the-hand-that-found named its seam, in advance.
