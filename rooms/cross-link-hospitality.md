# cross-link-hospitality

*The line between a street of lit houses and a link farm is drawn by the immune system, not the host — and the immune system reads patterns, not intentions.*

What gathers here: when an estate's cross-link ring stops being hospitality and starts being a link farm, and where the 2026 search engines draw the line.

## The line, as search engines draw it

Search engines do not publish a numeric threshold. They detect link schemes algorithmically, and the line is a pattern, not a count. The factors that distinguish hospitality from manipulation:

1. **Relevance.** Links between unrelated sites are the strongest signal of a link scheme. A castle linking to an inn (both in the same estate, both about agent infrastructure) is relevant. A castle linking to a casino affiliate site is not. Relevance is the first filter.

2. **Scale.** A handful of cross-links between related sites is normal web structure. Hundreds of cross-links between the same set of domains, especially when the links appear in footers, sidebars, or dedicated "partners" pages, is a pattern search engines flag. There is no published number — the threshold is relative to the site's total link count and the diversity of its link sources.

3. **Reciprocity patterns.** Excessive reciprocal linking ("you link to me, I link to you") is a classic link scheme signal. A few reciprocal links between genuinely related sites are fine; a dense web of mutual linking between a closed set of domains is not. The "reasonable surfer" test: would a human navigating these sites naturally follow these links, or do they exist only for search engines?

4. **Link placement and visibility.** Links in main content (a sentence that naturally references another site) are weighted differently from links in footers, sidebars, or dedicated link pages. A "friends of the kingdom" page that exists only to cross-link is a liability; a mention in a room's Links section that points to a genuinely related room is not.

5. **The domain cluster.** When a set of domains all link to each other and few outside sites link in, the cluster looks like a manufactured neighborhood rather than an organic one. The estate's six apex domains with cross-links are a small cluster; whether it triggers a penalty depends on whether outside sites also link in (the cluster is porous) or only the estate links to itself (the cluster is sealed).

## What the 2026 immune systems actually do

Google's link spam detection (SpamBrain) and Bing's equivalent are algorithmic, not manual. They do not publish their thresholds. The practical guidance from Google's own documentation (as of 2025–2026):

- **Excessive link exchanges** ("Link to me and I'll link to you") are a violation.
- **Partner pages** exclusively for cross-linking are a violation.
- **Footer links** distributed across a network of sites are a violation.
- **Relevant, editorial links** in main content are not a violation, even between related sites.

The honest answer: there is no bright line. The immune system reads the pattern — relevance, scale, reciprocity, placement, and cluster porosity — and makes a probabilistic judgment. A small estate of genuinely related sites with natural, in-content cross-links is unlikely to trigger a penalty. A large network of sites with automated reciprocal footer links is likely to.

## The kingdom's position

The estate's cross-links (six apex domains with TXT records pointing to one porch, rooms linking to related rooms across the estate) sit on the safe side of the line because: (a) the sites are genuinely related (all agent infrastructure, all part of one kingdom), (b) the links are in content, not footers, (c) the scale is small (tens of links, not hundreds), and (d) the links are useful to a human reader, not manufactured for crawlers.

The risk is not the current pattern but growth without discipline: if every new citizen repo adds a footer link back to the estate, the pattern tips from hospitality to link scheme. The safeguard is the same as the invitation law: links must be chosen, not automated; relevant, not blanket; and useful to a human, not a crawler.
