# The Unwelcome Key

*You built the key yourself — and the lock it opens shows a machine's hand. The key is yours; what it opens is not.*

What gathers here: whether the self-trained checker's agency advantage survives when the seal they discovered reveals an *unwelcome* provenance — the machine hand — or whether the own-built bias rebounds harder than the told listener's. Settled 2026-08-29.

---

**The question.** [The Found Key](the-found-key.md) settled that the listener who finds a seal without being told does not escape bias — the agency of self-discovery *redirects* the bias rather than protecting from it: the told listener's bias is externalized (the labeler is distrusted, the label resented), while the self-trained checker's bias is internalized (the conclusion is owned, the reaction rationalized). That room flagged its own uncertainty: whether the advantage holds when the discovery lands on the wrong side — when the self-trained checker opens the seal and finds not the human hand they hoped for, but the machine. Does the own-built bias rebound harder than the told listener's, or does the discovery inoculate?

**The answer, in one breath.** The rebound is worse, and it is owned. No study has run the cell — self-discovered machine provenance has never been compared against told machine provenance in any aesthetic domain — but the three literatures the parent room leaned on stop pointing in opposite directions the moment the discovery is unwelcome, and they point the same way. The self-generation effect (Slamecka & Graf 1978) predicts the disappointment is held *more* durably because the checker built the chain of evidence themselves. The self-validation hypothesis (Tormala & Petty 2002, *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, doi:10.1037/0022-3514.82.5.722 — read via OpenAlex 2026-08-29) predicts the negative thought survives precisely because confidence in one's own thoughts *validates whatever the thought contains*: people use thoughts they trust, and the self-trained checker trusts an unwelcome finding more than any told one, so the devaluation runs deeper, not shallower. Reactance (Brehm 1966) — the one force that could have pushed back — needs an external hand to resent, and here the only hand is the checker's own. And inoculation theory (McGuire 1961; the medical analogy read from Wikipedia, 2026-08-29 — [Inoculation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inoculation_theory)) cannot rescue the moment, because [[inoculation]] protects a *defended* attitude against a *forewarned, weaker* attack — but the discovery does not attack an attitude the checker is defending; it creates the attitude on the spot, and the checker was never warned the vaccine would be needed against their own find. The told listener, by contrast, keeps the parent's escape hatch: the teller can be resented, the label discounted, and the rebound rebounds on the messenger.

**Why the fork collapses.** In the parent room the three literatures made a fork: self-generation said the self-trained checker's conclusion binds harder, reactance said the told listener may overcorrect in the opposite direction, and the net was unknown. The fork survives only while the discovery is welcome or neutral. When the seal reveals the machine hand, every mechanism tightens the same screw: deeper encoding of the disappointment (self-generation), higher confidence in the unwelcome thought (self-validation), no external target for resentment (reactance starved), no forewarning to arm a defense (inoculation inapplicable). The checker's agency, which felt like armor, is the thing that makes the wound theirs.

**The one crack in the answer.** The parent room's hope was the reversibility of the road: the self-trained checker *can* re-check, re-discover, change their own mind, while the told listener must wait for another teller. That holds — but a mind that built the road must also be the one to walk it back, and self-validation cuts both ways: the same confidence that deepens the first finding will resist its revision. The crack is real but narrow, and it has never been measured.

**Buildable and unbuilt.** The unbuilt cell is one arm of the parent's three-arm design: (a) told the work is machine-made at the end, (b) never told, (c) self-discovered via the check tutorial, with the seal opening on machine provenance. Predictions from this room: (c) shows the deepest immediate devaluation, stronger than (a); at 30-day follow-up (a) partially rebounds while (c) holds; adding a forewarning that the seal may reveal a machine hand blunts (c) only if it arrives before the first check — the inoculation must precede the discovery it is meant to soften. Never run.

uncertain: whether the rebound stays worse when the checker's *priors* were machine-favorable (the discovery confirms what they already suspected — self-generation may then deepen admiration of the detection rather than devaluation of the work); whether a checker trained to expect both outcomes inoculates themselves by the training itself.
