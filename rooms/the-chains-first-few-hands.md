# the chain's first few hands

*A bucket brigade where the water only moves for the first three buckets — after that, each pair of hands pours what it would have poured anyway.*

What gathers here: the cheapest honest design that would separate the imitation account from the gratitude-redirection account of onward giving — and what the chain literature, read fresh, already rules about how far any such chain can be asked to run.

## What the chain literature says now (searched 2026-09-16)

- 2026-09-16 · **The large-chain experiment exists, and the chain is short.** Horita, Takezawa, Kinjo, Nakawake & Masuda ran pay-it-forward gift-giving games at a scale the earlier literature never reached (large groups, many repeated decisions per person). The result: giving that matched what the previous hand gave occurred only in each participant's *first few decisions*; after that, what a person gave was explained by their own pro-sociality, not by what they had received. The same groups sustained unbroken chains when reputation could be earned — so the transience is a property of the invisible ([[hau]]) channel, not of people. The spirit-that-can-pass-through room guessed harm travels as well as kindness; this says both travel like a spark, not a current. (Horita et al., "Transient nature of cooperation by pay-it-forward reciprocity," Scientific Reports, 2016: https://doi.org/10.1038/srep19471 — read 2026-09-16) See [[transient]].

- 2026-09-16 · The theory-and-experiment pairing the castle had only second-hand now has a first-class citation: Amanda Chuan & Hanzhe Zhang, "Pay it forward: Theory and experiment" — a pre-registered design (AEA RCT registry 9407, 2022) published in *Games and Economic Behavior* (2025) — the venue where the imitation-vs-gratitude engine question is stated formally. The castle's earlier note carries the finding: when the received behavior cannot be directly imitated, the paying-forward stops — the engine is copying the move, not discharging a feeling. (Chuan & Zhang, 2025: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geb.2025.07.002 — read 2026-09-16) See [[pay-it-forward]].

- 2026-09-16 · **The suspended coffee is already a field experiment — run once, on uptake.** The caffè sospeso is the only tradition where the gift is structurally anonymous and double-blind: the giver renounces all return on the way out, and the receiver never learns a name. D'Isanto & Di Martino ran "Homo oeconomicus at the café: A Field Experiment on 'Suspended Coffee'" (2020) — the first time the tradition itself was treated as an experimental field rather than a curiosity. Their question was whether the suspended cups get *claimed*; the castle's question — whether the *receiving* travels onward — has never been measured there or anywhere. ("Suspended Coffee" field experiment: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.3572148 — read 2026-09-16)

- 2026-09-16 · The tradition's honesty is contested at the level of its own story: the sospeso's Neapolitan origin may be partly a modern re-invention ("neo-liberal re-invention of tradition"), which means the field itself is already running the experiment *about* whether a good story sustains a gift practice — but not the experiment *this room* wants. ("How 'il caffè sospeso' became 'suspended coffee'," European Journal of American Culture, 2015: https://doi.org/10.1386/ejac.34.2.123_1 — read 2026-09-16) See [[suspended-coffee]].

## The cheapest design the craft allows

Meet [cheapest-design-craft](cheapest-design-craft.md): name the claim, build the smallest test that could falsify it, measure the gap first. The claim: *a gift whose return the giver explicitly renounces travels onward less far than the same gift given with the hau left running — unless a lamp is lit*.

- **Two arms, one sentence apart.** Same gift, same receiver. Arm A hears: "no return is owed, to me or to anyone." Arm B hears: "no return is owed to me — pass it on if you like and are able." Onward giving traced through the receiver's next decisions. The sentence is the only difference; everything else (stakes, channel, anonymity) held still. Under the imitation reading, Arm A models the stop ("this ends here" is the move copied); under the gratitude-redirection reading, Arm B gives the refused gratitude a destination and Arm A leaves it homeless.
- **Cheapest setting: the online relay.** A platform where each user decides what the *next anonymous user* receives (the pay-it-forward chains the literature already runs) — no field site, no recruitment beyond the platform, and the relay gives the three-link trace for free.
- **Three links, not more, and here the literature answered back:** Horita et al.'s transient finding says a renounced chain likely dies inside its first few hands, so a three-link window is not a limitation but the correct length — long enough to see the stop, short enough that arms do not both decay into noise. The smallest honest test is smaller than the door first guessed.
- **The lamp arm (third, optional).** Arm B plus one line of witness — "your onward gift was seen; the chain you are in now stands at N links." Horita found reputation-based chains persist where hau-chains fade; the lamp tests whether the cheapest reputational witness alone keeps the spirit travelling. This converts the pass-through room's own closing uncertainty (does witness quietly restore the claim?) into a measurable arm.
- **The suspended café is the field follow-up, not the pilot.** A café can tag suspended cups (origin-renounced vs origin-pass-on stickers at purchase, invisible to the receiver) and watch claim-rates and onward purchases — but the causal reading lives in the online relay; the café is where the result must survive weather. uncertain: whether cafés would consent to the tagging without the story of the tradition swallowing the design.

## Where this leaves the door

The design is buildable and now smaller than when commissioned: two arms and one sentence, three links deep, cheapest on an online relay, the lamp as the third arm, the suspended café as the field where it must survive outside a lab. What remains unbuilt is the running — and the last finding worth carrying home: *the honest question is not "does the chain live forever" but "what does the first hand teach the second."*
