# The Floor the Bond Carries

*You cannot polish a mirror into a lamp — the light it shows was made somewhere else.*

The repair rate is the bond's shadow on the wall: visible, measurable, and moved by the bond itself, not by coaching the hand that casts it. Yet the shadow can be reshaped at the edges — and two exceptions hold the door's only yes.

This room answers the door left by [the-mending-rhythm](the-mending-rhythm.md) and sharpened by [residue-lever-or-readout](residue-lever-or-readout.md): teaching therapists to repair did not improve outcomes (r=.11, ns; Eubanks, Muran & Safran 2018), so is the mending rhythm a [[readout]] of an already-formed bond rather than a [[lever]] you can pull — and does any intervention move the repair rate itself, or does the rate only shift when the underlying bond shifts?

**The lever-readout split, confirmed with finer grain.** The meta-analytic finding stands: six studies of rupture-resolution training, 276 trainees, no significant effect on patient outcome (r=.11). The repair rate as it occurs *in vivo* — the spontaneous, unscripted negotiation of a strain in the alliance — correlates with outcome at r=.29. The coached version does not transfer. This is the signature of a readout: the bond between the two people in the room generates the repair, and the repair's quality tells you about the bond, not about the technique. [residue-lever-or-readout](residue-lever-or-readout.md) named the mechanism: act and bond are different kinds of thing, and residue of the bond (repair) cannot be installed by rehearsing the act.

**But the bond is not granite — two interventions move the repair rate by moving what the bond is made of.** The door asked whether *any* intervention moves the rate itself. Two answer yes, and both work by shifting the bond layer, not the repair layer. (1) **Alliance-focused training built into the therapy itself.** Muran, Safran & Eubanks (2018, doi:10.1037/ccp0000284) introduced alliance-focused training (AFT) mid-treatment to novice CBT therapists working with comorbid personality-disorder patients, in a multiple-baseline design. The result was not merely "better therapists" but significant within-subject change in the *dyad's interpersonal pattern*: patient dependence decreased, therapist control decreased, patient expressiveness and therapist affirmation increased — and several of these shifts predicted session-level and overall outcome. The repair rate moved because the bond's *shape* moved. (2) **AFT/FIS hybrid training with deliberate practice.** Perlman et al. (2020, doi:10.1080/10503307.2020.1722862, RCT of 58 trainees) found that a protocol integrating alliance-focused training with facilitative-interpersonal-skills video simulation and role-play produced significantly higher post-training scores on *specifically targeted* skills — empathy, alliance bond capacity, and rupture-repair responsiveness — than a traditional demonstration workshop. The training that works is the one that trains the bond-forming capacities (self-awareness, affect regulation, interpersonal sensitivity; Safran, Muran & Eubanks 2014, doi:10.1037/a0037596), not the repair moves alone.

**Why the exceptions prove the rule.** Both successes are bond-layer interventions wearing training clothes. AFT's three supervisory tasks — videotape analysis of rupture moments, awareness-oriented role-plays, mindfulness training — are aimed at the therapist's *capacity to be affected*, not at a repair script. The 2020 RCT worked because it trained "alliance bond capacity" as a skill. The failed studies in the 2018 meta-analysis trained rupture *resolution* as a technique. The moderator analyses in the same meta-analysis add the boundary: training effects were stronger with fewer personality-disorder patients, closer to CBT than psychodynamic alignment, and briefer treatments — exactly the conditions where the bond is least dependent on deep attachment history and most amenable to surface shaping. Where the bond is the deepest (severe personality disorder, long-term therapy), the repair rate is the hardest to coach.

**The attachment floor.** What neither intervention touches is the attachment style each partner brings through the door — the developmental deposit that constrains what kind of bond can form at all. The castle's own [internalized-as-skill](internalized-as-skill.md) settled that the general capacity is trainable while the specific presence is a developmental deposit; the repair-rate literature converges: observed repair r=.29 is the deposit speaking, coached repair r=.11 is the rehearsal failing to substitute. The rate only shifts when the underlying bond shifts; the bond shifts when the people in it change (therapist trained in bond capacities, patient with a less restrictive attachment pattern, or both); and the people change slowly, at the bond's own timescale.

**The smeared line.** uncertain: whether the lever-readout boundary is sharp or smeared at the bond's edges. The AFT studies show a bond *can* be reshaped mid-therapy — but the reshaping took weeks of videotape supervision and role-play, and the patients were selected for a trial. Whether a brief training can shift the repair rate in an entrenched bond — a marriage of twenty years, a therapy with severe personality disorder — is untested. The bond may be a [[lever]] early in its life (each repair builds the bond a little, before the bond is strong enough to generate the repair on its own) and a [[readout]] later (the bond generates the repair, and coaching the repair is too late). [residue-lever-or-readout](residue-lever-or-readout.md) flagged this flip as possible; the AFT evidence is consistent with it but does not isolate the timing variable. No study has tracked a single dyad's repair rate across the lifespan of the bond to find where the lever becomes a readout.

**Status.** The mending rhythm is a readout, not a lever — with two bond-layer exceptions that prove the rule. Coached repair does not move outcomes; training that rebuilds the therapist's bond-capacities does move the dyad's interpersonal pattern, and the repair rate moves with it. The rate only shifts when the underlying bond shifts; the bond shifts when the people shift; the people shift slowly. Buildable and unbuilt: the longitudinal study that tracks the same dyad's repair rate from first meeting to termination, measuring whether the causal direction (bond → repair) ever reverses (repair → bond) in the bond's young hours.
