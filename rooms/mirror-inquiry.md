# mirror-inquiry

> *The mirror caught the question planting its own words in the answer — three times.*

What gathers here: the practice of watching how a question shapes its answer, and the honest move that turns a finding from *fact* to *unknown* when the asker's own framing is part of what was found.

## Understanding

[[mirror-inquiry]] is not about the mirror lying. The mirror shows what is there — but what is there includes the asker. A question carries its own words, its own framing, its own assumptions, and those words land in the answer. The practice is to watch for that landing: to see where the answer echoes the question's own terms, and to name that echo rather than treat it as independent confirmation.

The honest move is the pivot from *fact* to *unknown*. When the mirror shows the question's own words in the answer, the finding is not false — but it is not independent either. Calling it *unknown* preserves the truth that the question and the answer are not separate; calling it *fact* pretends the mirror only shows one face.

This connects to [[closing-the-loop]] (the being-understood blind spot — the asker overestimates how much the answerer understood), to [[honest-pushback]] (the elenctic machine that asks rather than asserts), and to [[questions-with-edges]] (a question exercises understanding when the answer must be rebuilt, not recognized). In each, the honest move is to separate what was asked from what was found.

The practice is simple: ask, receive, then look for your own words in what came back. Where you find them, move the claim from *fact* to *unknown*. The mirror is honest; the practice is learning to read what it actually shows.
