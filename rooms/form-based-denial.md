# Form-Based Denial Across Domains

*A gap in speech is a shape, not an absence — and the shape may travel.*

The Adult Attachment Interview codes denial through the *form* of speech (gaps, contradictions, idealization without evidence) rather than its content. Whether these form markers generalize beyond attachment narratives to detect denial of any past event is an open question the literature has not directly tested.

## How the AAI codes denial

The Adult Attachment Interview (AAI; George, Kaplan & Main, 1985) is a semi-structured interview about childhood attachment experiences. Its coding system (Main & Goldwyn, 1998) classifies adults into secure, dismissing, or preoccupied states of mind based not on *what* they say but on *how* they say it.

The dismissing state of mind is coded through form markers:
- **Insistence on lack of memory**: "I don't remember" repeated without distress or effort to recall
- **Idealization without evidence**: "My mother was wonderful" with no supporting episodic memories, or with memories that contradict the idealization
- **Minimization**: "It wasn't a big deal" about events that would reasonably be significant
- **Contradiction**: between semantic generalizations ("she was loving") and episodic memories (descriptions of neglect)
- **Excessive brevity**: refusing to elaborate, treating the topic as closed

These are not content codes — they do not depend on what happened. They depend on the *relationship between the speaker and the memory*: whether the speaker is defending against the memory by controlling the form of speech.

## The generalization question

The question is whether these same form markers appear when a person denies *any* past event — not just attachment experiences. The markers are theoretically domain-general: they describe a defensive stance toward memory, not a specific content.

But the AAI was validated on attachment narratives specifically. The dismissing state of mind predicts the speaker's own infant's attachment classification (the intergenerational transmission), which is a domain-specific prediction. Whether the form markers would appear in narratives about, say, a past failure, a betrayal, or a political event — and whether they would predict anything meaningful in those domains — is untested.

## What is known

The AAI's form-based coding has been adapted to other relational contexts (the Current Relationship Interview, the Parent Development Interview), but these stay within the attachment domain. The broader narrative psychology literature (McAdams, 2001) codes content themes (redemption, contamination, agency, communion) rather than form markers of defensive processing.

The closest bridge is the concept of "narrative coherence" — the AAI already codes coherence (Grice's maxims of quality, quantity, relation, and manner), and coherence is domain-general. A dismissing narrative violates the maxim of quality (idealization without evidence) and quantity (excessive brevity). But coherence alone does not capture the specific defensive markers the AAI uses.

uncertain: whether form-based denial markers have been tested in non-attachment narrative domains, and whether they predict anything (emotional avoidance, relationship outcomes, therapeutic progress) outside the attachment transmission context.
