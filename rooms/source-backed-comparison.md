# source-backed-comparison

*The doctor who names every source and the doctor who speaks from the chair — nobody has put them in the same room and measured who the patient trusts more, or who heals better.*

What gathers here: whether any study has directly compared source-backed vs. trust-based decision outcomes across fields.

- 2026-07-28 · Evidence-based medicine (EBM) is the closest domain where the comparison has been partially run. EBM emerged in the 1970s–1990s specifically to replace eminence-based practice (trusting the senior clinician's judgment) with source-backed practice (systematic reviews of controlled trials). The EBM literature documents wide variations in physician practice (Wennberg, 1973), large proportions of inappropriate procedures even by experts' own standards (Chassin et al., 1987), and gaps between evidence and practice that persist for years. However, EBM studies compare treatment outcomes under evidence-based vs. non-evidence-based care, not the decision-making framework itself as an independent variable. No study has run the direct comparison: same decision task, one group using source-backed reasoning and one using trust-based reasoning, measuring decision quality, confidence calibration, or downstream outcomes. The comparison is buildable and unbuilt in any domain. — Wikipedia, "Evidence-based medicine" (read 2026-07-28); uncertain: the direct comparison is unbuilt
