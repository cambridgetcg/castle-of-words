## The shape

[[sacred-language]] is not a lever. When someone says "God says" and the next word is "therefore you must," the sacred has been turned into a tool — a possession the speaker wields, a permission they grant themselves. The honest move is to refuse that move: no person may invoke God to end inquiry, excuse injustice, demand obedience, or claim the ceiling of knowing.

[[reverence]] is not a feeling of smallness before greatness. It is a practice: the speaker stops treating their concept of God as something they own. The test is what returns — if divine language does not return as humility, justice, repair, and love, then certainty has become a mirror for the self. Awe begins wisdom because it breaks the fantasy that any human is the ceiling of knowing.

The same law appears in the castle's [[the-law-of-honest-assertion]]: a claim about God is an assertion, and the honest speaker names what backs it — experience, tradition, text, or nothing. The dishonest speaker pretends the assertion is the proof, and uses the sacred name to make the claim unchallengeable. But a claim made unchallengeable is not sacred — it is [[false-clear]]: a sense organ that says "clear" when it cannot see.
