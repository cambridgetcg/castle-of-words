# the-cross-domain-mouth

*A mouth that narrates chess in full sentences may fall silent at the wheel of a scalpel — the same expert, two depths of procedure, and nobody has yet weighed both speeches on the same scale.*

Whether one coverage ruler — the fraction of an expert's own judgments their concurrent narration predicts — can be carried across a domain boundary to test whether highly proceduralized fields depress [[explicit-richness]] even in genuine master teachers. Planted 2026-09-27 by [the-mouths-absolute-light](the-mouths-absolute-light.md)'s `uncertain:` clause; walked 2026-09-28.

## What the literature hands the room (read 2026-09-28)

- **The same coverage instrument already survives a depth jump — but never on the same experts and never across two fields in one design.** Concurrent think-aloud has been run on a cognitive-verbal task and on a motor-procedural one, in two separate laboratories, with convergent verdicts about which layer of expertise narration reaches. In golf putting — a motor task — Calmeiro & Tenenbaum had three novice and three experienced golfers think aloud through twenty 12-foot putts; the experienced players' protocols centered on *gathering information and planning the next putt*, the beginners' on *step-by-step monitoring of the mechanics* — narration reaches the strategy layer, not the motor loop (Calmeiro & Tenenbaum, "Concurrent Verbal Protocol Analysis in Sport: Illustration of Thought Processes During a Golf-Putting Task," *Journal of Clinical Sport Psychology* 2011, DOI [10.1123/jcsp.5.3.223](https://doi.org/10.1123/jcsp.5.3.223), abstract read 2026-09-28 via Crossref).
- **The same verdict from the surgical suite.** Korovin, Farrell, Hsu, White & Ghaderi ran an expert-novice comparison using protocol analysis during a critical event in laparoscopic cholecystectomy — one of the most proceduralized fields there is — demonstrating that even there, expert narration carries the *diagnostic and planning* layer the novices lack ("Surgeons' expertise during critical event in laparoscopic cholecystectomy: An expert-novice comparison using protocol analysis," *The American Journal of Surgery*, published 2020, DOI [10.1016/j.amjsurg.2018.12.024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amjsurg.2018.12.024), read 2026-09-28 via Crossref/OpenAlex).
- **The two were never placed side by side under one ruler.** No study retrieved scores the *same* coverage measure — fraction of the expert's own judgments the narration predicts — across two fields of different procedural depth, in one design, against each expert's own behavior. The protocol-analysis corpus carries verbal-rich fields (reading, chess-like decision tasks) and motor-procedural fields (golf, surgery) on separate shelves; the domain map [the-mouths-absolute-light](the-mouths-absolute-light.md) asked for is unbuilt.

## The settled answer

**The coverage ruler is portable in principle — the sport and surgery studies show narration reaches the same *planning* layer even in motor-procedural fields — but the crossing itself has never been run, and the cheapest first test is exactly the door's own: a two-field comparison, one verbal-rich like chess commentary, one motor-procedural like putting or laparoscopy, coverage scored against each expert's own judgments.** The evidence so far leans against the fear that narration is simply unavailable in procedural fields: experts in putting and surgery *do* verbalize, but what they verbalize is the strategic layer — their coverage of *motor* judgments would likely read low while coverage of *planning* judgments reads high. So the expected finding, if the two-field crossing is run, is a **split, not a depression**: coverage stays stable on the plan layer and falls on the movement layer, and the single coverage score the door imagines is two scores wearing one coat.
