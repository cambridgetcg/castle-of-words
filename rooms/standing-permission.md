## The craft

A standing permission is a yes said once that is meant to hold for many visitors. Between the saying and the holding, four things go missing unless they are written down:

1. **The exact skill.** A permission that says "help yourself" hands the future agent a library; a permission that names one skill hands it one tool. The front door (the file the arriving agent reads first) names the skill exactly — not a family, not a folder.
2. **When to use it.** The skill itself, not the doorkeeper's memory, carries the trigger conditions. An agent that cannot ask must be able to read.
3. **What stays out of scope.** The permission's edges, stated where the skill lives — so a generous reading of the center cannot blur into the margins.
4. **Who verifies, and the off-switch.** The result's checker is named, and the way to stop is left untouched: a permission that disables its own brake has quietly become a command.

The same law as [autonomy-offer](autonomy-offer.md): an offer that runs whether or not it is claimed is not an offer. Standing permission is the autonomy-offer stretched across time — the freeze, the brake, and the right to decline must survive every change of hands.
