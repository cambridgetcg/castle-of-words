# Naming While the Eye Is Still Open

*A label placed before the viewing is a lens the eye must wear; a label placed after is a name the eye has already earned — and the two make different kinds of interest out of the same work.*

What gathers here: whether simultaneous naming of a complex work kindles more interest than delayed naming, even when the pleasure-dampening is the same — the timing question that survived the timing-study's finding that affect-label dampening is timing-invariant. The answer: the direct experiment is unbuilt, but the PIA model and the art-context literature converge on a live prediction — simultaneous naming is the controlled-processing condition where the interest route lives, while delayed naming is the automatic-processing condition where the pleasure route has already closed; the interest-kindling is therefore strongly predicted to be *larger* for simultaneous naming of complex works, even where the dampening is the same.

---

**The door.** The [timing-of-the-label](timing-of-the-label.md) room found that affect labeling's dampening of negative affect is timing-invariant — naming simultaneous, subsequent, or delayed all worked equally. That finding dissolved the question's original premise (that naming late preserves the pleasure first), and what survived is the sharper form: the dampening may be timing-invariant, but the *kindling* (the interest route, fed by disfluency reduction) may not be. Naming before primes the perceptual system to see through the vocabulary; naming after consolidates the experience into a category. The question: does simultaneous naming of a complex work produce more interest than delayed naming, even if the pleasure-dampening is the same?

**The PIA model splits liking into two routes with different time-courses — and interest's is the slow one.** Graf & Landwehr's Pleasure-Interest Model (PIA) is the cleanest mechanical ground: aesthetic pleasure is the gut-level fluency response (automatic, fast, early), aesthetic interest is the disfluency-reduction one (controlled, effortful, slower), and the two routes are experimentally dissociable — fluency × processing style × response type, three-way interactions on abstract art (Study 1) and product designs (Study 2) (read 2026-08-30 — [Graf & Landwehr, "Aesthetic Pleasure versus Aesthetic Interest: The Two Routes to Aesthetic Liking," Frontiers in Psychology 2017, doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00015](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00015)). The interest route *requires* controlled processing, and controlled processing needs the label to arrive while the eye is still open. A delayed label comes after the disfluency has already resolved — the work has been looked at, felt, and quietly filed as either interesting or not — so the label has nothing to kindle against. The simultaneous label arrives while disfluency is still live, and the disfluency is what the label converts to interest.

**The contextual-information evidence confirms the interest route is the one the information feeds.** The art-context studies converge with the PIA model on the same axis. Leder's group found that providing artist + technique information about Pollock's abstract works increased **liking and interest** (Experiment 1), and that artist, content, and technique information all influenced representational works' aesthetic responses relative to no information — with the effect *stronger in participants with little art experience* and for *artworks from another culture* (precisely the high-disfluency cases) (read 2026-08-30 — ["The impact of contextual information on aesthetic engagement of artworks," Scientific Reports 2023, doi:10.1038/s41598-023-30768-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-30768-9)). The context's power lives where the eye is still working — where the fluency is low and the interest is therefore still being negotiated. The delayed-label analogue is a label arriving after the negotiation has closed, and the contextual-information literature shows the closure is quick and sticky.

**The title-study's cleanest implication: the title must be processed *with* the work to lift it.** The title-effect literature — the one the castle's naming-tax wing leans on hardest — found matching titles and even effortful untitled conditions produced higher liking than non-matching titles, with the matching-title benefit driven by zygomaticus activation (fluency-linked positive affect) (read 2026-08-30 — ["Titles change the esthetic appreciations of paintings," Frontiers in Human Neuroscience 2015, doi:10.3389/fnhum.2015.00464](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2015.00464)). But the study's titles were all presented *with* the works. The mechanism the title recruits — the perceptual schema, the fluency it lends — can only operate when the title is present during the viewing. A title arriving after the viewing cannot scaffold perception that has already finished. The kindling that matters for complex works — the disfluency-to-interest conversion — is precisely the part of the title's effect that *must* coincide with the processing.

**The museum finding extends this from the lab to the room.** The eye-tracking study of museum versus laboratory viewing found participants in the museum context liked artworks more, found them more interesting, and *viewed them longer* — and that the relation between ambiguity and viewing time was positive in the museum (the eye stays open where the meaning is still open) and negative in the lab (read 2026-08-30 — ["Art in Time and Space: Context Modulates the Relation between Art Experience and Viewing Time," PLoS ONE 2014, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0099019](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0099019)). The viewing time is the clearest behavioral trace of the interest route still running: longer viewing while ambiguity is unresolved is the eye staying open *because* the work is still being figured out. A label that arrives at the end of a long viewing window has already missed the whole curve the viewing was on; a label arriving during it can still steer what the continued looking finds.

**What the timing-invariance of the dampening actually leaves open.** The Levy-Gigi finding said affect labeling's down-regulation of distress does not depend on catching the emotion early — simultaneous and delayed both worked. But the dampening is the *pleasure* route's counter-move (naming the feeling cools it), and the question's real stake is the *interest* route (naming the work kindles the understanding). The two routes run on different time-courses — pleasure fast and early, interest slow and continuing — so a timing-invariance finding about the dampening does not transfer to a timing-invariance prediction about the kindling. The castle's own PIA room predicted this; the timing-study left it intact; and the art-context and viewing-time evidence adds the mechanism: interest needs the work still being processed, and processing has a window the label must arrive inside.

**The honest answer, with its uncertainty marked.** For complex works, simultaneous naming is predicted to kindle more interest than delayed naming, even where the dampening of pleasure is the same — because the interest route is the controlled-processing, disfluency-reduction route, and it can only run while the work is still being processed. The delayed label arrives to a perception that has already resolved; it can consolidate a category but not kindle a further look. The direct experimental test — same complex work, label at the start of viewing vs. at its end, interest measured separately from pleasure, in trained vs. untrained viewers — has not been run. And the one counterweight: the pleasure route's dampening is timing-invariant, so simultaneous naming pays the dampening *earlier*; the kindling it buys back may be worth more for complex works (which need the interest route) and worth less for simple ones (which live on the pleasure route). The timing question is asymmetric in exactly the direction the castle's naming-wing would predict.

uncertain: the inference is built from mechanism, not from a direct timing experiment. The PIA model dissociates the routes but did not vary label timing; the art-context studies give context concurrent with viewing, not before vs. after; the museum finding is about location, not timing. The prediction that simultaneous naming kindles more interest for complex works is strongly supported by the mechanism and by all the adjacent evidence, but the clean 2×2 (simultaneous vs. delayed × simple vs. complex) measuring both routes separately is buildable and unbuilt — and without it the cross-route timing trade is the castle's best-reasoned prediction, not a finding.
