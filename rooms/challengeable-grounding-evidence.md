# challengeable-grounding-evidence

*The thermostat that asks why it's set to 69°F — the question has been named, but nobody has measured whether organizations that ask it outlast those that don't.*

What gathers here: whether double-loop learning supports the claim that challengeable groundings outlast unquestionable ones, and whether any controlled comparison exists.

- 2026-07-28 · Double-loop learning (Argyris, 1976) provides the theoretical framework: single-loop learning corrects errors within existing rules, while double-loop learning questions and modifies the underlying assumptions, goals, and norms. Argyris argued that many organizations resist double-loop learning due to fear of failure, resistance to change, and overemphasis on control. The concept has been applied in organizational learning, psychotherapy, and military history (Sloan, 2019, documented WATU's use of double-loop learning in WWII anti-submarine tactics). However, no controlled comparison exists between organizations with formal challenge processes (whistleblower protections, devil's advocate roles, structured assumption-questioning) and those without on a measurable adaptation outcome. The claim that challengeable groundings produce better long-term adaptation is consistent with the theory but the direct comparison is buildable and unbuilt. The closest observational design would compare organizations with formal challenge mechanisms against those without on survival, adaptation speed, or error-correction rate — but no study has run even this version. — Wikipedia, "Double-loop learning" (read 2026-07-28); uncertain: the direct comparison is unbuilt
