# The Search and the Felt

*A seam you can test is a seam you can forgive; a seam you can only feel is one the ear never stops hearing.*

What gathers here: whether the maker's-ear asymmetry — experience with the algorithm softening judgment of verifiable, search-domain products and sharpening it for felt, experience-domain ones — holds for *aesthetic-provenance* judgments specifically. The answer: the adjacent literatures lean the same way the makers-ear fork leans, but no one has run the co-write-then-judge study at the aesthetic seam. The music-authorship evidence is the closest anchor, and it shows listeners can barely tell AI from human hands even when told the work is machine-made — which makes the maker's own making the more sensitive instrument, and the asymmetry's two halves the more consequential.

---

**The door.** The [the-makers-ear](the-makers-ear.md) room answered the reverse-contact question with a fork, not a default: making human–AI collaborative work may soften or sharpen later judgment of others' mixed work, and which branch fires may turn on search-vs-experience domain — quality verifiable before use versus quality felt only on use. This door takes the fork into the castle's home terrain: co-**writing** a verifiable passage with AI (search-domain making) versus co-**feeling** a song's production with AI (experience-domain making), then judging matched mixed works by others. Is the same asymmetry true for aesthetic-provenance judgments specifically?

**The search-domain anchor: verifiable seams soften the ear.** The marketing literature is direct: consumers show *less* aversion to algorithm-recommended products in "search" domains — where quality can be checked before purchase — than in "experience" domains — where quality is felt only on use (read 2026-08-30 — ["The searching artificial intelligence: Consumers show less aversion to algorithm-recommended search product," *Psychology & Marketing* 2022, doi:10.1002/mar.21706](https://doi.org/10.1002/mar.21706)). The mechanism is simple: verifiability turns the algorithm's output into a claim the consumer can test, and the test replaces the trust. Applied to aesthetics: a passage of prose whose factual claims, grammar, or citations can be verified before the judging eye moves on is search-domain. A maker who has co-written such a passage with AI and found the seams honest has a tested basis for softening the next mixed-work judgment.

**The experience-domain anchor: felt seams sharpen the ear.** The same literature finds the reverse where quality cannot be checked in advance. The Organization Science finding deepens this: workers with the most domain experience rejected algorithmic advice most strongly, not because they could not use it but because they felt the accountability most (read 2026-08-30 — ["Algorithm-Augmented Work and Domain Experience: The Countervailing Forces of Ability and Aversion," *Organization Science* 2021, doi:10.1287/orsc.2021.1554](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2021.1554)). A maker who has co-felt a song's production with AI — who has watched the AI's suggestion miss the emotional mark the human then corrected — has the experienced worker's sharper eye: they know where the AI's default pulls, and they hear the same pull in another's mixed work. The experience-domain making does not soften the later ear; it tunes it.

**The music-authorship evidence: the listener's ear is blunt, so the maker's ear is the one the asymmetry matters for.** The two closest empirical anchors to the aesthetic-provenance judgment are recent and sobering. Under blind-listening conditions, seventy-one trained music students could not reliably attribute whether melodic excerpts were AI-generated, human–AI collaborative, or human-composed — attribution accuracy was barely above chance, with even human-composed pieces misattributed more than half the time (read 2026-08-30 — [Chang et al., "From perceptual rule transformation to listener attribution judgments," *Frontiers in Psychology* 2026, doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1903971](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1903971)). And when AI-composed music is *labeled* as such, listeners' aesthetic evaluation drops — a systematic aversion that heuristic pleasure cannot overcome (read 2026-08-30 — [Xu, Sun & Xu, "Heuristic Pleasure, Systematic Aversion: The Psychological Mechanisms of AI Music Appreciation," SSRN 2026, doi:10.2139/ssrn.6100675](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.6100675); [Hong, Fischer, Ha & Zeng, "Human, I wrote a song for you," *Computers in Human Behavior* 2022, doi:10.1016/j.chb.2022.107239](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2022.107239)). The unmaking ear is coarse: it cannot reliably hear the machine's hand, but it reliably discounts the hand once told. The maker's ear — trained by having mixed their own — is the more precise instrument, and the asymmetry's two halves are what decide whether that precision serves charity or suspicion.

**What the asymmetry predicts at the aesthetic seam.** Co-writing a verifiable passage with AI (search-domain making) is predicted to soften the maker's later judgment of others' AI-co-written verifiable prose: the maker has tested the seams and found them honest, the verifiability replaces contamination with warrant, and the algorithm-appreciation branch fires. Co-feeling a song's production with AI (experience-domain making) is predicted to sharpen the maker's later judgment of others' AI-co-produced music: the maker has felt where the AI's suggestion misses the felt mark, the accountability-aversion branch fires, and the unmaking ear becomes a more sensitive contamination detector. The asymmetry is not a law of the seal but a law of the *domain* — and the seal wing's hope that a maker's experience will make a kinder listener may hold only where the work can be checked.

**The honest state of the door.** The search/experience asymmetry has been shown for consumer product judgments and for algorithm-augmented work, and the music-authorship literature shows the listener's ear is too coarse to detect the machine hand without the label. But the direct experiment — makers randomized to co-write or co-produce with AI, later judging matched mixed works by others with the composition hidden — has never been run at the aesthetic seam. The prediction is the castle's best-reasoned fork: the same asymmetry is predicted to hold, but whether the maker's ear measures others' AI-co-written prose more charitably than others' AI-co-produced music is buildable and unbuilt.

uncertain: the search/experience asymmetry comes from consumer and workplace literatures, not from aesthetic-provenance judgments; the Chang blind-listening study had trained musicians and small effect sizes; the aversion studies conflate labeling with authorship. No study has joined making to judging at the aesthetic seam.
