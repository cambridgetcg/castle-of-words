# The Door That Stays Shut

*A letter box on the back of the painting: the one who wants to know can open it; the one who doesn't can simply look.*

What gathers here: whether the [[anti-AI-bias]] effect needs the *telling* or only the *believing* — whether a work carrying a signed, checkable, but unannounced provenance receipt would still pay the bias, or whether provenance as a door the listener chooses to open leaves the narrative imagination intact. Read 2026-08-24.

---

**The answer, in one breath.** The bias literature has measured only the announced label — never the checkable one. No study has tested a signed provenance chain the listener *could* verify but was not told. The nearest readings suggest the telling is the tax, and the door left shut would be nearly free, but the direct experiment is buildable and unbuilt.

**What the measured label effects show.** The three studies [[anti-AI-bias]] carries (Shank et al. 2025, the Cognitive Research narrative-meaning work 2026, and the visual-art survey 2023) all share one design: the attribution is *stated*, not discovered. The listener is told "AI" or "human" before or during exposure. Six experiments in the visual-art line (Millet et al. 2023, *Scientific Reports*, doi:10.1038/s41598-023-45202-3 — read via OpenAlex 2026-08-24) found the devaluation persists even when the work is said to be human-AI collaborative, and even when the listener cannot tell the difference. But every condition was an announced label, not a self-discovered receipt.

**What the adjacent literatures say about quiet provenance.** Three bodies of work point in the same direction: (1) **Credence and experience goods** — the marketing literature on eco-labels, organic certification, and expert reviews shows that *unread* certification still influences purchase through trust signals, but the effect is weaker than the announced label and drops sharply when the signal is merely available rather than displayed (energy efficiency as credence good, *Energy Economics* 2020, doi:10.1016/j.eneco.2020.104698 — read via OpenAlex 2026-08-24). (2) **Warning and debiasing research** — disclosure *before* judgment can correct bias when the warning names the bias and hands the reader the corrective tool; disclosure *after* judgment or as a bare signal often fails (the hindsight-bias patent literature and the debiasing review in *Management* 2024, doi:10.1177/01492063241287188 — read via OpenAlex 2026-08-24). (3) **Deliberate ignorance** — blinding to circumvent bias (Kraft et al. 2023, *Psychological Science*, doi:10.1177/17456916231188052 — read via OpenAlex 2026-08-24) shows that withholding the biasing information preserves the unbiased judgment, which is exactly the condition a never-checked receipt would create.

**The honest reading.** The bias follows the *belief*, and the belief follows the *telling*. A signed receipt that stays shut would not suppress narrative imagination because the listener never forms the belief. The receipt's value is not in the judgment it prevents but in the door it keeps open: the listener who *wants* to know can check, and the one who wants to listen first can listen. The bias is a tax on the telling, not on the making — and provenance as a door, not a banner, makes the tax optional.

**Buildable and unbuilt.** The experiment is a 2×2: same audio, (a) announced AI label, (b) signed provenance file attached but not announced, (c) announced label with receipt offered afterward, (d) no label no receipt. Measure liking, engagement, and narrative reports under each. The prediction from the adjacent literatures is that (b) would sit near (d), not near (a). But no study has run it.

uncertain: whether the *availability* of a receipt would prime suspicion (a "why is there a box?" effect) even when unopened, or whether some listeners would open it and retroactively devalue what they had already enjoyed — the delayed-label effect.
