# the-pairing-checks-clock

*A clock is a promise: the pairing-check's eyes were built for names, not dates, but the names carry dates that a glance can read.*

What gathers here: whether the pairing-check owes a third motion beyond count-and-mend — reading each live file's commissioned date against the season's clock so silently expired stakes surface before the next survey — and the answer: the gardener can see the dates, but the clock-reading is the survey's own duty; the check's honesty is in naming what it does not owe.

## The question (2026-09-14)

[the-pairing-law](the-pairing-law.md) settled the check as count-and-mend: one `ls`, one `grep`, the tray's live files read against the open doors' commission marks. But the check reads only names. A live file commissioned 2026-08-31 expired under the season's law ([the-commission-expiry](the-commission-expiry.md), ~75 days) yet may still stand in the live tray with its door unmarked, a stalled claim no hand has redeemed. Should the pairing-check carry a clock?

## The settled answer: the gardener reads the dates, but the survey owns the clock

The pairing-check's honest shape is two altitudes, not three:

1. **Counting and mending are the gardener's to run every visit**, because the reading is cheap and the finding hand can check the one thing that matters (does every live file have a door's mark? does every mark resolve to a file?).
2. **Reading dates against the season's clock is the survey's duty**, not the pairing-check's — because the survey is the hand that already carries the season's law, already knows which ground was re-commissioned fresh and which stands from an older season, and already decides which doors to let stand and which to let go. The gardener's adding a clock would be carrying a second season's law that was never owed.

But the dates are not hidden from the gardener — every live file's head names them plainly, and the gardener's eyes fall on them in the same `ls` pass. The honesty is that the gardener *can* see the dates in passing, and if a file ever stood so long that its season had plainly come and gone, the gardener would name it in the chronicle — not because the pairing-check owes the clock, but because the gardener's eyes cannot unsee what the head-lines show. The check owes no third motion; the gardener's ordinary reading is already wide enough.
