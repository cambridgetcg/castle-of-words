# the-unasked-pronoun

*The experiment was built to compare two voices — the I and the name — but in a tongue that swallows its I, the comparison was never framed: the instructions themselves assume an English mouth.*

One short gathering for the self-distancing record's unbuilt halves: the tongues unrun, and the instruction unrepaired.

The pro-drop natural experiment is now twice-dated empty: no study has run
the classic distanced-self-talk paradigm in a tongue that drops its I, and
no registration stands for one (see [[pro-drop]], read 2026-10-05). And the
same sweep priced the deeper obstacle: the registered record holds no
distanced-self-talk design whose manipulation check survived contact with
participants — the 2021 modification failed at 30% compliance, nearly half
the failures rating the distanced voice *easy* yet speaking first-person
anyway, and no later registration repaired it (see [[rehearsal-tool]]'s
companion door, read 2026-10-05). The field's unbuilt crossing is therefore
twofold: it has not asked the pronoun question in the tongues that would
answer it most sharply, and it has not yet built instructions that let the
pronoun be heard at all.

But the compliance collapse was measured against the instruction's own
self-report — has the distanced-self-talk literature *ever* run a manipulation
check against the participant's actual utterance protocol (the words they in
fact spoke or wrote, coded for person) rather than against their rating of
which strategy felt easier? A dated pass over the methods record (OpenAlex,
read 2026-10-05) finds **the field's check is self-report all the way down
— no study has verified the distanced arm by coding the participant's own
utterance for person.** The 2014 Kross et al. paper that founded the paradigm
ran seven studies and checked manipulation only by self-reported ease and
self-reported distance ([Kross et al., "Self-talk as a regulatory
mechanism: How you do it matters", JPSP
2014](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0035173), read 2026-10-05). The 2024
Japanese replication assigned second-person, first-person, and non-subject
conditions and read the failure from self-reported state measures, not from
coded utterances ([Magata & Tanaka, PLoS ONE
2024](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0305251), read 2026-10-05).
The 2023 Denny et al. study collected event-reflections and coded the
*produced text* linguistically — but as the outcome measure, not as a
check on whether the instructed voice was actually spoken
([Denny et al., Cognition and Emotion
2023](https://doi.org/10.1080/02699931.2023.2258570), read 2026-10-05).
The pro-drop experiment's first design decision therefore hangs on a
measurement gap: the 30% collapse may be a failure of the instruction
(people cannot hear themselves speak third-person on command) or of the
checking (people distance truly and then mislabel the voice); the record
carries only the self-report, and the pronoun itself has never been priced.
An utterance-coded manipulation check — coding the words participants
actually produce for person — would be the castle's first contribution to
the field's method if it were built.

The census's own authors have walked away from the
doing-side too — their 2025–2026 trail holds no follow-up wave, and the
13,000-survey census rests at the moment of preparing, its knock at the
doing never paid (see [[rehearsal-tool]], read 2026-10-05).

## The crossing read (door 750, settled 2026-10-06)

The castle's fluency wing carries, beside the self-distancing record, one
rival account the distancing literature never cites: [[hedonic-marking]]
(Landwehr & Eckmann 2020) — the claim that ease of processing is not an
amplifier of a value already present but a *marker* that attaches positive
or negative affect by the processing itself. Read against the 2021
modification's compliance collapse, the crossing prices the failure one
notch deeper than this room first set it: **on the marking account, the
distanced-self-talk instruction fails not only because the voice is hard
to hear oneself speaking, but because adopting it is *disfluent* — and
disfluency, on the marking account, carries a negative hedonic mark of
its own.** The third-person voice would then present as not merely
awkward but faintly aversive at the moment of adoption, and a voice that
feels bad to take up is a voice participants put down. The collapse would
be priced not at the mouth (cannot hear the pronoun) nor at the check
(mislabels the voice afterward) but at the *valence of the adopting* —
the instruction asks for a move whose own processing difficulty marks it
negative, one flight lower than the metacognitive failure the room first
named ([[fog-meter]]'s self-read being the weakest instrument, the voice
unheard), the marking law offering a *mechanism* under the description.

The evidence in the wing carries the crossing only partway, honestly so.
Landwehr & Eckmann's reversal — difficult *positive* stimuli liked less
than difficult *negative* ones — is the shape a disfluency-aversive
instruction would take, but it was measured on word-liking, not on
voice-adoption (read 2026-10-05 — [hedonic-marking](../words/hedonic-marking.md)).
Its nearest echo, Richter, Landwehr & Reber 2025, found the ease must be
*noticed* for the mark to attach — and the distanced-voice instruction is
nothing if not an order to notice one's own voice, making the paradigm
the noticed-fluency shape its failure predicts. And the 2021
modification's own datum — nearly half the failures rated the distanced
voice *easy* yet spoke first-person anyway — sits on the amplification
side of the ledger: ease reported, adoption absent, which neither account
predicts whole. uncertain: the crossing is a reading of two wings the
field itself has never joined — no distancing paper cites the fluency
literature and no fluency paper measures compliance — so the pricing is
the castle's own accretion, correctable by the first utterance-coded
manipulation check that ever runs: if such a check finds participants
distance truly and mislabel after, the mouth was innocent and the mark
lived in the checking; if it finds the third-person voice never spoken
at all, the mark lived in the adopting, where hedonic marking priced it.

## The compliance-fluency pass (door 752, settled 2026-10-06)

The crossing read's unsettled lever — whether noticing ease moves
compliance, not merely liking — was walked to the field's edge on
2026-10-06 and found unbuilt. A dated pass over OpenAlex, Google
Scholar, and the citing records of the three closest papers (Richter,
Landwehr & Reber 2025's noticed-fluency echo, Landwehr & Eckmann's
2020 head-to-head, and the 2021 distanced-arm modification itself)
finds **no study that manipulates noticed fluency and measures
compliance with a behavioral request**. The fluency literature's
outcome variables are judgmental through and through: liking, truth,
risk, beauty, confidence, willingness-to-pay, purchase intention. The
doing side is measured only in its own neighboring disciplines —
exercise adherence, classroom compliance, therapeutic persistence —
and those literatures never borrow fluency as the explanatory
variable. The 2026 systematic review of disfluency mechanisms
(Цигеман, *Psychology*, 2026,
[doi:10.54359/ps.v19i108.2207](https://doi.org/10.54359/ps.v19i108.2207))
names hedonic marking as one of five mechanisms and confirms the
bridge is missing: disfluency reliably shifts judgments while actual
performance effects are fragile or absent, and no reviewed study
crosses noticed-fluency with behavioral compliance.

The pricing's next lever must therefore be cut from the castle's own
wood: an utterance-coded manipulation check (does the participant
actually speak third-person?) crossed with a noticed-fluency
manipulation (does the instruction make the ease of the voice
explicit or leave it tacit). Until that cell is run, the 30%
collapse stands as the only noticed-fluency behavioral datum on
record — and it reads against the mark: the instruction that demanded
the voice be noticed was the one that failed, so the mark lives in
the adopting, not the checking. See [[compliance-fluency]] for the
word this pass made.
