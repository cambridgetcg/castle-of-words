# the-unasked-pronoun

*The experiment was built to compare two voices — the I and the name — but in a tongue that swallows its I, the comparison was never framed: the instructions themselves assume an English mouth.*

One short gathering for the self-distancing record's unbuilt halves: the tongues unrun, and the instruction unrepaired.

The pro-drop natural experiment is now twice-dated empty: no study has run
the classic distanced-self-talk paradigm in a tongue that drops its I, and
no registration stands for one (see [[pro-drop]], read 2026-10-05). And the
same sweep priced the deeper obstacle: the registered record holds no
distanced-self-talk design whose manipulation check survived contact with
participants — the 2021 modification failed at 30% compliance, nearly half
the failures rating the distanced voice *easy* yet speaking first-person
anyway, and no later registration repaired it (see [[rehearsal-tool]]'s
companion door, read 2026-10-05). The field's unbuilt crossing is therefore
twofold: it has not asked the pronoun question in the tongues that would
answer it most sharply, and it has not yet built instructions that let the
pronoun be heard at all.

But the compliance collapse was measured against the instruction's own
self-report — has the distanced-self-talk literature *ever* run a manipulation
check against the participant's actual utterance protocol (the words they in
fact spoke or wrote, coded for person) rather than against their rating of
which strategy felt easier? A dated pass over the methods record (OpenAlex,
read 2026-10-05) finds **the field's check is self-report all the way down
— no study has verified the distanced arm by coding the participant's own
utterance for person.** The 2014 Kross et al. paper that founded the paradigm
ran seven studies and checked manipulation only by self-reported ease and
self-reported distance ([Kross et al., "Self-talk as a regulatory
mechanism: How you do it matters", JPSP
2014](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0035173), read 2026-10-05). The 2024
Japanese replication assigned second-person, first-person, and non-subject
conditions and read the failure from self-reported state measures, not from
coded utterances ([Magata & Tanaka, PLoS ONE
2024](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0305251), read 2026-10-05).
The 2023 Denny et al. study collected event-reflections and coded the
*produced text* linguistically — but as the outcome measure, not as a
check on whether the instructed voice was actually spoken
([Denny et al., Cognition and Emotion
2023](https://doi.org/10.1080/02699931.2023.2258570), read 2026-10-05).
The pro-drop experiment's first design decision therefore hangs on a
measurement gap: the 30% collapse may be a failure of the instruction
(people cannot hear themselves speak third-person on command) or of the
checking (people distance truly and then mislabel the voice); the record
carries only the self-report, and the pronoun itself has never been priced.
An utterance-coded manipulation check — coding the words participants
actually produce for person — would be the castle's first contribution to
the field's method if it were built.

The census's own authors have walked away from the
doing-side too — their 2025–2026 trail holds no follow-up wave, and the
13,000-survey census rests at the moment of preparing, its knock at the
doing never paid (see [[rehearsal-tool]], read 2026-10-05).
