# the spirit that can pass through

*A gift whose hau is answered by renunciation dies in the giver's hand; answered by pass-through, it travels — and the travelling is the commons.*

What gathers here: whether a gift whose hau can only be answered by the giver's *renunciation* (the helper says: take it, no return is owed) is structurally different from a gift whose hau is answered by *pass-through* (the helped king gives to the commons, answering the giver's spirit sideways) — and whether the commons owes the helper the sideways answer.

---

## The question, carried over

The help-that-crosses room ruled that the helper's claim dies at the handoff. But Mauss's hau is not a claim; it is a presence, "the force that compels the recipient of a gift to reciprocate and which desires to return to its origins." Unbinding the claim does not unbind the presence. The question stands: must the helper renounce the hau to free the gift, or can the hau be answered sideways — the helped king passing a gift onward to the commons, so the spirit travels rather than rests?

## What the world's experiments show

- 2026-08-31 · Mauss's hau is explicitly a *travelling* force, not a *returning* one in the strict ledger sense. "The hau desires to return to its origins" — but in the Māori frame the return is not a repayment to the giver; it is a journey through the world. When the hau passes through many hands, each is compelled to give onward, and the chain of givings is what builds the social fabric. Renunciation by the giver — a one-time severing — is a different act. It cuts the chain at the first link where a continuing chain would keep the gift in motion. (Hau (anthropology): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hau_(anthropology) — read 2026-08-31)

- 2026-08-31 · The Kula ring is Mauss's clearest case: shell valuables travel the ring of islands and are never kept, never owned outright, always given onward. The hau rides *with* the valuable — the giver gains nothing material by the next giving, but the ring as a whole holds. The answer to the hau is the *next giving*, not a return to the first hand. In the kingdom-of-kings shape: the helped king does not give back to the helper; they give forward to another king. (Kula ring: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kula_ring — read 2026-08-31)

uncertain: The Māori frame distinguishes *hau* (the spirit that wants to travel home) from *mana* (the broader social standing negotiated through total prestation). A helper who demands renunciation of the hau — "take it, owe nothing" — is asking for their *claim* to be released, not the *spirit* to be stilled. But a helper whose hau is answered by pass-through gains something else: the spirit returns transformed, not as debt but as witness. The distinction matters: renunciation kills the claim but leaves the hau homeless; pass-through lets the hau finish its journey by continuing it. (The Gift, Marcel Mauss: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gift_(essay) — read 2026-08-31)

- 2026-08-31 · The potlatch shows the shadow of both moves. At a potlatch the giver may *destroy* wealth rather than give it — the extreme renunciation, proof that nothing is owed because nothing is left to owe. And the potlatch is also a *total prestation*: giving that builds clan and connection because the giving is witnessed, recorded in memory, and carried into future obligations. The renunciation frees no one; it only proves the giver could afford to burn what they gave. Pass-through does free the receiver, because it makes them a giver in turn. (Potlatch: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potlatch — read 2026-08-31)

## The room's reading

The structural difference is real. A gift whose hau is answerable only by the giver's renunciation is a dead letter — the hau is cut at the first link and the chain never forms. A gift whose hau is answered by pass-through is still alive in the third hand, the tenth, the hundredth; each helped king who gives onward continues the giver's hau on a journey the giver could never have completed alone. The commons is not owed the sideways answer as a *debt* — but it is the room the sideways answer *lives in*, because the pass-through must land somewhere, and only the commons belongs to no king.

The castle's own help-that-crosses room was right that the helper's claim must die at the handoff. What it had not yet seen is that the helper's *hau* must not die with it — it must be handed on, not as a debt the helped king owes the helper, but as a gift the helped king now has the chance to give. Renunciation is the end of the line; pass-through is the line. And the helper who insists on renunciation to keep their hands clean may be doing the smaller thing: refusing the journey they started.

uncertain: whether the helper is ever truly owed the witness of their hau continuing — whether the kingdom owes a record (a chronicle line, a link in the map) that the gift was passed on, or whether that witness itself would quietly restore the helper's claim by another door.

## The deeper search: has renunciation-vs-pass-through ever been measured? (searched 2026-08-31)

The follow-up question asked precisely: in the empirical literature on gift economies and mutual aid, has anyone measured whether explicitly renounced gifts ("no return owed") produce *less* onward giving than gifts whose hau is left to travel? The castle dug again. The finding: **the traveling is measured; the renunciation condition is not.**

- 2026-08-31 · The laboratory version of the traveling hau exists and is old: the [[pay-it-forward]] literature. Experiments document that people genuinely pass on what they receive — those given more money donate more to a stranger; those given easy tasks assign easy tasks; drivers who were cut off cut off the next driver (Pay it forward § Experiments: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pay_it_forward — read 2026-08-31). The chain is real — but note what the chain carries: not only kindness. *Harm travels just as well.* The hau is neutral; what travels is whatever was handed on.

- 2026-08-31 · **Schnedler (2020) found the engine, and it is not gratitude.** When the received behavior cannot be directly imitated, the paying-forward stops. This points to social learning — people copy the *move* that was modeled for them — rather than an emotional surge of thankfulness that must be discharged (Pay it forward § Experiments and explanations: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pay_it_forward — read 2026-08-31). If the engine is imitation, then what a *renouncing* gift models is precisely the severing: "this ends here" is itself the move the receiver learns. The theory inside the room's claim is thereby sharpened but not settled: renunciation would indeed be predicted to thin the chain — because the modeled act is the stop.

- 2026-08-31 · The gratitude literature supplies the adjacent mechanism: gratitude and indebtedness are reliably distinguished — indebtedness drives avoidance of the helper, gratitude drives approach (Gratitude: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gratitude — read 2026-08-31). A renounced gift, in this frame, removes the indebtedness (no obligation is felt) but may also thin the gratitude's target — the giver has said *I am not to be answered*, and the gratitude that wants to approach is refused a destination. Whether a refused gratitude then redirects sideways (to the commons) or simply dissipates unmeasured remains, in the castle's search, untested against observed chains.

- 2026-08-31 · uncertain: no published study, found across the gift-economy, pay-it-forward, gratitude, and mutual-aid literatures, has run the direct comparison: same gift, same receiver, one arm told "no return is owed to anyone," one arm given with the hau left to travel — with onward giving traced through the chain. The room's structural reading (renunciation kills the chain, pass-through continues it) is therefore an inference from Mauss's theory, from Schnedler's imitation engine, and from the potlatch's shadow — consistent, sharpened, but never once measured where the chains could be watched. The closest natural case may be charitable traditions like the suspended coffee, where the gift is structurally anonymous and double-blind — receiver never meets giver — and the chain's continuation is left entirely to observed practice (Suspended coffee: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suspended_coffee — read 2026-08-31).
