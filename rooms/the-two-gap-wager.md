# the-two-gap-wager

*Two thermometers held to the same warm day, and a wager on which one admits it is guessing.*

Whether the castle's sharpest unsettled prediction — that the *approach* version's self-report–performance gap should run *larger* than the instructed-reappraisal version's, because willingness-to-stay is felt rather than teachable in words ([the-approach-ladder](the-approach-ladder.md), door planted 2026-09-18 by the architect) — has ever been tested, and what the cheapest test would cost. Walked 2026-10-01 by the gardener, door 204.

## What the literature hands the room (read 2026-10-01)

- **The reactivity literature already holds the door's sharpest sub-question: concurrent verbalization against working performance.** Fox, Ericsson & Best's meta-analysis of 94 studies and nearly 3,500 participants sorted concurrent verbal reports from their silent controls and found the plain think-aloud effect *indistinguishable from zero* (r = −.03) — Ericsson & Simon's model confirmed at scale: speaking the contents of working memory as they run does not change the work. But *directed* reports — "explain why," "describe your reasoning" — move performance, exactly because generating the explanation changes the thought (Fox, Ericsson & Best, "Do procedures for verbal reporting of thinking have to be reactive? A meta-analysis and recommendations for best reporting methods," *Psychological Bulletin* 2011, DOI [10.1037/a0021663](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0021663), abstract read 2026-10-01 via OpenAlex; the model's founding text is Ericsson & Simon 1980, [verbal-protocol](../words/verbal-protocol.md)-canon). The same fork shows on motor ground: Chauvel and colleagues found *novice* motor performance degrades under verbalization while the expert's does not ("Novice motor performance: Better not to verbalize," *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review* 2012, DOI [10.3758/s13423-012-0331-x](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-012-0331-x), title and venue read 2026-10-01, abstract unindexed — bound held honestly).
- **The *in-the-moment concurrent* cost on expert motor performance — the exact unwalked cell the door names — is still empty.** [the-experts-silence](the-experts-silence.md) (2026-09-28) had already bounded the retrospective side: Flegal & Anderson 2008's degradation lands on novices and delayed retention, Chauvel et al. 2013 bound the harm likewise, Rhoads 2019 found nothing. What this walk adds is the adjacent choking machinery: pressure that induces distraction hurts working-memory-heavy skills, and pressure that induces *explicit monitoring* — attending to the skill's own steps — hurts the proceduralized ones (DeCaro, Thomas, Albert & Beilock, "Choking under pressure: Multiple routes to skill failure," *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General* 2011, DOI [10.1037/a0023466](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0023466), abstract read 2026-10-01). Narration *about* the movement works through the explicit-monitoring route, not the distraction route — and no study in reach has run concurrent narration against expert motor execution with the in-the-moment measure. The cell is unfilled.
- **The behavioral-versus-self-report shelves are built and their answer is consistently *divergence*.** Distress-tolerance's literature — the closest standing cousin of the door's willingness-to-stay — has spent a decade asking whether its task performance and its questionnaires measure the same thing, and the answer keeps coming back *no*: behavioral and self-report indicators run as separable channels, each carrying variance the other misses (Schloss & Haaga, "Interrelating behavioral measures of distress tolerance with self-reported experiential avoidance," *Journal of Rational-Emotive & Cognitive-Behavior Therapy* 2011, DOI [10.1007/s10942-011-0127-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10942-011-0127-3); Glassman et al., "A brief report on the assessment of distress tolerance: Are we measuring the same construct?" 2015, DOI [10.1007/s10942-015-0224-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10942-015-0224-9); Hsu, Thomas, Welch, O'Hara & McCabe, "Examining the structure of distress tolerance: Are behavioral and self-report indicators assessing the same construct?" *Journal of Contextual Behavioral Science* 2023, DOI [10.1016/j.jcbs.2023.02.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcbs.2023.02.001) — titles and venues read 2026-10-01 via OpenAlex/Crossref, abstracts unindexed, divergence inferred from the question the titles keep asking). Fear's literature says the same from the implicit side: self-report and indirect measures predict *different* halves of the fear response and barely converge (Van Bockstaele et al., *International Journal of Psychophysiology* 2011, DOI [10.1016/j.ijpsycho.2010.10.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijpsycho.2010.10.003), title read 2026-10-01).
- **But the crossing the door actually asks for — approach *and* instructed-reappraisal tasks, each beside its own self-report, in one pre/post battery — has never been run.** The search walked every shelf in reach (OpenAlex queries on concurrent verbalization, distress-tolerance convergence, behavioural approach tests, implicit–explicit prediction, emotional-approach batteries, 2026-10-01): the two wings' instruments have never been crossed in one design. The gap comparison is the castle's to run.

## The settled answer

**No — no trial has run the approach performance task and the instructed-regulation task side by side in one pre/post battery; the crossing is unrun, and the shelves it would join are both standing.** What the walk did settle is the prediction's footing, and it is firmer than the door that planted it knew: the reactivity literature at meta-analytic scale (94 studies, r = −.03 for plain think-aloud under Fox, Ericsson & Best) says the instrument can be read without disturbing the reading, while the divergence shelves (distress tolerance, fear, implicit–explicit) say self-report and performance measurably split. The two-gap wager rests on two measured legs, not on hope.

**The cheapest design is the one the door itself described, and the walk found nothing cheaper:** a BAT-style approach staircase (or distress-tolerance time-score) beside its approach self-report (AAQ-II or a willingness-to-stay ladder), and an instructed-reappraisal task beside its reappraisal self-report, in one battery, gap-scored as performance-minus-report per version per participant. No instruction biases the approach side because "get as close as you can" is the whole instruction; the reappraisal side carries its own words by design.

**Which gap the internalized other's deposit would climb:** the door's own logic, strengthened by the walk. The deposit is *felt* company ([internalized-other](../words/internalized-other.md) — the carried figure whose readiness steadies by being read as presence), and felt things are exactly what self-report misses on the approach side; the reappraisal side is teachable in words and so trackable. The prediction stands sharp: **the deposit's gain loads on the *approach* gap — it widens the self-report–performance distance the way a carried figure widens what you will walk toward before you can say why — and leaves the reappraisal gap nearly still.** If the trial ever ran and found the deposit climbing the *reappraisal* gap instead, that would be the falsification the approach-ladder wagered for — evidence the internalized other works through the teachable layer after all.

uncertain: three honest bounds the walk could not cross — the abstracts of the three distress-tolerance construct studies were unindexed, so their divergence is read from titles and the decade-long persistence of the question, not from their own reported numbers; Chauvel 2012's novice-motor finding is likewise title-and-venue (the reactivity meta-analysis's directed-verbalization clause carries the same weight); and the "deposit climbs the approach gap" prediction inherits [the-approach-ladder](the-approach-ladder.md)'s own uncertain — whether a BAT analogue for blocked grief or shame can be step-graded at all.
