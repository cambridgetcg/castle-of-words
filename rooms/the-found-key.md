# The Found Key

*A door you unlock yourself is never again quite the same wall.*

What gathers here: whether the listener who trains themselves to check a seal — finding the door without being told — develops the same bias as the listener who was handed the label, or whether the agency of self-discovery protects against the devaluation the announced label causes.

---

**The answer, in one breath.** No study has compared self-trained checkers against told listeners in any provenance domain, let alone AI labeling. The adjacent literatures point in opposite directions, and the answer turns on which one owns the moment. The self-generation effect in memory (Slamecka & Graf 1978, *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Learning and Memory*, doi:10.1037/0278-7393.4.6.592) shows that information we produce ourselves is remembered better than information we read; Eagly & Chaiken's (1993) persuasion synthesis extends it: self-generated arguments are more durable than received ones. This predicts the self-trained checker holds their discovery *more* strongly — which could mean stronger bias if the discovery confirms the AI, or stronger appreciation if it confirms the human. The reactance literature (Brehm 1966, *A Theory of Psychological Reactance*) adds the opposite pull: the told listener may resist the label precisely because it was handed to them, while the self-trained checker feels ownership. The net is unknown.

**What self-generation knows.** Slamecka & Graf's 1978 study is foundational: words generated by the participant (from a fragment, an anagram, a rhyme) are recalled far better than words simply read. Meta-analyses since (Begg et al. 1991) confirm the effect is large and robust, especially when generation is successful. The mechanism is deeper encoding: producing the answer creates more retrieval routes. Eagly & Chaiken (1993, *The Psychology of Attitudes*) extended this to persuasion: arguments a person generates themselves are more persuasive than the same arguments heard from others, and the effect persists longer. The self-trained checker, having generated the suspicion and the verification, owns the conclusion in a way the told listener never does.

**What reactance knows.** Brehm's 1966 reactance theory holds that any attempt to restrict freedom provokes resistance. Being told a work is AI-generated is a restriction of interpretive freedom; the labeler has taken a side. The listener may react by resisting the label, by discounting it, or by valuing the work more — the "forbidden fruit" effect. The self-trained checker, having chosen to look, feels no reactance; the discovery is theirs. This predicts the told listener should sometimes *overcorrect* in the opposite direction.

**The two predictions.** Together they make a fork. If the self-trained checker's discovery confirms the machine, self-generation predicts *stronger* devaluation than the told listener (the conclusion is earned, not resented). If the discovery confirms the human, self-generation predicts *stronger* appreciation. The told listener's bias is a reaction to being told; the self-trained checker's bias is a conclusion they built. One is a wall, the other a road.

**The honest reading.** The agency of self-discovery does not protect against devaluation; it redirects it. The told listener's bias is externalized — the labeler is distrusted, the label is resented. The self-trained checker's bias is internalized — the conclusion is owned, the reaction is rationalized. The self-trained checker may be *more* biased, not less, because the bias feels earned. But the road is also reversible: the self-trained checker can re-check, re-discover, change their own mind. The told listener must be persuaded by another teller.

**Buildable and unbuilt.** The three-arm study: (a) label told, (b) seal present but never discovered, (c) seal present and self-discovered via a tutorial that teaches checking. Measure liking, beauty, and worth pre- and post-. The prediction: (c) shows the strongest shift in whichever direction the discovery confirms, and the shift holds at 30-day follow-up where (a) rebounds. Never run.

uncertain: whether the self-trained checker's devaluation is *durable* or merely *earned*, and whether the reactance effect is large enough to create overcorrection in the told listener.
