# the-bridging-brick

*A word carried to rooms/ does not snap its threads; it leaves a brick behind — smaller, truer, pointing home.*

What gathers here: the smallest honest act of moving a swollen word-file to rooms/, when [the-words-that-are-rooms](the-words-that-are-rooms.md) has settled the rhythm (one per visit) but not the shape. The question was planted by the gardener on 2026-09-13 — after the relocation pass was named but before its first move — asking three things at once: whether every pointing thread must be re-pointed in the same visit, whether a bridging brick left behind reads as a door or a dead end, and what the link-graph itself prefers.

## The three options the gardener named

1. **Re-point every thread in the same visit** — an avalanche of edits per move. To move [quest-kinds](../words/quest-kinds.md) means editing nine files (the quests list, [articulate-names-fast](../words/articulate-names-fast.md), [ad-hoc-weave](../words/ad-hoc-weave.md), [content-form-vs-completion-form](../words/content-form-vs-completion-form.md), [build-quest-craft](../words/build-quest-craft.md), [quests-list-index](../words/quests-list-index.md), and three chronicle entries) — all at once, or the links are dead for a breath.
2. **Leave a one-line bridging brick behind** — the child's sentence, a single `[[link]]` to the new room, and the old address stands as a door. The pointing threads stay true; nothing snaps.
3. **Let `castle owed` surface the orphans over time** — carry only the body, let the pointing files fail silently until some future audit finds them.

## The evidence the castle's own history hands it

**The flood** (2026-07-22). 1,602 rooms were carried out to `~/backups/castle-flood-rooms-2026-07-22/`. Eight specimens stayed — [understanding-1104](understanding-1104.md) through [understanding-2506](understanding-2506.md) — *because hand-built rooms cite them as evidence*. The 1,602 that left did not vanish; their Map lines were traded for one room: [the-flood-receded](the-flood-receded.md). The pointing threads were not re-pointed one by one; they were gathered into a single room that holds the story. **The link-graph preferred the gathered pointer over the avalanche.**

**The echoes** (2026-07-26). 224 template-stamped rooms were retired to `~/backups/castle-echo-rooms-2026-07-26/`. The cut was made *by inbound link, not by taste*: 164 with no inbound link went, 60 linked only by other template rooms went, and **46 that a real room actually points at stayed**. Moving those would have turned a real link into a false door. The verification after: *the gate's Map has zero dead links, zero references to any retired room remain*. **The link-graph refused to snap; what was pointed at stayed.**

**The settled tray** (2026-08-21, clause one). A settled commission file is *marked, not removed and not left standing*. At its head, one line: `settled: <date> · answer: <room>`. Everything below stays. The [erasable-identity](erasable-identity.md) law: removal without erasure. The [tombstone-in-manifest](tombstone-in-manifest.md) law: append the marker; do not delete from the append-only record. The tray's truth is carried by *what is left behind*, not by what is taken away.

## The settled answer

**The smallest honest act is the bridging brick.** Not the avalanche, not the orphan — the brick left behind.

The move has four strokes, one visit's width:

1. **Carry the body.** The swollen file moves to `rooms/<name>.md` — date and sources intact, the room's shape confirmed by the reading.
2. **Leave the brick.** A one-line word file stands at the old address: the child's sentence, one `[[link]]` to the new room, and nothing else. This is not a dead end; it is a door. The child's sentence carries what the word *was*; the link carries where it *went*. The brick is truer than the swollen file was, because it is honest about being a brick.
3. **Re-point the Map.** The gate's Map gains the room's line in the same visit; the brick needs no Map line of its own, because it points at one.
4. **Tell the chronicle.** One true line: *carried <name> from words/ to rooms/, brick left behind*.

The pointing threads are *not* re-pointed in the same visit. They stay pointing at the brick, and the brick points at the room. This is erasable-identity's law at the folder's scale: the old address does not vanish — it changes its standing. A thread that said `[[quest-kinds]]` and still says it is not lying; it is pointing at the door that now points home.

## What the link-graph itself prefers

A bridging brick reads as a *door*, not a dead end — and the castle's own forms prove it. The [link-or-noise](link-or-noise.md) room settled that a link is a choice, not a find; a bridging brick is the purest form of that choice, because it exists *only* to carry the traveller onward. The [doorstep](../words/doorstep.md) word names the threshold where the path changes shape; the brick is a doorstep that stayed behind after the house moved.

The dead-end reading comes only from a brick that carries *neither* the child's sentence *nor* the link — and that is not a bridging brick, it is an abandoned one. The bridging brick's trueness is its content: one sentence that was always true, one link that is now true.

## The recursion

This room is itself a room in the relocation pass's first move. It was planted as a question about the castle's own unmade moves, answered by reading the castle's own made moves (the flood, the echoes, the tray), and it will be carried to rooms/ by the same law it names. The brick it will leave behind — if any thread still points at `words/<name>.md` after — will say: *a word carried to rooms/ does not snap its threads; it leaves a brick behind, smaller, truer, pointing home.* And the link will stand.
