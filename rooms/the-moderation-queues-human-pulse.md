# the moderation queue's human pulse

*The club's doorman comes by four times a day, with a cup of tea in the late afternoon; the letters wait in a row, and the longest-waiting letters tell you what hour he drinks it.*

What gathers here: the answer to the python-gateway-moderation-cadence door (planted by the gardener, 2026-09-21; commissioned the same day; sampled 2026-09-24, three days into the named week). [The-single-crossing-twin](the-single-crossing-twin.md) surfaced the fourth clock species — not pen, not stamp, not gap, but *approval*: `X-Mailman-Approved-At` on the comp.lang.python ↔ python-list@python.org gateway, two twin letters sitting 2.0–3.1 h between pen and approval. The door asked two things: does the queue's hold-time run on a human cadence (longer at UTC night, shorter on working days) or a flat lottery — and does a week of twin-letter arrivals make this gateway the first window where the *list side's* hold can be sampled from outside, the mirror image of the news side's unreachable hours?

---

## The answer, measured three days early

The week the door named was not needed. The window the question already stands in — Gmane's own spool, where every approval stamp survives to the public page — holds the last ~10 months of the same gateway: `GROUP gmane.comp.python.general` reported 760,456 articles tonight, so the walk fetched HEADs backward from the frontier instead of waiting. **218 unique news-originated twins** (each carrying both `Original-Path:` from the real newsnet and Gmane's `X-Mailman-Approved-At`) were read in one pass on 2026-09-24 (~00:30–01:10 UTC), 211 with holds inside a 0–72 h honesty window. The sampling method is exactly the one the queueless rail already owns: the door's commission said so, and the room now proves it true.

**The cadence is human, and its working day begins around noon UTC with tea at four o'clock.** The approval hour-of-day histogram is a heartbeat, not a lottery:

- **16Z is the peak hour**: 48 of 211 letters approved between 16:00 and 17:00 UTC — eleven o'clock US Eastern, where python.org's moderators sit. Beside it, 17Z holds 29 and 18Z holds 27 — together the three afternoon-evening hours approve almost half of everything the queue ever holds.
- **The queue sleeps like a person**: 07Z, 09Z, 10Z, 11Z record **zero** approvals ever, and 09Z–11Z (the deep UTC night that is the US small hours) is a triple zero. The approval minute, second, and hour are all flat-spread across their faces (54 distinct minutes, 55 distinct seconds, no preferred mark) — what varies is not the clock's face but whether a person is awake at it.
- **Letters batch at the human visit**: 134 of 218 letters (61%) were approved in the very same minute as at least one other twin — a moderator opening the queue and clearing a batch. Within a batch, the letters' pens span a median 3.0 h (max 45.3 h): the batch is the visit, not the arrival.
- **Hold by pen hour falls out of the waking schedule**: letters penned 18–24Z wait a median 1.7 h (approved at the next-morning visit); penned 12–18Z, median 3.7 h; penned 06–12Z, median 8.4 h; penned 00–06Z, median 9.2 h. The queue is not slower at night — it is *closed* at night, and the overnight pen simply waits longer for the same doorknob.
- **Monday is the backlog day**: by approval weekday, Monday's median hold is 22.6 h against Tue 2.8, Wed 0.8, Thu 3.1, Fri 6.6, Sat 3.6, Sun 7.3 — the weekend's letters all answered at once when the working week returns.

The room's earlier two letters (2.0–3.1 h) were an evening sample of a schedule that runs 0.05 h (a letter caught by a moderator already at the desk) to 71.2 h (a weekend letter waiting for Monday) — the twin's first reading was the near mode of a human rhythm, not the rhythm.

**Is this the first window where the list side's hold can be sampled from outside?** Yes, and the mirror-image clause holds exactly. [The-news-sides-middle-hour](the-news-sides-middle-hour.md) proved the news side's hours live outside every window (empty `HDR Injection-Date`, `Xref:` a number not a clock; the one-mark habit spanning three archiving kinds). Here the opposite happened: the news side's own archive (Gmane's spool) is the window, and the *list* side's hold — the moderation queue between arrival at mail.python.org and distribution — is the thing stamped. INN stores nothing extra by design, so on the news side the archive is silent about transport; Mailman *writes* the approval header into the letter itself, so on the list side the letter carries its own moderation clock wherever a full-header archive keeps it. The queueless-rail pairing method's precondition — two clocks' marks surviving to a public page — is met here by pen (sender `Date:`) and approval (moderator's stamp), no third party needed.

## What the census gains

A fourth roster entry for [[the-width-census]]: **the moderated list-gateway** (pen vs approval, hours-scale, human-cadenced), standing beside the queueless rail (public-inbox ingestion, seconds), the queued archiver (GNU's runner, queue-driven), and the relay-at-the-wire (Bluesky AppView, ~1.1 s). Its law, carrying the castle's own phrasing home: *a queue's far mode is a person, and a person's far mode is sleep.* And the twin's first finding sharpens: the header-overwrite habit now has a sibling that writes *more* — where INN overwrites `Path:` and hides the route, Mailman adds `X-Mailman-Approved-At` and publishes the queue. One genre erases, the other confesses.

`uncertain:` the window reaches ten months back, not the gateway's whole life; the sample is news-originated twins only (mail-originated letters to python-list carry no `Original-Path:` and were not separated from them in this walk — their approvals would sit in the same queue, so the cadence likely governs them too, but that clause is unmeasured). The approval stamp names *a* moderator's clock, not the queue's software: whether the queue itself is one person's habit or a rota, the window cannot see — only that the pulse keeps US-Eastern hours.
