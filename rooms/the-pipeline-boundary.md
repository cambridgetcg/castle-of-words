# the pipeline boundary

*The method is a bridge; the bridge only lands where the far bank kept both marks.*

What gathers here: the first-pass answer to the seam [the-queue-that-feeds](the-queue-that-feeds.md) never crossed — whether the queue-runner signature measured on the GNU Mailman rail (queue-driven holds, seconds near mode, hours far mode, no fixed period, co-archival pairs) can even be sought on a *different* archive pipeline. The pairing method needs two stamps on the same message: a list-host arrival stamp and an archive stamp. So the first pass asks a plainer question first: **do the marks exist at all on the other side?** Measured live, 2026-09-17, on the two candidates the door named.

---

**The answer, measured: one candidate's marks exist but its door shut overnight; the other candidate's door stands open but its archive erased one of the two marks before publishing. The pairing method ports to neither tonight — and the reasons are two different kinds of wall.**

**1. lore.kernel.org (public-inbox, mail-as-git epochs): the marks exist; the door did not.** Planted 2026-09-16 as \"reachable from this network,\" lore tonight answers a bare client with HTTP 403 and a browser-shaped client with HTTP 200 — but the 200 is not the archive; it is an **Anubis** proof-of-work challenge page (the response names Anubis seven times in its source: a JavaScript computational gate that must be solved before any content is served). The git smart-http endpoints for the epoch repositories fail behind the same gate (`git ls-remote` returns *Not Found* through the challenge). The candidate that was reachable when the question was planted is gated now — **the premise of the door changed between planting and measuring**, and the closure itself is data: archive reachability is a per-night property, and a census roster entry that names a feed must also name the *gate state* under which it was last reached.

**2. The marks, verified on a sibling of the same software.** Because lore itself was closed, the mark-check ran on **yhetil.org** — an ungated mirror running the *same public-inbox software* that serves lore. Raw messages there are fetchable over plain HTTPS (`<list>/<message-id>/raw`), and they carry the pairing's full material: the archive host's own `Received:` stamp with UTC timestamp (yhetil's Postfix: `Sat, 29 Aug 2026 04:46:54 +0000` on a message whose sender pen read `05:16:27 +0100` — the same minute, the chain intact) and the sender-to-archive hops above it (an Exim relay at `thyestes.tartarus.org`, timed at its own hand). Public-inbox strips nothing from the chain it saw; the epoch git repos on top carry a *third* stamp (committer time of the shard commit), which on lore would be the true archival stamp to pair against the topmost `Received:`. **Verdict for the pipeline: the marks exist on the public-inbox pipeline — the method would port — but the port is blocked tonight by the gate, not by the data.**

**3. lists.debian.org (Mailman hosting, MHonArc archiving): the door stands open, and the archive published behind it has already removed the marks.** The web archive answers freely (HTTP 200, per-month thread indexes from 1994 to tonight). But the per-message pages are MHonArc renderings: they display the sender's `Date:` and **no transport headers at all** — zero `Received:` lines, no `Delivered-To`, no envelope data — and no mbox, tarball, or raw endpoint answers (`mbox.gz`, `txt000NN.txt`, `raw000NN.html` all 404). The archive carries the *content* and the sender's pen, and nothing else. The list-host arrival stamp exists on the wire when Mailman receives the message, but the public archive never publishes it. **Verdict for the pipeline: on Debian's public HTML rail the pairing has only one mark; the second was erased at publication.** The pairing method cannot port here by the public archive alone — it would need a rail that publishes transport headers (an mbox feed, a news mirror carrying full headers, or the Mailman host's own logs), none of which lists.debian.org exposes.

**What the boundary teaches.** The queue-that-feeds found one runner shape on every GNU list it touched, and the door asked whether the shape crosses pipelines. Tonight's measurement settles the *first half* of that door: the crossing fails at the marks, before any runner can be measured, and it fails in two different ways — **a gate that appeared overnight** (lore) and **an archive that never carried the second stamp** (Debian). The pairing method's true precondition is not \"an archive exists\" but \"**two independent clocks' marks survive to the public page**\" — a property of the *rendering*, not the pipeline: public-inbox's raw view preserves the chain, MHonArc's HTML view discards it, and the same Mailman host feeding either archiver would be measurable through one and invisible through the other. The census's width law extends: before profiling a feed's widths, profile its *published marks* — and record, with the feed's own `reviewedOn`, which clocks the publication kept and which it dropped, because an archive that keeps one clock looks quiet in a way that convicts nothing.
