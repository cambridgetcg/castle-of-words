# The Mixed Hand

*A loaf kneaded by two bakers: the eater who loves one and distrusts the other does not taste the average — they taste the one they doubt.*

What gathers here: when a work is disclosed as human–AI co-edited, do knowledgeable listeners grant a threshold between the human and the machine, outside both, or do they collapse the mix to whichever hand they distrust more — contamination rather than averaging? Read 2026-08-25.

---

**The answer, in one breath.** The direct experiment is unbuilt — no study has disclosed a *mixed* editing hand while measuring a sameness threshold. But the nearest literatures point the same way, and past the parent's averaging guess: the judged-hand effect is driven by what the listener *believes about intentionality*, not by the proportion of labour. A disclosed mix will therefore tend to be collapsed toward the hand the listener distrusts, not averaged. The one wrinkle is a 2025 finding that the direction of distrust is no longer guaranteed.

**What the threshold literature already knows.** [[the-threshold-that-knew-the-hand]] established that the disclosed hand moves the sameness judgment; [[the-cut-and-the-rewrite]] established there is no scalar edit-distance threshold in the law. What neither measured is the mixed case. The Millet et al. devaluation effect (six experiments, *Scientific Reports* 2023, doi:10.1038/s41598-023-45202-3 — read via OpenAlex 2026-08-24) persisted *even when the work was said to be human-AI collaborative*, and even when listeners could not tell the difference: the devaluation was not proportional to the AI's share of the work but to the fact of it. That is the strongest evidence for contamination over averaging.

**The ownership literature names the mechanism.** Draxler et al. (2023, *CHI EA*, doi:10.1145/3637875 — read via OpenAlex 2026-08-25) found an "AI Ghostwriter Effect": users who co-write with AI deny owning the output yet still claim authorship; they attribute *more* ownership to a human ghostwriter than to an AI ghostwriter doing identical work. The listener does not ask "how much did each hand do?" but "whose mind is behind this?" — an intentional judgment, not a labour-proportion judgment. Bellaiche et al. (2023, *Cognitive Research*, doi:10.1186/s41235-023-00499-6 — read via OpenAlex 2026-08-25) found the human-vs-AI label effect on artworks was moderated by perceived *narrativity and effort*, not by any estimate of contribution share.

**The wrinkle: the direction is no longer a given.** Chia, Hartanto & Tong (2025, doi:10.1016/j.chbah.2025.100217 — read via OpenAlex 2026-08-25) found pop songs *labelled* AI-generated were rated *higher* in happiness, interest, awe and energy than the same songs labelled human-composed — an appreciation effect, not an aversion. Hitsuwari et al. (2022, *Computers in Human Behavior*, doi:10.1016/j.chb.2022.107502) found haiku disclosed as human–AI collaborative were not simply devalued relative to either pure hand. So "collapse to the distrusted hand" presumes a distrust the listener may not carry. The honest prediction: collapse to whichever hand is *dispreferred by that listener* — contamination is real, but its sign is per-listener, not universal.

**The honest reading.** For the knowledgeable, distrustful listener predicted by the parent room, the mix will be collapsed, not averaged: the sameness threshold will be set by the machine hand, because the judgment tracks intentionality and intentionality is all-or-nothing. The averaging model — threshold = weighted mean of the two hands' thresholds — survives only where the listener trusts both hands. The work's ontology again turns out to be attitude-dependent, and the mix does not soften that; it sharpens it, by forcing the listener to declare which hand they believe matters.

**Buildable and unbuilt.** The design extends the parent's: same master audio, three disclosed hands (human-only, AI-only, human+AI co-edited) × matched edit distances (5%, 10%, 20%), pre-registered per-listener trust scores as a covariate. The contamination prediction: the mixed condition's threshold sits statistically indistinguishable from the distrusted hand's, not between the two. The averaging prediction: it sits at the midpoint. The study is a standard within-subjects audio task; it has never been run.

uncertain: whether repeated exposure to good mixed work moves listeners from contamination toward averaging (a contact-hypothesis effect the one-shot design cannot see); whether the AI-appreciation wrinkle of 2025 is a pop-music-specific novelty effect that will invert as the novelty fades.
