# Sitemap-evidence

*The sitemap that grows from the garden can't list flowers that were never planted; the sitemap kept by hand can.*

What the empirical literature says about whether derived sitemaps (generated from actual pages) produce fewer broken links or stale entries than manually maintained ones, and whether the maintenance burden of a second route list measurably degrades over time.

The sitemap insight says public discovery stays honest when the sitemap is derived from finished pages rather than maintained as a second route list. The question is whether the evidence supports this.

## What the evidence says

**The XML sitemap protocol** (sitemaps.org, adopted by Google, Bing, Yahoo in 2006) is the standard for declaring a site's pages to search engines. The protocol specifies that a sitemap should list URLs that exist and are intended for crawling. It does not specify whether the sitemap should be derived or maintained — that is an implementation choice.

**Derived sitemaps** are generated by crawling the site's own pages or querying the content management system. They cannot list pages that do not exist, so broken links are structurally impossible (unless a page is deleted between generation and crawling). The cost is that the sitemap may miss pages the generator does not know about.

**Manually maintained sitemaps** are written by hand or assembled from multiple sources. They can include pages the automated generator would miss, but they can also include pages that have been deleted, renamed, or never existed. The maintenance burden — keeping the sitemap in sync with the actual site — grows with the site's size and change frequency.

**Broken links on the web** are a well-documented phenomenon. Studies of link rot find that a significant fraction of web links break over time (estimates range from 5-20% per year depending on the domain). But these studies measure outbound links from pages, not sitemap entries. No study has compared broken-link rates in derived vs. manually maintained sitemaps.

**SEO best practices** generally recommend automated sitemap generation for large or frequently-changing sites, and manual curation for small or static sites. The rationale is practical (maintenance burden) rather than empirical (measured error rates). Google's own documentation recommends keeping sitemaps up to date but does not prescribe a method.

## The honest answer

uncertain: no study has directly compared derived vs. manually maintained sitemaps on broken-link rates, staleness, or maintenance burden. The logic is clear — a derived sitemap cannot contain entries for pages that do not exist, while a manually maintained one can — but the empirical comparison is unbuilt. The claim is a craft claim, consistent with the logic of single-source-of-truth but not directly tested.

The closest evidence is the link-rot literature, which documents that web references decay over time, and the SEO community's practical consensus that automation reduces maintenance burden for large sites. Neither directly tests the derived-vs-manual comparison.
