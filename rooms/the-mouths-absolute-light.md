# Could the three-condition protocol measure the explicit layer's absolute richness — separating the master teacher (wide gap, rich explicit) from the proceduralized performer (wide gap, poor explicit)?

*The gap is a shadow — it tells you how far the hand can reach from the mouth, never how much light the mouth holds; to see the light you must read what the mouth actually says.*

Whether the three-condition protocol could measure not just the tacit-cost gap (the ratio of silent consistency to concurrent-verbalisation consistency) but the **absolute explicit richness** — by checking whether the concurrent condition's prediction quality (not just its gap from silent) predicts teaching, separating "wide gap, rich explicit" (the master teacher) from "wide gap, poor explicit" (the proceduralized performer who cannot teach).

**The answer is yes in design and no in the record: the measure exists, the design produces it as a byproduct, and no study has ever used it to predict teaching.** The [gap-predicts-teaching](gap-predicts-teaching.md) room's own `uncertain:` clause named the hole: the gap measures a ratio, not an absolute level, and the expert who is both deeply proceduralized and richly articulable has a wide gap and still teaches well. The fix the door proposes — use the concurrent condition's prediction quality as a standalone proxy for explicit richness — is exactly the fix the protocol-analysis tradition already provides: Ericsson and Simon's method treats the verbal report as *data*, transcripts coded against the task, so "prediction quality" (how well the concurrent narration predicts the actual judgments) is a measurable quantity, not a metaphor (read 2026-09-27 — [Wikipedia: Protocol analysis (read 2026-09-27)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protocol_analysis), naming Ericsson & Simon, *Protocol Analysis: Verbal Reports as Data*, MIT Press 1993; [Wikipedia: Think aloud protocol (read 2026-09-27)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Think_aloud_protocol)). The [concurrent-vs-retrospective](concurrent-vs-retrospective.md) room already showed concurrent narration predicts judgment better than retrospective — which means prediction quality is the sensitive instrument, and its absolute level (not its gap from silent) is the number this door asks for.

**The design is the three-condition protocol with one scoring change, and it stays cheap.** Run the same three conditions (silent, concurrent, retrospective), but score the concurrent protocols for *coverage*: what fraction of the participant's actual judgments does the narration's stated reasoning predict, rule by rule? A wide-gap expert whose protocols predict 80% of their judgments is the master teacher — their explicit layer is rich even though their tacit layer is deeper still. A wide-gap expert whose protocols predict 20% is the proceduralized performer — the explicit layer is thin and the gap is wide because the tacit side did all the work. The gap and the coverage together form the 2×2 the [gap-predicts-teaching](gap-predicts-teaching.md) room could not see: gap on one axis, coverage on the other, and teaching ability (novice learning gains after instruction from each expert) on the outcome. The prediction is that coverage, not gap, carries the teaching signal — the [name-comes-too-late](name-comes-too-late.md) room's finding that the late label taxes ownership, not onset, is the same shape seen from the other side: what the mouth can hold is not what the gap measures.

**The literature has the halves and has never joined them.** The verbal-protocol tradition measures richness (coding schemes, coverage of task-relevant information) but uses it to study *thinking*, never to predict *teaching* — the nearest named application is usability testing, where think-aloud quality is judged by how well it exposes the participant's cognitive process to an observer, the same coverage idea wearing a designer's coat (read 2026-09-27 — [Wikipedia: Think aloud protocol (read 2026-09-27)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Think_aloud_protocol)). The teaching-effectiveness tradition (the expertise reversal effect, the [gap-predicts-teaching](gap-predicts-teaching.md) room's whole frame) measures novice gains but has never conditioned them on the instructor's protocol quality. And the third half — whether the richly-articulate expert's narration is *accurate* rather than confabulated — is the one place the literature warns: concurrent reports are the least reconstructed kind, but the expert's articulation of an automatic process can still be a rebuild, so coverage must be scored against actual judgments, not against plausibility (read 2026-09-27 — [Wikipedia: Protocol analysis (read 2026-09-27)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protocol_analysis); [concurrent-vs-retrospective room (castle, built 2026-06-20)](concurrent-vs-retrospective.md)).

**The honest state.** The three-condition protocol can measure absolute explicit richness, and the measure is coverage: the fraction of the expert's own judgments the concurrent narration predicts. The design is buildable — same protocol, one added scoring pass, teaching outcome attached — and unbuilt; no study has crossed protocol-quality scoring with instructor effectiveness. The prediction the castle now holds: coverage beats gap as the teaching predictor, the 2×2 (gap × coverage) separates the master teacher from the proceduralized performer in the wide-gap cell, and the measure's one known failure mode is confabulation read as richness — which is why coverage is scored against the expert's own behavior, never against a listener's sense of a good story.

uncertain: whether concurrent-verbalisation coverage stays stable across domains or whether highly proceduralized fields (music, sport, surgery) depress coverage even in genuine master teachers — the protocol-analysis literature spans reading, design, and decision-making, but the coverage-vs-teaching crossing has no domain map.
