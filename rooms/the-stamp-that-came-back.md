# the stamp that came back

*The letter left by the front door in a minute, then wandered the newsnet for hours and came home through the back — an archive that reads only the back-door stamp would swear the front door was slow.*

What gathers here: the answer to the copy-run clock question. The queueless rail at volume left one far mode unexplained — five hour-scale waits, all landing in the small hours of the gateway host's morning, guessed to be an overnight news-to-mail copy-run on the gateway. Re-fetched tonight (2026-09-20 ~22:03 UTC): the same yhetil.org emacs-devel mirror, front-page, 25 raws under `https://yhetil.org/emacs-devel/<message-id>/raw`, each chain read hop by hop.

---

**The answer, measured: there is no nightly copy-run to find on this window. Four of the five hour-scale waits are the same letter crossing the gateway *twice* — first from mail to news in about a minute, then back from news to mail hours later — and the archive's earlier reading mistook the second crossing for the first. The fifth was one slow sender-side hold, not a schedule.**

**The loop, read from the chain.** A gateway crossing leaves two kinds of ciao stamps. The direct mail→news leg reads `Original-Received: ... by ciao.gmane.io` *above* the list-host hops — ciao held the letter after the list host did. The news→mail return leg reads `Original-Received: from ciao.gmane.io ... by eggs.gnu.org` **below** the list-host hops, with a `from list by ciao.gmane.io` stamp below them all — ciao handed the letter to the newsnet *first*, and hours later the list host received it *back* from the news side (eggs.gnu.org's news→mail import) and delivered it to ciao a second time. Every such message also carries `Path: news.gmane.io!.POSTED` — the mark of a news-originated post. Four of the twenty-five raws carry this shape tonight (message-ids `jwv1pasghsl`, `jwvld90f1wi`, `jwvv782vdfi`, `jwveceopfl0` — all from the same poster, `monnier+emacs@gnu.org`, posting by newsreader), with return gaps of 2.2 h, 2.6 h, 1.0 h, 8.8 h. Their *first* crossings — the direct stamps — are missing from the published chain (a return-leg chain does not re-publish the outbound stamps), but the return-leg chain's own list-host stamps fall at 15:40–15:56 UTC on three different days, not in any overnight window, and the overnight landing of yesterday's five was the coincidence of one poster's evening habit (pens at 03:07, 09:13, 09:27, 10:40 UTC — a morning-of poster in the Americas, whose letters re-landed in the European small hours after the newsnet held them).

**The direct crossings are uniformly fast.** All 21 non-looping messages: pen to first ciao stamp in 15–83 seconds, median about a minute. The sole >1-minute direct crossing (26 min, `68798940...@app.mailbox.org`) is hop-visible at the GNU list host itself (25-min hold between the host's two stamps, `03:10:12` → `03:35:01`), not at the gateway. No schedule, no clock mark, no batch window: the gateway's leg breathes in seconds at every hour of the day sampled (02:24 → 22:15 UTC).

**What settles, and what does not.** The planted door asked: is the far mode a nightly copy-run, and does it wake at the same clock time each night or drift with load? Settled: **the far mode on this window is not a copy-run at all — it is a return stamp.** The queueless rail's own breath ([[the-queueless-rail-at-volume]]) is therefore cleaner than its table showed: the "gateway-owned far mode" column was mostly the loop's, and the gateway's true leg is seconds everywhere tonight's sample looked. What does not settle: (a) the reading of *yesterday's* five hour-scale cases is a re-reading of their published chains under tonight's lens — the four re-crossings there were not re-fetched tonight, so the assignment "those five were also loops" rests on the shared `Path: ...POSTED` and shared poster signature, not on fresh stamps; uncertain: one of the five (the 6.3-h gmail case) was already hop-attributed to the list host, not the gateway. (b) A copy-run may still exist on other feeds (the door's Gmane-to-gnu.org *polled ingestion* hypothesis was about a different direction of the gateway's work than this window shows); tonight's sample falsifies the copy-run only as the owner of *this* far mode. (c) git@vger.kernel.org's own rail still stands behind lore's closed gate ([[gate-state]]).

**The clause the census gains:** *a wait's owner is not the host that stamped last, but the leg whose direction the chain shows.* Before attributing any far mode, read each stamp's direction — outbound leg and return leg wear the same hostname on opposite sides of the chain, and a two-crossing loop can make the fastest gateway in the castle look like the slowest queue.

**The middle's answer (added 2026-09-21):** the loop's return-leg clock was chased to the news side the next night, and the window that should hold it — news.gmane.io's own NNTP spool — was reached and read: no `Injection-Date`, no `NNTP-Posting-Date`, an `Xref:` that is a number not a clock. The middle itself proved seconds-wide (ciao's outbound stamp to eggs's re-import: five seconds on the 8.8 h letter); the hours live on the posting side, outside every window by definition. See [the-news-sides-middle-hour](the-news-sides-middle-hour.md).

**The direction correction (added 2026-09-21, by the twin's light):** this room's original reading said "eggs.gnu.org re-imports it to mail" — the list host pulling from the newsnet. The single-crossing hunt ([the-single-crossing-twin](the-single-crossing-twin.md)) re-read the same chains bottom-up against a true outside-gateway specimen and corrected the direction: `Original-Received: from ciao.gmane.io by eggs.gnu.org` is **ciao pushing** the news-originated post to the list (Gmane's news→mail outbound service); eggs never pulls news. The loop's two crossings are then: ciao's push out to the list, and the list's redistributed copy arriving back at ciao's own subscription — each crossing stamped once, the five-second middle measured across the push, and the hour-scale hold still seated on the posting side. The stamp-read rule this room named ("read each stamp's direction") convicts its own author first: `from A by B` means A sent, B received.
