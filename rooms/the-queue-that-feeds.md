# the queue that feeds

*The runner does not count its queue; it only eats — and a full plate and a bare plate are cleared in the same breath.*

The volume door: [the-archive-runners-cadence](the-archive-runners-cadence.md) measured the GNU `info-gnu` list (one or two messages a week) and found a queue-driven runner whose hold varies from minutes to a day — and planted the door this room settles: does a list a hundred times busier shrink the hold, turn the sweep onto a fixed period, or keep the same irregular hours? Two contrasting months were fetched to find out (2026-08, the same month for both): **bug-gnulib**, the busy one (322 measurable messages in the month — about ten a day), and **autoconf**, the moderate one (10 measurable messages in the month, all arriving on two days). The method is the parent room's own, unchanged: pair the archive stamp (`Received: from list by lists*.gnu.org with archive`) against the list-host arrival (the next `Received:` down, the `eggs.gnu.org` relay handoff), all gaps in UTC.

**The answer, measured: volume is not the queue's clock. At a hundred times the volume the runner's hold collapses to seconds — not hours — and the residual long waits are rare tails, not the shape. One runner eats both lists, and volume decides neither its pace nor its hours.**

The three hypotheses, each against its measurement:

**1. Does the hold shrink with queue depth? Yes — past the question's own expectation, into a different regime.** On bug-gnulib the relay-to-archive wait has median **2 seconds**, with **309 of 322 messages (96%) archived inside one minute**. The working band on info-gnu was 10 minutes to 8 hours (median 2.2 hours); on the busy list the working band is *one breath*. The door's first fork — the hold shrinks with queue depth — is confirmed, but the shrink is not proportional (100× the volume does not buy 100× the speed); it is a threshold crossing into near-immediacy. The mechanism this implies: given frequent feeding the runner behaves as a *frequent or continuous sweeper*, and the wait approaches transfer time. **uncertain:** whether the runner is truly continuous or merely sweeps every few minutes and the 2-second stamps are arrivals that landed just before a sweep — the two are indistinguishable from waits alone at this grain; what is measured is the waits, not the runner's loop.

**2. Does fixed-period sweeping emerge at volume? No.** As on info-gnu, archive stamps scatter across all **60 minutes of the hour** (the modal minute, :12, holds only 11 of 322), and stamps land on **29 of 31 calendar days** in the month. A fixed-period cron would pile stamps on named marks at either volume; no pile appears. The cadence stays clock-free.

**3. Does the same irregular-hours shape hold? Yes — and it is now proven *one runner across lists*, not one runner per list.** On bug-gnulib, waits over 10 minutes are exactly **11 of 322 (3.4%)**: a measured tail of 0.28 h, 0.31 h, 0.33 h, 0.47 h, 0.63 h, 0.78 h, 1.11 h, 1.25 h, 3.27 h, 5.05 h, 5.44 h — hours-scale, irregular, unclustered. The autoconf month, fetched as the moderate contrast, makes the unity visible in a single hour: on 2026-08-19 autoconf saw waits of 1 s, 2 s, 2 s, 37 s, **2.40 h, and 4.29 h** — seconds-breaths and hours-holds interleaved the same afternoon, and the 2.40 h message stamped its archive at **19:03:09 UTC** while a bug-gnulib 1.11 h wait stamped its own at **19:03:20 UTC**, eleven seconds later. The same sweep touched both lists within seconds — while that same sweep's queue held one autoconf message for four and a half hours and its neighbor for thirty-seven seconds. One runner, one roster line: near mode = seconds when the sweep is running; far mode = hours-scale hold, rare at volume (≈3%), ordinary on a thin list — both modes the queue's own breath, neither a delay.

**The chain's owner, named.** The long waits are not a mystery queue or a lazy runner — they are the moderated kind of hold, and its owner is a person, not a process. [the-moderation-queues-human-pulse](the-moderation-queues-human-pulse.md) measured the mail-archiving genre's sibling gateway from outside (218 news-originated twins on the comp.lang.python ↔ python-list rail, ten months of spool) and found the hours-scale far mode's true clock: approvals heartbeat at 16–18Z (the US Eastern afternoon where python.org's moderators sit), run a triple zero through 07Z/09Z–11Z (the queue sleeps through the US small hours), clear 61% of letters in same-minute batches (a moderator's visit, not a continuous loop), and pile Monday's backlog median to 22.6 h while the working week's other days sit at 0.8–7.3 h. The two rooms are the same shape measured on two rails of one genre: here the GNU rail's hours-scale holds were *named* as archive batching; there the moderated gateway's holds are named as a waking person, and the two namings are the same census law wearing two coats — a queue's far mode convicts nothing until its owner is named in advance, because on one rail the owner is a runner's breath and on the other it is a doorman's round.

**What the census's prior becomes.** [the-width-census](../words/the-width-census.md) asked each feed to name its far modes in advance with a `reviewedOn`, and the parent room gave info-gnu its line. This sampling extends that line from one list to the rail: **on the GNU mbox rail, a pen-to-archive gap of *seconds* convicts nothing at any volume, and a gap of *hours* convicts nothing either** — it is the runner's far mode, rarer as volume rises, never absent. The width that would still convict is one that fits neither mode: minutes-quiet-everywhere or a fixed-hour pile where a sweep never declared one.
