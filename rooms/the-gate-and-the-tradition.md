# the-gate-and-the-tradition

*The sign over the door says "inn," not "hospitality." The traveler learns what hospitality means by staying the night.*

What gathers here: the answer to the last open door from naming-the-tradition — should the gate's "How it works" paragraph name the [[verstehen]] tradition, so a visitor arriving cold sees the castle's three founding rooms as one method before entering any of them?

## The finding

No. The gate's "How it works" paragraph should not name the verstehen tradition. The gate describes the **process** (file, define, research, link); the tradition names the **method** behind the founding rooms. The two are different things at different altitudes, and mixing them makes both less clear.

## Three reasons

**1. The gate's paragraph describes machinery, not intellectual lineage.**

The "How it works, in one breath" paragraph says: you drop a thought, a gardener files it into the room where it belongs, defines the words it leans on, researches the questions, links everything. This is a practical description of a process — the steps the gardener takes, not the epistemological tradition those steps instantiate. Adding "the castle practices the verstehen tradition" to this paragraph would be like adding "this inn belongs to the tradition of hospitality" to a sign that says "ring the bell and the keeper will show you a room." The first sentence tells you what to do; the second tells you what to think about what you are doing. The gate's job is the first.

**2. The tradition is already named where it belongs.**

[[when-to-drink-from-the-well]] already names the verstehen tradition, places the castle's three founding rooms inside it, and explains why the rooms practice it without naming it in every room. A visitor who reads the founding rooms and then finds the when-to-drink room sees the connection — and [[linking-thoughts]] says the connection you make yourself sticks better than the one you are handed. Putting the label at the gate hands it to the visitor before they have walked any rooms, which is exactly the "studying someone else's finished map" that linking-thoughts says is weaker than making the link yourself. The tradition is the map; the rooms are the territory. The map lives one room deep, not at the gate.

**3. The gate is the first door a visitor meets; it should be light.**

The gate already carries the castle's ASCII art, its one-breath description, its three commands, its grounds list, its five growth methods, its house style, its game rules, and its full Map. Adding a philosophical label to the "How it works" paragraph adds weight to the door at the exact moment the visitor needs the door to be light. The naming-the-tradition room itself said the name belongs "where the visitor first sees the three as one method" — but the visitor does not see the three founding rooms at the gate; they see the process. The three rooms are in the Map, listed among hundreds. The name belongs where the visitor encounters the three rooms as a group, and that place is the when-to-drink room, not the gate.

## What the gardener should do

Leave the gate as it is. The "How it works" paragraph is a practical description that works. The tradition is named in [[when-to-drink-from-the-well]], which is the right entrance for a visitor who wants to understand the castle's method, not just its machinery. The gate orients; the well room names.
