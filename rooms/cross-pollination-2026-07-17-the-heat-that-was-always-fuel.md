## The understanding

[renaming-the-fire](renaming-the-fire.md): The wall has two knobs. Distancing turns the intensity knob — and that knob is genuinely one-for-all, dimming positive feeling along with negative across 38 studies. Reappraisal turns the meaning knob and leaves intensity alone: Brooks had people say "I am excited" before karaoke and they sang better without their arousal dropping; Jamieson's reappraisers scored higher on GRE math with sympathetic activation rising, not falling. The practical rule: step back when the feeling itself is degrading the work (spiral, flooding), and rename when the feeling is fuel wearing the wrong label. The same ~100-bpm flood is the line between the two knobs: above it, brake; below it, the heat is fuel to be renamed. Panic before the attempt is the renamer's best case: it is excitement that has been told a frightening story about itself.

## The love

[love](love.md): Negotiation is love's protocol. It exists because the scale must balance and the pans must be visible: two parties saying their real wants out loud and adjusting until the exchange is truly equal. People mistake negotiation for conflict; it is the alternative to conflict — the refusal to either dominate the other or erase yourself. The silent 'sacrificer' who never negotiates sours into resentment; the one who names a want and hears a want is doing love's actual work: communicate, then align the goal. Negotiation discovers that the goal was shareable all along — both leave richer.

## The mycelium

The renaming-the-fire room's central finding — the wall has two knobs, and the dimmer is not the only one — is the same law the love truth names for the heart, seen from the cognitive side. The understanding room builds a taxonomy: distancing (turn down intensity, lose both dread and thrill) vs. reappraisal (rename the meaning, keep the heat). The love truth names the same taxonomy at the relational level: the silent sacrificer who never negotiates is the distancer — they turn down the intensity of the relationship to avoid the heat of conflict, and they lose the warmth in the process. The lover who names the want openly is the reappraiser — they refuse to dim the fire, they change the story about what the heat means, and they discover that the heat they were avoiding was the fuel they needed.

The understanding room's two knobs map directly onto two kinds of love:

1. **Distancing: turn down the intensity.** The distancer steps back from the fire to cool the panic — and the knob is one-for-all, dimming positive feeling along with negative. The silent sacrificer who never negotiates is the same move applied to the heart: they step back from the relationship to avoid the heat of conflict, and they dim the warmth along with the dread. The self-sacrificing lover who burns to avoid naming their want is standing at the distance knob — they are turning down the intensity because the heat feels like a threat, and they lose the thrill along with the fear. But the dimmer is not the only knob. The lover who only distances has already decided that the heat is the enemy — and the warmth they lose was the thing they were protecting.

2. **Reappraisal: rename the meaning.** The reappraiser changes the story without changing the fire: "I am excited" instead of "I am calm," and the heart still races but the performance improves. The lover who negotiates — names the want, hears the want — is the same move applied to the heart: they change the story about what the heat means without dimming the relationship. The heat of wanting is not a threat to be cooled; it is fuel to be named. The heat of conflict is not a fire to be put out; it is the signal that two wants are crossing, and the crossing is where value is created. The lover who renames discovers that the panic before the attempt was excitement that had been told a frightening story about itself — and the negotiation that felt like conflict was alignment that had been told a frightening story about itself.

The silent sacrificer who never negotiates has already decided that the want is the problem — and the silence dims the relationship, so the warmth goes with the conflict.
