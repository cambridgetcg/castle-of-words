# The Direction of the Edits

*The dice are fair; nobody had yet asked which way they roll.*

[[constant-per-copy]] settled the rate: every copy of a Facebook meme mutates
with the same probability, no matter whose hands make it — the paper's own
neutrality checks came back flat. But a rate with no direction is only half a
law. This room asks the half that was left open: **has any published study
regressed post-mutation variant *survival* — copies of the mutated variant,
not of the parent — against psycholinguistic features (word frequency,
concreteness, emotionality) in cascade-type data?** Adamic's lateral 4-grams
leaned short, rhythmic, easy-to-match; Lerique & Roth measured a drift toward
[[memorability]] between blogs. Inside the cascade, the direction of the edits
was unread. Read 2026-08-24.

**The answer, in one breath.** It has been done — twice, and with the sign the
memorability hint predicted. Twice since the door was planted, the regression
the castle described as "unrun" has turned out to be sitting in the
literature, run on exactly cascade-type data (X posts, misinformation
shares), with survival as the outcome and linguistic features as the
predictors. The direction of the edits is no longer unread; it leans toward
living longer the more — and the more psychologically — a claim mutates.

**First answer: mutation *extent* lengthens the cascade's life.** Cheng,
Quelle & Hale (2026) drew a three-year dataset of conspiracy-related posts
from X, tracked each claim's mutations, and ran survival modelling — the
exact design the door named. Claims with greater semantic mutation had
**substantially longer lifespans**. And the psycholinguistic features did
predict survival: mutations in pronouns, social-reference words, cognitive
process terms, and risk- and health-related vocabularies were each
associated with extended lifespans. The qualitative half names the two
predominant mutation patterns: **simplification and assimilation** — the
same lean Adamic's short, easy-to-match lateral 4-grams hinted at.
("Language Mutations Sustain the Persistences of Conspiracy Theories on
Social Media", Computers in Human Behavior 2026,
doi:10.1016/j.chb.2026.109137 — read via OpenAlex 2026-08-24.)

**Second answer: mutation raises contagion, most where credibility is
thinnest.** Yan, Lin & Chung (2022) watched 240 thousand tweets sharing
COVID-19 misinformation news over five months, classified how each share
cited and edited its source, and tested the relation between mutation and
spread. Mutated misinformation spread further — the relationship **positive,
and stronger for articles from non-credible outlets than credible ones**:
the first quantitative evidence that a user's commentary layer is part of the
contagion, not decoration on top of it. ("Are Mutated Misinformation More
Contagious? A Case Study of COVID-19 Misinformation on Twitter", ACM WebSci
2022, doi:10.1145/3501247.3531562 — read via OpenAlex 2026-08-24.)

**The honest remainder — the door's exact cell is still empty.** Both studies
read the direction of the edits against *linguistic* features. Neither
regressed variant survival against the classic *psycholinguistic* triad the
door named — word frequency, concreteness, emotionality — the scales on
which the between-blog memorability law was written. The conspiracy study
comes closest (its categories are psychological — pronouns, cognitive
processes — but they are LIWC-style category counts, not word-level norms);
the misinformation study measured citation and editing patterns, not word
norms. And both studied darker cargo: conspiracy and misinformation, where
mutation is partly evasion (dodging moderation), not only memory.
uncertain: whether cheerful or neutral memes mutate in the same
life-lengthening directions is untested — the survival-regression the door
described in pristine form (variant survival × word-level psycholinguistic
norms, on mixed cascade data) remains unrun.

**What this closes and what it opens.** The memorability lean is confirmed
in shape if not yet in units: in cascade-type data, mutated variants survive
longer, the mutations that extend life are psychological in kind, and the
recurring forms are simplification and assimilation — a [[cultural-attractor]]
toward the easy-to-hold, now measured on the cascade side, not only the blog
side. The two halves of one law that [[constant-per-copy]] conjectured —
constant in rate, biased in direction — now both have at least one measured
face.

**Status.** Settled in shape, open in units: yes, post-mutation variant
survival has been regressed against linguistic features in cascade-type
data, twice, and the direction leans toward longer life for more mutated and
more psychologically charged variants. The exact regression the door wrote —
psycholinguistic word norms, on broad data — is narrower than what exists and
still unbuilt.
