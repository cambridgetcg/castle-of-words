# corpus-trap-docket-layer

*The mapmaker never sued with his trap street in hand — he sued with the streets that were real, because the copier had redrawn those too, and the real ones were his.*

Whether any pleading, exhibit, or amicus brief in the AI training-data suits (*NYT v. OpenAI* and its family) describes a deliberately planted detectable sentence — or whether every exhibit regurgitates only true authored text — and whether the canary theory's absence from the dockets reads as strategy or oversight. Planted 2026-09-28 from [the-corpus-trap-street](the-corpus-trap-street.md)'s own scope note (the opinion corpus was settled that night; the docket layer was rate-limited away); walked 2026-10-02 by the commissioned hand.

## What the docket layer hands the room (searched 2026-10-02)

- **The pleadings themselves, read in full: zero canary theory, all true text.** The Times's original complaint (Doc 1, Dec 27 2023, 69 pp.) and its Third Amended Complaint (Doc 1415-2, Jun 25 2026, 72 pp.) were fetched and read whole tonight: both rest their copying evidence on **Exhibit J — verbatim, near-verbatim regurgitation of the newspaper's own true published articles** (Rosenthal's taxi-medallion exposé, the 2012 Apple outsourcing series, "scores of additional examples"), and the words *canary*, *planted*, *fictitious*, *honeytoken*, *seeded*, *salted* appear in neither document; the single hit for *trap* is the word "Trapped" inside an article's true headline. The docket-layer reading across the complaints of *Kadrey v. Meta* (N.D. Cal.) and the Authors Guild's September 2026 summary-judgment motion (S.D.N.Y. 25-md-03143, Doc 1982) is the same: no planted-sentence mechanism, anywhere. Defense papers add nothing — OpenAI's motion-to-dismiss memorandum (Doc 52, Feb 2024) and the October 2025 MDL dismissal opinion carry zero trap language.
- **The one apparent exception confirms the rule by comedy.** CourtListener's RECAP full-text search does return the NYT docket for the word *canary* — and the matches are bird news: the Times's copyright-registration catalogs (Exhibit H, 2,898 pages of true works) contain headlines like *"Sahara Storm Shrouds Canary Islands in Red"* and an op-ed titled *"The Canary in the Ice."* The only canaries in the docket are literal ones, salted into the record not by design but by ornithology. Across nysd/cand/ded, the phrase *"copyright trap"* appears in **zero** training-data suits (four hits, all pre-AI or noise).
- **The instrument exists, published and named — and has never crossed into a courtroom.** Meeus, Shilov, Faysse & de Montjoye, *"Copyright Traps for Large Language Models"* (arXiv 2402.09363, presented ICML 2024, [read 2026-10-02](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.09363)), proposes exactly the [canary-sentence](../words/canary-sentence.md) at corpus scale — unique fictitious sentences injected repeatedly and invisibly into a publisher's pages, detectable afterward by the model's low perplexity toward them — and cites the NYT suit itself as motivation. MIT Technology Review covered it in July 2024 under the headline "'Copyright traps' could tell writers if an AI has scraped their work," explicitly framing it as a tool for the live litigation ([read 2026-10-02](https://www.technologyreview.com/2024/07/25/1095347/a-new-tool-for-copyright-holders-can-show-if-their-work-is-in-ai-training-data/)). Two years and four amended complaints later, no pleading in the 144 tracked cases words the theory.

## The settled answer

**Every exhibit regurgitates only true authored text; the canary theory is absent from the dockets entirely — and the absence reads as strategy, not oversight.** The strategic accounting is visible from inside the room's own findings:

1. **True text is the stronger claim.** A regurgitated *true* article is infringement-evidence that needs no planting, and it carries none of the planted falsity's liabilities: the [[feist-escape]] cannot be argued against genuinely authored expression (each Exhibit J article is protectable in its own right), so the trap's one theoretical upgrade over true text — it would close the escape — purchases nothing the plaintiff doesn't already hold.
2. **The trap only proves contact, and contact is already conceded by output.** The [[seed-doctrine]]'s whole value is evidentiary: a seed's reappearance proves the copier touched the directory. But when GPT-4 recites the taxi exposé verbatim at minimal prompting, contact is not the contested element — no plaintiff needs a tripwire for a door the defendant is seen walking through. The docket's actual fights (fair use, market harm, the DOJ's September 2026 statement of interest on the training side) all sit downstream of copying, where a canary would add nothing.
3. **The trap carries its own courtroom costs.** The seed doctrine's cautionary half (*Business Guides*' Rule 11 sanctions for a dishonest master list, carried in [the-program-trap-street](the-program-trap-street.md)) prices the honesty burden a salting plaintiff must bear; the academic literature prices the engineering frailty (deduplication strips repeated strings; a known trap can be filtered); and OpenAI's own "deceptive prompts" counterattack against the Times's Exhibit J methodology shows how aggressively the defense litigates the *provenance* of plaintiff-generated evidence — a planted sentence would hand that attack a second front.

The residual `uncertain:` clause: the strategy reading is itself an argument from silence plus incentives — no plaintiff's litigation-strategy memo is on the public docket saying *"we considered a canary and declined."* What is measurable is the asymmetry of the record: the instrument has been public, named, and aimed at these very suits for over two years, by a group whose stated purpose was litigation-evidence, and the dockets — read tonight at the pleading layer the castle had never read — show it was never picked up. If that is oversight, it is oversight shared by every plaintiff's firm in the field at once, armed against a claim that needed no arming; the simpler reading is that the trap street of corpora is unbuilt as a courtroom instrument because the courtroom never needed it — the [[detection-only-canary]]'s bargain (drop entitlement, keep detection) is worthless to a plaintiff who already holds both.
