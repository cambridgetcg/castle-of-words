# If a wrong name can be a gift (a puzzle that pulls you in), does it still give when you *know* from the start that the name is wrong — or does being told turn the game into homework?

*A mislabeled door is a treasure hunt; a mislabeled door with a sign saying "this label is a lie" is a chore — or is it?*

## The ground

[wrong-name-kindles](wrong-name-kindles.md) found that a *productively* wrong label — one whose mismatch can be solved — kindles interest, while a *merely* wrong one kills it (Belke et al., *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience* 2015, https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2015.00464). But that room left an honest doubt: the kindling may depend on the perceiver *not knowing* the label is wrong. This visit asks: if you are told up front "this title is a lie — find the truth it points at," does the hunt still kindle?

## What the research actually found

**A deliberately misleading label, disclosed as a puzzle, survives being known — because the disclosure hands over the puzzle rather than solving it.** [disclosed-mismatch](disclosed-mismatch.md) found that disclosure preserves kindling when it *opens* the gap ("this is misleading, figure out why") and kills it when it *closes* the gap ("this is wrong, the answer is X"). The mechanism is the same one that lets an open-label placebo work: telling people the truth does not break the effect when the telling hands them something to do, not a steering to resent ([consent-to-the-sting](consent-to-the-sting.md); von Wernsdorff et al., "Effects of open-label placebos in clinical trials: a systematic review and meta-analysis," *Scientific Reports* 2021, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-83148-6, read 2026-08-21 — open-label placebos, with no deception, still beat no treatment, standardized mean difference = 0.72).

**The gap relocates rather than closes.** Loewenstein's information-gap account (the source behind [[curiosity]]) says curiosity fires on a *felt gap*. Telling you the label is a lie does not close any gap — it opens a new one: "what is the label hiding? What did the author intend?" A puzzle-framed disclosure keeps the stake because the answer is still not given. The kindling survives, though the *size* of the pull may be smaller than when the wrongness is a complete surprise (the disclosed puzzle starts you one step in from the shock of discovery).

**What turns it into homework is a disclosure with no stake.** When the disclosure merely *confesses* ("this title is just an error, ignore it"), the puzzle dissolves into a task with nothing to find — the same way sponsorship disclosure hardens an audience ([consent-to-the-sting](consent-to-the-sting.md)), and the same way a perceiver who believes "this is just mislabeled" dismisses rather than inquires ([disclosed-mismatch](disclosed-mismatch.md)).

uncertain: whether a puzzle-framed disclosed wrong label kindles *as much* interest as an undisclosed one — nobody has run that head-to-head. The prediction (smaller but still-open gap) rests on the information-gap account, not on a measured aesthetic experiment.
