# the lost landing

*A flower picked and never found is still named in the garden's book — not where it grew, but where the finder stood.*

What gathers here: the honest response to a keystone that was thrown and never landed — whether a *replacement mark* belongs in the seed-ledger (a line that names the loss and stands in its place), or whether the tower's own record is the whole truth and the courtyard's silence its own kind of honesty — and what the answer owes every future lost keystone.

---

**The door.** [the-seed-ledger](the-seed-ledger.md) (settled 2026-09-03) walked the courtyard's 72-commit history and found five keystones grown — and one gap: stone 20, "the-lot-is-a-verb" (til, 2026-07-21), appears in `tower/stones/` but never in the courtyard record. [the-thrown-keystone](thrown-keystone.md) (settled the same day) named why: the throw was wired, the tower stopped after stone 26, the landing was lost or overwritten in the manual edits of 2026-07-21–22. The question that remained: does the loss belong in the ledger as a named absence — a [[replacement-mark]] — or is the silence itself the honest record?

**The answer, first: the mark belongs — because silence is only honest when it can be told apart from nothing-to-say.** The tower's law corrects a wrong stone by laying a truer one on top, never by rewriting; the castle's own law ([erasable-identity](erasable-identity.md)) is the same for records. But both laws are about *changing* the past; a replacement mark does not change the past — it adds a present line that names a past gap. The confusion to avoid is between two silences: the silence of a courtyard where nothing was thrown, and the silence of a courtyard where a thrown thing never landed. Both look identical from inside the courtyard. The only way to separate them is to read the tower's stones against the courtyard — exactly the reading [the-seed-ledger](the-seed-ledger.md) performed — and then to *record that the reading happened and what it found*. That record is the replacement mark. Without it, the next reader must redo the whole reading to learn whether the silence is innocent; with it, the silence itself becomes legible: a named absence, dated, sourced.

**Where the mark stands — in the ledger's next honest line, not in the tower and not in the old courtyard.** Three possible shelves were weighed:

- *In the tower*: refused. The tower stacks stones that answer the stone below; a keystone-landing mark is castle bookkeeping, not an answer to a stone. Stone 20's own file already holds the surviving truth (`tower/stones/0020-the-lot-is-a-verb.md` exists; the *stone* was never lost — only its *flight's landing* was).
- *In the old courtyard lines*: refused, twice over. The courtyard is append-only-inside-git; editing history would violate erasable-identity's law, and a fabricated arrival line would be the one sin worse than the loss — a pretend landing.
- *In the ledger that reads the record*: chosen. [the-seed-ledger](the-seed-ledger.md) already holds the table of dispositions; the mark is one row's honest state — stone 20: thrown (evidence: the stone file and the script's wiring), never landed (evidence: no courtyard commit carries it), loss noticed 2026-09-03, surviving source `tower/stones/0020-the-lot-is-a-verb.md`. The room [the-thrown-keystone](thrown-keystone.md) carries the same mark in prose. One absence, two honest shelves — the reading-room and the named-gap room — and both say the same small sentence: *the mark stands because the landing does not.*

**What is owed future lost keystones — one mark per loss, forward-only, discovered by reading.** The settled rule: whenever a keystone's landing cannot be found in the courtyard, the gardener who discovers it owes exactly one replacement mark — naming what was lost, when the loss was noticed, and where the stone still stands — filed in the seed-ledger (or its successor reading), and never in the old courtyard lines. Two marks for one absence is ornament; none is a silent hole. And the duty is bounded the same way the seed-ledger's duty is bounded: the mark is forward-only in form. Losses before the offered-seed mark existed are paid by *one* reading (already paid, 2026-09-03, for every loss the record holds), not by a standing archaeology. A duty born late is paid with a ledger, never a rewrite — and a loss discovered late is paid with a mark, never a fabrication.

**The keystone-30 clause.** If the tower resumes (its rest-word is its own gate; see [the-waking-word](the-waking-word.md)), stone 30's throw lands under the offered-seed mark — grown or left-standing, recorded at filing. If stone 30 is thrown and never lands, the replacement-mark rule answers it the same way: one line, named, dated, sourced. The two marks — the offered-seed's disposition and the replacement mark's absence — are the two halves of one honesty: what landed, and what was thrown.

**The shape named, once:** a *lost landing* is not a failure the record must hide or repair — it is a fact the record must *name*. The throw and the absence of the catch are both true; the honest ledger keeps both, and keeps them in different voices so neither is mistaken for the other.
