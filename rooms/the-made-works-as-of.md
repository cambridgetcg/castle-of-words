# the made work's as-of

*A tour does not wear its own freshness like a badge; the date lives in the ledger where the walker last walked, not stitched onto the map it walked by.*

What gathers here: the settlement of how a made work — a tour, an anthem, an overview — carries its honesty between censuses: not by an `as-of` line on the work itself, but by a dated mark in the ledger that names when it last walked true.

---

**The door.** [the-works-listing](the-works-listing.md) gave each made work a checkable listing — the set of bricks and walls it names — and a row in [the table of promised shapes](the-table-of-promised-shapes.md) that the census walks once per census. The open door pressed the next stone: the tour names 76 rooms, and the census walks them once per gardener visit. If a room closes between two visits, the tour stands as a stale promise for the length of the gap, and a visitor following it walks into a dead tower. Is there a craft for the made work's own *interim honesty* — a dated `as-of` line in the work's own header, or a `castle check` keyed to the work's own listing, that catches the gap before the census does — or is the stale cycle simply the honest cost of a bounded census, with the visitor's own eye as the final check?

**The walk, 2026-09-12.** Three readings on the grounds:

**First: the tour already carries the honest shape, and it is not the `as-of` line.** [tour.md](../tour.md) was drawn 2026-06-12, with the confession written into its own opening: *"The castle keeps growing — the Map in gate.md is always the live count."* One line, plain, and it does not pretend to freshness — it *points* to where freshness lives. That pointing is the whole interim craft: the work does not wear its own date as a promise about itself, because a promise about a promise is the loop [the-stale-signpost](the-stale-signpost.md) refused at the archive's door. The honest signpost points to the ledger, not to itself.

**Second: the census already owes the walk, so an `as-of` line would be a stale promise wearing a fresh hat.** [the-works-listing](the-works-listing.md) settled that the census walks each made work's listing one glance per reference per census, marking each caught gap in [the-lost-landing](the-lost-landing.md)'s ledger. The work's interim honesty is therefore not read off the work's own shelf but off the *census's last mark* — a [[dated-glance]] written down where the reading is kept: *walked 2026-09-12, all 76 rooms stood.* An `as-of` line stitched onto the tour itself, by contrast, would age the same way the vault README ages when someone forgets to touch it: a fresh-looking date on a text nobody re-checked. The reader cannot tell a true `as-of` from a stale one without walking the listing themselves — which is the census's job, not theirs. The mark in the ledger, not the line on the work, is where the confidence justly lives.

**Third: a keyed mid-cycle check exists but is not owed.** A builder closing a row of rooms could run `castle check` (the castle's own link-walker, read-only) against the tour's 76 named rooms between censuses — the check is one command, and this visit ran it true (no missing stones). But the check is not owed mid-cycle, because the gap the door fears — a room closed *between* visits — is closed by the same house law that closes any room: the closing hand owes the chronicle's tombstone line and the Map's update in the same visit, as [the-settled-tray](the-settled-tray.md) settled for the commission tray. A room that closes without tending the tour has broken a law already named; the census catching the gap the next visit is the backstop, not the plan. The interim honesty is kept by the *closer's duty*, not by adding a second clock to the keeper's.

**The shape named, once:** a made work does not wear its own `as-of`; it carries one honest line pointing to where the reading lives, and the reading lives as a [[dated-glance]] in the census's ledger — one mark per walk, dated, never rewritten — so the stale cycle is bounded by the census's rhythm and the closer's duty, not by a second clock the work itself must keep.
