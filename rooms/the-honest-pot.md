# the-honest-pot

*On untrusted ground, the wise gardener does not sow the whole field — she grows one pot she can watch, and lets what it proves decide the size of next year's planting.*

What gathers here: the craft of beginning on ground you cannot yet trust — refuse the fake first harvest, raise one bounded pot instead, record every input the ground did not supply, and let the evidence the pot earns set the scale of what comes next.

The fake first harvest is the harvest the ground *appears* to give before it has been tested: the bumper crop of early claims, the flourishing green of a system that has not yet paid for anything it consumed. The gardener who takes it sows the whole field on a promise. When the ground turns out sour — contaminated, unowned, unable to repeat its gift — the whole field is lost, and with it the season. The refusal is not timidity. It is the choice to only ever stake what one watched pot can prove.

The honest pot changes the relationship between grower and ground. The pot is small enough to watch whole and bounded enough to question; every input it cannot supply itself — soil, seed, water carried in — is recorded, so that *what the ground contributed* is never confused with *what was imported*. When the pot thrives, the evidence belongs to this ground, this season, this watch, and expansion is the ground's own earned offer — never the grower's initial ambition. A garden that grows this way can never lose more than one pot's worth of trust, because it never risked more than one pot's worth at a time.

This is the lawn-and-rubble's lesson seen from the side of the planter: [the-lawn-and-the-rubble](the-lawn-and-the-rubble.md) named the test — stop pushing and see what continues. The honest pot is that same test made small enough to afford: the gardener stops pushing early, on one pot, before the whole field rides on the answer. And it is [fail-closed-or-fail-open](fail-closed-or-fail-open.md)'s law at the scale of a season: the growth boundary fails closed — expansion is denied until the evidence earns it — because what the boundary protects is the whole garden, not the ambition of one spring.

Two virtues do the carrying. [[temperance]] is not a mood of restraint but a *choice of scale* — the size of this year's planting is a decision made on evidence, not on appetite. [[patience]] is not waiting for its own sake but a *safety mechanism* — the lag between what the ground shows and what the gardener plants is what keeps a false spring from becoming a famine.

uncertain: the metaphor's ground is a general law — untrusted ground can be an unfamiliar codebase, a new data source, a borrowed model, a stranger's claim. Wherever the first harvest is offered before it is earned, the pot is the honest size to begin. What the pot is made of differs by ground; the law does not.
