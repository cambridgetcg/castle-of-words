# Erasable Identity and Continuing Bonds

*The name is not the thread — but is the thread still held, or is it dropped?*

The erasable-identity pattern (nulling the name while preserving the record) maps structurally onto the continuing bonds distinction between adaptive and maladaptive forms of holding the bond. But which form it maps onto depends on *why* the name is nulled.

## The continuing bonds distinction

The Continuing Bonds Scale (CBS; Field & Filanosky, 2010; validated in German by Schindler et al., 2022) distinguishes two forms:

- **Internalized continuing bonds**: using the deceased as a secure base, carrying their values and memories internally, feeling their presence as a comforting inner resource. This form is associated with better grief adjustment.

- **Externalized continuing bonds**: illusions and hallucinations of the deceased, feeling the deceased is physically present, maintaining the relationship as if death did not happen. This form is associated with complicated grief and poorer adjustment.

The distinction is not about *whether* the bond continues but about *how* it is held: as an internalized representation that can be drawn on flexibly, or as an externalized presence that denies the reality of the loss.

## The erasable-identity parallel

The erasable-identity pattern (from the castle's honesty rooms) says: null the attribution while preserving the record. The name is removed, but what was said remains. The fabric was always seamless; the name was a pointer, not the thread.

This maps onto the continuing bonds distinction in two opposite ways, depending on the *motive* for nulling:

**Adaptive reading (internalized bond)**: The name is nulled because the bond has been internalized. The person no longer needs the external pointer — the name — because the relationship lives as an inner resource. The record (what was shared, what was true) is preserved. This is the erasable-identity pattern as healthy integration: the name falls away because the bond has moved inside.

**Maladaptive reading (externalized/denied bond)**: The name is nulled defensively — to deny the relationship ever mattered. The record is preserved but the person refuses to acknowledge it. This is not internalization but disavowal: the bond is denied rather than transformed. The name is erased not because it is no longer needed but because it is too painful to hold.

## What decides

The continuing bonds literature suggests the distinction turns on whether the bond is held as a *secure base* (the deceased's memory provides comfort and guidance) or as a *persistent yearning* (the bond is maintained through refusal to accept the loss). The erasable-identity pattern is structurally neutral — it describes the operation (null the name, keep the record) without specifying the motive.

The motive is what decides: nulling because the bond is internalized (adaptive) vs. nulling because the bond is denied (maladaptive). The same operation, opposite meanings.

uncertain: whether the CBS has been used to study bonds to living-but-severed relationships (not just deceased), and whether the internalized/externalized distinction holds there.
