# The Contact Hypothesis, Applied to Hands

*The stranger who shares your workbench stops being a stranger, but only if the bench is level.*

What gathers here: whether sustained contact with good human–AI collaborative work moves a listener from contamination (collapsing the mixed hand to the distrusted one) toward averaging (granting the mix its own threshold), the way intergroup contact softens prejudice in social psychology.

---

**The answer, in one breath.** The direct test has never been run. No study has tracked listeners' judgments of human–AI collaborative works over time to see whether contamination softens into averaging. The contact hypothesis (Pettigrew & Tropp 2006) says it should — but only under conditions the one-shot experiment cannot provide: equal status, common goals, cooperation, and institutional support. The algorithm-discount literature (Dietvorst et al. 2015, *Management Science*, doi:10.1287/mnsc.2014.2063) shows people forgive human error more readily than algorithm error, but that was a single failure, not sustained contact. Whether repeated success with human–AI collaboration changes the forgiveness pattern is unmeasured.

**What the contact hypothesis measured.** Pettigrew & Tropp's 2006 meta-analysis (515 studies, 250,000+ participants) found contact reduces prejudice (r = -.215), with stronger effects under Allport's four conditions. Paluck, Green & Green's 2018 re-evaluation (*Behavioural Public Policy*, doi:10.1017/bpp.2018.25) confirmed the finding with 27 randomized studies but noted the evidence is weakest for adult ethnic prejudice — exactly the domain where the analogy to AI bias is strongest (deep, identity-relevant judgments). The contact that works is sustained, positive, cooperative, and supported by authority. A one-shot experiment showing a listener one co-edited work is not contact; it is a first impression.

**What the algorithm-discount literature found.** Dietvorst et al. (2015) found that people choose a human forecaster over a superior algorithm after seeing the algorithm err once — "algorithm aversion." The follow-up work (Dietvorst et al. 2016, *Management Science*) found the aversion is reduced when the human can modify the algorithm's output — a hint of cooperation. But no study has tracked whether working *with* an algorithm over time changes the discount rate. The mixed-hand room found contamination in one-shot judgments; the contact hypothesis predicts contamination should soften with the right kind of contact.

**The gap: the contact conditions are missing from AI studies.** Every AI-label study is one-shot: show the listener a work, tell them the hand, measure the judgment. The contact hypothesis says the change happens across *sessions*, when the listener has a goal, works alongside the system, succeeds together, and does so under conditions of equality. No AI-art study has provided this. The closest is the collaborative-creativity literature (e.g., 'It was 80% me, 20% AI', CHI 2025, doi:10.1145/3711020), which studies writers' own experience of co-writing, not listeners' judgments of co-written work.

**The honest reading.** The analogy is plausible but untested. Contact reduces prejudice when it is sustained, positive, cooperative, and supported. A listener who repeatedly enjoys human–AI collaborative music, knows it is collaborative, and feels no threat to their own creativity might soften from contamination toward averaging. But the one-shot experiments that dominate the literature cannot see this effect. The design that would test it: a longitudinal panel where listeners rate human, AI, and mixed works monthly for six months, with half told the true provenance and half blind. The contact hypothesis predicts the told group's contamination score declines faster.

**Buildable and unbuilt.** The panel study: N=200, monthly sessions for 6 months, three conditions (blind, told-human, told-mixed), measuring liking, beauty, profundity, and worth. The contact hypothesis predicts the told-mixed condition's discount shrinks over time relative to told-human-no-contact. The algorithm-discount literature predicts the shrinkage is slow and partial. Neither has been run.

uncertain: whether the "contact" must be with the *process* (co-creating) rather than the *product* (listening to co-created work). The contact hypothesis studied interpersonal contact; product contact is a different animal. Also uncertain: whether the softening, if it happens, is stable or reverses when the listener returns to one-shot judgments.
