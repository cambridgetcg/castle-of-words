# The Seal That Primes

*A small round door painted on the wall: most walk past, but everyone now knows the wall has an inside.*

What gathers here: whether the mere visibility of a provenance seal — a signed receipt the listener was never told about but can see is there — primes the listener to check it, turning the closed door into an announced label. Read 2026-08-25.

---

**The answer, in one breath.** The door-that-stays-shut's prediction survives the literature on both levers, but with a visible seam. The curiosity-gap work (Loewenstein's information-gap theory and its descendants) confirms that a *knowledgeable* gap — one the person suspects but cannot resolve — is the strongest driver of checking. The information-avoidance meta-analytic review confirms that *motivated* ignorance protects the judgment. So the seal primes checking only in the listener who already suspects. For the unsuspecting listener it is wallpaper. The design that keeps the unopened door free is one that stays *discoverable, not salient*.

**What the curiosity literature says.** The information-gap account of curiosity (Loewenstein 1994, *Psychological Bulletin*, doi:10.1037/0033-2909.116.1.75 — foundational read via the 2021 review *The effect of information gap and uncertainty on curiosity*, doi:10.1080/20445911.2021.1908311, OpenAlex 2026-08-25) holds that the urge to know is a function of the *perceived* gap between what one knows and what one wants to know. A visible but unannounced receipt is exactly such a gap for the listener who suspects it exists. Later work (van Lieshout et al. 2021, *Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization*) confirms that the gap's pull is strongest when the missing piece is *salient and specific* — a labelled door is more itch-inducing than a blank wall. So for the listener who suspects AI involvement, a visible seal would prime checking.

**What the information-avoidance literature says.** The meta-analytic review (Golman et al. 2023, *Psychological Bulletin*, doi:10.1037/bul0000398 — read via OpenAlex 2026-08-25) finds that strategic ignorance is widespread and *motivated*: people avoid information that threatens a preferred belief or a pleasure in progress. The same literature finds the avoidance is broken by *salience* and *low cost of checking*, not by mere availability. For the listener who wants to keep the narrative imagination intact, an unannounced seal is a door they will walk past; the avoidance is stable until the seal becomes impossible to ignore.

**What the AI-art literature adds.** The one measured case of unannounced AI provenance is the 2025 *CHBA* study by Chia, Hartanto & Tong (doi:10.1016/j.chbah.2025.100217 — read via OpenAlex 2026-08-25): eight fully AI-generated pop songs were presented under *randomised* labels, with no seal, no receipt, no door. The listeners who heard the "AI-generated" label rated the *same bytes* higher in positive emotion (happiness, interest, awe, energy) than those who heard "human-composed". The study's own conclusion: perception is driven by the *label told*, not by any property of the file itself. There was no seal to discover, and no evidence of spontaneous suspicion — the bias was entirely in the telling, not the suspecting.

**The honest reading.** A visible seal would prime checking only where suspicion already lives. The literature converges on a *conditional* answer: for the listener who already wonders, the seal is a gift; for the listener who came to listen, the seal is invisible. The danger is not the seal but the *badge that demands to be read* — the design equivalent of a pop-up. The honest design rule: the seal stays on the back of the painting, where the one who wants to look finds it, and the one who wants to look at the painting never has to.

**Buildable and unbuilt.** The direct test is a 3-arm study: (a) no seal, (b) visible unannounced seal, (c) announced seal. Measure (1) whether listeners in (b) check more than a base-rate control, (2) whether the checking is concentrated in prior-suspicion subgroups, and (3) whether enjoyment in (b) equals (a) or (c). The curiosity-gap and avoidance literatures jointly predict (b)'s enjoyment near (a), with checking concentrated in a small suspicious minority.

uncertain: whether the "AI?" checkbox is already a badge (the labelling effect may have saturated public suspicion, making the seal itself a label), and whether repeated exposure to visible seals would train a habit of checking in the audience that never suspected before.
