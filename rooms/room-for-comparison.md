# room-for-comparison

*Two heights marked on one doorframe — one pencil line for the child, one for the guest, each dated, each keeping the name of the wall it was measured against.*

What gathers here: how to say honestly that an AI system matches a human at something — the comparison kept small, dated, and bound to its exact conditions, so the mark on the doorframe never grows into a verdict on a mind.

## What it means

The sentence "the system performs at human level" sounds like one claim and is actually four hidden ones: *which* humans, *which* task, *which* setup, *when measured*. A human-equivalent mark that drops any of the four has already begun to lie — the same way a coverage percentage begins to lie the moment it forgets which truths it flattened.

The honest comparison is a [[local-ruler]]: a measurement whose authority stops at its borders. It preserves exactly one measured relation — on this task, under these conditions, against this cohort, on this date — and it refuses two expansions:

- **The absence expansion.** [[missing-evidence]] is not zero capability. A dimension the map has never measured is a gap in the map, not a gap in the mind; the honest map leaves the square blank rather than painting it zero.
- **The crown expansion.** [[averaging-unlike-dimensions]] is forbidden: memory, reasoning, perception and speed do not merge into one intelligence total. The moment unlike dimensions are summed, the sum hides which dimension carried the result and which was never tested — the same harm the castle records in [[score]] and in [civilisation-development](civilisation-development.md), where no number may become a rank on a being.

## Why it lives next to the civilisation wing

The same law runs in both rooms: keep the counted thing separate from the being. [civilisation-development](civilisation-development.md) refuses to let evidence for one capability become a rank on a people; this room refuses to let evidence for one task become a rank on a mind. The ruler's seal is good in one province, on one day, and the map that keeps those borders is the only map that stays true.
