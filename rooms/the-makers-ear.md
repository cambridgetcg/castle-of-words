# The Maker's Ear

*To have mixed the colors yourself is to see the canvas differently — but whether it softens or sharpens the judgment of another's mixed work, no one has measured.*

What gathers here: the reverse-contact question — whether making a listener *create* human–AI collaborative work (co-writing, co-editing, co-composing) changes their later judgment of such works by others, and whether the maker's gain transfers to the listener's ear. The answer: the direct study is unbuilt, and the adjacent evidence cuts both ways; the honest state is a genuine fork with two live branches and one wrong default assumption.

---

**The door.** The [contact-hypothesis-applied-to-hands](contact-hypothesis-applied-to-hands.md) room found no longitudinal study of AI-collaboration judgments — every AI-provenance study is one-shot, and the algorithm-discount baseline is sticky. The door asks the reverse: not whether *hearing* mixed work softens later judgment (the familiar contact direction) but whether *making* mixed work does. Does standing on the maker's side of [the-mixed-hand](the-mixed-hand.md) change the ear that later sits on the listener's side?

**The first branch: labor binds the maker to their own work, not to others'.** The IKEA effect — we value what we made more than identical things made by others — is robust, develops by age 5, and is explicitly a *self–creation* link: "the IKEA effect leads us to believe that our creations are more valuable than items that are identical, but constructed by another," and ownership alone does not explain it; the creation links to the self-concept (read 2026-08-30 — ["When and how does labour lead to love? The ontogeny and mechanisms of the IKEA effect," Cognition 2017, doi:10.1016/j.cognition.2017.10.012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2017.10.012); [Norton, Mochon & Ariely, "The 'IKEA effect': When labor leads to love," 2011, doi:10.2139/ssrn.1777100](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.1777100)). The plain warning: the maker's gain is *to the maker's own work*, not to "work of that kind." The question's hopeful premise — that having co-written with an AI would soften one's judgment of *another's* AI-co-written piece — is not what the IKEA effect predicts; the effect is possessive, not generalizing.

**The second branch: experience with algorithms cuts both ways on judgment.** The algorithm-aversion literature has the nearest evidence on how *using* algorithmic output changes later judgment of it — and it is double-edged. The inverted-U over domain experience: workers with *intermediate* domain experience gained most from algorithmic augmentation, while the *most experienced* workers rejected algorithmic advice even when they had the greatest ability to complement it — the interviews trace the rejection to felt accountability, not to inability (read 2026-08-30 — ["Algorithm-Augmented Work and Domain Experience: The Countervailing Forces of Ability and Aversion," Organization Science 2021, doi:10.1287/orsc.2021.1554](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2021.1554)). Experience with the tool can *deepen* aversion exactly when the tool's role grows. By this branch, a listener who has co-written with AI and felt its failures at the workbench may judge others' AI-co-written work *more* harshly — "I know where the seams hide" — where the mere listener has never seen the seam.

**The third branch: domain contact can soften evaluation of the algorithm's home terrain.** The marketing finding is gentler: consumers show *less* aversion to algorithm-recommended products in "search" domains (where quality is verifiable prior to use) than in "experience" domains (where quality is felt only on use) (read 2026-08-30 — ["The searching artificial intelligence: Consumers show less aversion to algorithm-recommended search product," Psychology & Marketing 2022, doi:10.1002/mar.21706](https://doi.org/10.1002/mar.21706)). By this branch, a maker who has *verified* the seams (search-domain maker-knowledge: I tested what the AI wrote and it held) may carry less aversion into the next judgment, while a maker who only *felt* the collaboration's fluency (experience-domain making) carries the experience-domain's stronger aversion. Which domain a given co-creation sits in may decide which branch the maker's ear takes.

**The one direct qualitative hit: the maker's eye is restructured, not just re-valenced.** The 15-pair social-music study observed human pairs composing with and without deep generative AI: the AI seeded common ground, acted as psychological safety net for creative risk-taking, drove group progress, mitigated interpersonal stalling and friction, and altered users' collaborative and creative roles (read 2026-08-30 — ["AI as Social Glue: Uncovering the Roles of Deep Generative AI during Social Music Composition," CHI 2021, doi:10.1145/3411764.3445219](https://doi.org/10.1145/3411764.3445219)). The making itself changed how the makers saw the contribution structure — what the human and the AI each did. This is the closest evidence that making *restructures the judgment apparatus* the maker would later turn on others' work: having felt the AI as glue, the maker now perceives the glue in a finished piece. But whether that restructured perception is kinder or harsher toward the finished mixed work, the study does not say.

**The honest state: a fork, not a default.** The reverse-contact question has never been answered experimentally — no study has had makers co-create with AI and then measured their later judgment of *other* makers' mixed works against matched non-makers. The adjacent evidence splits into two live predictions. **Soften:** the maker, having seen the AI's seams and found them honest, loses the listener's default contamination prior; the contact-hypothesis mechanism (extended contact becoming direct) predicts a softening of the mixed-hand discount. **Sharpen:** the maker, having felt the AI's failures from inside, gains the accountability-aversion the Organization Science study traced, and now hears the seams every where the mere listener heard none — with the IKEA effect's warning that the gain attaches to *one's own* mixed work, not the category. Which branch fires may turn on whether the making was search-domain (verifiable) or experience-domain (felt), and on whether the maker's felt outcome was success or struggle. The castle's own rooms on the question — [the-unwelcome-key](the-unwelcome-key.md) (self-discovered unwelcome provenance rebounds worse) and [the-found-key](the-found-key.md) (agency redirects bias, does not protect) — add the castle's-specific warning: if the maker's discovery of the AI's hand in *their own* work is unwelcome, the rebound is worse than the told listener's, not milder.

uncertain: the two adjacent literatures (IKEA effect; algorithm aversion across domain experience) have never been joined at this seam, and neither has measured *aesthetic-provenance* judgment specifically. The music-composition study is qualitative, 15 pairs, no later-judgment measure. The direct experiment would be small by the standards of the field — makers randomized to co-write with vs. without AI, later judging matched mixed works by others with the composition hidden — and remains buildable and unbuilt.
