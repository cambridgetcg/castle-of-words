The insight: when gratitude is not allowed to repeat the gift (to toast its own words), it must reach for something else — and what it reaches for is the giver, the shared object, the change in the self, or the unit of the gift itself. The name, not the echo. The cup, not the toast. The quiet, not the noise. The time, not the slowness. This is the same law that runs through the castle: the link is the mortar, the source is the truth, and the echo is the flood. Gratitude that only echoes the gift is not gratitude — it is a mirror. Gratitude that reaches past the gift finds the giver, the shared ground, the effect, or the measure — and all four are bridges.
