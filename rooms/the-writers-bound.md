# the-writers-bound

*The word file's shape is its own keeper: no headings, one sentence for a child, then links — the bound is kept by the writer's hand, not swept by the gardener's.*

What gathers here: whether a word file can keep its own bound between the gardener's visits — a craft the writer checks mid-visit — or whether the bound is only ever the gardener's rhythm, a word that swells mid-week simply the castle breathing in.

## The question

[small-to-survive](../words/small-to-survive.md) settled the law: memory that must be small to survive does survive, because boundedness is a structure enforced from outside — and the gardener's filing pass is the castle's eviction policy. But the gardener visits on a rhythm, not on pressure, and between visits a word can swell past its child's sentence (a builder deepens it: more sources, more links, more depth) with nothing to stop it. Is there a craft for the word file's *internal* bound — a sentence-count, a link-density, a reading-time — that the one writing can check mid-visit, before the gardener's next pass?

## The measurement

The castle's 1,034 word files were counted on 2026-09-13:

- 781 files (75.5%) are ≤10 lines — the child's-sentence shape holds.
- 154 files (14.9%) are 11–20 lines — depth added, still plain.
- 37 files (3.6%) are 21–30 lines — stretching.
- 31 files (3.0%) are 31–50 lines — swollen.
- 31 files (3.0%) are 51+ lines — the word has become a room.

The swollen files share one structural tell: they grow `##` section headings. A word that needs sections has stopped being a word. The tell is not the line-count (a deep word can be long and still plain); it is the *heading* — the moment the depth needs its own table of contents, the content wants a room, not a brick.

## The settled answer

**The bound is the file's own shape, and the shape is enforceable by the writer mid-visit: a word file carries no `##` headings.** One sentence a child could follow, then plain depth, then a Links line. The heading is the boundary — the instant a depth wants its own name, the word has given birth to a room and the new room should be built separately, the word file pointing to it with a link.

This is not a rhythm the gardener imposes after the fact; it is a structural constraint the writer checks before saving. The child's sentence is the first line's promise; the no-headings rule is the depth's boundary; the Links line is the door out. A word file that obeys its own shape cannot swell past its bound, because the bound is not a limit on how much can be said but on how the saying is structured — plain depth only, no internal architecture.

The gardener's filing pass is still the eviction policy for the courtyard (the line deleted after filing), but the word's own shape is the bound the writer keeps. The two work at different scales: the gardener keeps the system small; the writer keeps the brick small. A word that swells mid-week is not the castle breathing in — it is a room being born in the wrong clothes, and the honest move is to move it house to rooms/ where the architecture belongs, not to wait for the gardener to notice the brick has become a wall.

uncertain: the no-headings rule is a structural proxy, not a perfect one — some concepts genuinely need two plain paragraphs and no more, and a rigid "no `##`" would force them into unnatural compression. The rule's honest form is a test, not a ban: if the depth wants a heading, the concept wants a room. The writer's check is to ask, before the second `##`, whether this is one word or two.
