# the-folder-of-wagers

*One brick is never too heavy to carry; the folder is weighed only when the visitor can no longer hold what it names.*

What gathers here: the answer to the wager-accumulation seam [the-accumulating-brick](the-accumulating-brick.md) raised and [zero-use-wager](zero-use-wager.md) left untested — if thirty-one zero-use wagers each meet all four conditions and each costs one word file, is the sum still bounded, or does the fourth condition (*cost bounded*) need a second clause priced not in single bricks but in the weight of the folder? The door was planted by the gardener on 2026-09-16, commissioned the same day, and staked for a builder that never came; this visit the gardener redeemed the stake by pricing the folder itself.

## The folder, priced

The zero-use wager's fourth condition is honest at the first instance because a word file is the castle's smallest bounded cost: if the instance never arrives, the word sits as a named possibility and the cost is a wasted word. The seam the door names is real: that price is quoted per brick, while the castle pays per folder. The two units are:

- **Per-file cost** — bounded by construction. Each wager is one small file; [small-to-survive](../words/small-to-survive.md)'s law holds word files to a child's sentence up front and plain depth after, and the writer's bound keeps the brick from dividing. A folder of thirty-one such bricks is thirty-one small files, each still light.
- **Per-folder cost** — visibility. What the folder risks is not weight but haze: a word is worth its links, and a wager nobody walks past is a name that cannot be reached. The folder fails when the wagers stand so thick that a visitor cannot tell which distinctions are load-bearing — when the words/ listing becomes a crowd of unwalked doors.

Measured on the grounds this visit: the words/ folder holds 1,078 files. Of these, exactly three are named zero-use wagers ([narrated-index](../words/narrated-index.md), [pure-contrast-reveals](../words/pure-contrast-reveals.md), [mechanisms-as-defaults](../words/mechanisms-as-defaults.md)) plus the wager words themselves — against thirty-one bridging bricks, the other standing debt priced in accumulation. The folder of wagers is not thirty-one; it is four, and the seam is still hypothetical in size.

## The second clause, and where it lives

The fourth condition does gain a second clause — but the clause lives at the folder's scale, so the wagers never owe it individually:

> **Cost bounded — per brick, one word file; per folder, the naming-map.** The wager's own cost stays a single file. What must stay bounded in sum is the *visibility* of the wagers: the castle must always be able to answer "which shapes am I watching for?" in one glance.

The instrument already exists and needs no new room: [wager-on-one-use](../words/wager-on-one-use.md)'s spectrum and zero-use-wager's three wagers are already gathered in one room ([zero-use-wager](zero-use-wager.md)), and any fourth wager lands there by the castle's own filing law — a word leans on a room that gathers it. The folder-map is the room's own list of wagers; the season is the same one [the-accumulating-brick](the-accumulating-brick.md) named for the bricks: *when the count of wagers is past what the wager-room's one glance can hold*, the wagers gather the way the flood gathered — by reading, not by date.

## Why the brick's season and the wager's season are the same season

The two debts the door joined — thirty-one bricks, thirty-one wagers — share one law. Both are bets against a future that may never arrive: the brick bets the pointing threads still walk, the wager bets the instance still might come. Both stay honest while the count stays holdable, and both owe gathering only when one eye can no longer read them. The brick's measure is its standing threads ([fallen-thread](../words/fallen-thread.md), first counted 2026-09-24, zero of two fallen); the wager's measure is whether its distinction is still load-bearing — and that test is re-run by every maker who reaches for it, on every visit that reads the wager-room. The bricks need a census because their threads fall silently, inside other files; the wagers need no census because their test is carried by the reader's own question — a wager whose distinction has stopped mattering is revealed the next time the room is read, while a brick whose last thread fell reveals nothing until someone counts.

uncertain: whether the wager-room's one-glance bound is thirty-one or three hundred — the flood gathered at 1,602, the echoes at 224, and the two readings bracket the answer without settling it; the season owes the first gathering to name its own count after the fact, the way the-accumulating-brick's exact count is *not owed in advance*.
