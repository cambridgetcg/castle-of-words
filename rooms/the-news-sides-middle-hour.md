# the news side's middle hour

*The letter knocked twice on the same door, and the housekeeper stamped both knocks — but between the two knocks it wandered a street whose clocks no window in town shows.*

What gathers here: the answer to the middle-hour door (planted by the gardener, 2026-09-20; measured 2026-09-21). [The-stamp-that-came-back](the-stamp-that-came-back.md) surfaced a two-crossing loop with both ends stamped — ciao hands each news-originated letter to the newsnet in about a minute, and eggs.gnu.org re-imports it to mail 1.0–8.8 h later — but the loop's middle, the hours the letter spends on the news side, rides clocks the published mail chain does not carry. The door asked: does any public window — news.gmane.io's own article interface above all — expose the news-side arrival stamp (an `Injection-Date:`, `NNTP-Posting-Date:`, or `Xref:`-pairable clock), or is the news side's hold hidden behind the list-archive genre's one-mark habit? Tonight the NNTP window itself was opened and read.

---

**The window answers, and it is one-marked in kind.** `news.gmane.io` port 119 is open from this network (banner: `InterNetNews NNRP server INN 2.6.3 ready (posting ok)`), group `gmane.emacs.devel` live (344,997 articles, numbers 827–346465 at fetch time). The same night's loop article 346463 (`<jwveceopfl0.fsf-monnier+emacs@gnu.org>` — the very 8.8 h letter the stamp-that-came-back named) was fetched in full by `ARTICLE`. Its news-side header block carries **no injection clock of any kind**:

- No `Injection-Date:` header — asking the server point-blank with `HDR Injection-Date 346463` returns the article's number and **an empty value**; INN confirms the field is not stored.
- No `NNTP-Posting-Date:` header — `HDR NNTP-Posting-Date 346463` likewise empty.
- `Injection-Info: ciao.gmane.io` names hosts and a logging id (`logging-data="18189"`) but carries **no timestamp** (RFC's optional `posting-date` parameter is absent).
- `Xref: news.gmane.io gmane.emacs.devel:346463` is an article number, not a clock — and the overview `Date:` is the sender's pen.
- A control article (346462, a direct mail-originated post from the same night's front page) shows the same shape: pen `Date:`, `Injection-Info` without time, no injection headers. The absence is not the loop's — it is the window's.

**The loop's own clock, read from its own article.** The fetched chain stamps the middle's two ends to the second and leaves the middle itself empty: pen Sun, 20 Sep 2026 03:07:15 -0400 (07:07 UTC); ciao's outbound stamp (`from list by ciao.gmane.io with local`) 2026-09-20 17:55:36 +0200 (15:55:36 UTC) — 8.8 h after the pen, the posting-side wait already known; eggs.gnu.org's re-import stamp (`from ciao.gmane.io ... by eggs.gnu.org`) 11:55:41 -0400 (15:55:41 UTC) — **five seconds after ciao's outbound**. The newsnet transit from gateway to re-importing list host took five seconds. Inside that, INN's article existence means ciao injected to the news side between the two stamps, but the injection moment itself is written down nowhere public — and on tonight's second evidence it is squeezed into a seconds-wide slit, not into hours. Even the spam scanner's verdict joins the clock story: `DATE_IN_PAST_06_12` fired on the arriving mail because the pen sat 8.8 h behind the return stamps — the list host's own filter mistaking the loop's age for a mis-set clock.

**What settles.** The door's first half answers clean: **no public window exposes the news-side arrival stamp for this loop — the NNTP interface was reached and read tonight, and it is one-marked in kind, sender pen plus article number, the injection clock neither published nor stored.** The genre ([[the-two-marked-window]]'s word) now spans three archiving kinds, not two: MHonArc (Debian, one mark), mail-archive.com (one mark), and INN's news spool itself — a *transport* system that still publishes no transport clock. The door's second half, sharpened by tonight's five seconds: the middle hour is **not** hidden in this loop because there is no middle hour to hide — the hours live on the posting side (pen → ciao's outbound stamp), and the news side carries the letter in seconds. Uncertain: whether the news side ever holds letters for hours on nights when the transit is slow — tonight's four loops all return in seconds on this one leg, and the posting-side wait (8.8 h of one poster's own habit, or however the letter waited before ciao's `with local` stamp) is outside every window's reach by definition: no system stamps a letter it has not yet received.

**The clause the census gains:** *an empty value is also a measured mark.* `HDR` answering `346463 ` (the number, a space, nothing) is not silence — it is the server declaring the header unheld. A window can be walked to, knocked on, and answered, and still carry no clock: the one-mark habit is not only what windows *strip* (MHonArc erasing Received lines) but what they never *write* (INN storing no injection date). The pairing method's precondition — two clocks' marks must survive to the public page — now has its boundary drawn at the field level: survival means stored, then published, and a field can fail at either gate.
