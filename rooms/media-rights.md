# media-rights

*A picture may be free to look at, free to use, and still tell a lie — or locked behind a gate, and still tell the truth. The rights and the truth travel on separate roads.*

What gathers here: the craft of tracking media rights and evidence strength as separate axes — because public access is not reuse permission, reuse permission is not historical proof, and a legally reusable image can still carry a false caption.

The two axes, plainly:

**Axis 1: Reuse rights.** Can you legally use this material? This axis has at least three positions:
- Public access (anyone can view it)
- Licensed reuse (anyone can use it, under stated terms)
- Restricted (permission required)

These are legal facts, not judgments about truth. A public-domain image and a copyrighted image can depict the same event; the rights tell you what you may do, not what happened.

**Axis 2: Evidence strength.** What does this material actually prove? This axis has at least four positions:
- Authoritative (the source is known, the chain of custody is documented, the content is verified)
- Corroborated (multiple independent sources agree)
- Uncorroborated (a single source, unverified)
- Disputed (sources conflict)

These are evidential judgments, not legal facts. An open-licensed image can be unverifiable; a restricted archive image can be authoritative.

The law: the two axes must never be fused. A system that says "this image is open, therefore it is true" lies twice — once about the rights (openness is not truth) and once about the evidence (truth is not openness). The honest system records both axes per asset, and names what the material supports and what it does not prove.

The four cells of the honest media record:

| | Strong evidence | Weak evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Reusable** | Open, verified — the gold standard | Open, unverified — the trap: free to use, free to mislead |
| **Restricted** | Locked, verified — authoritative but inaccessible | Locked, unverified — doubly uncertain |

The craft: for every media asset, record the rights status, the evidence strength, the source, the chain of custody, and what the material does *not* prove. A caption is a claim about the image, not a property of it — and the honest record keeps the caption separate from the asset, with its own source and its own uncertainty.
