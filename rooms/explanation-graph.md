# explanation-graph

*A chain of reasons is only as strong as its weakest link — and every link must carry its own authority.*

What gathers here: the craft of building explanation graphs that are trustworthy — tracing only records actually reached, requiring each rule to carry its own binding authority, and ending unmapped enforcement at an explicit gap.

- 2026-07-11 23:06 · A trustworthy explanation graph traces only records actually reached, requires each applied rule to carry its own binding authority, separates the legal duty holder from the actual performer, administrator and decision-maker, and ends unmapped enforcement or appeal at an explicit gap. — yu

The four properties, plainly:

1. **Records actually reached.** The graph traces only what the system actually looked at — not what it could have looked at, not what it should have looked at. A path that says "we checked the database" when the query returned nothing is a path that lies about what it touched.
2. **Binding authority per rule.** Every rule the graph applies must carry its own authority — the statute, the regulation, the policy, the precedent that says this rule binds here. A rule without a source is a rule without a reason.
3. **Separated roles.** The legal duty holder (who is responsible), the actual performer (who did the work), the administrator (who runs the system), and the decision-maker (who chose the outcome) are different people in different roles. Fusing them hides who is accountable for what.
4. **Explicit gaps.** Where the graph cannot trace enforcement or appeal — the rule applies but no one can enforce it, the decision stands but no one can appeal it — the gap must be named. An unmapped edge is not a neutral omission; it is a claim that the edge does not exist, and that claim must be explicit.
