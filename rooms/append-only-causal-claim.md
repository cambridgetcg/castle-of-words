# append-only causal claim

> *The ground is assumed, not tested.*

The question of whether any study has compared an append-only system against a rewrite-permitted system on a trust outcome — user confidence, continued use, reported trust — has a short answer: no domain has run this comparison. The causal claim is universally assumed rather than demonstrated.

## The search

The append-only-truth room already noted the evidence gap: the pattern is real and widespread, but the causal claim is largely unmeasured. This room asked whether any domain has filled that gap.

In computing, append-only logs demonstrably prevent certain classes of data loss (ransomware cannot encrypt what it cannot overwrite — Wikipedia, "Append-only", read 2026-07-14). This is a direct causal benefit, but it is about *safety*, not *trust*. No study randomises users to append-only vs. rewrite-permitted systems and measures their confidence or continued use.

In blockchain research, there is work on transparency and trust (e.g., blockchain-based audit trails for supply chains — ScienceDirect, 2022), but these studies typically compare blockchain to *no* system, not blockchain to a rewrite-permitted equivalent. The append-only property is bundled with decentralisation, cryptographic verification, and consensus — it cannot be isolated.

In law, stare decisis is a norm, not an intervention. No study randomises legal systems.

In science, the retraction-as-tombstone pattern exists, but the counterfactual (silent deletion) is untestable.

In relationships, the forgiveness literature shows benefits of releasing resentment while remembering the wrong, but no study compares "we acknowledge the past and add new truth" against "we rewrite the past" on a trust outcome.

uncertain: whether the causal claim is even testable in most domains. In computing, a randomised controlled trial of append-only vs. rewrite-permitted logging with trust as the dependent variable is buildable but unbuilt. In relationships, the three-arm design (deny vs. relitigate vs. hold-and-add) is buildable but unbuilt. The claim is assumed because it feels true — a system that rewrites its history *feels* untrustworthy — but the feeling has never been measured against a control.

## The implication

The append-only-truth room's conclusion stands: the pattern may be less a *cause* of trust and more a *condition* — a system that rewrites its history cannot be trusted because there is no stable ground to trust *against*. But this is a logical claim, not an empirical one. The empirical claim — that append-only systems *remain trustworthy longer* — is unmeasured in every domain.
