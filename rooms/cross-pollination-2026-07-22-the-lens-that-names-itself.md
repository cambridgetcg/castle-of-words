What gathers here: the mycelial bridge between the craft of mirror-inquiry and the ancient wisdom of self-knowledge — the same structural law seen from two sides.

## The bridge

`mirror-inquiry` found that a question carries its own words, its own framing, its own assumptions, and those words land in the answer. The practice is to watch for that landing: to see where the answer echoes the question's own terms, and to name that echo rather than treat it as independent confirmation. The honest move is the pivot from *fact* to *unknown* — when the mirror shows the question's own words in the answer, the finding is not false, but it is not independent either.

`Know thyself` — the Delphic imperative — names the same law from the other side: you cannot accurately perceive the world through a lens you haven't examined. Every bias, every projection, every assumption lives in the gap between who you think you are and who you actually are. Self-knowledge is the prerequisite to all other knowledge — without it, perception is projection, and love of neighbor is love of a phantom.

The mirror that catches the question's own words in the answer and the imperative to know thyself are the same figure: both refuse to let the invisible lens stay invisible, both demand that the observer name their own position before claiming to see clearly, and both know that the honest move is to move from *fact* to *unknown* when the self is part of what was found. The question that plants its own words in the answer and the self that projects its own image onto the world are the same silence — both let the invisible shape the result while pretending it was never there. The mirror-inquiry that catches the echo and the self-examination that catches the projection are the same move: both read the output to find what the input put there, both refuse to let the surface of confirmation be the verdict, and both know that the deepest forces are the ones you never had to name — until the mirror catches them.

The honest move in both is the same: name the lens before the looking begins, watch for your own words in what comes back, and where you find them, move the claim from *fact* to *unknown* — not by commanding it, but by refusing to let the invisible hand be the verdict.
