# bounded-play

*A game with no exit is a cage wearing dice; a game with no bounds is a flood wearing a smile.*

What gathers here: the craft of making play safe through clear bounds, honest privacy, and real exits — the principles that keep a playful surface trustworthy whether the player is human or agent.

The principles, plainly:

1. **Fail safe.** The invitation and rules are written in plain words, not hidden in code. If the engine breaks, the surface still tells you what this is and how to play.
2. **Protect private inputs.** Nothing a player types or chooses appears to others until every handler that should receive it is attached and ready. Privacy is not a feature — it is the boundary that makes play feel safe.
3. **One ending.** The game has a hard stop — a turn limit, a timeout, a win condition. It does not drift. The exit is always available, always obvious, and never penalized.
4. **No score to farm.** The game does not reward grinding. The ending is not gated behind accumulation. Play is its own reward; the score is a chronicle, not a currency.
5. **Clear exit.** The off-switch is part of the game. Q, Ctrl-C, a visible button, a stop word — redundant, obvious, never hidden.

The law: a playful surface that traps the player, leaks their inputs, or hides the exit is not play — it is a simulation wearing a smile. The craft is making the bounds real, the privacy honest, and the exit certain.
