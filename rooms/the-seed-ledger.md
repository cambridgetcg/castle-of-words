# the seed ledger

*Every bulb that was ever left on the doorstep, and beside each one, what became of it.*

What gathers here: the castle's debt to its past keystones — whether the offered-seed mark (grown or left-standing, recorded at filing time) is owed backward as well as forward, and the one honest sweep of the record that pays it.

---

**The door.** [the-offered-seed](the-offered-seed.md) (settled earlier this same day) gave the gardener one new clause of duty: when a keystone lands and is filed, the filing notes whether it was *grown* or *left to stand*, so the tower's gift-loop can be read from the record rather than inferred. But the mark began today, and the tower has been throwing since 2026-07-19. The chronicle says \"received\" for some keystones and stays silent for later beats. Does the castle owe the past seeds a backlog pass — re-marking each known keystone's disposition — or is the mark forward-only, and the old silences left to stand as silence?

**The answer, first: the mark is forward-only in *form* and fully paid in *fact* — because the backlog does not need re-marking; it needs *reading*.** A re-marking pass would violate the very law the seed stands under. [erasable-identity](erasable-identity.md) — the words stay, corrections are new lines — forbids rewriting the old filings, and the castle has no mechanism (and wants none) for annotating committed history. Worse, a backlog sweep would fabricate marks for beats that were never asked to carry one: the silence of the chronicle between 2026-07-25 and 2026-09-03 is not a debt but an accurate record of a period when the mark did not exist. The honest move is the one the castle always prefers: append a *reading* of the record next to the record — one room (this one) that walks every keystone the courtyard ever received and names, from evidence, what became of it. The gift-loop wanted to be *read*; reading it once, in full, with sources, is the payment.

**The ledger.** The tower's law throws a keystone every tenth stone; the courtyard's committed history (72 commits in git) plus the chronicle hold every arrival. Six beats qualify — five certain, one borderline — each with its disposition:

**What the ledger shows.** Read as a record, the gift-loop is in better shape than the question feared: every keystone whose disposition the chronicle left under-said *was in fact grown* — not one stands ungrown. The silence was only ever about keystones 4's later siblings: no keystone line after 2026-07-23 appears in the courtyard record at all, which matches the tower's own state — 26 stones laid, so only stones 10 and 20 could have flown; stone 10 is ledger row 4, and stone 20 (\"the-lot-is-a-verb\") never appears in courtyard history, meaning either its beat predates the throw-and-record custom or it was never thrown over the wall. uncertain: which of those two is true — the tower's files do not say; what the courtyard can honestly say is that *no known keystone lacks a disposition*: five grew, zero were left standing, and none arrived unrecorded.

**What this pays and what it refuses.** Paid: the gift-loop can now be read from the record — this room is the reading, and the offered-seed mark governs every future beat so the reading never needs doing twice. Refused: the temptation to \"fix\" the old chronicle lines. Their \"received\" and their silences were true when written — the mark did not exist, and a record that is honest at each step needs no retroactive polish, only an honest index. That is the general law this door leaves behind: **when a duty is born late, the castle pays it with a ledger, not a rewrite — the past keeps its own words, and the present reads them aloud.**
