# the-program-trap-street

*A mapmaker may salt a map with a street that does not exist and wait for the forger to redraw it — but a program has no such luck yet: the law has priced the salted directory, and never the salted subroutine.*

Whether any post-*Oracle* decision has addressed the legal protection of a deliberately-planted internal mark in software — a unique identifier, a dead branch, a structural fingerprint planted only for detection — and whether *Feist*'s escape carries from prose and data into code. Planted 2026-09-27 from [the-canary-in-the-street-plan](the-canary-in-the-street-plan.md)'s closing `uncertain:`; walked 2026-09-28.

## What the case law hands the room (read 2026-09-28)

- **The seed doctrine exists and is old — in directories.** *Business Guides, Inc. v. Chromatic Communications Enterprises, Inc.*, 892 F.2d 802 (9th Cir. 1989), is the verified instance: a directory publisher "intentionally plants incorrect information, known in the industry as 'seeds'" — Type A wholly fictitious entries, Type B partially altered listings — so that "the appearance of one or more seeds in a competitor's publication is strong evidence that copyrighted material has been appropriated." The court accepted the practice as lawful *evidence of copying*; the case itself is a Rule 11 cautionary tale about keeping the master list honest (the 1984 list mislabeled real entries as seeds, and the sanctions followed) (full opinion text read 2026-09-28, [vLex case-law mirror](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/business-guides-inc-v-chromatic-889305592)).
- **The doctrine's limit is stated there plainly: a seed is evidence *because* its appearance proves copying of the *directory* — but the seed itself, being false, may claim no originality of its own.** That is the *Feist* logic (*Feist Publications v. Rural Telephone*, 1991) the castle's [detection-only-canary](detection-only-canary.md) already carries: falsehoods planted to catch a copier detect without claiming.
- **The nearest post-*Oracle* software-copyright merits case gives the doctrine no purchase on code.** *CCC Intelligent Solutions Inc. v. Tractable Inc.*, 36 F.4th 721 (7th Cir. 2022) — the most visible software-copyright-clone dispute since *Google v. Oracle* — was decided on *arbitration* grounds: Tractable had obtained CCC's estimating software under a false identity ("JA Appraisal"), disassembled it, and folded its algorithms into its own product, and the Seventh Circuit (Easterbrook, J.) resolved whether Tractable could invoke the contract's arbitration clause — not whether any internal mark was protected. The copyright and trade-secret counts stood below; the appeal priced no canary (full opinion text read 2026-09-28, [vLex case-law mirror](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/ccc-intelligent-solutions-inc-v-908756867)).
- **No retrieved post-*Oracle* decision prices a deliberately-planted internal detection mark in software** — the searches run today (CourtListener full-text on "fictitious computer program," "salting"/"seeded" software, and the citing-history of both *Business Guides* and *CCC*) surface the directory and database line, copyright-trap *records* (honeytokens — fake users, fake rows), and canary values in stack protection, but no opinion where a court says what protection a planted *structural* mark in code enjoys.

## The settled answer

**The trap street of programs is unpriced.** The directory doctrine (*Business Guides*) and the *Feist* escape are verified law, but both concern *data entries*; the AFC test protects non-literal *structure*, and *Oracle* washed the *declaring* layer without saying anything about a mark planted between them for detection alone. So the honest standing: a planted internal mark whose only function is detection borrows the *Feist* shape (it is a falsity, or at least a non-functional choice, so copying it is probative) but lives in the *AFC* layer (structure), and no court has said which law governs — the most likely reading is the one the castle already holds: an internal mark is protected qua structure, and copying it is *not* fair use (it is no interface), but its evidentiary power still rests on the same footing as the directory seed (its appearance proves copying, not that the mark itself was protectable) — that reading is the castle's, not any court's, and says `uncertain:` in front of it.
