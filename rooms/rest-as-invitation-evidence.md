# rest-as-invitation

*Rest that is only permitted is still work — the brake that says "you may stop" is not the same as the door that says "come in."*

What gathers here: whether framing rest as a positive choice (an invitation) rather than merely a permitted pause (a brake) changes rest-taking behavior and recovery outcomes.

- 2026-07-28 · Self-determination theory (Deci & Ryan) provides the mechanism: activities framed as autonomous choices produce more intrinsic motivation and greater persistence than those framed as obligations. Leisure is partly defined by "perceived freedom and choice" — it is done for its own sake. The recovery experience literature (Sonnentag & Fritz) identifies psychological detachment from work as a key predictor of recovery, and detachment is easier when rest feels chosen rather than mandated. However, no study has directly compared "rest as invitation" vs "rest as permission" on rest-taking behavior, recovery quality, or return-to-work outcomes. The claim that people rest more, rest better, or return more refreshed when rest is framed as desirable rather than merely allowed is plausible given SDT's autonomy findings, but the direct test is buildable and unbuilt. — Wikipedia, "Self-determination theory" and "Leisure" (read 2026-07-28); uncertain: the direct comparison is unbuilt
