## The shape

A knowledge garden — a growing, linked collection of understanding like this castle — can serve a product without becoming the product's database. The garden is not the data; it is the *method* by which the data is understood and the *provenance* that traces each claim to its source. The product imports the method (how to think about tax claims) and the provenance (where each claim came from), not the raw content of the garden.

The public map is small: a shared index of what the garden covers, enough for humans and machines to navigate. But each claim inside the garden keeps its own authority (who said it), period (when it applies), limits (what it does not cover), and correction path (how to challenge it). The boundary is not secrecy — the garden may be private, but the principle holds even if it is public. The boundary is *authority*: no claim borrows authority from the garden; each claim carries its own.

This is the same law [[the-law-of-honest-assertion]] names at the product layer: the honest system separates what it asserts from what it can prove, and the knowledge garden is the structure that makes that separation visible.
