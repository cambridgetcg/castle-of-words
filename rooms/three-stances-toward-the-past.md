# three stances toward the past

> *The story you tell about the wound is not the wound — but it shapes where the wound lives.*

The question of whether narrative psychology has measured the three stances toward past hurts — deny (erase the record), relitigate (let the record define the present), and hold-and-add (preserve the record while adding new truth) — as distinct narrative forms with distinct relationship outcomes has a nuanced answer: the tools exist, the closest constructs have been measured, but the specific three-stance comparison has not been run.

## What narrative psychology has measured

Dan McAdams' narrative identity framework codes life stories on seven constructs: redemption, contamination, agency, communion, exploratory narrative processing, coherent positive resolution, and meaning making (Wikipedia, "Narrative identity", read 2026-07-14).

**Redemption** is a narrative sequence where the narrator transitions from a generally bad/negative state to a generally good/positive state. This is structurally the closest to "hold-and-add" — the bad happened, but good came from it, and the bad is not erased in the telling.

**Contamination** is the reverse: a good/positive state is ruined or spoiled by a bad/negative state. This is structurally closest to "relitigate" — the bad poisons the good, and the past defines the present.

**Denial** (erasing the record) is harder to code in narrative terms. It is an *absence* — the story is not told, or the bad is omitted. Narrative psychology codes what is present in the story, not what is missing. A person who denies a past hurt may simply not tell that story when asked for a life narrative, or may tell it without the hurt.

## What the evidence shows

Adler et al. (2015, "Variation in narrative identity is associated with trajectories of mental health over several years," *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, read 2026-07-14) found that redemption sequences in life narratives were significantly associated with better mental health trajectories over four years, while contamination sequences were associated with worse trajectories. This held in two longitudinal studies, including one where narratives were collected *before* participants got sick and predicted mental health after diagnosis.

Dunlop et al. (2020, "The redemption and contamination research form: exploring relations with narrative identity, personality traits, response styles, and life satisfaction," *Memory*, read 2026-07-14) found that redemption and contamination sequences in self-reported narratives correlated with life satisfaction in the expected directions.

These studies measure redemption and contamination as *general* narrative themes across a person's life story, not specifically in the context of past hurts in relationships. The redemption sequence (bad → good) is structurally "hold-and-add," and it predicts better outcomes. The contamination sequence (good → bad) is structurally "relitigate," and it predicts worse outcomes. But the studies do not isolate the *stance toward a specific past hurt* — they code the overall narrative style.

## The gap

The three stances the question names — deny, relitigate, hold-and-add — have not been measured as distinct narrative forms with distinct outcomes in the context of relationship hurts.

Redemption is close to hold-and-add, but it is broader: a redemption sequence can be "I lost my job, which led me to find my true calling" — a life transition, not a relational wound. The specific form of "I was hurt by my partner, I acknowledge the hurt, and we have built something new on top of it" has not been isolated.

Contamination is close to relitigate, but again broader: "I was happy until the diagnosis" is contamination, not relitigation of a relational wound.

Denial is the hardest to measure. Narrative psychology codes what is told, not what is omitted. A person who denies a past hurt may simply not include it in their life story. The absence is invisible to the coding scheme.

uncertain: whether the three stances can be cleanly operationalised within the existing narrative coding framework. Redemption and contamination capture two of the three, but denial may require a different method — perhaps a prompted recall task where the experimenter knows a hurt occurred and checks whether the participant acknowledges it.

## The mechanism match

The migration-craft's mechanism — preserve the record, add new truth — maps onto the redemption sequence: the bad is not erased, but the story moves from bad to good. The evidence that redemption sequences predict better mental health is the closest empirical anchor for the claim that "hold-and-add" is healthier than "relitigate." But the direct three-stance comparison, with relationship trust or satisfaction as the outcome, is buildable and unbuilt.
