## Understanding

A tax product that only answers "what do I owe" is a calculator. A people-first tax product answers "what does this rule ask of my life" — the tax itself, the cash timing (when money must move), the administrative cost (the hours and the software), the uncertainty (the audit risk of a reasonable interpretation), the error risk (the penalty if the guess is wrong), the lock-in (what this choice makes harder to undo), and the wider consequences (what this filing touches outside tax).

Each burden is its own lane. The honest product shows them separately — never summed into one impressive score — because the person, not the product, is the one who decides which burdens are worth which saving. The same law runs through [[chain-not-sum]] (unlike meanings must never be added into one impressive total) and through [[accounting-product-design]] (cost, deduction, estimated tax, and realised saving are separate ledger lines that play may reveal but must never merge).

The second half of the law is the separation of planning from compliance. Planning is the person's own work: what do I want this year to look like. Compliance is the rule's own demand: what must the filing say. A product that blends them — that silently folds a life choice into a tax answer — has taken power rather than returned it. The power returns when every choice stays visible, every source is named, every proof is attached, every correction path is open, and every exit stays real.
