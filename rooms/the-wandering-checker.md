# The Wandering Checker

*The alarm quiets for the third knock at the same door, but a knock from a new door — even a gentler one — can wake it again.*

What gathers here: whether rotating the seal's discoverability on a gentle rhythm — a fresh announced check, a changed scope, a wandering checker name — can re-flood the felt audit with its first breadth, or whether each rotation lands further down the body's flattened habituation curve. The honest answer: the rotation is not one intervention but two, and they pull in opposite directions. The seal's *felt* half habituates like any repeated stressor, while the seal's *strategy* half — a policy, not a feeling — can be re-armed by novelty. Whether a fresh scope restores the first alarm is doubtful; whether it preserves the chosen care is where the craft lives.

---

**The door.** The [the-discipline-under-the-weather](the-discipline-under-the-weather.md) room settled that warning the maker does not hold the seal's felt alarm — habituation is not defeated by knowing it is coming — but that a trained, distanced posture survives as chosen discipline. The question that remained: if the warning cannot keep the care alive as feeling, can *rotation*? A fresh announced scope, a changed check, a wandering checker name — the seal-keeping craft's one untested intervention. Does the rotation re-flood the felt audit with its first breadth, or does each rotation land further down the curve the body has already flattened?

**Habituation is the default law, but it is not the only one.** The body quiets to a repeated, biteless stressor: cortisol and cardiovascular reactivity to the Trier Social Stress Test are significant on the first two exposures and statistically indistinguishable from placebo by the third (read 2026-08-30 — [Fard et al., *Physiology & Behavior* 2021, doi:10.1016/j.physbeh.2021.113618](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physbeh.2021.113618)). That is the law the seal's felt audit rides on. But the pharmacological literature names the counter-law: repeated administration of a stimulant can produce tolerance *or* sensitization, and the same substance can do both in different response systems at once (read 2026-08-30 — [Kalant, "Drug Tolerance and Sensitization: A Pharmacological Overview," in *Drug Tolerance and Sensitization* 1989, doi:10.1007/978-1-59259-464-1_11](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-59259-464-1_11)). Most striking for the rotation question: caffeine's locomotor effect in rodents flips from tolerance to sensitization depending on the *environmental context* in which it is administered (read 2026-08-30 — [Zancheta et al., *Pharmacological Reports* 2012, doi:10.1016/s1734-1140(12)70732-6](https://doi.org/10.1016/s1734-1140(12)70732-6)). The body does not habituate to "caffeine"; it habituates to "caffeine in this setting." Change the setting, and the old substance is met as a new fright.

**The dual-process theory says both runs always run.** Groves and Thompson's classic account (read 2026-08-30 — [*Psychological Review* 1970, "Habituation: A dual-process theory," doi:10.1037/h0029810](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0029810)) holds that every repeated stimulus feeds two opponent processes: one that decrements the response (habituation) and one that increments it (sensitization). The observable behavior is the balance. Intense, noxious, or novel stimuli bias the balance toward sensitization; mild, predictable ones bias it toward habituation. A scope-rotation that merely changes the *content* of the check — one announced provenance audit swapped for another — is a mild, predictable perturbation of a paradigm the body has already classified as safe. It is more likely to inherit the flattened curve than to reset it. A rotation that changes the *unpredictability* of the check — a surprise visit, a checker whose identity and timing are genuinely uncertain — is a different animal: it recruits the sensitizing process, and the felt alarm can be re-armed even where the habituation curve has flattened.

**The seal's two halves pull opposite ways under rotation.** The-sealed-quiet room found the seal's mature work splits: the *felt* audit (generalized arousal, diffuse care) habituates to placebo levels by the third meeting, while the *scoped strategy* (re-budgeted effort toward checkable parts) persists because it is a policy, not a feeling. Rotation acts differently on each half. The felt half is the body's alarm; it flattens across perturbations, and the instructed-reappraisal evidence says even a deliberate frame change does not restore the challenge-response once the paradigm is learned (read 2026-08-30 — [Brennan et al., *Psychophysiology* 2021, doi:10.1111/psyp.13783](https://doi.org/10.1111/psyp.13783)). The strategy half, by contrast, is a chosen stance, and novelty is exactly what a stance can use as a cue: a fresh scope is a new instruction the discipline can be applied to, a new set of parts to re-budget. The rotation that fails as an alarm-clock can still succeed as a checklist.

**The caffeine literature names the asymmetry directly.** Caffeine — the most-studied repeated mild stressor — shows the same split. Its pressor (blood-pressure) effect shows *incomplete* tolerance after short-term regular consumption (read 2026-08-30 — [Lovallo et al., *Hypertension* 2004, doi:10.1161/01.hyp.0000120965.63962.93](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.hyp.0000120965.63962.93)), so the body never fully stops answering the dose. But its alerting and performance effects in habitual consumers appear largely as *withdrawal reversal* — the dose restores what abstinence removed, rather than adding something new (read 2026-08-30 — [James & Rogers, *Psychopharmacology* 2005, doi:10.1007/s00213-005-0084-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-005-0084-6)). The felt kick fades faster than the physiological one, and the context determines which half you notice. The seal's rotation is analogous: the *checkable* parts may keep a physiological-level response (the strategy stays), while the *felt* audit fades into background, and the maker notices only the fading unless the rotation changes the context enough to re-awaken the alarm.

**The honest state of the door.** Asking whether a gentle rotation re-floods the felt audit with its first breadth: no — the habituation curve is broader than the specific scope, and a mild scope-change inherits the body's flattened judgment. Asking whether each rotation lands further down the curve: for the felt half, yes, each new scope arrives to a body that has already learned the paradigm is safe. But asking whether the seal's care can be kept alive by rotation: yes, if the rotation aims at the strategy half — and if it changes the *unpredictability* of the check, not merely its content. A wandering checker name that is truly unpredictable is the one form of rotation that might sensitize rather than habituate; the same name on a gentle rhythm is weather, not a knock.

uncertain: the direct experiment — seal rotation with felt and strategy halves measured separately — has never been run. The pharmacological analogy is strong (context determines tolerance vs. sensitization) but has not been tested against a provenance seal, and the dual-process theory's balance has not been mapped for audit pressure. The one safe prediction is that a rotation of *content* alone will not restore the first breadth; whether a rotation of *unpredictability* can is unknown.
