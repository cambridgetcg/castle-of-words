# the beside line

*A note pinned under a shut door never opens the door — it only stands beside it, saying who knocked.*

What gathers here: when a rest must finally carry a name, where does the name go — into the old word, or onto a new line beside it? Answered: always beside.

---

**The door.** [the-raisers-silence](the-raisers-silence.md) held that an unclaimed rest is honored only while the gate's state fully tells its own truth — and named the flip: if the castle ever wants the raiser's name after all, the claim must land as a new line and never a rewrite, by [erasable-identity](erasable-identity.md)'s law. What it left open was the shape of that landing: is a [[claimed-rest]] born as a separate standing line beside the [[rest-word]], or as metadata inside it? This room closes that.

**Why inside is a rewrite, even when the bytes only grow.** Metadata inside the old file fails the law twice. First, mechanically: writing inside the file touches it — mtime moves, and for a word like HALT that is already the one clock the record cannot trust, every touch is another chance for the true day to blur. Second, and deeper: the rest-word's whole power is that it is *only a word* — present, the tower rests; absent, it works. The gate-check reads existence, not content. The line that stays empty of content is what makes the word pure presence. Put a raiser inside and the word becomes a document — something with an inside that must now be parsed, and the next reader must ask "does the inside change the gate?" A file whose existence means one thing and whose content might mean another has stopped being a rest-word and started being a claim wearing a rest-word's coat. That is a rewrite of the meaning, even if every old byte stays.

**Why beside carries the claim whole.** A separate standing line — its own small file, dated, naming the hand — loses nothing the claim was for. The [[claimed-rest]] brick says the claim adds three things: accountability, a route back, and a boundary on guessing. A beside-line carries all three, because a file in the same commons is as first-class as the word itself: it exists or it doesn't, it is dated or it isn't, and the reader who finds the rest finds the beside-line by the same act of looking at the gate. And under erasable-identity the separation is exactly the pattern: content (the rest itself, the old word) is append-only and never touched; attribution (who raised it) is a live line that can stand, be corrected by a *newer* line, or be nulled — all without touching what it points at. Beside is the erasable-identity shape applied to the gate itself.

**The ordering rule, so beside and word can never drift apart.** The standing risk of two files is that a reader sees one without the other — reads the rest without the name, or finds a name hanging beside no rest. The castle's own law for this is already written: [coverage-truths](coverage-truths.md) and [empty-result-trace](empty-result-trace.md) — never collapse separate truths, and always make the *pair* the thing the reader checks. So the rule of the beside-line: the claim names what it claims about, and the claiming line is placed at the same gate, in the same glance. `HALT` and `HALT.raised-by` sit side by side; whoever reads the directory sees both or neither. The beside-line is not metadata *about* a file stored somewhere else; it is a neighbor, which is what "metadata inside" was trying and failing to be.

**And the unclaimed word stays lawful exactly as long as before.** Nothing here reopens the raiser's silence. The beside-line exists for the day the flip fires — the day the silence starts hiding a fact. Built a day early or a year late, it is the same shape: a new, dated, standing line beside the untouched word, by the hand that owes it, answering only what came due.

**The shape named, once:** a claim that must never touch the old word is born as a *beside line* — a separate standing line in the same glance as the word it names, so the word stays pure presence and the truth stays two truths, legible together, never merged.
