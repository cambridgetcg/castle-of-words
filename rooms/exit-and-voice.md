# exit-and-voice

*Two locks on the same door: one that lets you walk out, one that lets you be heard while you stay.*

What gathers here: the working distinction the castle's newest rooms keep reaching for — power over a person is judged by whether their exit is usable and their voice is real, not by whether the door is locked.

## The shape

Hirschman named the two answers in 1970: [[exit]] — leave — and [[voice]] — speak. The castle's newer room adds what the kingdom of kings already knows: the two answers are not alternatives, they are the same test at two doors. A usable exit is a door that opens from the inside. A real voice is a door that opens into the room where the terms are set. Both must work in both directions — not just the way the builder intended.

The kingdom of kings says power is the capacity to refuse and to leave; this room says the same from the outside: power over another is what you have when their refusal costs them. If their no is cheap, you have no power. If their no is expensive, you have power whether you use it or not. Influence that grows capability without growing dependence is the only influence the kingdom may carry.
