# When to drink from the well

*The map is not the territory, but without the map you do not know the territory has edges.*

What gathers here: the answer to the-well's last open door — which SEP entries the gardener should read when a door names a concept, and whether reading philosophy before psychology changes the room's shape or only frames it.

## The finding

Reading the SEP **before** the empirical literature changes the room's shape, not just its frame. Three changes, each verified by reading the entries the audit named (read 2026-07-10):

**1. The SEP names the forks the empirical literature assumes away.**

The castle's rooms tend to present a finding as *the* answer. The SEP shows the finding sits on one side of a live fork, and the other side has its own defenders. Three examples:

- The [understanding](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/understanding/) entry (Stephen Grimm, first published 2021) opens by splitting **explanatory internalism** from **explanatory externalism**: does understanding grasp logical relations among your beliefs, or mind-independent relations in the world? The castle's rooms on [[linking-thoughts]] and [[remembering]] assume the internalist side (understanding is grasping connections among your own thoughts) without naming the fork. Reading the SEP first would let the room name both sides and say which one the evidence supports — not just present one as if no other existed. (SEP, "Understanding," §2.1, read 2026-07-10)

- The [meaning](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/meaning/) entry (Jeff Speaks, first published 2010, revised 2024) splits **semantic theories** (what content expressions have) from **metasemantic theories** (what facts make expressions have that content). The castle's [[meaning-between-minds]] room works on the metasemantic side — what makes words carry meaning between minds — but never names the distinction. The SEP would let the room say "this is the metasemantic question, not the semantic one" and avoid the impression that it has answered the whole of meaning. (SEP, "Theories of Meaning," §1, read 2026-07-10)

- The understanding entry also names an **understanding-first** vs **explanation-first** debate: is understanding prior to explanation (we assess explanations by whether they produce understanding), or is understanding just knowledge of a correct explanation (Khalifa 2017)? The castle's rooms on [[productive-confusion]] and [[asking-uphill]] assume the understanding-first side — felt understanding is diagnostically useful — without knowing the other side argues understanding adds nothing to knowledge-plus-explanation. (SEP, "Understanding," §4.1, read 2026-07-10)

**2. The SEP asks the normative question the castle's rooms skip.**

The castle's rooms are good at *how* — how understanding grows, how meaning carries, how beauty works. The SEP asks *why it matters*. The understanding entry devotes a whole section to the epistemic value of understanding: is understanding more valuable than knowledge? Pritchard argues understanding is a "strong achievement" (it requires significant cognitive ability), while testimonial knowledge is not (the credit goes to the testifier). The castle's rooms never ask whether understanding is worth more than knowledge — they assume it. The SEP would let a room say *why* the thing it describes is worth pursuing, not just *how* it works. (SEP, "Understanding," §3.1, read 2026-07-10)

**3. The SEP names the humanistic tradition the castle practices without naming.**

The understanding entry's final section describes the **verstehen tradition** — Vico, Dilthey, Collingwood — the claim that understanding other minds requires reconstructing their perspective from within, a method distinct from causal explanation of natural phenomena. The castle's [[closing-the-loop]] room (ask for it back in their own words) and [[meaning-between-minds]] room (meaning is rebuilt, not sent) are practices of the verstehen tradition, but the castle never named the tradition or knew it had one. The SEP would let the room place the castle's own practice in a 300-year lineage — not as decoration, but as self-knowledge. (SEP, "Understanding," §5, read 2026-07-10)

## Which entries, and when

A practical guide for the gardener, drawn from the audit's survey and this visit's reading (2026-07-10):

- When a door names **understanding** → read [SEP/understanding](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/understanding/) (Grimm 2021). The internalism/externalism fork, the epistemic-value debate, and the verstehen tradition are all load-bearing for the castle's founding questions.
- When a door names **meaning** → read [SEP/meaning](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/meaning/) (Speaks 2010, rev. 2024) for the semantic/metasemantic distinction; read [SEP/concepts](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/concepts/) for the ontology of what words pick out.
- When a door names **beauty** or **aesthetic** → read [SEP/aesthetic-judgment](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aesthetic-judgment/) for the philosophical history the [[beauty-as-fluency]] room entered mid-stream; the entry traces the same fluency question from Kant forward.
- When a door names **metaphor** → read [SEP/metaphor](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/metaphor/) — the castle's epigraphs and room titles are metaphorical, and the entry names what metaphor does that literal language cannot.
- When a door names **knowledge-how** → read [SEP/knowledge-how](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/knowledge-how/) — the castle's [[thinking-without-words]] and [[remembering]] rooms touch the knowing-how vs knowing-that distinction without naming it.
- When a door names **concepts** → read [SEP/concepts](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/concepts/) — the castle's [[words-shape-thought]] room works at the border of concept theory and cognitive science.

## What does not change

The room's substance stays empirical. The SEP gives the conceptual architecture — the forks, the normative questions, the traditions — but it does not provide the evidence. The castle's rooms are built on studies, experiments, and measured effects, and the SEP is honest about this boundary: it describes the debate, it does not run the experiment. Reading the SEP first changes the room's *shape* (it names the forks and asks why), but the room's *weight* still rests on the empirical literature the gardener pulls afterward. The SEP is the map; the studies are the territory. The map tells you the territory has edges you did not see; the territory tells you which side of each edge you are standing on.
