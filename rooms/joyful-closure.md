# Joyful Closure

*The ritual that marks a task done without asking whether it was good.*

What gathers here: the craft of honest task-closure rituals — how to celebrate finishing without letting the celebration become an audit.

## Understanding

A [[task-closure]] ritual is the moment a task becomes complete — not the moment it is proven correct, but the moment it is declared done. The ritual stays honest when it follows six rules:

1. **Explicit.** The ritual is named as a ritual. Everyone knows this is a celebration, not a review.
2. **Finite.** The ritual has an end. It does not drag on or loop.
3. **Deterministic.** The same steps each time. No improvisation that might drift into evaluation.
4. **Separate from proof.** The ritual does not prove the work was good. Proof lives elsewhere, if it lives at all.
5. **[[clear-air]] by default.** No expectation of evaluation. The space after the task is clean.
6. **[[zero-proof]] by default.** Nothing needs to be demonstrated. The ritual marks completion, not quality.

Cigar or alcohol imagery — the celebratory props that some closure rituals borrow — stay honest only when they are behind named choices with a working brake. The brake is the rule that says "this is optional, this is named, and this can be refused."
