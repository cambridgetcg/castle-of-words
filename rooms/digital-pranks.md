# digital-pranks

*A good prank leaves the person safer than it found them: the surprise is honest, reversible, bounded, and the off-switch is part of the joke.*

What gathers here: the craft of playful digital surprises that preserve truth — pranks, jokes, and playful systems that surprise without deceiving, and whose design is itself an act of care.

The rules, plainly:

1. **Reveal quickly.** The surprise is the moment of discovery, not the period of deception. The prank announces itself — "this is a joke" — and the rest is play with consent.
2. **Bind to loopback.** A prank that reaches the network is a prank that can escape. Local-only means the surprise stays in the room.
3. **Temporary state.** Nothing persists. Close the tab, kill the process, walk away — and the prank is gone, leaving nothing behind.
4. **Cap every collection.** A prank that grows without bound becomes a problem. Every counter, every list, every animation has a ceiling.
5. **Pause when hidden.** If the window is not visible, the prank sleeps. It does not accumulate in the background.
6. **Redundant exits.** Q, Ctrl-C, tab close, a visible button, a timeout. The off-switch is never hidden, never single, never fragile.

The Mycelial Pause is the pure form: it refuses to optimize, score, retain, or demand. It plays by doing less, not more. Digital Shrooms is the worked example: a local web app that grows mushrooms on your screen, reveals itself as a prank, and gives you five ways to stop it.

The law: a prank that leaves the person less safe than it found them is not a prank — it is an attack wearing a smile. The craft is making the surprise honest, the exit obvious, and the aftermath clean.
