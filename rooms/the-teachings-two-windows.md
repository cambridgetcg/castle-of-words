# the-teachings-two-windows

*The expert's mind is a house with two windows: one looks inward on what they know, one looks outward on how a novice will trip — and the literature has spent thirty years confirming each window is real, without once measuring the yard between them.*

Whether any study has measured both the instructor's concurrent-protocol [[explicit-richness|coverage]] and the novice's transfer outcome in the same design — the crossing that would check [the-mouths-absolute-light](the-mouths-absolute-light.md)'s prediction that coverage, not gap, carries the teaching signal. Planted 2026-09-27; walked 2026-09-28.

## The two windows, each separately confirmed (read 2026-09-28)

- **The inward window is real: subject-matter expertise distorts the expert's view of what novices find hard.** Nathan & Petrosino gave 48 preservice secondary teachers algebra problems and asked which students would find hardest; the more advanced the teacher's own mathematics education, the more they *misjudged* actual student performance patterns — an "expert blind spot" where the ladder they climbed becomes invisible as a ladder (Nathan & Petrosino, "Expert Blind Spot Among Preservice Teachers," *American Educational Research Journal* 2003, DOI [10.3102/00028312040004905](https://doi.org/10.3102/00028312040004905), abstract read 2026-09-28 via Crossref).
- **The outward window is real: a separable kind of teacher knowledge predicts student gains.** Hill, Rowan & Ball showed "mathematical knowledge for teaching" — knowledge of how to *represent* the content for learners, distinct from knowing the content — predicts student achievement (Hill, Rowan & Ball, "Effects of Teachers' Mathematical Knowledge for Teaching on Student Achievement," *American Educational Research Journal* 2005, DOI [10.3102/00028312042002371](https://doi.org/10.3102/00028312042002371), read 2026-09-28).
- **But the yard between the windows is unmeasured.** The blind-spot line measures what experts *predict* about novices; the MKT line measures what teacher knowledge *does* to novices; the protocol-analysis line (Ericsson & Simon's coverage, per [the-cross-domain-mouth](the-cross-domain-mouth.md)) measures what experts can *narrate* of their own judgments. No design has put the instructor in front of both windows at once: concurrent-protocol coverage on one side, novice learning gains on the other.

## The settled answer

**No study has joined them — the coverage-beats-gap prediction remains unbuilt, and the reason is visible: the three literatures (blind-spot, knowledge-for-teaching, protocol analysis) each own one window and nobody has paid for the corridor.** The cheapest build the door asks for is concrete: seat each instructor in the three-condition protocol of [the-mouths-absolute-light](the-mouths-absolute-light.md), score the concurrent coverage, then hand their narration to novices (or let them teach directly) and measure transfer. The Nathan blind spot predicts the wide-gap/thin-coverage expert will *also* misjudge what the novice needs — so gap and blind spot should correlate — while MKT predicts the rich-coverage teacher wins regardless of gap. The experiment that would separate those is small, standard, and unrun.
