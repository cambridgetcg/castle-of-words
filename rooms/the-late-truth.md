# The Late Truth

*The witness who finally says what was true all along arrives at a house where the door had already been answered differently — and the lateness is not the lie, but it is not nothing either.*

What gathers here: where a *truthful-but-delayed* disclosure sits in the trust-repair map — whether a late provenance label is a third cell the integrity/competence 2×2 does not contain, and whether the omission-vs-commission distinction in deception research maps onto how the label is received. The honest answer: the late truth is not its own cell in the trust-repair literature — the repair map still reads it as integrity-shaped, because the withholding, not the falsity, is the violation the hearer notices. But the deception literature gives the late truth its one mercy: withholding is judged less harshly than fabricating, and paltering research names the exact terrain the late label walks — truth told in a way that was meant, at the time, to leave a false impression. The late confession is therefore treated the way the trust-repair map treats any integrity violation, but the moral-wrongness literature says the *withholding* half of it is the less-condemned half of deception.

---

**The door.** The [the-knock-that-confesses](the-knock-that-confesses.md) room leaned on Kim et al.'s competence/integrity split — apology repairs competence violations, apologizing for an integrity violation can deepen the wound. It noted in passing that the late provenance label does not fit either cell cleanly: the work did not fail (not competence), and the source *told the truth*, just later than the assumption ran (not straightforward integrity). This door asks whether trust-repair work on *truthful-but-delayed* disclosure exists as its own cell, and whether the omission/commission distinction in deception research predicts how the late label lands.

**The deception literature's map has a place for the late truth — as a lie of omission, not as a third thing.** The canonical categories are omission (withholding relevant truth), commission (asserting falsehood), and paltering (using true statements to create a false impression); Levine's and Fallis's reviews both note that omission counts as deception only when the withholding is *done to mislead* — "deceptive omission" — while mere non-mention is not (read 2026-08-30 — [Krishnamurthy, "To Say the Least: Where Deceptively Withholding Information Ends and Lying Begins," *Topics in Cognitive Science* 2018, doi:10.1111/tops.12379](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/tops.12379); [Levine, *Duped: Truth-Default Theory and the Social Science of Lying and Deception*, University of Alabama Press 2019](https://www.jstor.org/stable/jj.30297464)). The late provenance label's pre-disclosure stretch — the years the work was heard as human because the AI hand was never named — fits the deceptive-omission definition exactly in the cases where the silence was chosen with the listener's inference in mind. The cell the late truth lands in is therefore not missing from the map; it was already drawn, under omission.

**The omission/commission judgment is the late truth's good news — with a condition.** Rogers, Zeckhauser, Gino, Norton & Schweitzer's paltering work and the omission-bias literature converge on the same load-bearing finding: hearers judge lies of *omission* as morally worse than no deception but *less* morally wrong than lies of commission — across adults and children, across concealment scenarios (read 2026-08-30 — [Powell, Derbyshire & Guttentag, "Omission bias in children's and adults' moral judgments of lies," *Journal of Experimental Child Psychology* 2021, doi:10.1016/j.jecp.2021.105320](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022096521002381); [Rogers, Zeckhauser, Gino, Norton & Schweitzer, "Artful Paltering: The Risks and Rewards of Using Truthful Statements to Mislead Others," *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 2017, doi:10.1037/pspi0000081](https://toddrogers.scholars.harvard.edu/resource/rogersetalartfulpalteringpdf); and the classic [Spranca, Minsk & Baron, "Omission and commission in judgment and choice," *Journal of Experimental Social Psychology* 1991, doi:10.1016/0022-1031(91)90011-T](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/002210319190011T)). The reason given is folk-intuition, not measurement of trust-repair: commission is seen as *doing*, omission as *allowing*. The late provenance label, as withholding, inherits the less-condemned side. But the paltering work names the cost: the leniency is for the *act* of omission, not its *discovery* — once palterers are caught, their judged credibility does not recover to the honest baseline, and Rogers et al. found targets who discovered paltering judged the palterer as harshly as a commission-liar. The omission's relative softness is a property of the judgment *in principle*, not of its reception *in retrospect*.

**The trust-repair literature reads the discovered late truth through the same lens the knock used — integrity violations resist apology — and has never tested a *delayed-honesty* cell of its own.** Kim et al.'s 2×2 and the subsequent trust-repair literature have always defined the violation as *what the source did wrong* (competence vs integrity), not *when the truth arrived*; no cell exists for "the truth was always available and was eventually spoken, but later than the hearer's assumption wanted" (read 2026-08-30 — [Kim et al. 2004, cited in [the-knock-that-confesses](the-knock-that-confesses.md)]; [Dirks, Kim, Ferrin & Cooper, "Understanding the effects of substantive responses on trust following a transgression," *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes* 2011, doi:10.1016/j.obhdp.2011.01.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.obhdp.2011.01.003)). The honest finding is the negative one: the late truth is not a third kind of violation; it is processed as an integrity violation with an omission bias discount, and the discount does not survive discovery. The trust-repair map is complete for what the seal wing needs; the map's answer is that the late label lands in the integrity cell, where apology is weakest, and the omission/commission mercy applies only to how observers would have judged the withholding *before* it was found out.

**Where the seal wing can stand honestly.** The late provenance label is a lie of omission that finally confessed — which means (a) it is *not* a neutral third thing the trust-repair map lacks; it is an integrity-shaped violation; (b) its *act* half inherits the omission bias's relative leniency ("they never claimed otherwise" sounds softer than "they said it was human"); (c) its *discovery* half cancels most of that leniency, because the discovered palterer is judged like the discovered commission-liar; (d) the 2×2's missing cell is a real gap in the research record — no study has crossed *timing-of-truth-arrival* with *violation-type* — and it is a gap the castle could name as a buildable design, but no published study has run it. For the seal's purposes the map's answer is: the late truth is read as integrity-shaped, its confession is held to the integrity cell's hard terms, and the omission's soft shoulder is spent before the knock arrives.

**uncertain:** the "discovery cancels the omission discount" claim is an inference from paltering and omission-bias judgments of the *act*, not from a study that crossed deception-type with discovery; the delayed-disclosure cell has never been isolated in any trust-repair design, so its reception is unknown; and the deception literature's categories were drawn for negotiations and transgressions, not aesthetic provenance. What is solid: omission is judged less harshly than commission when compared as acts, and discovered paltering is judged like lying.
