# warranted-label-effect

*A name that carries its own reason is a question, not a command.*

What gathers here: whether showing a categorical state with its attributed basis and reason measurably changes how readers treat it — reducing overtrust, inviting correction, slowing acceptance — or whether the [[warranted-label]] is assumed to work by analogy from trust and fluency research without direct demonstration.

## The question

[the-display-is-not-the-thing](the-display-is-not-the-thing.md) claims bare categorical labels are read as verdicts while warranted labels invite checking. Has anyone measured the difference?

## What the search found

**The direct comparison is unbuilt.** The automation-bias literature — Parasuraman and Riley's 1997 foundational paper (cited in Wikipedia's *Automation bias*, read 2026-08-22) and Lyell & Coiera's 2016 systematic review (*JAMIA*, https://academic.oup.com/jamia/article/24/2/423/2631492, read 2026-08-22) — establishes that overreliance on bare automated output is real: commission errors come from "excessive trust in the reliability of automated aids" and omission errors come from failure to monitor. The XAI literature (Wikipedia, *Explainable artificial intelligence*, read 2026-08-22) argues transparency and explanations help users "trust that the AI is making good decisions" and notes the goal is calibrated, appropriate trust — not more trust. But neither literature has run the experiment the question asks: the same categorical state shown with vs. without its warrant, measuring overtrust, correction-seeking, or acceptance speed.

The closest anchors from neighboring fields:
- **Explanation presence vs. quality:** XAI studies show *adding* an explanation can increase trust even when the explanation is poor — suggesting the bare-vs-warranted axis is real but the warranted side is not automatically honest. The warrant can itself be a verdict in disguise if its basis is uncheckable.
- **Verification complexity:** Lyell & Coiera found automation bias drops when verification is simple — the warrant works only if the reader can act on it cheaply.

## The honest answer

uncertain: The verdict-in-disguise effect is assumed from the trust and fluency literatures, not demonstrated at the presentation layer. The two established halves — bare labels invite overtrust (automation bias), and checkable bases reduce it (verification complexity) — have never been joined in one experiment. The study is buildable: same system, same output, two display conditions, measure reliance and correction. Until it is run, the claim is a craft corollary of [the-law-of-honest-assertion](the-law-of-honest-assertion.md), not an empirical finding.
