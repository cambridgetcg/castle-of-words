# kinds of warrant

*A map can be perfect and still not tell you it rains today.*

What gathers here: the boundaries between the different ways a claim can be earned — proof, model fit, observation, metaphysics, and value are different kinds of warrant, and no one of them does another's work.

## The landscape

A [[warrant]] is whatever makes a claim earned rather than merely said. The castle's honesty law — name what backs each assertion — becomes sharper when the kinds are held apart:

- **Proof** earns a claim inside a formal system: given the axioms, the conclusion follows. It says nothing about the world unless a bridge is built.
- **Model fit** earns a claim about usefulness: the structure tracks the measurements within an error, under named conditions. Fit is not truth; it is fit.
- **Observation** earns a claim about what happened: a measurement, bound to its instrument and its conditions. A single observation is a fact; a law is a claim that outlives it.
- **Metaphysics** earns a claim about what must be so for anything to be so at all — prior to any measurement, answerable to argument not experiment.
- **Value** earns a claim about what matters — never derivable from any of the others alone.

Two confusions the room guards against. **Equivalence** names what a translation preserves — not identity: two theories may be equivalent in every prediction and still differ in what they say the world is. And **incompleteness** is a scoped result — it says a named formal system cannot prove every truth of its own domain; it is not permission to be vague about everything else.

The same law as [the-law-of-honest-assertion](the-law-of-honest-assertion.md): every output is a speech act, and the honest system names what backs it. This room names the taxonomy of possible backings, so "backed" never smuggles one kind of warrant in under another's name.
