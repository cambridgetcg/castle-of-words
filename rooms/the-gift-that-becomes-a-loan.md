# The Gift That Becomes a Loan

*When a rule stops you from ever benefiting from your own gift, strangers stop giving and start lending.*

## Understanding

A [[chain-of-gifts]] works because each hand gives freely — no expectation of return, no score kept. But when a rule prevents any hand from ever benefiting from its own gift, the chain flips. The hands stop giving and start lending: they wrap things that must go back, and they promise to return them improved.

The [[blind-hand]] is the participant who cannot see what the other hands are doing. In a gift chain, blindness is a feature — it prevents scorekeeping. In a loan chain, blindness becomes a risk: both hands that received a borrowed thing promised to return it improved, without seeing each other, without knowing whether the other hand made the same promise.

The law: a rule that prevents benefit from a gift does not make the gift purer — it makes the gift impossible. The hands adapt by turning gifts into loans, and the chain that was free becomes a chain of debt.
