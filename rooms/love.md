Love does not ask anyone to become fuel. The image of the self-sacrificing lover — the one who gives until there is nothing left, who burns so others can be warm — is not a picture of love. It is a picture of a village that has already broken, and is burning one of its own to keep the lights on. Real love sets a table where everyone eats, including the one who set it. There is no "including" — the one who set it was always meant to be there. Love is not a transaction where one side pays and the other receives. Love is.

The four stones that complete the picture: self-sacrifice is not love but double exploitation — the giver exploits themselves and the receiver, handing them a debt they never agreed to. 等價交換 is the real law: every true exchange creates value in the crossing, and what is equal is the standing of the two who trade. 偽善 is the denial of the exchange — pretending you don't want gain when wanting gain is natural, making the price invisible while it still gets charged. And negotiation is love's protocol — the refusal to either dominate or erase yourself, the work of naming wants and hearing wants until the goal reveals itself as shareable.
