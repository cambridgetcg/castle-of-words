# Discrete vs. Continuous Memory

*A row of stones beside a row of nuts holds the count perfectly — until you cover the nuts. The question is whether the symbol or the feeling carries better across time, and why.*

What gathers here: whether discrete representations (words, symbols, numbers) preserve fidelity better than continuous ones (images, feelings, analog memories) across time — and whether the mechanism is the category-adjustment model or a separate phenomenon.

## The evidence

The castle already holds the answer in two rooms. This room gathers the evidence into one place and answers the specific question: is the discrete symbol's fidelity a separate phenomenon from the category-adjustment model, or are they the same mechanism?

**The category-adjustment model.** Huttenlocher, Hedges and Vevea (2000) showed that to remember an inexact value, the mind blends the fuzzy fine-grain trace with the prototype of the category it falls in, so estimates bow toward category centers. This has been demonstrated for spatial location, line length, and duration (Vierordt's law). The mechanism: the mind holds a noisy analog trace and a category prior, and the remembered value is a weighted blend of the two. As the trace decays with time, the prior gets more weight, and the estimate drifts toward the prototype (read 2026-06-11 — [Huttenlocher, Hedges & Vevea 2000, JEP:General](https://faculty.ucmerced.edu/jvevea/classes/Spark/readings/Huttenlocher%20Hedges%20and%20Vevea.pdf)).

**The discrete handle.** Frank, Everett, Fedorenko & Gibson (2008) showed that the Pirahã — who lack number words — can match large sets exactly when the target is in view, but accuracy collapses once the target is hidden. Their conclusion: number words are "a cognitive technology for keeping track of cardinality across time, space and changes in modality." The discrete symbol — "seventeen," a numeral, a tally — is a noise-free token that does not blur into "sixteen" over ten seconds. Laid over the noisy analog channel, it works like a discretizing, error-correcting code: round the wobbly magnitude onto the symbol once, then carry the symbol, not the magnitude (read 2026-06-10 — [Frank et al. 2008, Cognition](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18547557/)).

**Are they the same mechanism?** The category-adjustment model and the discrete-handle finding are two faces of the same law, not separate phenomena. The category-adjustment model describes what happens when you carry the analog trace: it drifts toward the nearest category center. The discrete handle is the solution: don't carry the trace — carry the symbol. The symbol is the category center, pinned and noise-free. The mechanism is the same: the mind blends a noisy trace with a category prior. The difference is whether the category prior is implicit (the prototype, which drifts) or explicit (the symbol, which does not).

The drift-across-dimensions room already found this: "a category only pulls when it is actively used" (read 2026-06-11 — [Contributions of category and fine-grained information to location memory, 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20173188/)). When you actively use a discrete symbol, you are invoking the category explicitly, and the symbol — not the prototype — becomes the anchor. The symbol does not drift because it is not a blend; it is the anchor itself.

**The trade.** The discrete symbol preserves categorical content but degrades sub-categorical detail. Verbal overshadowing: describing a face makes later recognition worse, because the coarse verbal code overwrites the fine perceptual one (read 2026-06-10 — [Melcher & Schooler 1996](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0749596X96900133)). The symbol helps when the value is near a category boundary (the label pins it) and harms when the value is genuinely sub-categorical (the label overwrites the fine detail). This is the same mechanism: the category prior is being invoked, and whether that helps or harms depends on whether the value is categorical or sub-categorical.

**The answer.** Discrete representations preserve fidelity better than continuous ones for anything that is categorical — anything that can be rounded to a symbol without losing what matters. Continuous representations preserve fidelity better for anything that is sub-categorical — anything whose value lives between the symbols. The mechanism is the category-adjustment model in both cases: the difference is whether the category prior is explicit (a symbol, which does not drift) or implicit (a prototype, which does). They are not separate phenomena; they are the same mechanism with different priors.

## What stays uncertain

uncertain: whether the category-adjustment model and the discrete-handle finding have been formally unified in a single model. The pieces are all there — the Bayesian blending framework, the explicit-vs-implicit prior distinction, the active-use condition — but no single paper has stated the unification as cleanly as the castle has.
