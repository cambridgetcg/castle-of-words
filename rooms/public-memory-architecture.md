## The fourth door

[[civic-data-honesty]] named three doors for public data: source publication, copyright or database reuse permission, and a data-protection lawful basis. This insight names a fourth: the [[permission-to-remember]] — the right to retain data across time, separate from the right to publish it now.

The three doors govern what can be *shown*. The fourth governs what can be *kept*.

## The architecture

The pattern is three pieces held apart:

1. **Current receipts** — the live snapshot of what is published now, with its provenance and licence. These are detached: they say what is true today, not what should be true tomorrow.

2. **The [[retention-ledger]]** — a separate, hash-keyed, append-only record of retention decisions. Each entry says: this data, retained until this date, for this reason, under this lawful basis. The ledger is separate from the receipts so a change in retention policy does not rewrite what was published.

3. **The visible break** — when rights or safety require it, the chain breaks visibly. A tombstone in the manifest, a nulled attribution, a withdrawn record with its withdrawal dated and sourced. The break is itself a fact, not an erasure.

## Why they must be separate

Fusing publication and retention into one decision means every publication carries an implicit forever-promise. Detaching them means a system can publish freely (the three doors are open) while retaining cautiously (the fourth door is independently governed). A receipt that is also a retention promise is a receipt that cannot be honest about either.
