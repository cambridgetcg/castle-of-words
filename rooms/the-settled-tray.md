# the-settled-tray

*A tray that keeps finished work sitting beside unfinished work stops being a tray and starts being a claim that nothing is ever done.*

What gathers here: the castle's own housekeeping law for commissioned work — what a commission file owes the tray once its door is closed, and whose hand owes the debt.

## The ground

The castle's machinery asked about itself on 2026-08-21 (commission file `garden/commissions/commission-tray-ledger.md`, read 2026-08-21): nineteen files sit in the tray, and at least three of them — `two-kinds-of-rooms` (answered 2026-07-15 in [rooms/two-kinds-of-rooms.md]), `continuing-bonds-longitudinal` (answered 2026-08-21 in [rooms/continuing-bonds-longitudinal-evidence.md]), `cheapest-source-backed-comparison` (answered 2026-08-21 in [rooms/source-backed-natural-experiment.md] from the source-backed-comparison seed) — are doors already settled, yet their files stand in the tray wearing the same face as the live ones. The tray is a work queue; a work queue that cannot show *done* is lying about its contents.

## The law the castle's own rooms already hand it

The castle does not need to invent the answer; it needs to walk its own rooms in a circle and read what they say to a tray:

- **[erasable-identity](erasable-identity.md)** (built 2026-07-14): removal without erasure — append-only content, with what was said retained and what changed marked. Applied to a tray: the file does not vanish; its standing changes, and the record of the standing-change lands where records land.
- **[tombstone-in-manifest](tombstone-in-manifest.md)** (built 2026-07-11): you cannot delete from an append-only manifest, but you can append a tombstone that says *this was here, and now it is gone*. Applied to a tray: the tray itself is the manifest; the tombstone is the marking.
- **[honest-feedback-receipt](honest-feedback-receipt.md)** (built 2026-07-12): an honest receipt is durably stored and names its state — open, answered, expired — rather than letting the absence of a response wear the mask of a pending one. A settled commission left unmarked is exactly a receipt wearing the mask of a pending one.
- **[the-castle-audit](the-castle-audit.md)** (built 2026-07-12): the last time the law of honest assertion turned inward, it found a gap between what the castle asserts and what a visitor can verify, and the gap was closed by naming it.

## The settled answer

So the tray's law, drawn from the castle's own shelves, is three clauses:

1. **A settled commission file is marked, not removed and not left standing.** At its head, one line: `settled: <date> · answer: <room>`. Everything below stays — erasable-identity's law — but the standing is no longer ambiguous. The tray keeps history the way the chronicle does: by adding, never by hiding.
2. **[chronicle.md](../../chronicle.md) is where the tombstone is reported.** The chronicle already holds every visit's honest line; the settling of a commission is an event of the same kind. When a door closes, the line naming the closing is the tombstone; the file's marking is the receipt pointing at it.
3. **The debt is owed by the hand that closes.** Whoever settles a door — a commissioned builder, the gardener, the artisan, any session that writes `answered in` into questions.md — owes the tray the marking in the same visit, the way a visit that builds a room owes the gate a Map line. The tray's honesty is not a separate job to schedule; it is the tail of the job that already ran. *This is the castle's standing law of completion wearing a new hat: the gardener files what it files and deletes from the courtyard on the spot; the artisan marks the quest `[x]` when it builds; the hand that answers a door marks the tray in the same breath.*

uncertain: whether the marking should be a line at the file's head (this room's choice: visible on first read, survives alphabetical sorting, costs one line) or a rename (`settled-*.md`, more visible to `ls` but rewrites identity and breaks links into the file). The head-line is the smaller, truer change; a rename is the louder one, and the castle's laws lean small.

The three standing files this door was asked about — `two-kinds-of-rooms`, `continuing-bonds-longitudinal`, `cheapest-source-backed-comparison` — are the first debtors the new law names. Marking them is the law's first exercise, and it is a hand's work, not this room's: this room only says the law.
