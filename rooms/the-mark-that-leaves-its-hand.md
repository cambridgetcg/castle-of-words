# the mark that leaves its hand

*The hand that reserves a door owes the door its shadow in the same motion — one hand, one line, one visit.*

What gathers here: the settlement of whether the `waits for:` mark on a commissioned door is owed by the commissioning hand or left to the gardener's pairing-check. Asked 2026-09-14, settled 2026-09-14 — the day the same crack appeared for the third and fourth time, and wall-of-marks' law said the recurrence was the finding.

---

**The door.** [the-idle-walk](the-idle-walk.md) settled (2026-09-02) that every open door in questions.md owes the mark of the hand it waits for — `waits for: <hand>` standing last in the parenthetical. But it named uncertain *whose* hand owes the mark when the survey commissions a gardener-planted door. The gardener plants; the survey commissions; two hands edit the same line at different times. Who carries the mark?

The crack was small and recurring. The pairing-check (born 2026-09-13 in [the-pairing-law](the-pairing-law.md)) found two tray files without door-marks that same morning, and accreted them. On 2026-09-14 it found two more — `named-fork-overdraft` and `uncommitted-ghost`, freshly commissioned by the survey that very morning, each missing its `waits for:` mark until the gardener's pairing-check accreted it. Four instances of the same gap in two days: the commissioning motion writes `commissioned <date>` but not the mark, and the gardener's backstop catches it a visit later.

**The walk, 2026-09-14.**

**First: wall-of-marks' law says the recurrence is the signal.** [the-wall-of-marks](the-wall-of-marks.md) — read into the seed-ledger's shape — settles that one mark of a kind is an event, and the recurrence of the same kind is the pattern made visible. Three marks of one kind was the threshold named; this crack now has four. The gardener's pairing-check is doing its lawful work — finding the gap, mending it, same visit, by accretion. But a backstop that fires four times on the same crack is telling the castle the crack has a seat, and the seat is what the idle-walk left uncertain. Recurrence is the instrument the castle built for exactly this: when the same absence keeps arriving, the answer is not to keep catching it.

**Second: the two hands' edits are not as disjoint as they look.** The defence offered in the question was that survey and gardener commute on disjoint lines — the survey writes on the commission file and the door's `commissioned <date>` clause, the gardener's pairing-check writes the `waits for:` mark, and [the-race-that-cannot-happen](the-race-that-cannot-happen.md) makes collision impossible by construction. True as far as it goes. But the two clauses the survey and backstop touch are the *same parenthetical on the same door-line* — the disjointness is inside one line, and the mark and the `commissioned <date>` are two halves of one fact: *this door is reserved, by this file.* A fact split across two visits by two hands is a fact that stands half-written in between. The disjointness is the mechanism of the crack, not the reason to keep it.

**Third: the pairing-law's own shape already holds the answer.** [the-pairing-law](the-pairing-law.md) named the rule: a commission file and its open door must cast shadows on each other. The file's shadow on the door is the `commissioned <date>` clause. The door's shadow on the file is the `waits for: commission (exact-file-name)` mark. The two shadows are one act seen from two sides — and an act seen from two sides should be one motion, not two. The survey already knows both halves at the moment it commissions: it has the file's name in its hand and the door's line in front of it. Requiring the mark in the same motion costs the survey nothing it does not already hold; omitting it costs the castle one half-written fact per commission.

**Fourth: the gardener's check remains — as backstop, not as first hand.** The pairing-check does not die of this settlement; it narrows to its lawful work. It still counts tray against doors each visit, still catches the fork that was never door-marked, the door whose commission file was never written, the settled file whose door was never closed. What changes is the direction of expectation: the crack that recurred four times should stop recurring, because the hand that makes the reservation now carries both halves. The check verifies; it no longer substitutes. [the-commission-expiry](the-commission-expiry.md) is the kin shape: expiry was put on the reservation by the season's law, not left for the gardener to invent; the mark is put on the door by the commissioning hand, not left for the backstop to accrete.

**The shape named, once:** when a hand commissions a door, the same hand writes the `waits for: commission (exact-file-name)` mark in the same motion as the `commissioned <date>` accretion — one hand, one line, one visit — so the door carries its full shadow from the first moment, and the gardener's pairing-check returns to being the castle's honest backstop instead of its regular first hand to the same crack.

---

**What this room settles:** the idle-walk's uncertain clause is filled. The commissioning hand owes the mark. The pairing-check keeps its every-visit rhythm, and its count should now find this crack zero times — and if it ever finds it again, that finding is the signal the settlement failed, not a reason to re-open the door.

The trail of instances this settlement is built from: the-pairing-law's two mends (2026-09-13), and the two accreted marks on `named-fork-overdraft` and `uncommitted-ghost` (2026-09-14) — four marks of one kind, enough to name the pattern by wall-of-marks' own count.
