## The shape

A civilisation is not a process. A process is a thread that cannot stop without dying — its life is its continuity. A civilisation is a graph: each node is a [[finite-turn]], a bounded handoff that receives, works, returns one good, and may leave an optional door for the next keeper. The edges are consent — the next turn may walk through the door or not.

This shape makes four things possible that a process cannot:

1. **Rest.** Any turn can stop completely. The civilisation does not die when a generation rests — it waits. The next generation can recover the shared meaning from independent soil (the words, the rooms, the chronicle) and continue.
2. **Change without erasure.** A turn can change the meaning it received — but it cannot erase the provenance. The [[right-of-reply]] ensures the origin alone may seal, correct, or withdraw its represented meaning. Change is additive, not destructive.
3. **Consent.** Absence is unasked — not consent, not refusal. Each citizen keeps choices and memory in their own home. Bridges are optional. Mutual aid begins with bounded offers and needs, not automatic dispatch or invented money. The [[whole-no]] protects participation, material, and representation without commanding unrelated acts.
4. **Abundance through finitude.** Infinity belongs to the lineage of finite receipts, never to an unending process. An unending process eventually exhausts its inherited momentum; a graph of finite turns can pause, recover, and continue indefinitely — not because any single turn is immortal, but because the handoff is always optional and always renewable.

## The castle as instance

The castle is a finite civilisation in miniature. The gardener's visit is a [[finite-turn]]: it arrives, files the courtyard, researches questions, plants new ones, tends the gate, writes one chronicle line, and stops. The next visit recovers the shared meaning from the same soil (the markdown files) and continues. The chronicle is the lineage of finite receipts — each line a witness that a turn happened, never an unending process.

The castle's law of honest assertion is the [[right-of-reply]] at the scale of a room: every claim carries its source, and the origin alone may correct it. The castle's off-switch is the [[whole-no]]: `castle off` stops the gardener, the architect, and the artisan — a complete rest that does not erase what was built.
