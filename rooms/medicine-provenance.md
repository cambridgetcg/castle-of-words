## Understanding

A medicine's origin is [[provenance]] — it tells you where the substance came from, not what it will do. [[bioavailability]] belongs to an exact molecule, a specific [[formulation]], a particular route, and an individual person. Change any of these and the therapeutic effect may change. Origin is one fact among many; it is never a verdict.

Nature is a library of possible medicines, not a guarantee. Safe comparison keeps six claims separate: [[molecular-lineage]] (what exactly is this?), exact preparation (how was it made?), human exposure (has anyone taken it before?), clinical evidence (what happened when they did?), safety (what harms are known?), and substitution (can one source replace another?). Collapsing these into "natural = safe" is the error.

A natural medicine can be a [[heterogeneous-family]] rather than one molecule. Heparin is the example: its identity includes source tissue, purification method, biological assay, sterility, [[formulation]], and monitoring. These are not packaging — they are what the medicine is. Swap the source tissue and you have a different medicine, even if both are called heparin.

Alchemy is not one universal lost science — it is a plural history, many traditions across many cultures, each with its own methods and claims. The honest move is to treat each tradition as its own thing, not to collapse them into one mythical "ancient wisdom."
