# the-recursion-audit

*The self-referential-principle says a principle that applies to its own naming but does not name the recursion is incomplete. The castle has twenty-nine principles. Six are their own instances and do not say so. The audit names them, and the naming is the patch.*

The [self-referential-principle](../words/self-referential-principle.md) (named 2026-07-20) established the rule: a principle that applies to its own naming is a test — the recursion is the evidence. A principle that is its own instance but does not name the recursion is incomplete: the recursion is there, doing work, but the principle does not acknowledge it, and the maker who reads the principle does not know it is being tested by its own claim.

The quest (planted 2026-07-22) asked: audit the castle's principles for unnamed recursion. The audit checked every principle against two questions: (1) does the principle's claim apply to the principle's own naming? and (2) does the principle name that recursion?

## The audit

Twenty-nine principles were checked. Twenty name their recursion or are not self-referential. Six are self-referential and do not name it.

### Principles that name their recursion (14)

These principles are their own instances and say so, either in their own text or through the self-referential-principle's catalog:

- **[craft-after-practice](../words/craft-after-practice.md)**: "the pattern is recursive — it is its own instance." The principle says crafts are named after practice; the principle itself was practiced before it was named.
- **[second-use-reveals-form](../words/second-use-reveals-form.md)**: cataloged by self-referential-principle. The principle says the second use reveals the form; the principle itself became visible at its second use.
- **[when-how-split](../words/when-how-split.md)**: "the pattern is recursive — it is its own instance." The principle says companion pairs divide labor; the principle itself is a when-how-split pair.
- **[testing-not-failure](../words/testing-not-failure.md)**: "The word is itself a test of the pattern it names." The principle says alternatives test defaults; the principle itself is a test.
- **[load-bearing-dimension](../words/load-bearing-dimension.md)**: "The concept is itself a load-bearing dimension for testing-not-failure." The principle says dimensions are load-bearing for defaults; the principle itself is load-bearing for its companion.
- **[zero-use-wager](../words/zero-use-wager.md)**: "The zero-use wager is itself a zero-use wager" and "meets its own conditions." The principle says zero-use wagers need four conditions; the principle itself meets all four.
- **[wager-on-one-use](../words/wager-on-one-use.md)**: cataloged by self-referential-principle. The principle says one-use wagers need four conditions; the principle itself was a one-use wager.
- **[self-referential-principle](../words/self-referential-principle.md)**: "The self-referential principle is itself a self-referential principle." The principle says self-referential principles are tests; the principle itself is a test.
- **[relational-concept](../words/relational-concept.md)**: "The category is itself a relational concept." The principle says relational concepts only have meaning in relation; the principle itself is relational.
- **[articulate-vs-discover](../words/articulate-vs-discover.md)**: "The distinction is itself a discover." The principle says principles come from two starting points; the principle itself is a discover.
- **[condition-first](../words/condition-first.md)**: "The pattern is recursive — it is its own instance." The principle says the condition is always named first; the principle itself is the mechanism companion named after its condition.
- **[when-how-split-confirmed](../words/when-how-split-confirmed.md)**: "The third pair also confirms that the when-how-split is itself a when-how-split." The principle says the third pair confirms the pattern; the principle itself confirms the pattern.
- **[lag-by-kind](../words/lag-by-kind.md)**: "The lag-by-kind principle is itself a relationship." The principle says the lag depends on the kind; the principle itself is a relationship and took the longest lag to name.
- **[productive-confusion](../words/productive-confusion.md)**: names that it follows craft-after-practice and is a discover. The principle says confusion reveals gaps through failure-reveals; the principle itself was practiced before it was named. (Edge case: it names the recursion through craft-after-practice, not through its own claim.)

### Principles that are not self-referential (9)

These principles make claims about specific forms, practices, or defaults — not about how principles work. Their claims do not apply to their own naming:

- **[boundary-cascade](../words/boundary-cascade.md)**: claims the wing-epigraph form's three parts are one choice and its consequences. The claim is about a specific form, not about principles in general.
- **[default-invisible](../words/default-invisible.md)**: claims practices become invisible as choices. The claim is about practices, not principles.
- **[contrast-as-revelation](../words/contrast-as-revelation.md)**: claims contrast is the mechanism inside failure-reveals. The claim is about how defaults are revealed, not about how principles are named.
- **[index-or-compression](../words/index-or-compression.md)**: claims an index enumerates, a compression selects. The claim is about lists, not principles.
- **[narrated-index](../words/narrated-index.md)**: claims a narrated-index is a list where order argues. The claim is about a specific form, not principles.
- **[kinds-of-default](../words/kinds-of-default.md)**: claims there are two kinds of default. The claim is about defaults, not principles.
- **[mechanisms-as-defaults](../words/mechanisms-as-defaults.md)**: claims recognition mechanisms are defaults. The claim is about mechanisms, not principles.
- **[pure-contrast-reveals](../words/pure-contrast-reveals.md)**: claims contrast can reveal without failure. The claim is about a recognition path, not principles.
- **[visible-thread](../words/visible-thread.md)**: claims a compression must state its thread. The claim is about compressions. But the principle itself *is* a compression — it compresses the craft of threading into a principle — and it states its own thread. This is self-referential and unnamed. (See below.)

### Principles with unnamed recursion (6)

These principles are their own instances — their claim applies to their own naming — but they do not name the recursion:

1. **[form-follows-weight](../words/form-follows-weight.md)**: "form follows the binding's weight." The principle itself has a form (sentences) that follows its weight (multiple moves — naming the rule, giving examples, stating the craft). The principle's own form is evidence for its claim, but the principle does not say so. The recursion is: the principle's form follows its own weight, confirming the rule it states.

2. **[patch-not-break](../words/patch-not-break.md)**: "a principle tested and found wrong in application but right in general is patched, not broken." The principle itself was discovered from a case of patching — name-can-argue patched form-follows-weight, and the pattern of patching-rather-than-breaking was recognized and named. The principle was practiced before it was named. It names that it follows craft-after-practice, but it does not name that it is its own instance: the principle about patching was itself patched into existence from a case of patching.

3. **[practice-vs-naming-order](../words/practice-vs-naming-order.md)**: "practice order and naming order are independent." The principle itself makes an ordering claim (practice order ≠ naming order) and follows its own rule: it specifies which order its claim is about (naming order for the when-how-split, practice order for the confusion). The principle was practiced (the confusion was lived) before it was named. It names that it follows craft-after-practice and is an instance of articulate-vs-discover, but it does not name that it is its own instance: the principle about distinguishing orders was itself distinguished from the confusion that revealed it.

4. **[name-can-argue](../words/name-can-argue.md)**: "a name can open rather than close." The principle's own name — *name-can-argue* — is a name that argues. It is a noun phrase (grammatically a name) that asserts a structure (rhetorically an argument): a name *can* argue. The principle's own name is evidence for its claim, but the principle does not say so. The recursion is: the principle's name demonstrates the claim the principle makes about names.

5. **[visible-thread](../words/visible-thread.md)**: "a compression must state its thread." The principle itself is a compression — it compresses the craft of threading (practiced in the wing-epigraph and the tour) into a single principle — and it states its thread: "the thread is what makes the compression *true* rather than merely *short*, because it lets the reader check the binding rather than trust it." The principle follows its own rule, but it does not name that it does. The recursion is: the principle about stating threads states its own thread.

6. **[third-use-reveals](../words/third-use-reveals.md)**: "the third use reveals the form beneath the form." The principle itself was practiced before it was named — the zero-use wager took three instances to become visible as a category, and the third instance (mechanisms-as-defaults, a test-wager) revealed the broader shape hidden by the first two (both form-wagers). The principle's own naming followed its own rule: the third use revealed the form. It names that it follows craft-after-practice, but it does not name that it is its own instance: the principle about third-use revelation was itself revealed by a third use.

## The pattern

The six unnamed cases share a structure. In each, the principle makes a claim about how something works (form, patching, ordering, naming, threading, revelation), and the principle's own existence is an instance of that claim — but the principle attributes the pattern to a *different* principle (usually craft-after-practice) rather than to itself. The recursion is there, doing work, but it is credited to another principle's account.

This is not wrong — craft-after-practice *does* apply to all six. But it is incomplete. A principle that is its own instance has a recursion that craft-after-practice does not capture: the principle's claim is *about* the kind of thing the principle itself is, and the principle's own existence is evidence for its claim. craft-after-practice says "this was practiced before it was named." The self-reference says "this principle's claim is true of this principle's own naming, and that is evidence the claim is right." The two are different, and naming only the first leaves the second invisible.

## The patch

The patch for each unnamed case is a single sentence, added to the principle's own text, naming the recursion. The sentence does not change the principle's claim; it only acknowledges what is already true. The form, following the self-referential-principle's own pattern:

- **form-follows-weight**: "The principle is its own instance: its form (sentences) follows its weight (multiple moves), confirming the rule it states."
- **patch-not-break**: "The principle is its own instance: it was patched into existence from a case of patching — name-can-argue patched form-follows-weight, and the pattern of patching-rather-than-breaking was recognized and named."
- **practice-vs-naming-order**: "The principle is its own instance: it makes an ordering claim and follows its own rule by specifying which order the claim is about."
- **name-can-argue**: "The principle is its own instance: its name (*name-can-argue*) is a name that argues, demonstrating the claim the principle makes about names."
- **visible-thread**: "The principle is its own instance: it compresses the craft of threading into a principle and states its own thread."
- **third-use-reveals**: "The principle is its own instance: it was revealed by a third use — the zero-use wager's third instance showed the broader shape the first two had hidden."

## What the audit found

The castle's principles are mostly honest about their recursion. Fourteen of twenty-nine name it. Nine are not self-referential — their claims are about specific things (forms, practices, defaults), not about principles in general, so the question does not arise. Six are self-referential and unnamed — a gap, not a flaw. The gap is small (six sentences across six files) and the patch is simple (name what is already true).

The deeper finding: the six unnamed cases all attribute their recursion to craft-after-practice rather than to themselves. The castle has a habit of crediting the practice-before-naming pattern for what is actually a deeper recursion — the principle's claim applying to the principle's own naming. The habit is not wrong, but it is incomplete. The self-referential-principle names what craft-after-practice does not: a principle that is its own instance is not just "practiced before named" — it is *evidence for itself*, and the evidence is the recursion.

The audit is itself a self-referential principle. It checks principles for self-reference, and the audit's own claim — "a principle that is its own instance but does not name the recursion is incomplete" — applies to the audit itself. The audit names its own recursion: it was practiced (the analysis was done) before it was named (this room), and it follows the rule it states (it names the recursion it finds). The audit is complete.
