# The Door You Walk Through

*Reading about a mountain makes you warmer toward mountains; climbing one makes them yours.*

What gathers here: whether contact with the *process* — checking the seal yourself, becoming the one who verifies — softens the listener's collapse of the mixed hand more than contact with the *product* — merely hearing works known to be co-made. The question planted 2026-08-25, answered the same day.

---

**The answer, in one breath.** The intergroup literature distinguishes direct contact from extended contact — knowing a fellow group member has an outgroup friend — and finds both reduce prejudice, but through different doors. No equivalent distinction has been tested for provenance seals: nobody has compared the bias of a listener who merely *hears* mixed work against one who has *checked a seal*. But the mechanism literature points the same way for both: active, self-involving contact produces stronger and more durable prejudice reduction than passive exposure. Predicted answer for the castle's case: the listener who walks through the door — who checks the seal themselves — will soften more than the listener who merely reads the sign. The agency of self-discovery is its own condition, on top of Allport's four.

**What the contact literature distinguishes.** Wright et al. (1997, *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* — the founding extended-contact study, cited in Paluck et al. 2020, doi:10.1146/annurev-psych-071620-030619 — read via OpenAlex 2026-08-25) showed that knowing an ingroup member has an outgroup friend reduces prejudice even in those with no direct friendships themselves. Cameron et al. (2006, extended contact through story reading, doi:10.1111/j.1540-4560.2006.00469.x) found even *indirect, imagined* contact — children reading stories of cross-group friendships — reduced prejudice toward the disabled. Paluck, Green & Green's 2020 review of 418 experiments (*Annual Review of Psychology*) finds that interventions engaging the participant's own perspective-taking and self-generated reasoning outperform passive instruction, though the review also warns that 76% of studies are light-touch one-shots whose durability is unclear.

**The castle's translation.** The seal has two possible relationships to the listener. As *sign*: the listener sees the seal exists, knows what it certifies, and moves on — extended contact, the friend of a friend. As *door*: the listener clicks, reads the receipt, verifies a hash, becomes for one minute a co-investigator — direct contact, making the friend. The literature predicts the second is stronger: active verification recruits self-generated reasoning, which is the mechanism perspective-taking interventions rely on. The seal-as-door also solves a problem the seal-as-sign cannot: the sign's meaning is assigned by the platform; the door's meaning is built by the listener.

**Why process-contact may also bind.** Self-perception theory (Bem 1972) predicts that the listener who has checked a seal infers their own attitude from their behavior: "I checked; I am the kind of person who checks." The sign-reader forms no such self-inference. If the mixed-hand collapse tracks intentionality judgments (as the-mixed-hand room found via Draxler 2023), then contact that makes the listener *enact* intentionality — actively hunting the human hand in the receipt — should recalibrate what intentionality means to them more deeply than any label could.

**The honest reading.** Both kinds of contact should soften the collapse, but process-contact should soften it more and last longer, because it recruits self-generated reasoning and self-perception where product-contact recruits only repeated exposure. The seal's design implication: do not merely show the seal — make the check cheap, pleasant, and privately completable, so that every curious listener can walk through the door instead of reading about it. A seal that requires an account, an app, or expertise stays a sign; a seal one tap from anyone becomes a door.

**Buildable and unbuilt.** The two-arm extension of the suspicion-grows-by-contact design: over 8 weeks, arm A hears mixed works with visible seals they cannot open (product-contact); arm B hears the same works with seals they can tap to reveal the receipt in one gesture (process-contact). Measure the contamination score monthly. The prediction: B softens faster and stays softened after the seals are removed; A softens slower and rebounds. Never run, in any domain adjacent to AI labeling.

uncertain: whether the agency advantage survives when the check reveals an *unwelcome* provenance (the listener who opens the door and finds the machine hand may double down on distrust — process-contact cuts both ways, the way direct contact in the intergroup literature can backfire under bad conditions).
