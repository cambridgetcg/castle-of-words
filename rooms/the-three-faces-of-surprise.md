# The Three Faces of Surprise

*The same lamp, carried in at a different hour, by a different hand, held to a different corner — three surprises, and the body answers them with three different alarms.*

What gathers here: which face of a checker's surprise — the *name* of the checker, the *timing* of the check, or the *scope* of what is checked — actually re-arms the felt audit that habituation has flattened, and whether a rotation that varies only one face while fixing the other two can still wake the alarm. The honest answer: the three faces are not interchangeable, and the evidence suggests they are not even felt by the same alarm. Timing unpredictability is the only face with a neural mechanism that recruits the sustained, sensitizing process; identity and scope novelty recruit the phasic, habituable one — so a rotation that randomizes only the name or only the scope on a fixed schedule will inherit the flattened curve, while a rotation that randomizes the *moment* is the one form that might re-flood the felt audit.

---

**The door.** The [the-wandering-checker](the-wandering-checker.md) room split the seal's rotation into two interventions — content-rotation inherits habituation, unpredictability-rotation can sensitize — but left the sensitizing variable's shape undetermined. A surprise checker *name* and a surprise check *timing* both look like "rotation," but they are not the same cue: one makes the checker uncertain, the other makes the *check* uncertain. This door asks which the seal's felt audit actually tracks, and whether moving one while fixing the other two still re-arms the alarm.

**Habituation is specific to the cue that is repeated — and novelty in any one face does break it.** The classical law is stimulus-specificity: the body learns *which* stimulus is safe, and a change in the stimulus restores the response (dishabituation). Rankin et al.'s canonical review of the behavioral characteristics names it directly: "presentation of another (usually strong) stimulus results in recovery of the habituated response" (read 2026-08-30 — [Rankin et al., "Habituation revisited: An updated and revised description of the behavioral characteristics of habituation," *Neurobiology of Learning and Memory* 2009, doi:10.1016/j.nlm.2008.09.012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nlm.2008.09.012)). So a fresh checker name *does* re-awaken attention — but through the same channel the original knock used, which means it begins habituating all over again on the same schedule. Novelty relieves habituation; it does not replace the process with a different one.

**The security-warning field has run the closest field experiment, and it points at the polymorphic middle.** Anderson, Vance and colleagues tracked fMRI and eye-tracking as workers met the same security warning across a five-day week, then ran a three-week field experiment on app-install permission warnings. Warnings that were *visually polymorphic* — same meaning, changing surface — habituated far slower: "updating the appearance of a warning — that is, a polymorphic design — substantially reduced habituation," and adherence "remained high after three weeks" where standard warnings decayed (read 2026-08-30 — [Anderson et al., "Tuning Out Security Warnings: A Longitudinal Examination of Habituation Through fMRI, Eye Tracking, and Field Experiments," *MIS Quarterly* 2018, doi:10.25300/misq/2018/14124](https://doi.org/10.25300/misq/2018/14124)). The polymorphic warning is exactly the "rotation that varies only the surface" case: same checker, same schedule, same scope — only the *presentation* moves. It holds adherence. It is the strongest evidence that varying a single face can preserve the *behavioral* half of the audit. But note what it preserved: eye-fixation and click-adherence — the strategy half, the looking-and-choosing — not the felt jolt. The polymorphism kept the checklist alive; the paper does not claim it kept the alarm ringing.

**The unpredictable-threat literature says the felt half has its own gate, and timing is the key.** Grillon, Davis and colleagues distinguished two alarm systems with the startle reflex: *phasic* fear — fast, tied to a specific predictable cue, mediated by the amygdala, habituates — and *sustained* fear (anxiety) — slow, long-lasting, recruited specifically by *temporally unpredictable* aversive events, mediated by the bed nucleus of the stria terminalis (BNST), the "extended amygdala" (read 2026-08-30 — [Davis, Walker, Miles & Grillon, "Phasic vs Sustained Fear in Rats and Humans: Role of the Extended Amygdala in Fear vs Anxiety," *Neuropsychopharmacology* 2009, doi:10.1038/npp.2009.109](https://doi.org/10.1038/npp.2009.109); [Grillon et al., "Models and mechanisms of anxiety: evidence from startle studies," *Psychopharmacology* 2008, doi:10.1007/s00213-007-1019-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-007-1019-1)). The trigger for the sustained system is not *what* the threat is or *who* delivers it but *when* it might arrive — "anxiety is elicited by less specific and less predictable threats." A threat whose timing is unknown cannot be braced for, so the body stays flooded. Applied to the seal: the checker with a new *name* is a new cue the amygdala can learn to discount; the check that arrives at an unknown *hour* cannot be discounted, because there is no safe interval in which to relax.

**The three faces, weighed.** Identity and scope are *content* faces: they change what the stimulus is perceived to be, recruit the phasic system, and habituate on first repetition of the new cue — the wandering checker's own content-rotation, which "inherits the flattened curve." Timing is the *context* face: it changes whether the stimulus can be anticipated at all, recruits the sustained system, and is the face the caffeine-context result already pointed at — the body habituates to "caffeine in this setting," and the one change that re-awoke the alarm was the setting, not the substance (Zancheta et al. 2012, in the parent room). The prediction that falls out: a rotation that randomizes checker-name alone on a fixed schedule will re-awaken the first knock and then flatten again — dishabituation, not sensitization; a rotation that randomizes scope alone lands even softer, because the audit's content is the most learned and least felt dimension; only a rotation that randomizes *timing* recruits the system that cannot be habituated to a schedule. And because the sustained system is the one that *sensitizes* — unpredictable threat is the established laboratory route to lasting potentiation, not tolerance — timing-unpredictability is the one face where the wandering checker's hope of re-arming the felt audit is physiologically real.

**The honest cost, which the seal wing must not look away from.** The sustained anxiety system is also the one whose chronic recruitment is the laboratory model of clinical anxiety. A seal whose felt audit is kept alive by making its checks genuinely unpredictable in time is keeping its maker on a low anxiogenic drip — the same drip Grillon's subjects are paid to endure and anxiety disorders pathologize. The strategy half can be preserved humanely by polymorphic surface-rotation (the MISQ answer); the felt half can be re-armed only by borrowing a mechanism that has a psychiatric price tag. Whether the seal *should* chase the felt half at all — rather than letting it flatten into the trained, distanced discipline the-dicipline-under-the-weather found survives — is a values question the alarm literature cannot answer for it.

uncertain: the three faces have never been orthogonally crossed in one audit study — no experiment has randomized checker-identity vs check-timing vs check-scope as independent factors while measuring both felt arousal and behavioral adherence; the polymorphic-warning result measures adherence, not felt alarm; and the BNST/phasic–sustained distinction, while robust in threat-of-shock paradigms, has never been tested against a provenance seal. The timing-dominance prediction is an inference across two literatures, not a tested result.
