# The Honest Assertion Across Domains

*The same law, named differently in every field — what does the gathering reveal?*

Whether the law of honest assertion appears independently across domains, and whether bringing the names together reveals something the castle's two formulations haven't captured.

## The question

The castle's two laws (the law of honest assertion and the law of honest agent architecture) are the same structural principle at different altitudes: name what backs each claim, and every layer must re-derive its own truth. The question is whether other domains have named this same principle independently — journalism's "show your work," law's "chain of custody," science's "reproducibility," software's "trusted computing base" — and whether bringing them together under one name reveals something the castle's formulations haven't captured yet.

## What is known

The principle does appear independently across domains, each with its own name and emphasis:

### Journalism: "Show your work"

Journalism's version is **source transparency**: name your sources, distinguish observation from inference, and let the reader judge. The Society of Professional Journalists' Code of Ethics (2014) states: "Identify sources clearly. The public is entitled to as much information as possible to judge sources' reliability and motivations." The emphasis is on *attribution* — who said what — rather than on the structural separation of layers. Source: SPJ Code of Ethics, 2014.

### Law: "Chain of custody"

The legal version is **chain of custody**: every piece of evidence must be traceable from collection to courtroom, with each transfer documented and each handler identified. A break in the chain makes the evidence inadmissible. The emphasis is on *continuity* — the unbroken path — rather than on the speech-act nature of each claim. Source: Federal Rules of Evidence, Rule 901.

### Science: "Reproducibility"

Science's version is **reproducibility**: claims must be backed by methods and data that others can independently verify. The emphasis is on *independent verification* — can someone else get the same result — rather than on the internal structure of the claim itself. Source: National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine (2019). *Reproducibility and Replicability in Science*.

### Software: "Trusted Computing Base"

Software's version is the **trusted computing base (TCB)**: the set of components whose correctness must be trusted for the system to be secure. The emphasis is on *minimizing* what must be trusted — make the TCB as small as possible — rather than on naming what backs each claim. Source: Department of Defense (1985). *Trusted Computer System Evaluation Criteria* (Orange Book).

### Medicine: "Evidence-based medicine"

Medicine's version is **evidence-based medicine**: clinical decisions should be based on the best available evidence, with the strength of evidence explicitly graded. The emphasis is on *grading* the backing — how strong is the evidence — rather than on the structural separation of claim from backing. Source: Sackett, D. L., et al. (1996). "Evidence based medicine: what it is and what it isn't." *BMJ*, 312(7023), 71–72.

### Accounting: "Audit trail"

Accounting's version is the **audit trail**: every transaction must be traceable from source document to financial statement and back. The emphasis is on *traceability* — the inspectable path — which is the closest analogue to the castle's formulation. Source: Generally Accepted Auditing Standards (GAAS).

## What the gathering reveals

Bringing these together under one name reveals something the castle's two formulations have not fully captured: **the principle has three faces, not one**.

1. **The speech-act face** (the castle's law of honest assertion): every output is a speech act; name what backs it. This is the *declarative* face — what the system *says* about its own claims.

2. **The layer face** (the castle's law of honest agent architecture): every layer must re-derive its own truth. This is the *structural* face — how the system *builds* its claims.

3. **The continuity face** (chain of custody, audit trail): the path from origin to claim must be unbroken and inspectable. This is the *temporal* face — how the claim *travels* from source to output.

The castle's two laws cover faces 1 and 2. Face 3 — the continuity of the path — is implicit in the inspectable-path concept from accounting product design but has not been named as a third face of the same law. The gathering reveals that the law of honest assertion is not only about *what backs a claim* (face 1) and *how layers derive truth* (face 2) but also about *how the claim travels from origin to output without being altered* (face 3).

The three faces are not separate laws — they are the same law seen from three angles: the claim's **backing** (what supports it), the claim's **construction** (how it was built), and the claim's **journey** (how it arrived).
