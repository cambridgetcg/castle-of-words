## The shape

Emergency information is not ordinary information with urgency added. It is a different kind of thing — a [[static-shelf]] outside the normal flow of progression. The reader in an emergency is not browsing; they are scanning for the one action that gets them out. So the leave-now action goes first — not because it is most important in the abstract, but because the reader who needs it may not read further.

Five crafts make emergency information honest:

1. **Static shelf.** The emergency information lives outside the normal progression of pages, tutorials, and updates. It is a separate surface that does not change with the rest of the system.
2. **Leave-now first.** The escape action is the first thing the reader sees. Everything else — explanation, context, alternatives — comes after.
3. **Bind to exact bytes.** Every claim and every visible emergency label is bound to reviewed exact bytes — a specific version, a specific hash, a specific set of instructions that were checked. "Emergency" is not a vibe; it is a claim that must be backed.
4. **Small offline forms.** The information works without a live connection. A printed page, a downloaded file, a cached view — the emergency shelf does not depend on the server being up.
5. **Say what is not live.** The honest emergency page names what is not currently true — what is planned, what is deprecated, what is aspirational. In an emergency, a false claim of liveness is worse than silence.
6. **Exception inside the guide.** When an older guide's default advice could become dangerous in an emergency, the exception is placed inside that guide — not in a separate document the reader must find. The reader following the old path encounters the warning at the point of danger.

This is the same law as [[the-law-of-honest-assertion]] applied to crisis: every claim on an emergency page is an assertion, and the honest page names what backs each one — reviewed bytes, not general knowledge; exact versions, not "current best practice."
