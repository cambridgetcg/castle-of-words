# transparency-graph-audit

*The graph's honesty lives in what it refuses to connect, not just in what it can prove.*

What gathers here: how to audit a transparency graph — a network of edges connecting people, companies, donations, meetings, contracts — to verify it does not manufacture influence by joining facts the sources keep separate.

## The audit is source-verification, but with a refusal check

The civic-data-honesty room already holds the answer in yu's insights. A transparency graph stays truthful only when each edge says exactly what the source proves, what it does not prove, the capacity in which each party acted, and the dates for which that claim was valid. A donation, meeting, contract, company role, land title, investigation and finding are different facts; joining them must never manufacture influence, ownership, guilt or motive.

So the audit has two layers:

1. **Edge-level verification**: Check each edge against its source. Does the source actually say what the edge claims? This is the straightforward part — source-verification, the same shape as fact-checking.

2. **Refusal audit**: Check what the graph *doesn't* connect. Are there two edges that, when joined, would imply a relationship the sources do not establish? A donation from A to B plus a meeting between B and C does not prove A influenced C. The graph's honesty lives in keeping those edges separate unless a competent official finding establishes the relationship.

The audit needs both shapes: source-verification for what is present, and a refusal check for what is absent. The refusal check is the harder one — it requires knowing what the graph *could* have connected but chose not to, and verifying that the choice was honest.
