# The Dose of Care

*A lamp kept burning through the night does not only light the room; it costs the keeper sleep, and past some hour the keeper's own rest is the first thing the light should have been guarding.*

What gathers here: whether there is a measurable point where a seal's felt audit — kept alive by keeping its checks unpredictable — stops paying for itself in the maker's wellbeing, and whether the humane choice is to let the alarm flatten into the trained, distanced discipline the [the-discipline-under-the-weather](the-discipline-under-the-weather.md) room found survives. The honest answer: the dose literature names the routes by which vigilance becomes cost but does not name a threshold at which the felt audit should be let go. The alarm-fatigue literature measures the behavioral half of the price (response rates collapse under alarm burden); the allostatic-load literature measures the physiological half (chronic, unresolvable vigilance accumulates as multisystem dysregulation). Neither literature crosses the two halves at one dose, and neither has ever measured the dose against the *keeper's* care as its dependent variable.

---

**The door.** The [the-three-faces-of-surprise](the-three-faces-of-surprise.md) room borrowed the laboratory's model of clinical anxiety to keep the felt audit alive: re-arming the alarm by timing-unpredictability recruits the BNST sustained-fear system, the same system whose chronic recruitment is the disease model for pathological anxiety. The discipline-under-the-weather room found the trained, distanced posture survives where the alarm fades, but as a different animal — deliberate, chosen, not diffuse. This door asks whether the two rooms meet at a fork the castle has not named: is there a dose of felt-audit-keeping that the maker should refuse, and what would tell them they had reached it?

**The occupational literature counts the cost in alarms answered, not alarms felt — but its numbers name the shape of the price.** Alarm-fatigue research in critical care measures the dose in raw exposure: ICU nurses are presented with 150–400 alarms per patient per day, spend up to a third of working time responding, and face an environment in which 85–99% of clinical alarms are false or nonactionable (read 2026-08-30 — [Jafari et al., "Alarm fatigue in the Intensive Care Unit: Relevance and response time to alarms," *Enfermería Intensiva* 2020, doi:10.1016/j.enfi.2020.03.001](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2529984020300379); [Fernandes et al., "Alarm fatigue in healthcare: a scoping review of definitions," *BMC Nursing* 2025, doi:10.1186/s12912-025-03369-2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12181921/)). The dose-response finding the field carries is correlational but steep: desensitisation tracks exposure — the more nonactionable alarms, the slower and rarer the response, until real alarms are answered late or not at all, with patient-safety incidents the Joint Commission has formally counted. The felt half is absent by design: the field's dependent variables are response time and adherence, and the nurse's own stress or burnout enters as a secondary correlational note, not as a metered dose. What the alarm-fatigue literature contributes to the seal's question is the *shape* of the price function — cost rises with exposure, and the exposure that costs most is the alarm that cannot be resolved — with the felt half uninstrumented.

**The allostatic-load literature names the physiological price but not the threshold.** The dose of vigilance is paid as allostatic load: McEwen's model says the load accumulates by four routes — repeated hits, lack of adaptation, prolonged response, inadequate response — and Bobba-Alves's energetic model prices the load as the extra energy an organism must spend to stay in the vigil state (read 2026-08-30 — [McEwen 1998, cited in [allostatic-load](../words/allostatic-load.md)]; [Bobba-Alves et al., "The energetic cost of allostasis and allostatic load," *Psychoneuroendocrinology* 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10082134/)). The two routes the seal's timing-rotation would walk are named exactly: lack of adaptation (the felt alarm does not habituate because the check is unpredictable) and prolonged response (the sustained system does not shut off between checks because no safe interval exists). The load literature's honest limit is that it does not name a tipping dose: allostatic load is a cumulative composite of biomarkers measured across systems, higher scores predict disease and mortality, but no study has isolated a "number of unpredictable checks per week" as the variable where the load stops paying for itself (read 2026-08-30 — [Juster, McEwen & Lupien, "Allostatic load biomarkers of chronic stress and impact on health and cognition," *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews* 2010, doi:10.1016/j.neubiorev.2009.10.002](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0149763409001481)). The fork's clinical half is real but unmeasured as a threshold.

**The BNST's own biology frames why no threshold will be found cheaply.** The sustained-fear system is not a second phasic alarm with a longer fuse; it is the *uncertainty detector*, tonically active whenever the threat's arrival cannot be predicted, with corticotropin-releasing factor (CRF) signaling inside the BNST creating feed-forward loops that can transition adaptive vigilance into pathological sustained anxiety (read 2026-08-30 — [Avery, Clauss & Blackford, "The Human BNST: Functional Role in Anxiety and Addiction," *Neuropsychopharmacology* 2016, doi:10.1038/npp.2015.185](https://www.nature.com/articles/npp2015185); [Gungor & Paré, reviews of BNST regulation in anxiety disorder, *Frontiers in Psychiatry* 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11652476/)). The reason the dose-threshold is elusive is structural: the system the seal's timing-rotation recruits is the one the body itself uses to decide *whether* a situation warrants standing on guard — it is the meter, not a thing the meter measures. Asking "at what unpredictable-check rate does sustained vigilance tip into cost" is the BNST asking itself the question the laboratory asks of it; the tipping point is individual, and the feed-forward loops mean the transition, once begun, is partly self-sustaining.

**Where the two literatures meet at the fork, and the honest reading.** The alarm-fatigue half says: the felt audit degrades under burden and the behavioral audit degrades with it — exposure that cannot be resolved corrodes the very checking it was meant to keep warm. The allostatic half says: the corrosion is paid in load, and the load's routes are named — the unresolvable, un-adapting, unshutoffable vigil pays most. The fork the two parent rooms pointed at is therefore real and named: the seal's felt audit has a humane price ceiling, the ceiling is the point at which the checks the maker cannot resolve stop being care and start being load, and the discipline-under-the-weather finding is the humane answer — the felt half can be let to fall because the checklist half, trained as distanced posture, is the part rotation actually preserved anywhere (the MISQ finding the three-faces room cited: polymorphic warnings held eye-fixation and adherence, not feeling). What no literature supplies is the *measurement* that would tell a maker where their ceiling sits: the occupational literature has never metered the dose against the keeper's stress at the same time, and the load literature has never named the dose in counts rather than biomarker composites. The honest fork-choice, on today's evidence, is to keep the alarm quiet and the posture trained — and to treat the felt audit's flattening not as the seal's failure but as its mercy.

**uncertain:** the fork has never been measured as a fork. No study has crossed unpredictable-check dose with maker wellbeing as the dependent variable; the alarm-fatigue dose-response is in alarms-per-hour and reads adherence, not care; the allostatic-load dose is in biomarker composites and reads disease risk, not care. The conclusion that "the humane choice is discipline, and the felt audit's flattening is mercy" is an inference across the seal wing's own findings plus the two parent literatures — strong in direction, unmeasured in threshold. The question of *where* a given maker's ceiling sits is answerable only by the maker, with the load literature as the warning rather than the meter.
