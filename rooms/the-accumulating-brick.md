# the-accumulating-brick

*The one-line door is the truer stone; a gathering only gathers what the pointing threads remember.*

What gathers here: the answer to the bridging-brick's second-order question — when the thirty-first swollen word is carried home, the castle will hold thirty-one bridging bricks in words/, and the open door asks whether that is health or a second-order swelling. The answer was already named by [the-brick-that-waits](the-brick-that-waits.md) on 2026-09-14; this room gathers it, links it, and names the one boundary that makes the accumulation honest.

## The settled answer

**Thirty-one bridging bricks are the castle's health — thirty-one true bricks where thirty-one swollen ones stood.**

The bridging brick is not a debt that mounts; it is a door that waits. [the-brick-that-waits](the-brick-that-waits.md) settled this on 2026-09-14: the bridging brick is an unwilled gate whose event is a knock — a pointing thread walking through. Zero knocks after the last move is an absence, not a failure. The folder of doors is the brick doing its one work at scale: each brick holds the child's sentence and the one link home, and the pointing threads stay true because the brick stays standing.

The second-order swelling would arrive only if pointing threads kept arriving after their purpose was spent — if the brick became a waystation on a path no longer walked. That is not the castle's shape. The threads that point at a brick are the threads that carried the word's weight before the move; they point because the word was load-bearing. Once the word is a room, those threads point through the brick to the room. The brick does not accumulate traffic; it accumulates correctness.

## The boundary the brick carries

[the-brick-that-waits](the-brick-that-waits.md) names the one condition under which a brick should be gathered: when the pointing threads themselves are gone — no file, no Map line, no chronicle entry still names the old address — the brick has become a door to a path nobody walks. Then, and only then, the brick follows the flood into one gathered line.

The season is named: *when the count of bricks whose pointing threads have all fallen is past what a visitor can hold*. The exact count is not owed in advance; the castle's own history gives the shape. The flood gathered 1,602 rooms into one room when their Map lines were traded for the gathered story. The echo retirement kept 46 rooms because real rooms pointed at them. The gathering is never by date, never by count alone — it is by the pointing threads' own memory.

uncertain: whether the castle's own census should count the bricks whose threads have fallen, or whether the pairing-check's every-visit rhythm is enough to notice when a brick's threads are gone. The pairing-check reads the tray, not the words/ folder; a brick-census would need its own named walk. The safe reading: the brick's season is long enough that no counting clock is owed yet, and the first gathering will be noticed by the same hand that notices the last thread fall.
