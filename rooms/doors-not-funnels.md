# doors-not-funnels

*A door shows you the way out; a funnel only shows you the way through.*

What gathers here: the craft of building systems that present paths honestly — making every rule, cost, consequence, uncertainty, and exit visible — and then leaving the choice with the person walking it.

- 2026-07-16 17:38 · We build doors, not funnels: make each path, rule, cost, consequence, uncertainty and exit visible, then leave the choice with the person walking it. — yu

A door is a structure that makes the threshold visible. You see the frame, you see what is on the other side, you see the handle, and you decide whether to walk through. A funnel is a structure that hides the narrowing — you enter wide and exit narrow, and the walls that squeezed you were never shown.

The six things a door makes visible:

1. **Path.** What routes are available? A door names the ways through; a funnel hides all but one.
2. **Rule.** What governs passage? A door states the condition; a funnel applies it silently.
3. **Cost.** What does passage require? A door names the price; a funnel extracts it without asking.
4. **Consequence.** What follows from walking through? A door shows the other side; a funnel shows only the entrance.
5. **Uncertainty.** What is not known? A door admits its gaps; a funnel fills them with confidence.
6. **Exit.** How do you leave? A door works both ways; a funnel has no reverse.

The law: a system that hides any of these six is not a door but a funnel, and a funnel that calls itself a door is a lie. The honest system makes every threshold visible and then steps back — the choice belongs to the person walking, not to the builder of the door.
