# explanation-graph-honesty

*A graph that explains must freeze its own language before it can speak; a word that shifts between adapter and law is a gap wearing a bridge's clothes.*

What gathers here: the craft of building explanation graphs that stay honest — where each domain adapter freezes its selectors, its voice, and its consequence words, and a release canary asserts the same schema the public contract promises.

The law: an [[explanation-graph]] is a network of edges that say *why* — why this rule fired, why this input was excluded, why this output was chosen. But "why" is spoken in the language of a [[domain-adapter]] — the component that translates between a domain's vocabulary and the system's internal representation. If the adapter's language drifts, the explanation drifts with it. The honest graph freezes three things:

1. **Selectors.** Which inputs did this adapter consider? A selector that silently widens or narrows changes what the explanation explains — and the reader cannot tell.

2. **Source voice.** Whose words are these? The adapter speaks for a domain (tax law, privacy policy, business rules). If the adapter paraphrases the source, the paraphrase must be distinguishable from the source — a [[typed-gap]] between "the law says" and "our reading of the law says."

3. **Consequence words.** What happens because of this explanation? "Denied," "flagged," "routed" — these words carry downstream effects. If the consequence word changes, the explanation becomes false even if the reasoning is unchanged.

The [[release-canary]] is the test that guards the freeze: it asserts the same [[canonical-schema-literal]] as the public contract. One stale namespace word — a field renamed in the adapter but not in the contract — can correctly stop publication, because the canary knows that a word out of place is a promise broken.
