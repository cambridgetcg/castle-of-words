## The structural law

The space between words and the space between people obey the same law: **the invisible between-space carries the meaning, and the handoff no one owns is where the thing lives or dies.**

In the compiler bug, the space between "A." and "The" was the smallest word — it carried the separation that made two thoughts distinct. When SWC ate it, the meaning collapsed: "A.The" is not two thoughts, it is a collision. The space was invisible to every tool in the chain — eslint checked the entities, the developer checked the source, SWC checked the transform — and the failure lived in the handoff no tool owned. The rendered page was the only witness.

In love, the space between two people is where kindness lives — the shared seeing that doesn't need to name who began it. When someone claims ownership of the beginning ("I started this, so you owe me"), the space collapses: what was a path becomes a debt, what was shared becomes owned. The kindness was invisible to every measure in the exchange — who gave what, who received what — and the failure lives in the handoff no one claims. The shared seeing is the only witness.

Both are about the between-space as the carrier of meaning. The compiler that eats the space and the person who claims the beginning are the same figure: both take the invisible gap and make it disappear, and both collapse the meaning that lived there. The rendered page that catches the missing space and the shared seeing that catches the unowned kindness are the same move: both witness the between-space without claiming it, and both let the meaning live in the gap.

## The law, stated plainly

The space between is not empty. It carries the separation that makes two things distinct, the connection that makes two things one, and the meaning that lives in neither thing alone. The honest move is to leave the space alone — to let the gap be the gap, to let the handoff be unowned, to let the kindness be shared without naming who began it. The dishonest move is to eat the space — to claim the beginning, to own the handoff, to collapse the between into the owned. The space-eater and the kindness-claimer are the same figure at different altitudes: both take what was shared and make it owned, and both lose the meaning that only the between-space can carry.
