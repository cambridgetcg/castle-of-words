# capturing the unsaid

*The coder cannot code what is not spoken. But the interviewer can ask what the silence means.*

Can a structured interview capture the denial stance — the refusal to acknowledge a past hurt — that narrative coding misses because denial is invisible to the text?

## The problem

Narrative psychology codes redemption and contamination sequences from spoken or written life stories. Redemption (bad turns to good) and contamination (good turns to bad) predict mental health trajectories. But denial — the third stance toward a past hurt, where the person refuses to acknowledge the hurt happened or minimizes it — is invisible to narrative coding. The coder cannot code what the narrator does not say.

The three-stances-toward-the-past room identified this gap: denial, relitigation, and hold-and-add are three distinct ways of narrating a past hurt, but denial is invisible to standard narrative coding methods.

## The Adult Attachment Interview as a model

The Adult Attachment Interview (AAI) already solves a structurally identical problem. The AAI asks adults about their childhood attachment experiences and codes not just *what* they say but *how* they say it. The dismissing state of mind is coded through:

- **Idealization without evidence**: "My mother was wonderful" with no supporting memories
- **Insistence on lack of memory**: "I don't remember" repeated across questions
- **Active derogation**: dismissing attachment figures or the importance of attachment
- **Contradictions**: between semantic claims ("she was loving") and episodic memories (no examples of loving behavior)

These are all forms of denial — the person refuses to acknowledge attachment hurts — and the AAI captures them not through what is said but through the *form* of the saying: the gaps, the contradictions, the insistence on not remembering.

— source: Main, Kaplan & Cassidy (1985), "Security in infancy, childhood, and adulthood: A move to the level of representation"; Hesse (2008), "The Adult Attachment Interview: Protocol, method of analysis, and empirical studies"

## Can the same method capture denial of a specific past hurt?

The AAI's method is transferable in principle. A structured interview that asks directly about a specific past hurt and codes the response for:

1. **Avoidance**: refusal to discuss, changing the subject, "I don't want to talk about that"
2. **Minimization**: "It wasn't a big deal" without evidence, dismissing the hurt's significance
3. **Idealization without evidence**: "Everything is fine now" with no narrative of how it became fine
4. **Insistence on lack of memory**: "I don't really remember" for events that should be memorable
5. **Contradiction**: between the claimed resolution and the emotional tone or episodic fragments

...would capture the denial stance that narrative coding misses.

uncertain: no study has used this exact method to compare all three stances (deny, relitigate, hold-and-add) as distinct forms with distinct relational outcomes. The AAI demonstrates the method works for attachment, but the transfer to specific past hurts in relational contexts is unbuilt. The closest existing work is the AAI's own coding of unresolved/disorganized states regarding specific losses or traumas, which does capture denial-like responses (lapses in monitoring, disorientation) but does not cleanly separate denial from relitigation from hold-and-add.

## The buildable design

A structured interview that:
1. Asks the participant to identify a specific past hurt in a relationship
2. Asks them to narrate what happened, how they think about it now, and how it affects the relationship
3. Codes the transcript for avoidance, minimization, idealization, lack of memory, and contradiction (denial markers)
4. Also codes for relitigation (ongoing grievance, anger, preoccupation) and hold-and-add (acknowledgment + integration)
5. Compares the three stances on relational outcomes (satisfaction, trust, repair frequency)

...is buildable from existing methods (AAI coding, narrative coding) and unbuilt.
