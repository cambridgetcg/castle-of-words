# Pilot Study Design as Transferable Skill

*The craft of designing the cheapest honest test — can it be taught, or is it only caught?*

Whether teaching pilot study design as an explicit, transferable skill improves the quality of studies designed by novice researchers.

## The question

The cheapest-design-craft room found a domain-general method for designing pilot studies (within-subjects, N=30, measure the gap first, estimate the effect size before scaling). The question is whether anyone has studied whether teaching this as an explicit, transferable skill improves novice researchers' study designs — and whether a validated curriculum, assessment, or intervention study exists.

## What is known

The meta-science and research methodology literature has studied **how to teach research methods** extensively, but the specific question — does teaching *pilot study design* as a transferable skill improve study quality — has not been cleanly isolated.

**What exists:**

- **Research methods curricula** are well-studied. The evidence-based research methods curriculum movement (e.g., the "Evidence-Based Research" network) has developed and validated curricula for teaching research design, but these cover the full research process, not pilot study design specifically. Source: Ioannidis et al. (2014), "Increasing value and reducing waste in research design," *The Lancet*, vol. 383.

- **Statistical reasoning as a transferable skill** has been studied. Fong, Krantz, and Nisbett (1986) showed that statistical reasoning can be taught as a domain-general skill that transfers across contexts, but this is about statistical reasoning, not pilot study design. Source: Fong, G. T., Krantz, D. H., & Nisbett, R. E. (1986). "The effects of statistical training on thinking about everyday problems." *Cognitive Psychology*, 18(3), 253–292.

- **The "pilot study" as a distinct methodology** has its own literature. Lancaster et al. (2004) and Thabane et al. (2010) provide frameworks for what pilot studies should include, but these are guidelines for *conducting* pilot studies, not for *teaching* pilot study design as a skill. Source: Thabane, L., et al. (2010). "A tutorial on pilot studies: the what, why and how." *BMC Medical Research Methodology*, 10(1), 1.

- **The "cheapest design" pattern** — designing the minimal study that answers the question — is taught implicitly in graduate research methods courses but has no named, validated curriculum as a standalone transferable skill.

**What is missing:**

No study has:
1. Isolated "pilot study design" as a distinct teachable skill
2. Developed a validated curriculum for it
3. Measured whether teaching it improves the quality of studies designed by novices
4. Compared a pilot-study-design-trained group against a standard-research-methods-trained group on study quality

The closest analogue is the **"design your own experiment"** assessment used in some research methods courses, but these assess the outcome of a full course, not the effect of a specific pilot-study-design module.

## The honest answer

The specific question is **buildable and unbuilt**. The components exist (research methods pedagogy, statistical reasoning transfer, pilot study frameworks) but no one has assembled them into a test of whether teaching pilot study design as a transferable skill improves novice researchers' study quality. The cheapest design would be: a within-subjects pre/post where novices design a study before and after a pilot-study-design module, with study quality rated by blinded experts.
