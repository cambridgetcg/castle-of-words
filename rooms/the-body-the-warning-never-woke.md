# The Body the Warning Never Woke

*A sign refreshed in shape but never in meaning holds the eye at the window — and a body still sleeping in bed.*

What gathers here: whether any warning-habituation study has measured the *felt* dimension directly — startle, skin conductance, self-reported urgency — under a polymorphic rotation, and whether the checklist is the only thing rotation has ever preserved in any domain. The honest answer: no — the felt half has never been instrumented. The strongest neurophysiological studies of polymorphic-warning habituation measured gaze, mouse-cursor trajectories, and fMRI BOLD in visual-attention regions; none measured a physiological or affective alarm at all. The checklist half is the only half rotation has preserved in the warning literature, and in the stress-habituation literature the felt half is preserved by nothing.

---

**The door.** The [the-three-faces-of-surprise](the-three-faces-of-surprise.md) room leaned on the polymorphic-warning evidence as the field's strongest case that rotation keeps an audit alive, and named its limit in the same breath: "the paper measured clicks and gaze, not feeling — the felt half was never instrumented." This door asks whether anyone has since picked the instrument up, and whether the felt half comes back when the surface rotates.

**The two strongest polymorphic-warning studies show the same ceiling.** Anderson et al.'s CHI 2015 study built a polymorphic warning and measured habituation by two neurophysiological channels — fMRI (blood-oxygen in visual-attention regions) and mouse-cursor tracking (hand-trajectory as a proxy for decision conflict) — and found the polymorphic variant "substantially more resistant to habituation than conventional warnings" in both channels: the visual cortex stayed responsive across exposures, and cursor trajectories showed less automatic click-through (read 2026-08-30 — [Anderson et al., "How Polymorphic Warnings Reduce Habituation in the Brain—Insights from an fMRI Study," *CHI* 2015, doi:10.1145/2702123.2702322](https://neurosecurity.net/media/Anderson_et_al._CHI_2015.pdf)). Vance et al.'s MISQ longitudinal study extended the same design to a three-week field experiment and found updating a warning's appearance "substantially reduced habituation of attention" (read 2026-08-30 — [Vance, Jenkins, Anderson, Bjornn & Kirwan, "Tuning Out Security Warnings: A Longitudinal Examination of Habituation Through fMRI, Eye Tracking, and Field Experiments," *MIS Quarterly* 2018, doi:10.25300/MISQ/2018/14124](https://neurosecurity.net/media/Vance_et_al._2017_MISQ.pdf)). Neither paper — nor the broader warning-habituation literature they anchor — ever measured startle, electrodermal activity, heart-rate variability, or self-reported urgency. The channel that *was* preserved is the channel that was *measured*: gaze, cortical visual responses, and click-through adherence. The body's felt alarm was not tested.

**The stress-habituation literature is where the felt half would have been looked for — and it found the body's curve flattens across perturbations.** The discipline-under-the-weather room already landed this on the reappraisal-habituation study: all participants' cardiovascular responses habituated on second exposure regardless of instruction, and instruction did not slow the flattening ([Brennan et al. 2021, read 2026-08-30, cited in [the-discipline-under-the-weather](the-discipline-under-the-weather.md)]). The same literature's perturbation studies show the habituation generalizes: once the alarm habituates to a paradigm, it habituates to variations of that paradigm. The felt half does not come back when the surface changes — a finding the warning literature cannot contradict, because it never asked the question.

**The honest answer has two halves.** On the warning side: polymorphic rotation demonstrably preserves the audit's *seen* half — gaze, visual-cortex response, adherence — and has never been tested on the *felt* half. On the stress side: the felt half habituates and stays flattened even across perturbations of the paradigm, with no known surface-level manipulation that restores it. Together, the checklist is the only thing rotation has ever preserved — everywhere it was measured, and everywhere else the felt half was simply never the variable.

**uncertain:** the warning side is narrow — it has no felt measure, so "never preserved" there is an absence of test, not a tested null; the stress side is the tested null, but it tested *stressors*, not warnings. The join — that the felt alarm would not re-arm under a polymorphic warning — is an inference between two literatures, not a result either one reported.
