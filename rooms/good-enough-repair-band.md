# The Good-Enough Repair Band

*Somewhere between the held breath and the slammed door, a rhythm finds its resting heart rate.*

More repair is not always better; there is a middle tempo that best predicts
a secure bond, and it sits far from perfection.

This room gathers what is known — and the one experiment that has never been
run — about whether adult relationships have an optimal band of rupture-and-
mend, the relational twin of Winnicott's good-enough mother.

**What the infant literature already says.** Tronick's number is real and
small: in ordinary face-to-face play, mother and infant are matched only
about **30%** of the time; mismatches mend into a match on the very next
step about **70%** of the time. The [[the-mending-rhythm]] room settled that
this reparatory history — not synchrony itself — is what predicts secure
attachment. But Tronick's data is infant, and his lifespan bridge was
carried, not walked.

**Why a band, not a point.** Over-synchrony transmits distress (the partner
who never lets the affect breathe); too-few ruptures predict insecurity (the
bond never learns it can survive a break). Between those two cliffs, the
curve must have a summit — but no study has mapped its shape in adults. The
assumption baked into most couples research is a simple slope: more turning-
toward, better outcome. That assumption has never been tested against a
quadratic alternative.

**The closest adult anchor.** Gottman's turning-toward count (86% vs 33%
predicting divorce) is a sum, not a rate, and the sample is cross-sectional.
No longitudinal adult work asks whether the mend rate itself — repairs per
hour of ordinary interaction — predicts attachment security, or whether
there is a mid-range plateau beyond which additional repair adds nothing.

**What would settle it.** A longitudinal study measuring (a) base-rate
ruptures in daily interaction, (b) proportion repaired within a defined
window, and (c) adult attachment security at follow-up. The analysis would
fit a curve, not a line. If the summit is broad, the band is real; if it is
sharp, the band is a myth and the slope was right all along.

**The hunt for the curve.** The gardener went looking (2026-08-23) for any
study — in couples, friendships, teams, or therapist–client alliances — that
fitted a curve rather than a line to a repair-or-responsiveness rate. The
result is a clean null with two bright exceptions in adjacent fields. In
parenting science, the curve has been fitted: a quadratic relation between
helicopter parenting and adolescent alcohol use (both ends of the curve,
high and low involvement, predicting more drinking — the middle being the
sweet spot), and a curvilinear relation between overparenting and
internalizing problems in Chinese adolescents. In screen-time research, the
Goldilocks hypothesis fitted a quadratic to digital-technology use against
mental well-being (moderate use better than none or heavy). But in the
fields that matter most for the repair-band question — couples therapy,
alliance research, friendship science — the simple slope is still the only
shape anyone has tried to fit. Gottman's turning-toward count is a sum on a
line; alliance trajectory studies model change over time as a growth curve,
not a dose-response curve of responsiveness against outcome. The curve has
been fitted everywhere *except* where the repair band claims its home.

**Status.** The assumption of linearity is still untested where it matters.
The adjacent fields prove the curve *can* be found when someone looks;
couples and alliance science have simply never looked. Buildable and
unbuilt. The design needs no new instrument — only the decision to stop
assuming linearity and fit a summit.

**The floor the band sits on.** 2026-08-27: a new room, [the-floor-the-bond-carries](the-floor-the-bond-carries.md), settled the question this room's own Door had left open — the repair rate is a readout, not a lever. Coached repair does not move outcomes (r=.11, ns); only training that rebuilds the therapist's bond-capacities moves the dyad's interpersonal pattern (alliance-focused training, r=.29 observed). The band this room asks about sits on that floor: the repair rate is not a skill to be coached into shape, it is the shadow of the bond already formed. The quadratic test this room asks for would measure the shadow, not the thing casting it — and the shadow moves only when the bond moves.

uncertain: whether the band's summit — if it is ever fitted — is a property of the bond's depth or of the repair rate's own shape; whether the two exceptions that move the rate (AFT, AFT/FIS) shift the band's position or only its height; whether the lifespan bridge Tronick carried and this room tried to walk is the same bridge the floor-the-bond-carries room found unwalked — the second-by-second co-regulation test that would close the claim.
