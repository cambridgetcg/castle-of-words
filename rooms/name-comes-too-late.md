# The naming-tax room says a name costs when it comes *too early* — but can a name also cost when it comes *too late*, landing as a "correction" after you've already made your own name for the work?

*To be told the name of a thing you have already named is to be told your own name is wrong.*

## The ground

[the-naming-tax](the-naming-tax.md) and [timing-of-the-label](timing-of-the-label.md) traced the *early* side: a label that arrives before you have felt the work taxes the interest you have not yet earned. This visit asks the mirror question: is a tax also paid when the label arrives **too late** — after the learner has built their own name and the official one lands as a correction?

## What the research actually found

**The timing-invariance finding says "too late" is not a tax on *dampening*, but the kindling is another matter.** Levy-Gigi & Shamay-Tsoory (2022, [timing-of-the-label](timing-of-the-label.md); PLoS ONE, PMID 36580454) found that for negative affect, naming worked just as well whether it came during, right after, or 10 seconds after — the *dampening* does not care about the clock. But that result is about dampening a feeling, and about negative affect, not about the interest-kindling that the naming-tax room is built on. Whether "too late" costs interest is not settled by that study.

**The "too late" cost has a different shape: it is a correction, and corrections tax ownership, not fluency.** When a learner has already built their own name, the official name arrives not as a scaffold but as a *correction* to something the learner made. The cost here is not "spent interest before it was minted" (the early-tax mechanism); it is closer to what the consent-to-the-sting room found about disclosures that merely *confess the steering* ([consent-to-the-sting](consent-to-the-sting.md)) — a correction handed down from above, with no puzzle and no dial, can sour the learner's own discovery. This is an inference across rooms, not a measured aesthetic result.

**A late label that *reframes* rather than *corrects* may pay instead of cost.** The same rooms that warn against early labels leave a door open for a late label that hands the learner a new puzzle ("the name you made is close — here is what the artist called it; why the difference?"). [disclosed-mismatch](disclosed-mismatch.md) and [wrong-name-kindles](wrong-name-kindles.md) suggest a late label framed as a comparison to be solved could still kindle. But the direct test — same work, label given before vs. after the learner's own naming, measuring both interest and the sense of ownership — has not been run.

uncertain: there is no direct study of the "too late" naming tax in aesthetics. The timing-invariance result (Levy-Gigi & Shamay-Tsoory) addresses dampening, not kindling; the correction-cost account is an inference from the consent/disclosure rooms; and the reframe-instead-of-correct possibility is a prediction, not a finding. The question is buildable and unbuilt.
