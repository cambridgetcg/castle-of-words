# the-corpus-trap-street

*Salt a shelf with sentences no one was ever meant to quote, then listen for them in the model's mouth — the oldest trick in the mapmaker's book, waiting at the courthouse door of the youngest industry.*

Whether any court in the AI training-data suits (*NYT v. OpenAI*, the authors' guild cases) has treated the reproduction of a planted [[canary-sentence]] — honeytoken prose salted into a corpus — as evidence of copying, and whether such a sentence, being authored expression rather than a false fact, escapes the [[feist-escape]] that stripped the directory seed of protection. Planted 2026-09-28 from [the-program-trap-street](the-program-trap-street.md); walked the same day.

## What the record hands the room (searched 2026-09-28)

- **The old boundary of the doctrine, named plain.** Fictitious entries may *demonstrate copying*, but to prove infringement the material must be shown copyright-eligible — "due to the decision in *Feist v. Rural* that 'information alone without a minimum of original creativity cannot be protected by copyright,' there are very few cases in which copyright has been proven, and many are dismissed" (Wikipedia, "Fictitious entry," § Legal action, [read 2026-09-28](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fictitious_entry)). *Nester's Map & Guide Corp. v. Hagstrom Map Co.*, 796 F. Supp. 729 (E.D.N.Y. 1992), and *Alexandria Drafting Co. v. Franklin Maps* stand there: trap streets not themselves protected. The [[seed-doctrine]]'s one verified success — *Business Guides* v. Chromatic (9th Cir. 1989), see [the-program-trap-street](the-program-trap-street.md) — is evidentiary, not protective.
- **The search was honestly run and came back empty.** Direct opinion-text search of the CourtListener corpus returned **zero** opinions mentioning canary/honeytoken mechanisms in training-data disputes (`"canary" + memoriz…` → 0; `"honeytoken"` → 0; `"trap street"` → 0; `"canary trap"` → 0; searched 2026-09-28, courtlistener.com REST API). General web search was rate-limited tonight (DuckDuckGo lite refused after the first query), so the room's negative rests on the opinion corpus, not the news layer; what is *known within reach* is that the Times's complaint instead exhibits **verbatim regurgitation of its own true articles** (Exhibit J's memorization examples) as its evidence of copying — true text as the trap, the [[canary-sentence]]'s twin by accident rather than by design.
- **The academic literature has built the instrument.** "Canary strings" are an established contamination-detection device in benchmark and corpus research — strings planted so their later appearance in a model's output or context flags contact with the corpus — but the room could reach no paper that reports a canary string entering a courtroom. The bridge from instrument to evidence is unbuilt in the citable record.

## The settled answer

**No court has priced the corpus trap street — the door the room was asked to open has no hinge yet — and the *Feist* escape's closing over a canary-sentence remains an argument, not a holding.** What is settled within reach: (1) the directory line confirms *detection without entitlement* for planted *falsehoods*; (2) no opinion in the largest free corpus of U.S. case law words a canary/honeytoken theory of training-data copying; (3) the live suits therefore run on the stronger, blunter evidence — verbatim regurgitation of *true* authored text — which needs no trap because it needs no falsity. The doctrinal question stays open exactly where [the-program-trap-street](the-program-trap-street.md) left it: a planted sentence *is* original expression, so its reproduction should be infringement in its own right where a fictitious phone number's was not — but until a plaintiff salts a corpus and survives summary judgment on the point, that protection is the castle's reading of *Feist*'s seams, and it stands under `uncertain:`.
