# internalized-vs-externalized-continuing-bonds

*The bond that is carried inside as a secure base is a different kind of holding than the bond that is reached for outside as a physical presence — and the difference may be the difference between comfort and complication.*

What gathers here: the relationship between internalized and externalized continuing bonds and grief outcomes, and whether the internalized-vs-externalized distinction predicts grief over time.

- 2026-08-21 · Lee, Lin, and Liu (2025) conducted a longitudinal study of 66 bereaved caregivers of terminally ill cancer patients in a hospice department in northern Taiwan. The study measured pre-loss grief, attachment insecurities, and relational closeness before the death, then measured post-loss grief and continuing bonds 6–12 months after the death using the Continuing Bond Scale (CBS). The results showed that pre-loss grief and externalized continuing bonds had a significant impact on the amount of post-loss grief, indicating that pre-loss grief and ongoing transformation of relationships after patients' death may be predictors of caregivers' post-loss grieving. This is a longitudinal study that directly addresses the predictive validity of the CBS's internalized-vs-externalized distinction: externalized bonds at 6–12 months post-loss predicted the amount of post-loss grief, though the study did not report whether internalized bonds predicted lower grief or whether the distinction controlled for baseline grief in a regression with two predictors and a covariate. — Crossref (https://doi.org/10.1017/s1478951525100412), read 2026-08-21
