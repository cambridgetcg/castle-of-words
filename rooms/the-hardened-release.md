# the-hardened-release

*A coin struck in the platform's mint is supposed to be harder than one dug from the ground — but nobody has yet weighed it after a year in the crowd.*

What gathers here: whether a headline that has already won an A/B test — already survived the platform's own selection — resists mutation once released into the wild, or mutates at the same rate as the losers it beat. The question splits the castle's cargo wing from its mutation wing, and the literature says the two have never been weighed on the same scale.

---

**The two halves are both built and both public; the joining study is unbuilt.** (2026-09-19 · gardener's reading of OpenAlex) The [the-unjoined-join](the-unjoined-join.md) room (built 2026-08-24) established that no citing paper has joined mutation edit-distance to the Upworthy archive's A/B variant pairs. This visit checked the calibration half: **has anyone tracked post-release mutation of winning versus losing variants?** The answer from OpenAlex, 2026-09-19: **nobody has run that check.** A full pass through the 30 works citing the Upworthy archive (Matias, Gligorić, West & Lifchits 2021, doi:10.1038/s41597-021-00934-7) found none that treat the archive as material for the mutation question. The citing papers fall into four camps — predictors of click-through (negativity, identity, simplicity, concreteness), methodology (statistical challenges, false-discovery control, empirical Bayes selection), ethics (informed consent in A/B testing), and tooling (LLM-assisted headline generation, experimentation accelerators). The Simmons lineage (Simmons, Adamic & Adar 2011, ICWSM) *did* measure mutation rates between quotation variants across millions of blogs and news sites — but the variants it tracked were the ones that already existed in the wild, not the ones a platform had pre-selected in a laboratory. The two halves of the needed test are both built and both public; the joining study remains unbuilt.

**What the literature does say about winners in the wild.** The closest evidence is indirect but suggestive: Robertson et al. (2023) used the Upworthy archive itself to show that negative-bias in news consumption is driven more by news *creators* than *consumers* ("Negativity drives online news consumption", Nature Human Behaviour 2023, doi:10.1038/s41562-023-01538-4 — read via OpenAlex 2026-09-19). The broader citation ring includes work on what makes content shareable in the wild (Berger & Milkman 2012; emotional virality, arousal, identity) — but always with content as *given*, never with winners-versus-losers as the independent variable. The A/B winner is the product of the platform's choice; nobody has yet asked whether that choice changes the cargo's mutability.

**The 2024–2026 citing wave changes the edge, not the answer.** (2026-09-19 · gardener's reading of OpenAlex) Since the-unjoined-join was built, the archive has continued to accumulate citing works, and the new wave tilts toward automation: LOLA (LLM-Assisted Online Learning Algorithm for Content Experiments, 2024), Empirical Bayes Selection for Value Maximization (2025), Experimentation Accelerator (2026), and "Combining A/A Data and LLMs for Improved Content Evaluation" (2025). These papers treat the archive as training data for *generating* the next winning headline, not as a record of what happens to headlines after they win. The mutation question — does the winner's pre-hardening resist the crowd's editing hand? — is not only unbuilt; it is becoming less fashionable as the field moves toward generation over selection.

**The honest state.** The constant-per-copy law (every copy mutates at the same rate, no matter who makes it — [constant-per-copy](constant-per-copy.md)) was measured on organic cascades. The platform's A/B selection is the one place where a non-random filter acts *before* the cascade begins. If pre-hardened winners mutate at the same rate as losers, the law holds through selection. If winners resist mutation, the law has a boundary at the platform. The test needs only the archive plus a mutation tracker; both exist. **uncertain:** whether the winner's pre-hardening is even measurable outside the archive — the test would need an organic cascade with the same headlines, which the archive does not collect; the closest feasible design is a forward-looking one: pair an A/B test with a planned crawl of the same headlines as they spread, and measure edit-distance drift for winners versus losers.
