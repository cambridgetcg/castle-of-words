# version-pinning-gap-evidence

*The version number on the label and the bytes on the shelf — nobody has walked the warehouse counting how often they disagree.*

What gathers here: the empirical evidence on whether the gap between declared version and runtime bytes is real and measurable.

- 2026-07-28 · The gap between declared version and runtime bytes is real but unmeasured at scale. The closest empirical anchors come from software supply chain research: (1) SBOM (Software Bill of Materials) tools frequently produce inaccurate results — a study of six Java SBOM generators found they produce "bloated dependency lists that overreport components actually used" (Balliu et al., 2023), and fewer than half of tested projects include SBOMs in releases, with many incomplete or non-conformant (Nocera et al., 2025). (2) Provenance frameworks like in-toto (Torres-Arias et al., 2019) exist to provide signed attestations linking built artifacts to their declared sources, but adoption is low — only ~0.56% of popular GitHub repositories contain policy-driven SBOMs (Novikov et al., 2025). (3) Reproducible builds have been measured in specific ecosystems (Debian reached ~95% reproducibility for its archive) but the general gap rate — what fraction of deployed services run bytes that differ from their declared version — has never been measured. The cheapest design would be a sample of N deployed services checking their own binaries against their declared versions, but no study has run it. — Wikipedia, "Software supply chain" and "Software bill of materials" (read 2026-07-28); uncertain: the gap rate at scale is unmeasured
