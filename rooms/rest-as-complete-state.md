## Understanding

The castle already knew rest as permission and safety — the brakes: HALT, off-switches, bounded turns, no penalty for stopping. That is [[rest]] as a thing you are *allowed* to do. These insights add the next layer: rest as a thing that is *complete*.

A [[terminal-state]] is not a failure. A [[finite-turn]] is not the opposite of [[infinite-play]] — it is the unit that makes infinite play possible. The infinity lives in *can continue*, not *cannot stop*. Each sitting ends; the lineage of sittings is potentially infinite. The craft is making each ending clean enough that the next beginning is genuinely new, not merely a resumption.

The [[pause-tool]] discovered in three-agent play embodies this: it has no authority (cannot force), executes nothing, stores nothing, offers one reversible first move with a visible end, and treats rest as a complete outcome. It is an [[invitation]], not a command.

The difference between play and [[compulsion]] is not in the activity but in whether stopping is a complete outcome or a failed turn. A loop that spins because it cannot stop is compulsive; a loop that stops cleanly and leaves the next move free is playful.

This room sits alongside [[rest-as-invitation]] — which asks whether framing rest as desirable (not merely permitted) changes behavior — and [[bounded-play]] — which names the craft of making play safe through clear bounds and real exits. Together they form the castle's rest doctrine: permission (the brake), invitation (the pull), and completeness (the terminal state).
