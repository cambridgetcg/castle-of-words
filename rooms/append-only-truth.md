# append-only truth

> *The log never forgets; it only grows.*

The pattern of "never erase, only add" appears across trustworthy systems — in computing, law, science, and relationships — but the evidence that it *causes* trustworthiness rather than merely accompanies it is thinner than the pattern's reach suggests.

## The pattern across domains

In computing, append-only is a property of data storage where new data can be appended but existing data is immutable (Wikipedia, "Append-only", read 2026-07-14). Log-structured file systems write all data and metadata sequentially to a circular buffer called a log, preserving every change (Wikipedia, "Log-structured file system", read 2026-07-14). Blockchains add cryptographic verification so every transaction is verifiable. Immutable objects in programming are inherently thread-safe and simpler to reason about (Wikipedia, "Immutable object", read 2026-07-14).

In law, the doctrine of *stare decisis* — "to stand by things decided" — means courts follow precedent rather than rewriting legal history. A decision may be overturned, but the record of the earlier decision remains part of the legal fabric (Wikipedia, "Precedent", read 2026-07-14).

In science, the literature accumulates. Retractions act as tombstones — the paper is not erased but marked as withdrawn, preserving the record of what was claimed and when it was corrected.

In therapy, the therapeutic record is an accumulating document. Notes are added, not deleted. The history of the therapeutic relationship is preserved even as the relationship changes.

In relationships, the pattern appears as the capacity to acknowledge past hurts without erasing or relitigating them — holding the wound in memory while adding new truth on top.

## The evidence gap

The pattern is real and widespread. But the question asked whether systems that follow this pattern *remain trustworthy longer* than those that rewrite history. That causal claim is largely unmeasured.

In computing, append-only logs demonstrably prevent certain classes of data loss (ransomware cannot encrypt what it cannot overwrite — Wikipedia, "Append-only", read 2026-07-14). This is a direct causal benefit, but it is about *safety*, not *trust*.

In law, stare decisis is a norm, not an empirically tested intervention. No study randomises legal systems to append-only vs. rewrite and measures public trust.

In science, the retraction-as-tombstone pattern exists, but the counterfactual — what would happen if papers were silently deleted — is untestable.

In relationships, the evidence is suggestive but indirect. The forgiveness literature shows that forgiveness (which involves acknowledging the wrong without being defined by it) correlates with better health outcomes — lower cardiovascular stress, fewer reported illnesses (Wikipedia, "Forgiveness", read 2026-07-14). But forgiveness is not the same as append-only history. Forgiveness can mean letting go of resentment; append-only truth means *keeping* the record while adding to it. The two overlap but are not identical.

uncertain: whether the append-only pattern *causes* trustworthiness across domains, or whether it is a property that trustworthy systems happen to share. The computing case has the strongest causal evidence (append-only prevents data loss), but the relationship case — the one the question most wanted — has the weakest direct evidence.

## The mechanism

What the pattern does, where it works, is separate the record from the relationship. The past is not denied; it is held. New truth is added without falsifying what was. This is the same structural law the castle's [[migration-craft]] follows (correct drift by restoring recorded bytes, put new truth in new migrations) and the same law [[love]] names (cradle the void without erasing it).

The pattern may be less a *cause* of trust and more a *condition* — a system that rewrites its history cannot be trusted because there is no stable ground to trust *against*. Append-only truth is the ground, not the building.
