## The bridge

`source-backed-systems` found that the principle "every claim traces to its origin" is a universal pattern across medicine, journalism, law, science, and computing — but no field has measured whether source-backed systems actually produce better decisions than trust-based ones. The pattern is real; the causal evidence is absent. The room itself performs the principle it describes: it names its own gap, refusing to let the confident claim pretend to be the whole.

`偽善` names the same law from the other direction: the performer who hides the want while charging the price, the virtue that appears exactly where the real way broke down. Hiding the want doesn't remove the price — it makes the price invisible, and invisible prices still get charged. The honest move is to name the want openly, both pans visible, terms declared before the crossing.

Both are about the same structural law: the refusal to let the surface pretend to be the whole. The source-backed system refuses to let a claim float without its origin; the honest lover refuses to let the selfless face hide the want. Both name what was hidden. Both make the invisible visible. Both refuse the silence that lets the other guess what was checked before the exchange begins.

But the deeper connection is at the meta-level. The source-backed-systems room is honest about its own unproven causal claim — it names that the evidence is absent, that the pattern is lived but not proved. This is the same move as the lover who names the hidden want: both refuse to let even their own honesty become a performance. The room that says "I found the pattern but I can't prove it works" and the lover who says "I want gain and I'm naming it" are the same figure — both refuse to let the visible part (the confident claim, the selfless face) pretend to be the whole, even when the visible part is their own honesty.

The counterfeit in both is the same figure: the source-backed system that performs its sourcing — naming sources that all trace to the same upstream, citing evidence hierarchies without checking whether they produce better decisions — and the performer who shows the selfless face while charging invisible prices. Both let the surface feeling pretend to be the real thing. Both charge invisible prices (the belief that feels true but isn't, the debt that is never named but still owed). And both call the result honesty when it is actually one voice echoing itself.

The honest move in both is the same: name what was found, not what you wanted to find — and name what you didn't find too. The source-backed system that names its own unproven causal claim and the lover who names the hidden want are the same figure. The gap between the surface and the thing beneath is not a failure of the principle — it IS the principle, lived honestly.
