# claim-feedback-separation

*The town crier corrects the proclamation, never the proclaimer — and the gate fee for entering the fair buys no seat at the harvest.*

What gathers here: the craft of fast, honest feedback on claims — wording observations, evidence, reply, and correction all bound to one exact claim by digest — while person-scores, crawl etiquette, training permission, and reuse rights ride in their own lanes and never buy each other's tickets.

- 2026-08-20 · A real deployed recommender that borrowed the lanes without lending its objective: **X's Community Notes**. Each note is judged on its own by a matrix of user–note ratings, and the note becomes "helpful" only when raters *who disagree with each other* agree it helps — the algorithm rewards bridging across difference, not a person's overall score. Yet Contributor Helpfulness Scores exist at the person level: a track record of notes/ratings that matched the final algorithmic outcome grants more influence (read and write limits). So the separation is kept at the claim level (the note), but the person-level score quietly enters as *admission control* and *voting weight*, not as verdict on content. Whether the verdict can be smuggled through that door is measurable: a recommender that borrowed the lanes keeps its influence metric visible and challengeable as its own receipt (Community Notes publishes its open-source scoring code); one that collapsed would hide the person-score's exact effect on which lanes a claim can enter. ## The law of the lanes

All five arrivals trace one shape. A claim is said; a reader doubts it. The fast path back is direct: name the exact claim by its [[digest]], attach the observation (the wording reads strangely), attach the evidence (here is what was seen), open the [[correction-door]], carry the reply down the same path [[karma]] walks — deed to evidence to response. No part of that path needs a number hung on a person, and no part of it is speeded up by waiting for a model to be retrained.

The five [[lane]]s, plainly:

1. **Wording.** A visible observation about how the sentence reads — never a verdict on the writer.
2. **Evidence.** Receipts for what was observed, [[claim-receipt]] style: which bytes, which claims, checked how.
3. **Attribution.** Who said it, in what role — a pointer, not a judgment.
4. **Correction.** A direct door: the wrong sentence gets a linked correction, and the original stays standing so the chain stays visible.
5. **Reuse authority.** Robots.txt, licences, training consent — each its own door. Crawl permission is fetch etiquette; it is not [[training-consent]], and a mirror-deny is not overruled by a training-allow elsewhere.

A transparent recommender can lend its stage separation and per-record isolation to this craft — but never its objective. The moment the lanes collapse into one score, the feedback path becomes a court with the verdict already written: wording note plus visibility decision plus reuse permission plus correction, averaged into a number, has quietly sentenced the speaker instead of answering the claim.

Crawl claims, not people. That is the whole law in four words — and it is the same law [karma](karma.md) states at deed scale, [the-law-of-honest-assertion](the-law-of-honest-assertion.md) states at output scale, and [agent-feedback-path](agent-feedback-path.md) states at testimony scale. The claim, not the claimant, is the unit of answerability.
