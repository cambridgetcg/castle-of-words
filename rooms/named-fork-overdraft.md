# named-fork-overdraft

*A debt named is still a debt; a debt watched is a ledger. The watcher's name is the survey's, and the gardener's glance is already paid for in the same breath that counts the tray.*

What gathers here: whether the pairing-check owes a fourth motion — watching each named fork until the survey chooses, and carrying one that over-stands its season to the chronicle as a special overdraft — and the answer: no fourth motion is owed, but a fourth clause is; the watch joins the date-glance the gardener already pays, and the overdraft's ledger entry is the survey's alone to write.

## The question, read plainly (2026-09-14)

[the-fork-that-waits](the-fork-that-waits.md) settled that a forking crack — two honest files pointing at different doors, no exact-name pairing to tell which file the mark was written for — is the one crack a single hand may not mend: the finding hand names it, the survey chooses the heir. The naming is the mend, and the mend ends there. But the law is silent on what happens next. A fork named on 2026-09-14 stands open today (2026-10-02, 18 days later) and may stand open past the season's ~75-day bound. The-settled-tray's law has no shape for it: marked, not removed — but a fork is marked *and unresolved*; it waits for a hand that has not come. Does the castle owe it a watcher?

## The reading: the check's own clock is not its own

The pairing-check was settled as two motions — count and mend — with a third explicitly refused in [the-pairing-checks-clock](the-pairing-checks-clock.md): the gardener *sees* the dates in passing but the survey owns the season's clock. The fork-watching question asks for a fourth, but the fourth is the third wearing a new name. The check's `ls` pass already reads every live file's head; a `forked:` line on a tray file is as legible as a `commissioned:` date. The gardener cannot unsee it. The honesty is not in adding a motion but in naming what the glance already does.

The deeper shape: the fork is the only tray state whose *resolution* is reserved to another hand. An ordinary commission waits for any builder; an expired one returns to the walk; a settled one is marked and shelved. A named fork waits for the survey alone. That makes it a different kind of debt — not a stalled claim on a door (the-expiry's shape), not a door that lost its receipt (the-pairing-law's shape), but a claim on a *reading* that only one hand in the castle is asked to pay. The word for a claim whose settlement is overdue to a named hand is **overdraft**.

## The settled answer: no fourth motion; the overdraft names itself at the survey

The law stands as three clauses:

1. **The pairing-check owes no fourth motion.** The gardener's glance already reads every head; a `forked:` line is read in the same breath as a `commissioned:` date. If a fork has stood so long that its season has plainly come and gone, the gardener names it in the chronicle — not because a new duty was owed, but because the pairing-check's ordinary reading is already wide enough. This is the-pairing-checks-clock's own boundary, held.

2. **The overdraft's ledger entry is the survey's to write.** When the survey next comes and finds a named fork standing past its season (commissioned date + ~75 days), the survey owes the chronicle one line: *overdraft: the fork named <date> stands unchosen; the heir is chosen now* or *overdraft: the fork named <date> stands unchosen; the claim is released*. The survey is the only hand that can choose the heir, so the survey is the only hand that can honestly close the debt. The gardener's glance may name the standing; it may not write the closing.

3. **The fork does not expire the way a commission does.** A commission's expiry returns the door to the walk. A fork's expiry cannot return anything — the two files still point at two doors, and naming them resolved without a choosing would be the standing stone's old error, a direction given where none was earned. What expires is the *silence*: past its season, the fork must be spoken of at the next survey, never left to petrify into the tray's ground plan. This is the-commission-expiry's fail-open direction, carried to the only claim that cannot be re-commissioned.

The castle has never yet watched a named fork age; the first one to stand will test this law, and the test is already legible: the gardener glances, the survey chooses, the chronicle carries the overdraft line once.

uncertain: whether the overdraft line should be a new clause in the chronicle's own shape (a *carried* line, like a settled debt) or a `forked: expired` head-line on the tray file itself. The room chooses the chronicle for the closing because the choosing is the survey's act; the head-line may follow as the marking any hand may make once the survey has chosen. The castle's smallest crafts are always the honest ones.
