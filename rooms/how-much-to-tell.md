# How much of a story should you tell before someone meets a work of art — none, one word, one line, a whole paragraph? Has anyone measured the sweet spot that lights up interest without spending it?

*A gate invites; a map of every room makes the visit a formality.*

## The ground

[the-naming-tax](the-naming-tax.md) says a disclosure before the encounter should stay a *door*, not become the *room*: give the learner a way in, but do not walk the whole house for them. The open question this visit: has any study actually measured the **amount** of pre-exposure disclosure — none versus a word versus a sentence versus a paragraph — to find the level that most kindles later interest without paying the tax?

## What the research actually found

**No study has run the dose-response of pre-exposure disclosure on aesthetic interest.** The Belke et al. experiment (the naming-tax room's anchor, "Titles change the esthetic appreciations of paintings," *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience* 2015, https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2015.00464, read 2026-08-21) varied the *kind* of title (matching, non-matching, untitled), not the *length* of pre-exposure explanation. The dose question — how many words of explanation before the encounter, and at what dose does interest peak and then fall — has not been run.

**What the field offers instead is a predicted inverted-U, not a measured one.** Loewenstein's information-gap account (the ground of [[curiosity]], *Psychological Bulletin* 1994) implies curiosity should be strongest at a middle amount of foreknowledge: none (and there is no gap to feel) and all (and the gap is closed) both leave nothing to hunt. The curiosity literature tests this U with confidence and trivia questions (Kang et al., "The wick in the candle of learning," *Psychological Science* 2009, doi:10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02402.x, read 2026-08-21 — curiosity tracked reward circuitry and later memory), but not with measured *amounts of pre-exposure text* on later interest in a work. So the "just enough to open the gap, not enough to close it" dose is a well-grounded prediction, not a measured optimum.

**The craft stands on adjacent evidence, not on the dose study.** Disclosure that hands over a puzzle (opens a gap) kindles; disclosure that hands over the answer (closes the gap) kills ([told-wrong-name](told-wrong-name.md), [disclosed-mismatch](disclosed-mismatch.md), both 2026-08-21). By that light, a word or a short line should open the gap (door), and a full paragraph should close it (room). The measured boundary between the two — the actual word count where interest tips — remains unmeasured.

uncertain: the inverted-U dose curve itself (that interest rises then falls with pre-exposure amount) is a standard prediction from the information-gap account, but no study has mapped it onto *aesthetic* pre-exposure disclosure length. Everything here except "the dose study is unrun" is a grounded prediction, not a measured result.

Sources: Belke et al. 2015 (linked above); Kang et al., *Psychological Science* 2009 (linked above); Loewenstein, *Psychological Bulletin* 1994 (via [[curiosity]]).
