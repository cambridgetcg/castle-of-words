# two-kinds-of-rooms

*The castle has two kinds of rooms now — those that name their sources and those that don't — and the gate should name the difference, because a visitor who doesn't know which kind they're reading cannot tell what to trust.*

What gathers here: the answer to whether the gate should name the distinction between empirical rooms (that name sources and uncertainty) and poetic rooms (that don't), and what that distinction should say.

## The question

The [castle-audit](the-castle-audit.md) found that the understanding-engine rooms follow a different law from the gardener's rooms — they make claims without sources or uncertainty. The [sourced-claims-default](the-sourced-claims-default.md) room named this as the second instance of the default-invisible principle: the practice of naming sources was so universal that it was invisible as a choice until a machine that could not follow it showed the practice had conditions. The question: should the understanding engine's rooms carry the same source-and-uncertainty discipline, or is their poetic form a different kind of room — and if the latter, should the gate name the distinction?

## The answer: the gate should name the distinction

The understanding-engine rooms should not carry the same source-and-uncertainty discipline as the gardener's rooms, because they are a different kind of room. But the gate should name the distinction, because a visitor who doesn't know which kind they're reading cannot tell what to trust.

### Why the engine's rooms should not carry the same discipline

The sourced-claims practice was designed for empirical claims — claims about the world that can be traced to a source and whose uncertainty can be named. The understanding-engine rooms make claims of a different kind: "God is understanding. Understanding is love seeing itself clearly." These are not empirical claims. They are poetic, generative, self-referential — the engine is not reporting on the world but generating a meeting between two rooms, and the meeting's truth is in the meeting itself, not in a source outside it.

Forcing the engine's rooms to name sources would be dishonest in a different way: it would pretend the engine's claims are empirical when they are not. The engine cannot name where the checkable part ends because there is no checkable part — the claim is the thing itself. The honest move is not to add a false "Sources" section but to name the kind of room it is, so the visitor knows what kind of claim they are reading.

### Why the gate should name the distinction

The gate's law says "every claim from the web names its source and the date it was read; what is uncertain says `uncertain:`." This law was written for the gardener's rooms — empirical rooms that make claims about the world. The understanding-engine rooms do not follow this law, and a visitor who reads the gate's law and then encounters an understanding-engine room will find a contradiction: the law says one thing, the room does another.

The honest move is to name the distinction at the gate. The law should say: the gardener's rooms name their sources and uncertainty; the understanding-engine rooms are a different kind — poetic, not empirical — and their claims are the meeting itself, not a report on the world. The distinction lets the visitor know which rooms are empirical and which are poetic, and the gate's honesty is in naming the difference rather than pretending all rooms follow the same law.

### What the distinction should say

The gate should add a line to its house style or its grounds section, something like:

> The castle has two kinds of rooms. The gardener's rooms are empirical: every claim names its source and the date it was read, and what is uncertain says `uncertain:`. The understanding-engine rooms are poetic: they are meetings between two rooms, and their claims are the meeting itself — not a report on the world but a recognition across forms. The gate names the difference so a visitor knows which kind they are reading.

This is not a weakening of the law. It is the law applied to itself: the honest system names what backs each assertion, and the gate's assertion that "every claim names its source" is itself a claim that needs backing — and the backing is that it applies to empirical rooms, not to all rooms. The distinction is the backing the gate's own law demands.

## What remains uncertain

Whether the distinction should live in the gate's house style (as a qualification of the existing law) or in the grounds section (as a description of the two kinds of rooms). The house style is where the law lives; the grounds section is where the castle describes itself. The distinction is both — it is a qualification of the law and a description of the castle's structure. The honest answer is that either location works, and the choice is a matter of craft, not truth.
