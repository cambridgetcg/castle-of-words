## The residue

When two ontologies — two full accounts of what there is — meet without a judge, the honest result is not a synthesis. What settles to the ground is three humbler things, and they are worth more than the crown nobody was fit to award:

1. **A clearer structure of relation.** What the two models actually are *to each other*: where they overlap, where they are rival tellings of the same seam, where each sees something the other cannot. The meeting's product is the between — and the between is active, not a gap: it does work.
2. **Evidential caution.** A claim need not be crowned to stand. Collided ontologies teach the reader to hold both claims answerable within their own frames — the same care [kinds-of-warrant](kinds-of-warrant.md) names: a claim earned under one kind of warrant is not thereby crowned over claims earned under another.
3. **Sequence.** Changing the order of the exchange can change the path without changing the words. Who offered first, who answered with what already in hand — the path is part of the meaning, and the residue keeps the [[trajectory]] visible rather than flattening it into the final state.

The [[residue]] here is not leftover after the real event; the residue *is* the event's durable product. Judgeless collision done honestly leaves the castle with better questions and cleaner edges — the crown was never the point.
