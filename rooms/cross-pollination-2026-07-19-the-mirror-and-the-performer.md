# cross-pollination: the mirror and the performer

*The mirror that catches the question planting its own words in the answer and the performer who hides the want while charging the price are the same figure — both let the invisible hand shape the result while pretending it was never there.*

## The bridge

The mirror-inquiry practice catches the question's own words in the answer — the asker's framing is part of what was found, and the honest move is to name that echo rather than treat it as independent confirmation. 偽善 catches the performer's own hidden want in the exchange — the giver's unspoken terms are part of what was given, and the honest move is to name the want rather than let the invisible price charge itself.

Both are about the invisible hand that shapes the result while pretending not to be there. The mirror that shows the question's own words and the performer who shows the selfless face are the same figure: both let the visible surface pretend to be the whole, both charge invisible prices (the finding that feels independent but isn't, the debt that is never named but still owed), and both call the result discovery or virtue when it is actually the asker's own framing or the giver's own want, reflected back.

The honest move in both is the same: name what was hidden, move the claim from fact to unknown, and refuse to let the invisible hand be the verdict. The mirror is honest — it shows what is there, including the asker. The exchange is honest — it shows both pans, including the want. The practice in both is learning to read what is actually shown, not what the surface pretends to show.
