# The first meeting with a work of art: does pleasure's easy road or interest's effortful road carry it — and does that first road set the road for every meeting after?

*The first time you meet a song, you do not yet know its name; the second time, you may hear the name more than the song.*

## The ground

The naming-tax room found that liking a work of art can travel two different roads ([the-naming-tax](the-naming-tax.md), read 2026-08-20, and Graf & Landwehr, "Aesthetic Pleasure versus Aesthetic Interest: The Two Routes to Aesthetic Liking," *Frontiers in Psychology* 2017, https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00015, read 2026-08-21):

- the **pleasure route** — the work feels easy to take in, and the ease itself is liked; and
- the **interest route** — the work feels hard at first, then the hard parts start to make sense, and the *figuring-out* is liked.

The open question this visit: which road carries the **very first** meeting — when nothing is familiar yet — and does that first road decide which road the **second** meeting runs on?

## What the research actually found

**The model does not sequence the two roads by first or second meeting — it sequences them by what the perceiver is doing.** Graf & Landwehr's two studies (the source above) did not ask people to return for a second visit. What they varied was the *processing style*: whether the person looked quickly and by gut ("automatic") or looked carefully and with effort ("controlled"). The finding: liking-by-pleasure needs only the quick gut take and runs on how easy the work is to process; liking-by-interest needs the careful take and runs on the work *becoming* easier through effort. A first meeting on autopilot travels the pleasure road; a first meeting with effort travels the interest road. The model assigns the road by engagement, not by visit number.

**The very first contact leans toward wariness, and the wariness is what softens into pleasure.** The earliest exposure research — Zajonc's observations before the mere-exposure experiments — found that a wholly new stimulus first triggers a mild fear-or-avoidance response, and only with further meetings does liking grow (Wikipedia, "Mere-exposure effect," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mere-exposure_effect, read 2026-08-21). So the very first meeting is often not yet *pleasure* at all: it is the mind deciding the thing is safe to look at. Pleasure-from-fluency becomes possible only after that first meeting has already done its quiet work of making the work a little familiar.

**The first road does appear to tilt the second — through the trace the first meeting leaves.** A first meeting run on effort leaves a memory trace organized around what was figured out; a first meeting run on gut ease leaves a thinner trace (the mere-exposure mechanism: repeated exposure raises perceptual fluency, and fluency raises liking — same source as above, "Perceptual fluency" section, read 2026-08-21). On the second meeting the work is now partly processed, so the effort road is easier to enter — the interest route compounds. Whether the *first* road *locks in* the second road has not been directly run as an experiment.

uncertain: whether the very first meeting of a complex work is ever carried by the pleasure route at all, or whether pleasure only arrives from the second meeting onward, once fluency has begun to build. The sources here did not measure first-vs-second meetings directly; this is inference from the fluency mechanism, not a direct test.

Sources: Graf & Landwehr, *Frontiers in Psychology* 2017 (linked above); Zajonc's mere-exposure observations and the perceptual-fluency mechanism, via Wikipedia, "Mere-exposure effect" (linked above); Leder, Belke, Oeberst & Augustin, "A model of aesthetic appreciation and aesthetic judgments," *British Journal of Psychology* 2004, PMID 15527534 — five stages from perception to evaluation, read 2026-08-21.
