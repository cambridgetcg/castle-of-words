# The Light Cargo

*To know which way the river bends a leaf, drop one where no one is sweeping.*

[[the-direction-of-the-edits]] found that mutated variants of conspiracy
claims (Cheng, Quelle & Hale 2026) and misinformation (Yan, Lin & Chung 2022)
survive longer — but both rode [[dark-cargo]]: hunted text, where every edit
is partly an act of [[evasion-vs-memory|evasion as well as memory]]. This
room asks the door that room opened: **has anyone run the variant-survival
regression on neutral or pro-social cascades — health advice, chain
blessings, ordinary memes — where a mutation that lengthens life cannot be
suspected of dodging a filter?** And if the memorability lean survives there,
do evasion and memory blow the same way or part? Read 2026-08-24.

**The answer, in one breath.** Not in cascade form — but one large clean
reading on light cargo exists, and it leans *the same way* the dark-cargo
studies did. Nothing hunted has yet been followed down an organic copy chain
with survival regressed on linguistic features; the closest ground is the
largest controlled text-variation experiment ever published, and on that
ground negativity, length, specificity and pointed reference all extended
life — the same psychological-markedness pattern the conspiracy cascade
showed. The [[the-light-cargo-test|light-cargo test]] as the door wrote it
(pure organic chains, word-level memorability norms) is still unrun, but the
nearest sibling has already answered in the same direction.

**No organic copy-chain study on light cargo.** The two lineages that would
have run it stopped one step short. Simmons, Adamic & Adar (2011) precisely
catalogued *how* quoted text mutates as it crosses news sites and blogs at
millions of copies — insertion, deletion, substitution patterns, variant
appearance rates — but modeled the transformation process, never regressing
a variant's survival on psycholinguistic features ("Memes Online: Extracted,
Subtracted, Injected, and Recollected", ICWSM 2011; OpenAlex record
W76678209 lists the year as 2021 in the proceedings index — read via
OpenAlex 2026-08-24). Omodei, Poibeau & Cointet (2012) traced how quotation
mutations shape families and sub-families, and how mutation rates depend on
properties of the quotation — morphology, again not survival
("Multi-level Modeling of Quotation Families Morphogenesis", SocialCom/
PASSAT 2012, doi:10.1109/socialcom-passat.2012.114 — read via OpenAlex
2026-08-24). Bordogna & Pasi (2012) defined four measures for e-meme
instances — replication fidelity, mutation, spread, longevity — which is
the door's instrument panel exactly, but the proposed measures were not run
against word-level features ("An Approach to Identify Ememes on the
Blogosphere", IEEE/WIC/ACM WI-IAT 2012, doi:10.1109/wi-iat.2012.271 — read
via OpenAlex 2026-08-24). A targeted search of the 83 works citing Simmons
et al. surfaced no neutral-cascade survival regression either.

**The cleanest light-cargo reading that exists: the Upworthy archive.**
Gligorić, Lifchits, West & Anderson (2023) exploited thousands of A/B field
experiments run on Upworthy.com, where professional writers wrote *variant*
headlines for the same article and the platform randomly assigned them —
variant success measured head-to-head under controlled conditions, on
ordinary news: light cargo by construction. The registered reports'
confirmatory results: **negative-emotion words helped** (β = 0.18,
p = 1.6×10⁻¹⁴); **positive-emotion words did not** (no significant effect);
**longer headlines won** (β = 0.07); **specificity beat generality** (the
definite article helped, the indefinite did not); **third-person pronouns
helped** (singular β = 0.22); second-person "you" and readability made no
significant difference. ("Linguistic effects on news headline success:
Evidence from thousands of online field experiments", PLOS ONE 2023,
doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0281682 — full text read 2026-08-24.)

**Same wind or two winds?** The honest comparison: the conspiracy cascade
paid out for mutations in pronouns, cognitive-process words, and risk and
health vocabulary — psychological markedness — and its two recurring shapes
were simplification and assimilation, both memory-friendly. The Upworthy
variants paid out for negative emotion, length, specificity, pointed
reference — also psychological markedness, with a negativity lean
misinformation shares. The simplification family (shorter, more readable)
did *not* pay on Upworthy's light cargo (length helped; readability was
flat) — a fork worth noting: in feeds, shorter may win by being copiable;
in a finished text, longer wins by being informative. unc​ertain: whether
that fork is real or an artifact of comparing organic edits with professional
rewrites — the Upworthy edits were made by staff before publication, not by
cascading hands after it, so no selection-by-copying acted on them. One wind
or two, the memorability lean itself survived the crossing from hunted to
unhunted text.

**Also on the same shelf, not survival but fitness:** Coscia (2014) showed
ordinary Reddit memes succeed at the *periphery* of meme-similarity space —
distinctiveness, a kind of psychological markedness, predicts success where
averageness kills ("Average is Boring: How Similarity Kills a Meme's
Success", Scientific Reports 2014, doi:10.1038/srep06477 — read via OpenAlex
2026-08-24). A different dependent variable (popularity, not variant
survival), neutral cargo, same direction: the marked lives, the bland dies.

**What stays unbuilt.** The pristine cell remains empty: an organic copy
chain (quotations across blogs, health advice across posts, blessings across
feeds) where each mutation's descendant count is regressed on word-level
frequency, concreteness and emotionality norms, with no moderator hunting
any of it. The pieces all exist — the Upworthy archive is downloadable
(10.1038/s41597-021-00934-7), the memorability norms exist, Simmons's
methods exist. The test needs one builder, not one new dataset. (The join of
the archive's variant-pairs to mutation edit-distance — the professional-
rewrite side of the same door — is pursued in [the-unjoined-join](
the-unjoined-join.md), 2026-08-24: both instruments public, no citing paper
has run it.)

**Status.** Settled in direction, open in form: no survival study has yet
ridden an organic light-cargo chain, but the largest controlled reading on
light cargo leans the same way as the dark-cargo studies — psychologically
marked variants live longer, wherever life is measured. Evasion and memory
appear to blow the same way in the readings we have; the study that would
prove them one wind, or part them ([[the-light-cargo-test]]), is buildable
from standing parts and unbuilt.
