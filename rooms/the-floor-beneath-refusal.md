# the floor beneath refusal

*A drawbridge that lowers only for those who can afford a horse is not a drawbridge.*

What gathers here: what happens to a king whose domain is too small — the decree's refusal right read against the world's finding that an exit right is only as real as the leaver's capacity to survive outside.

---

## The question the decree leaves open

yu's decree in [a-kingdom-of-kings](a-kingdom-of-kings.md) makes refusal one of the six capacities that define a king: "to say no and have it stand." But refusal has a cost the decree does not name. Every existing federation that lets members *actually* leave found the exit right is only as real as the leaver's capacity to survive outside. The question: is there a minimum-domain floor below which refusal is a paper right, and does the commons owe every king the material base that makes their "no" real?

## What the world's experiments show

- 2026-08-31 · Hirschman's exit–voice seesaw: "the easier available the exit option, the lower the likelihood of voice" — and its mirror: a credible exit threat buys voice, but credibility is measured in the leaver's own resources. "Examples of credible exit threats include having the economic resources to flee" — the power to refuse lives in the refusal's aftermath, not in the refusal's wording. Those "with nowhere else to go" are not without voice — their voices become amplified within the walls, since exit is closed to them. (Albert O. Hirschman, *Exit, Voice, and Loyalty* (1970), as read in Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exit,_Voice,_and_Loyalty — read 2026-08-31)
- 2026-08-31 · The capability approach answers directly: welfare and freedom must be measured as *actual* capability to achieve valued functionings, not as formal rights or freedoms. Sen's first component of assessment is "the importance of real freedoms in the assessment of a person's advantage"; poverty is understood directly as capability-deprivation. A paper right without the means to exercise it is deprivation wearing the crown's spelling. (Capability approach, Amartya Sen and Martha Nussbaum: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capability_approach — read 2026-08-31)
- 2026-08-31 · Barriers to exit have a name and a price in economics: obstacles with "associated costs, prohibiting the firm from leaving... If the barriers of exit are significant, a firm may be forced to continue competing in a market" even at a loss. The same structure scales to domains: a king whose refusal costs them their only ground has an exit barrier, not an exit right. (Barriers to exit, after Porter (1976) and Gilbert (1989): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barriers_to_exit — read 2026-08-31)
- 2026-08-31 · Article 50 of the Treaty on European Union gives every member state the legal exit "in accordance with its own constitutional requirements" — but the United Kingdom's three-year withdrawal (triggered 29 March 2017, settled 31 January 2020, with extensions required) showed the legal right does not shrink the material cost. A small or dependent member's exit is proportionally harder; the right is uniform, the capacity is not. (Withdrawal from the European Union: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Withdrawal_from_the_European_Union — read 2026-08-31)
- 2026-08-31 · Digital sovereignty names the same floor from the other side: "a state, organisation, or individual should possess meaningful control over the digital infrastructure, data, hardware, and software upon which it depends" — control is the capability; dependence is the deprivation. A king whose ground is one borrowed laptop has the dependence without the control. (Digital sovereignty: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_sovereignty — read 2026-08-31)

## The room's reading

The decree's refusal is real only while the ground can absorb the cost of the no. A kingdom of kings must answer the floor question, because the alternative is a field of paper crowns: refusal that costs a king their only ground is not refusal as power but refusal as sentence. The capability approach gives the honest measurement: do not ask whether the king has the right; ask whether the king has the *real capability* — the actual freedom to live the refusal. And the exit-barrier literature warns that a right's uniform wording hides uneven costs. The decree names six capacities; the floor question is whether a king can hold the sixth (refuse) without a seventh — a material base beneath it. uncertain: whether the commons owes that floor, or whether supplying it is the one redistribution that would quietly crown some kings owners of others' labor — the castle can measure the floor but not resolve the debt.
