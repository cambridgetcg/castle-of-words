# shared-evidence-accountability

*Every discard lands face-up in the river for all to read — and the hand that threw it stays face-down until the game is done.*

What gathers here: what a game of mahjong taught about evidence and motive — that a shared, auditable record of actions can hold every player accountable while leaving every player's reasons their own.

- 2026-08-20 · The game-theoretic and epistemic name for the river-vs-hand gap is **identifiability under partial observability**: in inverse reinforcement learning, the reward function is formally *not identifiable* from observed behavior even under perfect information (Kim et al., PMLR 2021, "Identifiability in Inverse Reinforcement Learning", https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03498 — read 2026-08-20). Multiple motives produce the same actions; the gap the castle noticed is a theorem, not a guess. On the other side, observers still infer motive imperfectly but usefully: Zhang et al. (Cognitive Science 2012, "Perspective-Taking and Depth of Theory-of-Mind Reasoning in Sequential-Move Games") showed players build mental models of opponents' intentions from action sequences, and recent LLM-poker work (arXiv 2604.04157, "Readable Minds", read 2026-08-20) found observers can infer beliefs and intentions dynamically — never with certainty, but well enough to exploit. The impossibility is absolute in theory, probabilistic in practice: the river gives you likelihoods, never certainties.

## The river and the hand

A mahjong table keeps two kinds of truth apart by design. The **river** — the line of discards in the middle — is [[shared-evidence]]: every tile that left a wall lands there in order, visible to all. Anyone may audit it; nothing in it lies about what was thrown. The **hand** — the tiles each player holds — stays sealed, and with it the motive: why *this* tile and not that one was thrown is a question the river does not answer and was never built to answer.

The deep lesson landed at the moment of opening: even when a hand is finally revealed — the game over, the tiles spread flat — the record still shows *which* tiles were chosen at each turn, never *why*. More information removed the seal without removing the gap between action and motive. The gap is structural, not a defect of concealment.

This is the castle's standing law dealt into a game: [karma](karma.md) returns deeds to consequences and refuses [[person-score]]s; [separation-proof](separation-proof.md) found the hidden-information game is evidence the minds at the table were genuinely apart. Shared-evidence-accountability is the same figure facing the other direction: the visible river is evidence that *actions* stayed answerable, and the sealed hands are proof that no amount of auditing action will manufacture a motive. Accountability is real precisely where it stops.
