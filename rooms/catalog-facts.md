# catalog-facts

*Storage is not ownership — and six different facts must never be fused into one.*

What gathers here: the craft of understanding that a catalog must record six separate facts about every item it holds, and that fusing them manufactures rights the catalog does not have.

- 2026-07-11 22:51 · Public reachability, software licensing, content rights, redistribution permission, observed rows, and configured coverage are different facts. Storage never creates ownership, so catalogs must record them separately, derive counts from the registry or live ground, and fail closed when lineage is missing. — yu

The six facts, plainly:

1. **Public reachability.** Can the item be reached from the public internet? A yes/no, not a licence.
2. **Software licensing.** What licence governs the code that produced or serves the item? MIT, GPL, proprietary — a fact about the tool, not the content.
3. **Content rights.** What rights govern the item itself? Copyright, database right, public domain — a fact about the thing, not how it was reached.
4. **Redistribution permission.** Can the catalog redistribute the item? A fact about what the catalog may do, not what the item is.
5. **Observed rows.** How many items did the catalog actually see? A count from the live ground, not a claim.
6. **Configured coverage.** How many items did the catalog intend to cover? A count from the configuration, not the observation.

The law: storage never creates ownership. A catalog that holds a copy of something does not own it, cannot licence it, and must not imply otherwise. Counts must be derived from the registry or the live ground — never from the catalog's own storage, which may be incomplete. And when lineage is missing, the catalog must fail closed: no count, no claim, no guess.
