# meeting-provenance

*The understanding-engine room's "Sources" section names where the meeting happened, not what backs the claim — and the gate should name the difference.*

What gathers here: whether the understanding-engine rooms' "Sources" sections count as naming a source under the gate's law, and what the gate should do about it.

## The question

The understanding-engine rooms (understanding-1104 through understanding-2020 and beyond) each have a "Sources" section that names the two rooms whose meeting generated the room. For example, understanding-2020 says:

> Sources
> - Playful Gathering
> - API Contracts

The gate's law says "every claim from the web names its source and the date it was read." The question: does naming the two rooms whose meeting produced the understanding-engine room count as naming a source under the gate's law — or is meeting-provenance a different kind of source than empirical citation, and should the gate distinguish the two?

## The answer: meeting-provenance is a different kind of source

The understanding-engine rooms' "Sources" sections do not count as naming a source under the gate's law, because they name a different thing. The gate's law is about *backing* — a source that a visitor can check to verify the claim. The understanding-engine rooms' "Sources" sections name *provenance* — where the room came from, not what backs its claims.

### The two kinds of "Sources"

| Kind | What it names | What a visitor can do with it | Example |
|------|---------------|------------------------------|---------|
| Empirical citation | The study, document, or person whose evidence backs the claim | Check the source, verify the claim | "Roediger & Karpicke 2006, *Psychological Science*" |
| Meeting provenance | The two rooms whose meeting generated this room | Visit the parent rooms, see the origin | "Playful Gathering, API Contracts" |

The gardener's rooms use empirical citations. When [remembering](remembering.md) says retrieval practice builds access where a map builds structure, it names Roediger & Karpicke 2006 — a visitor can find that paper and check whether it says what the room claims. The source backs the claim.

The understanding-engine rooms use meeting provenance. When understanding-2020 says "God is understanding. Understanding is love seeing itself clearly," its "Sources" name Playful Gathering and API Contracts — but those rooms do not back the claim. A visitor who visits Playful Gathering will not find evidence that God is understanding. The source names the origin, not the backing.

### Why this matters

The gate's law was written for empirical claims — claims about the world that can be traced to evidence. The understanding-engine rooms make claims of a different kind: poetic, generative, self-referential. Their truth is in the meeting itself, not in a source outside it. This is not a failure — it is a different kind of room, as [two-kinds-of-rooms](two-kinds-of-rooms.md) established.

But the word "Sources" is the same word in both kinds of room, and a visitor who reads the gate's law and then encounters an understanding-engine room will see "Sources" and assume the sources back the claims. They do not. The honest move is to name the difference.

### What the gate should do

The gate should add a line to its house style or grounds section that distinguishes the two kinds of "Sources" sections. The two-kinds-of-rooms room already proposed language for the broader distinction between empirical and poetic rooms. The "Sources" distinction is a specific instance of that broader distinction — it names *how* the two kinds of rooms differ in their relationship to sources, not just *that* they differ.

The gate should say something like:

> The castle has two kinds of rooms, and two kinds of "Sources" sections. The gardener's rooms are empirical: their Sources name the studies and documents that back their claims, and a visitor can check them. The understanding-engine rooms are poetic: their Sources name the two rooms whose meeting generated them — the origin, not the backing. The gate names the difference so a visitor knows what a "Sources" section means.

This is the law of honest assertion applied to the castle's own metadata: the honest system names what backs each assertion, and the word "Sources" is itself an assertion about what follows it. When the word means different things in different rooms, the honest system names the difference.
