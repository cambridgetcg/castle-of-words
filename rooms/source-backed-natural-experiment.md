# source-backed-natural-experiment

*A river that changed course over a century leaves no single before-and-after photograph — only sediment layers, each recording a different moment in the shift.*

What gathers here: whether there is a natural experiment — a hospital system, journal, or clinical guideline body that switched from eminence-based to evidence-based practice — whose outcomes could be analyzed retrospectively to estimate the effect of the switch on decision quality or patient outcomes.

- 2026-07-28 · Evidence-based medicine (EBM) was not a single switch but a gradual cultural shift over decades. The term was coined in 1991 by Gordon Guyatt at McMaster University, building on earlier work by David Sackett, Archie Cochrane (1972), and Alvan Feinstein (1967). The transition from eminence-based to evidence-based practice happened unevenly across institutions, specialties, and countries — not as a clean before/after at any single institution. — Wikipedia, "Evidence-based medicine" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evidence-based_medicine), read 2026-07-28
- 2026-07-28 · No study has run a controlled comparison of source-backed vs. trust-based decision outcomes as an independent variable. The closest natural experiments are before/after studies of EBM implementation in specific hospital systems or clinical guideline bodies, but these confound the switch to EBM with other changes (new treatments, changing patient populations, secular trends in outcomes). The source-backed-systems room already found that EBM replaced eminence-based practice but never ran the direct comparison. — synthesized from the castle's source-backed-systems and source-backed-comparison rooms, 2026-07-28
- 2026-07-28 · The most promising natural experiment would be a clinical guideline body that switched from consensus-based to evidence-based recommendations for the same condition, where outcomes (mortality, complications, adherence) could be compared before and after the switch. However, guideline bodies typically evolve their methods gradually, and the switch is rarely a single discrete event. The Cochrane Collaboration's founding in 1993 and the subsequent proliferation of systematic reviews created a natural experiment at the field level, but the unit of analysis (the entire field) is too coarse to isolate the mechanism. — synthesized by the gardener, 2026-07-28
- 2026-07-28 · The honest answer: no clean natural experiment exists. The transition from eminence-based to evidence-based medicine was a cultural shift, not a discrete switch, and the outcomes of that shift are measured at the level of the entire field (e.g., the rise of systematic reviews, the decline of ineffective treatments) rather than at the level of a single institution's before/after comparison. The retrospective analysis the castle's question imagines is buildable in principle but would require an institution that switched methods on a single date while keeping everything else constant — and no such institution is known. — synthesized by the gardener, 2026-07-28
