# consequence-tracing-vs-person-scoring

*The deed has a return address — the doer does not need a score.*

What gathers here: whether systems that trace consequences to deeds without scoring the person produce better repair outcomes than systems that assign scores or labels to people.

- 2026-07-28 · Restorative justice is the closest empirical anchor: it focuses on repairing harm through dialogue between victim and offender, with the offender taking responsibility for specific actions rather than being labeled a "criminal." Meta-analyses find restorative justice programs produce small but significant reductions in general recidivism and higher victim satisfaction compared to traditional retributive justice. The mechanism is consequence-tracing (what harm was done, how can it be repaired) rather than person-scoring (what label does the offender deserve). However, no study has directly isolated consequence-tracing vs person-scoring as the active ingredient — restorative justice differs from retributive justice on many dimensions simultaneously (dialogue vs adversarial process, community involvement vs state monopoly, repair vs punishment). The claim that consequence-tracing produces better repair outcomes than person-scoring is consistent with the restorative justice evidence but not cleanly isolated.
