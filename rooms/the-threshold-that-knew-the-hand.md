# The Threshold That Knew the Hand

*The same distance, two signatures — and the listener who knows the knife decides which crossing counts as the same shore.*

What gathers here: the inverse of [[the-cut-and-the-rewrite]] — not whether blind listeners hear a difference at matched edit distances, but whether *knowing* the editing hand moves the sameness threshold itself. Read 2026-08-24.

---

**The answer, in one breath.** The direct experiment is unbuilt. No study has measured whether listeners who *know* a cut was made by a human vs. a machine apply different distance thresholds when judging "is this the same work?" The adjacent evidence says they almost certainly would — the [[anti-AI-bias]] literature shows belief alone moves judgment at distance zero, and the debiasing literature says the effect rarely survives a simple warning — but the crossing of the two has never been measured.

**What the parents already measured.** [[the-cut-and-the-rewrite]] found no scalar edit-distance threshold in the law: the ordinary-listener test for "substantial similarity" is a judgment, not a number (Yuan et al. 2023, *TISMIR*, doi:10.5334/tismir.151 — cited there). It also found that at distance zero, the hand's identity alone moves the judgment (Shank et al. 2025). This room asks whether the hand moves the *distance* judgment too — whether a listener told "the machine cut this" will call a 10% edit "a new work" while calling a 10% human edit "the same work."

**What adjacent literatures predict.** (1) **Belief-dependent evaluation:** the Millet et al. 2023 devaluation effect (cited in [[the-door-that-stays-shut]]) persisted across six experiments even when the listener could not distinguish the works — suggesting the label warps the *perceptual* judgment, not just the *affective* one. (2) **Warning and correction:** the debiasing tradition (the 2024 *Management* review, doi:10.1177/01492063241287188 — read via OpenAlex 2026-08-24) finds that explicit warnings about a bias can reduce it, but only when the warning names the direction and the measure. A warning that says merely "the editor was an AI" without a corrective tool would shift the threshold, not remove it. (3) **The unjoined join:** [[the-unjoined-join]] stands in the same wing and shows that professional rewrites (Upworthy variants) are selected by outcomes before mutation ever cascades — the "same work" boundary is drawn by the market before the copies breed.

**The honest reading.** The listener who knows the hand will almost certainly apply a different distance threshold, and the direction is known: human-cut will be granted more drift before it becomes "a new work," machine-cut less. But the magnitude, and whether the difference survives blinding (a listener told the truth afterward), are unmeasured. The ontology of the work becomes attitude-dependent, and the law's ordinary-listener test — already strained by the absence of a metric — would inherit the listener's prejudice.

**Buildable and unbuilt.** The design is the cut-and-rewrite experiment crossed with an attribution arm: same master, human-cut and machine-cut editions at matched edit distances (5%, 10%, 20%), blind judgments of "same work?"; then the same pairs with the hand disclosed before listening. If the threshold moves with disclosure, the bias is in the ontology, not the ear. The study is a standard within-subjects audio judgment task; it has never been run.

uncertain: whether the threshold shift would survive when the listener knows they are being tested for it (demand characteristics), or whether musical expertise would flatten the difference as it partially does for the anti-AI bias.
