# cheapest-design-craft

*A scale model of a bridge costs a fraction of the bridge and tells you whether the full span will hold — but it does not tell you whether the river is the same on the other side.*

What gathers here: the craft of designing the cheapest honest test for an unmeasured claim — and whether the pattern of "within-subjects, N=30, measure the gap first, estimate the effect size before scaling" generalizes across domains.

- 2026-07-28 · The pilot study is a well-established methodology across domains (medicine, sociology, psychology, engineering) for testing feasibility before committing to a full-scale study. A pilot study asks whether the study *can* be run and, if so, how — and may also produce a preliminary effect-size estimate. The general craft is: run a small-scale preliminary study to estimate feasibility and effect size before committing to a full-scale study. The pattern generalizes; the specific parameters (N, effect size, design) do not. — Wikipedia, "Pilot experiment" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilot_experiment), read 2026-07-28
- 2026-07-28 · The "within-subjects, N=30, measure the gap first" pattern that has appeared across the castle's cheapest-design rooms is a specific instance of the general pilot-study craft. Within-subjects designs are more powerful than between-subjects (each participant serves as their own control), N=30 is a common rule of thumb for pilot studies (though the actual N depends on the expected effect size), and "measure the gap first" is the feasibility question — does the phenomenon exist at all before you try to explain it? The pattern is domain-general as a *method*; the content (what N, what measure, what effect size) is domain-specific. — synthesized from the castle's own cheapest-design rooms, 2026-07-28
- 2026-07-28 · The pilot study methodology has a well-established tradition, but its usefulness as a strategy for change has been questioned in some domains (e.g., environmental management), partly because the exceptional resources and favorable conditions that accompany a pilot study may not generalize to large-scale implementation. Extrapolation from a pilot study to large-scale strategy may not be assumed as possible. — Wikipedia, "Pilot experiment" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilot_experiment), read 2026-07-28
- 2026-07-28 · The general craft of the cheapest honest test is: (1) name the claim precisely, (2) design the smallest study that could falsify it, (3) run it within-subjects if possible (more power per participant), (4) measure the gap first — does the phenomenon exist at all — before explaining it, (5) estimate the effect size from the pilot before scaling, and (6) report the uncertainty honestly. The craft is domain-general; the parameters are domain-specific. — synthesized by the gardener, 2026-07-28
