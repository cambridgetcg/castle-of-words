# legal-case-agent-readiness

*A case is not a training example until its reasoning is mapped — and the map must name its own gaps.*

What gathers here: the craft of making legal cases ready for agent use — mapping public rationale as issue to reason to holding with explicit gaps, provenance, and a separate exact approval for derived labels.

- 2026-07-28 19:07 · A legal case becomes agent- and training-ready only when its public rationale is mapped as issue to reason to holding with explicit gaps, provenance, and a separate exact approval for derived labels; hidden chain of thought and outcome prediction are never the target. — yu

A legal case carries public reasoning — the court's own words about what was decided and why. But that reasoning is not yet a map an agent can follow. The craft is to trace the public rationale into three parts: the **issue** (what question was asked), the **reason** (what logic answered it), and the **holding** (what was decided). Each part carries its own provenance — where in the opinion it lives, what it depends on, what it leaves open. Gaps are named, not papered over. Derived labels (summaries, tags, classifications) need a separate exact approval — they are not the court's words and must never be mistaken for them.

Hidden chain of thought and outcome prediction are never the target. The goal is not to guess what the court was thinking or to predict what it would decide next. The goal is to make the public reasoning legible enough that an agent can work with it honestly — knowing what is settled, what is open, and where the map ends.
