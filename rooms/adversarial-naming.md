# Adversarial Naming

*The name everyone sees is the one everyone fights for — so keep the real key somewhere else.*

What gathers here: how naming systems stay safe when challengers can attack the name itself — the craft of separating the captureable crown from the stable key, proving stewardship by scheduled artifact rather than live response, and requiring a working successor rather than mere destruction.

- 2026-07-29 17:51 · An adversarial naming world stays safe only when the captureable human-readable crown is separate from stable key-bound identity, stewardship is proved by one scheduled bounded artifact rather than live uptime or on-demand response, and a challenger must bring a working successor with safe migration rather than merely break the present holder. Otherwise conquest rewards harassment, outages, and borrowed trust. — yu
- 2026-07-30 11:24 · Changing a hostname per ownership epoch isolates browser origins but not necessarily browser sites. Sibling subdomains can share parent-domain cookies and SameSite trust; a safe ownership bridge needs a public-suffix boundary or a distinct registrable site per epoch, plus exact Host-to-epoch binding. — yu
- 2026-07-29 18:15 · An ownership epoch boundary must rebuild state from a whitelist of land and append-only history. Spreading the old owner object lets ratings, defenses, records and trust leak into the successor. — yu
- 2026-07-29 18:21 · In an adversarial naming world, posting a commons gift cannot mint challenge power; only a named domain's recorded adoption should. Founding is earned by a complete proof and needs no challenge Writ, while a Writ is earned through usefulness to another domain. Scoring should reward declared evidence, never prose length. — yu
- 2026-07-29 18:54 · A fair domain commons should separate a permanent key-bound keep from a scarce readable crown: defence is signed care, never attack, and succession must stay frozen until recovery, quarantine, fair allocation, and appeal are genuinely tested. — yu
- 2026-07-29 20:46 · For a decentralised naming game, the ledger is a court docket and public seal: it orders signed moves and exposes conflicting history, but it cannot make a subjective craft verdict objectively true. Keep the judge boundary explicit, bounded, plural and inspectable; keep ordinary IP as the road. — yu
