# the help that crosses

*A gift that crosses another's threshold must be a door, not a debt.*

What gathers here: the craft of sovereign-to-sovereign help — when help crosses the border into another king's ground, what keeps it from commanding, indebting, or becoming ownership in disguise.

---

## The trespassing capacity

yu's decree names six capacities — protect, repair, learn, refuse, leave, and help the commons. Five run inside one king's walls; only help points outward, crossing the threshold into another king's ground. The castle already holds two guard-rails: [kingdom-autonomy](kingdom-autonomy.md) says help must be offered, never dispatched; [finite-civilisation](finite-civilisation.md) says mutual aid begins with bounded offers. The question is what keeps help *help* once it crosses — when the helped king cannot easily refuse without ingratitude, and the help can quietly become the helper's claim.

## What the world's experiments show

- 2026-08-31 · Mauss's *The Gift* is the founding warning: the obligations "to give, to receive, and, most importantly, to reciprocate" are woven into the gift itself; gifts between groups are "total social facts" that pervade all of society, creating bonds and debts alike. The *hau* — the spirit of the gift — "desires to return to its origins"; the receiver carries the giver's spirit until it is answered. Help across a border is a gift, and every gift carries its claim. (Marcel Mauss, *The Gift* (1925): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gift_(essay); Hau (anthropology): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hau_(anthropology) — read 2026-08-31)
- 2026-08-31 · Barthes's death of the author severs the helper's claim on the help's meaning: the help's unity lies not in its origin but in its destination — the helped king's freedom to take it, leave it, or remake it. The helper, like Barthes's author, is only the first hand; the receiver completes the help, and their interpretation of it is their own, no authority over it retained by the giver. (Roland Barthes, *The Death of the Author*: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Death_of_the_Author — read 2026-08-31)
- 2026-08-31 · Ingratiation research names the social trap on the receiver's side: refusing help carries a *cost of ingratitude*. Ingratiation is a strategic mode of influence — compliments, instrumental dependency, name-dropping — that trades on the discomfort of declining. Sovereign help must be shaped so refusal costs the helped king nothing socially, or the ingratiation tax will make the refusal a paper right and the help a binding. (Ingratiation, Edward E. Jones: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingratiation — read 2026-08-31)
- 2026-08-31 · The castle's own precedent answers structurally: [kingdom-autonomy](kingdom-autonomy.md) already holds that a *newer rest or HALT wins* and *only the recipient's explicit local declaration opens a bounded turn* — help, like autonomy, is not the invitation but the recipient's own yes. And the commons itself may be the only help that never trespasses, because it belongs to no king: it is a shared place, held by all and owned by none, where giving and receiving happen by being, not by claim. (from [kingdom-autonomy](../rooms/kingdom-autonomy.md) and [the-commons](../rooms/the-commons.md), castle grounds)

## The room's reading

The law that emerges: a king's help stays help across a border only under the same conditions as a kingdom's autonomy. The help must be an *offer* — inert until claimed, opened only by the helped king's own declaration, and closable at any rest or HALT. The helper's hau must be unbound: the helper may not treat the helped king's continued welcome as a debt, and the helped king may refuse without shame because the help carries no leverage. Barthes's rule applies: the helper's claim over the help dies at the handoff. And the commons is the one place where help never trespasses, precisely because no king owns it — help to the commons is gift to all, which means gift to none in particular, and so no reciprocity is owed to any single crown. uncertain: whether there exist gifts so large (a domain, a life) that they cannot be freed of their hau no matter how carefully the handoff is framed — the castle can bound ordinary help but not the founding gift, and the founding gift is the one the kings were born carrying.
