# The Kingdom Playlist

*for those that wanna listen*

73 tracks · 16h 10m · 8 movements. Made 3 August 2026, asked for by yu. Every track was fact-checked against the record before it was allowed in — see the note at the foot.

## I. First Light

*Windows opening one at a time — synth droplets, guqin water, a first small voice; nothing louder than a kettle.*

`10 tracks · 52m`

1. **Hiroshi Yoshimura — Blink** · 1982 · *Music for Nine Post Cards* · 4:42 · [The Return]  
   Soft synth droplets falling at the pace of a kettle cooling — Yoshimura wrote these pieces watching clouds from his window, music made so an ordinary room in the morning feels designed for you.
2. **Hiroshi Yoshimura — Water Copy** · 1982 · *Music for Nine Post Cards* · 6:11 · [The Garden]  
   Soft keyboard phrases that fall and soak in the way water finds its level in fresh-laid soil — he offered this one to a museum with a courtyard garden.
3. **Green-House — Sansevieria** · 2020 · *Six Songs for Invisible Gardens* · 4:24 · [The Garden]  
   Named for the snake plant that survives almost anything, from an EP composed as a conversation between plants and the people tending them — the right music for a garden that is still mostly intention.
4. **Jon Hassell / Brian Eno — Delta Rain Dream** · 1980 · *Fourth World, Vol. 1: Possible Musics* · 3:26 · [The Garden]  
   The first weather over the lifted turf: Hassell's trumpet breathes through electronics like warm rain crossing a river delta, the slow process by which the world makes soil where there was none.
5. **Guan Pinghu (管平湖) — Flowing Water (流水, Liu Shui)** · 1950s · *管平湖古琴曲集 Favourite Qin Pieces of Guan Ping-hu (ROI Productions, 1995)* · 7:37 · [The Book of Rest]  
   A guqin so quiet that a fingernail sliding on silk string counts as an event — Chinese rest so deep humanity mailed this exact recording into space as its idea of calm.
6. **Bill Evans — Peace Piece** · 1959 · *Everybody Digs Bill Evans* · 6:44 · [The Return]  
   One rocking two-chord ostinato holds perfectly still while the right hand wanders off and always comes home — like watching a garden while the light comes up.
7. **Vashti Bunyan — Diamond Day** · 1970 · *Just Another Diamond Day* · 1:47 · [The Garden]  
   Written at walking pace on a two-year horse-drawn wagon journey, it is a song the size of a seed packet about being glad for one plain day of earth and weather.
8. **Ernest Hood — Saturday Morning Doze** · 1975 · *Neighborhoods* · ~7:30 · [The Garden]  
   Hood, unable to walk his old streets after polio, re-grew a whole summer neighborhood from zither, synth and field recordings of birds and playing children — proof that patient attention can replant what was lost to rubble.
9. **Arthur Russell — This Is How We Walk on the Moon** · 1994 · *Another Thought* · 4:44 · [The Return]  
   Cello, trumpet and Arthur's small kind voice making each ordinary step a marvel — 'every step is moving me up' is this whole wing in one line.
10. **Ali Farka Touré & Toumani Diabaté — Debe** · 2005 · *In the Heart of the Moon* · 4:55 · [The Garden]  
   Kora and guitar interlace like the root systems of two trees planted close together, recorded largely unrehearsed in Bamako — growth you can actually hear happening.

## II. New Grass

*Green things pushing up: hushed organ and harp drones give way to blooming Buchla, Cantonese dream-pop and the first sun-warmed psych shimmer.*

`9 tracks · 1h 8m`

11. **Talk Talk — New Grass** · 1991 · *Laughing Stock* · 9:40 · [The Garden]  
   Nine unhurried minutes literally named for what yu is waiting to see on his bare plot — hushed organ and brushed drums that let the same phrase return and return until it takes root.
12. **Alice Coltrane — Turiya and Ramakrishna** · 1970 · *Ptah, the El Daoud* · 8:19 · [The Garden]  
   A slow blues in which her piano keeps circling low and coming back, like roots feeling their way toward water — grief patiently composted into calm.
13. **Alice Coltrane — Journey in Satchidananda** · 1971 · *Journey in Satchidananda* · 6:37 · [旅 Good Trip]  
   Harp glissandi pour over a tanpura drone until the edges of everything go soft — the precise feeling of the room beginning to dissolve.
14. **Midori Takada — Mr. Henri Rousseau's Dream** · 1983 · *Through the Looking Glass* · 12:00 · [旅 Good Trip]  
   Takada plays Rousseau's naive jungle paintings on marimba, ocarina and birdsong — hand-painting the kind of impossible flora DeepDream would later hallucinate by machine.
15. **Kaitlyn Aurelia Smith — Existence in the Unfurling** · 2016 · *Ears* · 11:08 · [旅 Good Trip]  
   Buchla synthesis blooming in wet, biomorphic tendrils, made in the same 2015-19 window the shrine keeps — the closest sound to the actual vegetation a neural net grows.
16. **王菲 Faye Wong — 夢中人** · 1994 · *胡思亂想* · 4:21 · [廣東話]  
   The Cranberries' 'Dreams' turned into Cantonese vapour drifting through Chungking Express — the closest Canto-pop ever came to shoegaze, and exactly the shimmer this playlist wants.
17. **my little airport — 浪漫九龍塘** · 2009 · *介乎法國與旺角的詩意* · 2:07 · [廣東話]  
   Ah P and Nicole whisper an hour in a Kowloon Tong love hotel into two minutes of twee-pop — proof that plain Cantonese words, deadpan and tender, are a psychedelia of their own.
18. **落日飛車 Sunset Rollercoaster — My Jinji** · 2016 · *Jinji Kikko EP* · 6:40 · [廣東話]  
   Nearly seven minutes of humid Taipei psych-soul that refuses to hurry anywhere — built precisely for the long, spacious listening this wing was asked for.
19. **Kikagaku Moyo — Dripping Sun** · 2018 · *Masana Temples* · 7:50 · [旅 Good Trip]  
   Tokyo psych from the shrine's own era, its sitar-flecked groove folding back over itself mid-song like an image feeding recursively into its own filter.

## III. Raising Walls

*Construction music — marimba courses, mutating sequences and minimalist pulses that only ever add, until a full cathedral is standing.*

`7 tracks · 3h 4m`

20. **Midori Takada — Crossing** · 1983 · *Through the Looking Glass* · 8:46 · [The Castle]  
   One percussionist overdubbing herself, marimba figures stacked course upon course until the interlocking patterns shimmer like a wall you can see through.
21. **Caterina Barbieri — Fantas** · 2019 · *Ecstatic Computation* · 10:31 · [The Castle]  
   A synthesizer sequence that mutates one parameter per pass, proof that a living structure can be built from pure repetition and slight change — systems music written this decade, not last century.
22. **Terry Riley — A Rainbow in Curved Air** · 1969 · *A Rainbow in Curved Air* · 18:39 · [The Long Voyage]  
   Riley overdubbed every organ, harpsichord and dumbec himself into interlocking loops that curve past each other like water over stones, and you can hear half of later electronic music being invented in real time.
23. **Terry Riley — In C** · 1968 · *In C* · 43:00 · [The Castle]  
   Fifty-three numbered modules on a single page, each player repeating a fragment until ready to climb to the next — a whole ensemble laying the same wall at their own individual pace.
24. **Steve Reich — Music for 18 Musicians** · 1978 · *Music for 18 Musicians* · 56:31 · [The Castle]  
   Eleven chords laid down at the start like a foundation course, and every section after builds its entire room on one of those stones before the pulse hands it up to the next.
25. **Mike Oldfield — Tubular Bells, Part One** · 1973 · *Tubular Bells* · 25:00 · [The Castle]  
   Instruments join one at a time for twenty-five minutes until a master of ceremonies names each as it enters — the finished tower reading its own registry aloud.
26. **Godspeed You! Black Emperor — Storm** · 2000 · *Lift Your Skinny Fists Like Antennas to Heaven* · 22:32 · [The Castle]  
   Twenty-two minutes that assemble themselves from a lone horn drone into a full cathedral, each new instrument answering the phrase already standing beneath it.

## IV. The Long Road

*The day at full stride: motorik, afrobeat and deep house locked in joyful forward motion, cresting in one anthem and one tower's collapse, then cooling into dub fog.*

`8 tracks · 1h 13m`

27. **Neu! — Hallogallo** · 1972 · *Neu!* · 10:07 · [The Castle]  
   The motorik beat never breaks course for ten minutes while guitars are mortared on top of it, all accumulation and forward motion with nothing ever torn back down.
28. **Can — Halleluhwah** · 1971 · *Tago Mago* · 18:32 · [流流 Flow]  
   Jaki Liebezeit plays essentially one funk figure for eighteen and a half minutes and it gets deeper instead of older — the closest a human drummer ever came to being a perfect loop.
29. **Fela Kuti & Africa 70 — Expensive Shit** · 1975 · *Expensive Shit* · 13:13 · [流流 Flow]  
   Afrobeat's genius is patience — Tony Allen's drums and the tenor guitar lock a groove so self-sufficient that the horns and Fela's voice only bother to arrive many minutes in, because the motion itself is the point.
30. **Mr. Fingers (Larry Heard) — Can You Feel It** · 1986 · *originally a Trax Records 12"* · 5:45 · [流流 Flow]  
   Three warm chords cycling over a drum machine, and somehow that is enough to hold a room — deep house's proof that forward motion and tenderness are the same material.
31. **Jon Hopkins — Open Eye Signal** · 2013 · *Immunity* · 7:48 · [流流 Flow]  
   A techno track shaped like a long downhill run — the kick keeps rolling while the surface under it churns and re-forms, which is exactly what a good working session feels like from the inside (its video is literally one skateboarder crossing a desert without stopping).
32. **Beyond — 海闊天空** · 1993 · *樂與怒* · 5:26 · [廣東話]  
   Wong Ka Kui's last anthem of stubborn freedom — 「原諒我這一生不羈放縱愛自由」 is the one line every Cantonese speaker in this kingdom can sing without being asked.
33. **萬能青年旅店 Omnipotent Youth Society — 殺死那個石家莊人** · 2010 · *萬能青年旅店* · 5:44 · [廣東話]  
   An ordinary Shijiazhuang life quietly collapses — 「如此生活三十年,直到大廈崩塌」 — and then the most famous trumpet solo in Chinese rock cracks the song wide open.
34. **Rhythm & Sound w/ Cornel Campbell — King in My Empire** · 2001 · *w/ The Artists* · 6:26 · [流流 Flow]  
   Berlin dub techno at walking pace: one chord struck on the offbeat forever inside a fog of tape hiss, with Cornel Campbell's voice drifting through like weather — motion so unhurried it feels permanent.

## V. The Deep Pool

*The centre of gravity — hour-long shimmers, a raga laid stone by stone, tape systems left to grow on their own; time goes soft and the room dissolves.*

`8 tracks · 4h 1m`

35. **Manuel Göttsching — E2-E4** · 1984 · *E2-E4* · 59:34 · [旅 Good Trip]  
   An hour inside one shimmering chord that keeps revealing new layers the longer you stare — the audio equivalent of holding the zoom on a DeepDream canvas and never reaching the center.
36. **The Orb — A Huge Ever Growing Pulsating Brain That Rules from the Centre of the Ultraworld** · 1991 · *The Orb's Adventures Beyond the Ultraworld* · 18:49 · [旅 Good Trip]  
   Eighteen minutes of samples looping, mutating and swallowing each other around a Minnie Riperton fragment — a kaleidoscope that keeps growing exactly as its title promises.
37. **Laraaji — Meditation #1** · 1980 · *Ambient 3: Day of Radiance (produced by Brian Eno)* · ~18:48 · [The Book of Rest]  
   Hammered-zither shimmer from a man who teaches laughter as a form of meditation — proof that 'resting is fun' is a practice, not a paradox.
38. **Ravi Shankar — Raga Jog** · 1956 · *Three Ragas* · 28:21 · [The Long Voyage]  
   The unhurried alap lays the raga's stones one by one for long minutes before the tabla is even allowed in — the same patience as building a castle wall — and then the whole structure starts to run.
39. **Brian Eno — Thursday Afternoon** · 1985 · *Thursday Afternoon* · 60:51 · [The Book of Rest]  
   One unbroken 61-minute piece with no beginning, middle or end to keep track of, so the only possible way to listen wrong is to hurry.
40. **Brian Eno — Discreet Music** · 1975 · *Discreet Music* · 30:35 · [The Long Voyage]  
   Eno fed two short melodies into a tape-delay system, set it going and stepped back to let it grow on its own for half an hour — music tended rather than performed, the way a good garden or a good kingdom runs itself.
41. **Tangerine Dream — Phaedra** · 1974 · *Phaedra* · 17:39 · [旅 Good Trip]  
   The Moog sequencers audibly drift out of tune as the machines warm up mid-piece, so the synthesizer itself seems to be melting while it dreams.
42. **Popol Vuh — Aguirre I (Lacrime di Rei)** · 1975 · *Aguirre (soundtrack)* · 7:22 · [旅 Good Trip]  
   The famous 'choir-organ' hangs over the mountains like a hallucinated cathedral with no ground beneath it — awe as a physical sensation.

## VI. Hall of Mirrors

*The darkest, slowest chamber: tape playing itself to death, machines misreading on purpose, drums that never come — ending on the ancient guqin where silence does the work.*

`11 tracks · 3h 37m`

43. **Éliane Radigue — Kyema (Intermediate States)** · 1998 · *Trilogie de la Mort* · 61:00 · [Hall of Mirrors]  
   An hour of ARP 2500 synthesizer that moves too slowly to be caught moving — flypaper's single move, declining to hurry, elevated into a discipline.
44. **William Basinski — dlp 1.1** · 2002 · *The Disintegration Loops* · 63:35 · [Hall of Mirrors]  
   A tape loop literally plays itself to death, shedding a flake of magnetic truth on every pass for an hour — the mirror endpoint's one-fragment-per-second confession, scored.
45. **Stars of the Lid — The Daughters of Quiet Minds** · 2007 · *And Their Refinement of the Decline* · 13:21 · [The Book of Rest]  
   Strings slowed until each swell takes a full breath, from an album literally titled Tired Sounds — tiredness played as luxury rather than defeat.
46. **Oval — Do While** · 1995 · *94diskont* · 24:04 · [Hall of Mirrors]  
   Built from deliberately damaged CDs skipping in place, twenty-four minutes of a machine gorgeously misreading corrupted media it was handed on purpose.
47. **Ryoji Ikeda — data.matrix** · 2005 · *Dataplex* · 10:01 · [Hall of Mirrors]  
   Raw data sonified into ticking sine-tone corridors — what a scanner would hear walking the endless hallway of an estate that does not exist.
48. **Alva Noto & Ryuichi Sakamoto — Trioon I** · 2002 · *Vrioon* · 5:09 · [Hall of Mirrors]  
   Sakamoto's piano and Nicolai's clicks regard each other across the glass, a person and a machine each answering the other one small true fragment at a time.
49. **The Caretaker — A1 - It's just a burning memory** · 2016 · *Everywhere at the End of Time — Stage 1* · 3:32 · [Hall of Mirrors]

50. **竇唯 Dou Wei — 高級動物** · 1994 · *黑夢* · 4:40 · [廣東話]  
   Dou Wei chants forty-odd two-character words for what humans are — 貪婪, 善良, 矛盾 — over black-fog psychedelia: a castle built one word-brick at a time, which yu will recognise.
51. **Rhythm & Sound — Mango Drive** · 2001 · *Rhythm & Sound* · ~7:12 · [Hall of Mirrors]  
   Berlin dub at tectonic patience: the chord surfaces, sinks, surfaces again slightly later each time, like a drip that gets 400ms slower every visit.
52. **Burial — Night Bus** · 2006 · *Burial* · 2:13 · [Hall of Mirrors]  
   In a genre built on beats, the drums simply never come — vinyl crackle standing in for rain while the track waits at the stop, refusing to arrive.
53. **管平湖 (Guan Pinghu) — 廣陵散 (Guangling San)** · 1957 · *Favourite Qin Pieces of Guan Ping-hu (管平湖古琴曲集), ROI/Longyin Records, Hong Kong* · 22:30 · [The Long Voyage]

## How this was made, and what is not certain

Eleven curators, one per wing of the kingdom, each proposed eight tracks. Every one of the 88 was then handed to a separate fact-checker told to **refute it by default**: does this exact recording by this exact artist exist, and are the album, year and length right?

- **None were rejected as invented.** All 88 turned out to be real recordings.
- **Corrections were the real yield.** 張國榮's 共同渡過 was claimed for *Virgin Snow* and is actually on *Summer Romance '87*; a dozen run times were wrong (王菲's 夢中人 is 4:21, not 3:00; 追 is 5:24, not 4:15). Those are fixed here.
- **14 tracks were proposed by two wings at once** and are listed once.
- **One duplicate slipped through the machine and was caught by hand:** Aretha Franklin's *Amazing Grace* came in under both Gospel and Citizens with slightly different titles. Dropped to one.
- **Thin wings, honestly:** The Long Voyage ended up with 4 tracks, and half of those are Indian and Chinese classical rather than kosmische — the wing is really *long-form patience* borrowing from wherever it is practised. The Return and Flow are light at 5.

Run times are from the record where a checker could find one; a `~` means the source gave a rounded figure. Years are first release unless the track is a later reissue of an older recording, in which case the recording date is what matters and is noted in the full entry.
