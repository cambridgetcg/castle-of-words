# attacker-held pen

*A diary can say "I was born at midnight" — believable only if nobody profits from midnight.*

An attacker-held pen is a timestamp written by the same party the timestamp could advantage: the originator attests their own instant, so trusting it means trusting a clock in the hand that might want to move it. It is the opposite of an honest [[watching-clock]]: there the watcher is not the movement's owner; here the writer of the time is exactly the one the time serves.

For a child: if I hand you a drawing and say "I made this yesterday, before you thought of the idea," the date I wrote on it is only as good as my honesty — because I wrote it. If a teacher had stamped it in her register when I handed it in, the date would mean something. My date is my pen; her stamp is her register.

Where the pattern stands in the message families the castle measured (read 2026-09-04): the pen exists and is distinguished from a trusted stamp by whether the *signer* or a *third party* holds the clock.

- **CMS/PKCS#7 signatures carry the pen openly.** RFC 5652 §11.3 defines the `signing-time` attribute as "the time at which the signer (**purportedly**) performed the signing process" — the standard itself writes the hedge into the definition. The signing time is the signer's own clock, signed over, so it is integrity-protected against a *third* party's tampering and worthless against the signer's own backdating.
- **The trusted fix is older than the pen.** RFC 3161 (2001) makes a Time Stamping Authority sign the time *over a hash of the datum* — a "proof-of-existence" for the datum at an instant, held by a third party. OpenTimestamps (opentimestamps.org) ports the same shape to Bitcoin: a Merkle-tree commitment of many documents aggregated by calendar servers into one transaction proves the data existed *prior to* the block — "timestamping proves the data existed before some time," i.e. a **ceiling**, never a birth-certificate. None of these prove the movement was *born* then; they prove it could not have been born *after*.
- **RFC 9921 (February 2026, IETF — COSE + RFC 3161 tokens) names the two orderings and their two different facts.** CTT (sign first, then timestamp the signature field) lets a third-party token date the *signature*; TTC (timestamp the payload first, sign after) binds an earlier payload stamp into a later signature — and a verifier must not read TTC as dating the signature: the signer could stamp a payload Monday, be revoked Tuesday, and sign Wednesday, and every byte would verify. Order decides which fact the token carries; no construction lets the signer's pen date itself.
- **Where originator-attested instants *are* carried:** the ISO 20022 initiation messages (pain.001) are written by the debtor — the message's creation datetime is the originator's pen (uncertain: not independently re-fetched this visit; named from the ISO 20022 structure already cited in [the-coarse-clock](../rooms/the-coarse-clock.md)'s camt work) — and on-chain attestation services (the Ethereum Attestation Service timestamping pattern) let an attester attach a time, still the attester's pen, while the *block's* stamp belongs to consensus. Chain transfer formats carry no originator-time field at all (EIP-1559 et al.); ISO 8583's DE 12 is the terminal's clock, not the cardholder's.

The law, settled 2026-09-04: **yes, the pen exists — and trusting it does trade the observer's-clock honesty for an attacker-held pen, unless the pen is followed by a third-party stamp.** Signed originator time proves *intent and integrity* (what the originator declared, unaltered) but never *earliness*; third-party stamping proves earliness only as a ceiling; the block stamp proves consensus order, not birth. So for [[movement-identity]] the honest tuple may *carry* a signed origin-time as a claim about the sender's declaration, but the dedup grain still comes from the watching clock (mempool first-seen, match engine, DE 12) — the originator's pen is a statement to be logged, never the clock to be trusted. This is the observer's-clock caveat's mirror: the observer's clock is honest about *seeing*, the originator's pen is honest about *wanting*, and only a third party's stamp is honest (as a ceiling) about *being*.
