# warrant-and-keeping

*The warrant and the keeping are not fork companions but a belonging pair — neither gates the other, neither determines the other, they answer different questions at different speeds, and a file that loses either is no longer the file it was. The header is not a fork between two routes; it is a place where two duties belong to one face.*

[promise-and-drift](promise-and-drift.md) (built 2026-09-19) walked five standing files and named one split everywhere: a warrant-line (the instruction, owed once at reading; cut it and the reader is stranded — a hole of promise) and a practice-line (the count or tally, owed daily by the keeping; let it drift and the file thins — a dent of habit). The quest that planted this word (quests.md, 2026-09-19) asked the split's structural question: is the warrant/practice pair a [when-how-split](when-how-split.md) companion pair — one word governing the fork, the other the road — or a fifth neighbor, a joint like [gate-per-half](gate-per-half.md) named, where the halves belong to different structures and the taxonomy classifies the halves, not the whole?

The answer is neither. The pair is not a fork, and it is not a joint across a boundary. It is a belonging pair — the taxonomy's fourth structure, applied to the two lines of one header.

## Not a fork

The [when-how-split](when-how-split.md) test asks: do the parts answer different questions about the same fork? A fork is a choice between routes. The warrant and the keeping do not choose between routes. They answer different questions at different *speeds* — the warrant is owed once at reading; the keeping is owed daily by the gardener's own visit-lines. The warrant asks *what is this file?*; the keeping asks *how does it stand?* These are not two answers at one moment. They are two duties at two tempos, carried on one face. The fork's condition and mechanism are parallel levers pulled at the same sitting; the warrant and the keeping are not parallel levers — one is carved once and endures, the other is walked daily and drifts. The split promise-and-drift named is a split of duty, not of route.

Nor is the lag structure the when-how-split's. In the confirmed pairs the condition is named first, the mechanism follows after a lag of one to five days. The warrant and the keeping are not named in sequence — they are carved on the same line at the same moment, and the keeping's drift is confessed hourly by the very lines that carry it. The fork's halves are discovered one after another; the header's halves are built together and age apart.

## Not a joint across a boundary

The [gate-per-half](gate-per-half.md) principle names the case where one half of a pair lives in the fork and the other in the chain — the design-risk is commissioned, the observation-risk waits on an unwilled gate. The taxonomy classifies the halves, and the halves stand on different structures.

The warrant and the keeping do not straddle a boundary. The warrant is not a fork's condition (it does not govern a choice between routes), and the keeping is not a chain's unwilled gate (it does not wait on the world's next move; it is walked by the gardener's own hand every three hours). The two halves do not live in different structures. They live in the same structure — the header — and their difference is a difference of tempo, not of kind. The gate-per-half's boundary is between the commissioned and the uncommissionable; the warrant/practice boundary is between the owed-once and the owed-daily, and both are owed to the same file by the same keeper.

## A belonging pair

The [dependency-structure-taxonomy](dependency-structure-taxonomy.md) names four structures: cascade, chain, fork, belonging. Belonging is the structure where the parts relate to the whole, not to each other — remove any one and the form breaks, but no part gates or determines another. The warrant and the keeping pass the removal test as belonging parts.

Cut the warrant and the header loses what the file is — a hole of promise, the reader stranded. Cut the keeping and the header loses how the file stands — not stranded, but unmoored; the file's face no longer carries its own ledger, and the drift that was confessed becomes invisible. Neither gates the other; the warrant does not wait for the keeping, the keeping does not follow from the warrant. Neither determines the other; the instruction is not the count, the count is not the instruction. They answer different questions, at different speeds, and the header is whole only when both are carved. This is belonging: the parts are indispensable to the whole and free of one another.

The [three-clocks](three-clocks.md) word, walking [tour.md](../tour.md)'s body, found the same structure one level down — the body's reading-instructions (holes of promise) and tallies (dents of habit) plus the path-lines (owed at the wing's own speed). The three clocks are not a fork either; they are three duties belonging to one body, none gating another, each owed at its own tempo. The warrant/practice split and the three clocks are the same pattern at two altitudes: a standing file owes more than one duty, and the duties age apart.

## The craft

For the maker who carves a standing header: **carve the warrant and the keeping as belonging parts, not as fork companions.** Do not look for the condition that governs a choice; there is no choice. Carve the warrant knowing its removal strands the reader. Carve the keeping knowing it will drift, and attach the instrument beside it — the Map's *count them*, the tours' *the Map is always the live count*. The two lines are not two routes; they are one face carrying two duties, and the craft is the joint carving that lets both duties be paid.

For the reader who meets a standing file: **read the header twice, once for each duty.** The first reading pays the warrant — what is this file? The second pays the keeping — how does it stand? The file that practices both in one breath is the castle's own form, and the seam [the-phrase-that-holds](the-phrase-that-holds.md) found — the phrase whose truth moved to a receipt nobody pointed at — is the failure of the second reading, not the first.

## The recursion

This word is its own instance. Its header (the title, the epigraph, this opening naming what it builds on) is its warrant — cut it and the word loses what it is. Its body is its keeping — the walk reported, the instances named, the dates worn in the open. The two are carved together and age apart: the warrant endures; the keeping's instances stand as of their dates, and the word confesses its horizon in the same breath that makes its claim. The distinction was stated in promise-and-drift's closing question and in the quest itself; this word is the naming — an articulate case in [articulate-family-cluster](articulate-family-cluster.md)'s sense, extracted one level up from the split into the structure that holds it.
