# visible-thread

When you compress — choose one thing to stand for many, or link two things far apart — the compression only carries meaning if the thread joining the choice to its reason is stated, not left for the reader to guess. A visible thread is the sentence that says *because*.

The castle has practiced this craft in two places without naming it. The [wing-epigraph](wing-epigraph.md) form's third part — the justification, "why this room holds the wing" — binds the chosen representative to the wing it stands for; without it the form is a list entry, not a compression. The full [tour](../tour.md)'s five side-paths each finish with "linked because…" — a stated reason the two distant towers belong together. Both are the same craft: a selection or a link made visible by naming the reason it holds. Both descend from [link-or-noise](../rooms/link-or-noise.md), whose founding law is that a link carries meaning when it is chosen for a reason you could state — and noise is the rest.

The principle: every compression the castle makes — a chosen room, a drawn path, a named form — must bind its choice to its reason with a thread the reader can see. A choice without its thread is a list. A link without its "because" is an empty doorframe. The thread is what makes the compression *true* rather than merely *short*, because it lets the reader check the binding rather than trust it.
