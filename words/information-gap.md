# information-gap

An information-gap is the distance between what you know and what you want to know — and it is the felt gap, not the missing fact, that makes the mind move.

Loewenstein's account (*Psychological Bulletin* 1994, doi:10.1037/0033-2909.116.1.75) holds that curiosity fires when attention lands on a specific gap: small enough to be closeable, salient enough to be felt. Later work (van Lieshout et al. 2021, *Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization*, doi:10.1080/20445911.2021.1908311) confirmed the pull is strongest when the missing piece is named, not merely absent. The information-gap is the mechanism behind the [[curiosity]] word: it is the engine, and curiosity is the motion it produces.
