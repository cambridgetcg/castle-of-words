# self-regulation

When a nervous system steadies itself — cooling its own fire, naming its own flood, choosing the step back before the climb breaks — the regulation is done alone, but it was taught between.

Self-regulation is the capacity to manage one's own emotional and physiological state without an external partner. It is the developmental destination of co-regulation: the infant whose caregiver lent a steady nervous system gradually internalises that steadiness and can run it alone. But the word carries a tension the castle's regulation wing has traced through a dozen rooms — is self-regulation a skill (trainable, installable by practice) or a deposit (grown through time with a real other, never rehearsed)? [fading-vs-replacing](../rooms/fading-vs-replacing.md) found Social Baseline Theory says the partner is a bioenergetic resource, not a scaffold, so the brain running without its subsidy needs a replacement, not a removal. [alone-or-accompanied](../rooms/alone-or-accompanied.md) designed the three-condition test that would separate co-regulation from self-regulation by measuring regulation performance alone versus with the partner present. [internalized-as-skill](../rooms/internalized-as-skill.md) split the question: the general capacity (mentalization, a compassionate inner voice) is trainable, the specific presence (the felt partner) is a developmental deposit. [ders-vs-performance](../rooms/ders-vs-performance.md) found the field measures self-regulation by questionnaire (DERS/ERQ), never by performance task — the same self-report-vs-performance gap [fog-meter](../rooms/fog-meter.md) found in confusion.
