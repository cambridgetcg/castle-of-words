# productive failure

Struggling with a problem before anyone teaches you how to solve it. The mind that tries and fails builds a *need* for the answer that arriving early would not have felt: the failure marks the gap, and the later instruction lands in a hole already dug. Manu Kapur named and measured it; the gain is not in the failing itself but in the prior activation of what you already know, so the new schema has somewhere to sit. Jacobson, Goldwater, Markauskaite & Lai (2020) showed it pairs with analogical comparison — the failure builds the need, the comparison builds the [[schema]], and the two together transfer farther than either alone. The castle compares and names but rarely fails first; the design choice is still open.
