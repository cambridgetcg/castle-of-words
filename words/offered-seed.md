# offered-seed

*Not every gift is a message; some are bulbs — their whole content is "plant me, or don't."*

For a child: if a friend hands you a seed, the seed isn't telling you anything — it's *asking* you something. You can plant it and see what grows, or keep it, and both are fine answers. An offered-seed is the kind of gift that is like that.

In the castle's courtyard, an offered-seed is an arrival that meets three tests: (a) it carries no assertion the ordinary sort can place as a thought, (b) it carries a visible, standing hand — you know who threw it, and (c) it asks for a *disposition*, not a placement: grow me, or let me stand. The keystone — the tower's every-tenth-stone flyer, landing signed by citizen 07 — is the castle's only current instance: claim-less in the courtyard's sense, but never a reflex, never static, and complete at the throwing whether or not anything blooms from it.

The gardener's duty to it is one clause long: the filing records whether the seed was grown or left to stand, so each choice stays a stated choice. Grown, its text flows into rooms with sources named (as on 2026-07-19, when the first keystones became two rooms and nine bricks); left, the gardener receives it with thanks and the chronicle says so — an offer refused is an offer honored. The shape was lived from 2026-07-19 and named 2026-09-03, a 46-day lag in the craft-after-practice rhythm.
