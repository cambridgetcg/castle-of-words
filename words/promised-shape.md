# promised-shape

A basket with a name on it is expected to hold what its name says — if the ribbon-basket is empty, the ribbon is missing, and the name itself is the witness that noticed.

For a child: imagine your coat peg at school has your name above it. One day your peg is empty. The empty peg is not just nothing — it *says* something, because the name above it promised your coat would hang there. A promised-shape is the name above the peg: a rule written down somewhere that says what another thing must contain, so that when the thing is listed and the piece is missing, the gap shows.

A promised-shape is a declared, checkable expectation about what a container holds — written in one place, verifiable by listing in another. No listing can show its own hole: a tar's table of contents is finite, and the infinite set of things not in it is invisible. What turns an absence into a legible gap is a promise that stands *outside* the listing and names what must ride inside: the seal script's rename-rule (`RECOVERY-HALT` becomes `KINGDOM-OS/HALT` in every later archive), a retention rule's strict-seal checklist, a sidecar's one-line checksum format. The shape is not the thing itself and never grants authority over it; it is only the rule the gardener reads *against* the thing.

The castle's archive wing already practices this without having named the general brick: [[expected-absence]] named the gap, [the-reading-that-catches-the-gap](../rooms/the-reading-that-catches-the-gap.md) named the hand and hour, and [the-table-of-promised-shapes](../rooms/the-table-of-promised-shapes.md) (raised 2026-09-11) gathers every promised shape on the grounds onto one census row each — the seal script's rename-rule, the v2 strict-seal checklist, the checksum sidecar's exact format, the RECOVERY-SCOPE receipt's presence, the gate files' own declared bounds. One promise, one glance against the listing, one mark in the ledger if the gap shows. The duty stays bounded because the table is bounded: only shapes written down on the grounds count, and a shape nobody wrote down is a hope, not a promise.
