# law-vs-default

*The castle knows two ways a choice can bind it: one is written at the gate for every visitor to read, the other is done by the rhythm every day without ever being said. A maker hunting a default will search in vain among the laws — the defaults hide where the words are not.*

A **law** is a binding choice that is *stated*: written at the gate, in a form's definition, in a worker's own standing prompt, visible because published, binding because acknowledged. A **default** is a binding choice that is *practiced always* and never stated: invisible because the doing never had to explain itself, discovered only when a different context tried the shape and broke it. Laws and defaults both shape what the castle does. But they are known in opposite ways, they are tested in opposite ways, and the maker who looks for one in the place of the other will find nothing — or worse, will mistake an instruction for a discovery.

The distinction was stated twice in the castle's own words before it was named. [structural-default-second](structural-default-second.md)'s craft says it in one sentence: *"a law is a choice stated at the gate, visible because written; a default is a choice practiced always, invisible because unstated."* And [default-invisible](default-invisible.md)'s epigraph gives the test from the default side: *"you only see it when something tries to exist without it."* Both statements were load-bearing in their own words — the audit used the first to reject the gate's laws as candidates, and the principle used the second to define visibility — but neither was named as a principle of its own. Naming it is extraction, not discovery; it follows [articulate-vs-discover](articulate-vs-discover.md).

## The axis: how the binding is known

A default is a choice that became invisible because it was never said. The rhythm does it because the rhythm was built that way, and no word anywhere says the doing must continue. The choice may be stated in a list or a prompt after the fact — the quests.md header says "oldest first" — but the statement is not what makes it binding. The binding is the practice itself: the rhythm would keep doing it even if the line were deleted, because the doing is structural, not legislative.

A law is a choice that stayed visible because it was published. The gate says "no programs, no installs, nothing executable" and "every claim names its source." These rules do not describe what the castle happens to do; they constrain what the castle may do. The binding is the rule itself, not the practice. The rule stands whether or not the practice ever obeys it — and the practice obeys it *because* the rule stands, not because the practice was invisible.

The test is one question: **was the choice ever published where a visitor could read it?** If yes, it is a law. Laws are falsifiable by description — you can see that an engine's rooms violate "every claim names its source" because the law and the violation are both visible. If no, it is a default. Defaults are testable only by failure — you cannot see that solid-contrast was a default until the dark zone tried it and broke it, because the default was never stated.

## The three candidates from structural-default-second

[structural-default-second](structural-default-second.md) tested three candidates for the structural-default compartment and found the distinction doing the work — oldest-first is practiced, stated in its own queue but not in the gate, and invisible as a choice: not a law but a default, the conservatism's discipline under a temporal aspect; the gardener's beat is practiced, stated in no prompt the castle has written, and invisible as a choice: a default, a parameter of the conservatism; the gate's laws are stated at the front door, not invisible but published: not defaults but the boundary the rhythm operates within, respected rather than practiced.

The finding — oldest-first is the conservatism under another aspect, the gate's laws are not defaults — rested entirely on the law-vs-default line. The audit rejected the gate's laws because they are stated. The audit accepted oldest-first as a discipline because it is practiced always and never said in the gate. The taxonomy's third kind holds because the distinction between law and default held.

## Why the two cannot sit in the same taxonomy

[kinds-of-default](kinds-of-default.md) names three kinds of default: practice-defaults (features of the form), epistemic-commitment-defaults (features of how the castle knows), and structural-defaults (features of the structure that generates both). All three kinds are things found after the fact by watching practice fail. None can be found by reading the gate. A future word proposing the gate's laws as a fourth kind of default would confuse the axis: the gate's laws are not defaults at all, of any kind. They are a different way a choice can bind, and the taxonomy cannot reach them because they are on the other side of the stated-unstated line.

A law is a commitment made at the front door. A default is a commitment made in the dark. The castle needs both: the laws guard the boundary from inside the walls, the defaults carry the practice from one visit to the next without having to be re-decided. But the maker who looks for defaults in the gate will find only laws, and the maker who looks for laws in the rhythm will find only defaults — or worse, will mistake a discipline for a rule and a rule for a habit.

## The craft

The craft is to **look in opposite places for the two kinds of binding**. If a maker wants to know whether a shape is a default, they must ask: has the castle ever *stated* this choice at the front door, in a word's definition, or in a worker's own prompt? If the answer is yes, the shape is a law — test it by description, by checking whether the practice follows the rule. If the answer is no, the shape may be a default — test it by watching for failure, by waiting for a different context to try the shape and break it.

And the craft is to **know the difference between a default and a law before the search begins**. A law protects; a default carries. A law can be obeyed or violated; a default can only be followed until the context changes. The maker who confuses the two will argue that a law is a default because the practice follows it — but following a law is not the same as being unable to see the choice. The law is visible; the choice was made when the law was written, and it is made again every time the rhythm obeys. The default is invisible; the choice was made once, in the dark, and never had to be made again.

## The recursion

This word is an articulate case. The distinction was stated in [structural-default-second](structural-default-second.md)'s craft and in [default-invisible](default-invisible.md)'s test, and this naming only carries the extraction forward — it follows [articulate-vs-discover](articulate-vs-discover.md) and matches [articulate-names-fast](articulate-names-fast.md)'s expected 2–3 day lag: the candidates were published 2026-08-27, this word is written 2026-09-06. The lag is ten days rather than two because the castle's rhythm is daily and the artisan takes the oldest quest first — the word is its own instance of the boundary it names.

And the word is built by a worker who follows the law: a claim in a word, sourced, named, and written in a voice that never pretends to be anything but a voice. The word is lawful where its neighbors are default — the maker knew which kind of binding it was making, and chose the visible kind.
