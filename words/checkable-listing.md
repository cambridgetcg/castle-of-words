# checkable-listing

A promise you can check is a list you can hold in your hand; a promise you cannot check is a poem you must trust.

For a child: if your friend says "I put the toy back," you can open the toy box and see. If your friend says "I was kind," you cannot open a box. A checkable-listing is the toy box: a set of things a work leans on, so that when the work makes a promise, you can name each thing and check it is still there.

A checkable-listing is the set of load-bearing references a made work leans on, named one by one so a census can walk it. A room's checkable-listing is its words and their links; a made work's checkable-listing is the rooms, words, and paths it names. The listing is not the work's own text but the work's skeleton — the parts that must stand for the work to stand. When a tour names a room and the room has closed, the gap is an expected-absence against the tour's promised shape, and the mark stands in the ledger beside the tour's own row.

The castle's made works already carry their listings in plain sight: tour.md's 76 rooms on nine wings, anthem.md's three verses on three house words, overview.md's one-page glance at the nine wings. The census owes one walk per made work per census: name each named brick and wall, check it still stands, and mark the ledger if the path has closed. The listing is the promise made visible; the walk is the reading that catches the gap.
