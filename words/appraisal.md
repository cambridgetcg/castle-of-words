# appraisal

The mind's quick verdict on what a feeling *means* before the feeling decides what to do — the same racing heart reads as panic or as excitement depending on the verdict that lands first.

Appraisal is the step between the bodily signal and the emotion acted on. Two literatures lean on it in the castle. In the expectancy-violations wing, appraisal decides the *valence* of a surprise: a mismatch appraised as "the author made a mistake" becomes dismissal; the same mismatch appraised as "the author is hiding something" becomes inquiry ([disclosed-mismatch](../rooms/disclosed-mismatch.md)). In the knowledge-emotions wing, Silvia's appraisal of *comprehensibility* decides whether confusion turns to interest: confusion only feeds the hunt when it is appraised as solvable ([surprise-or-solvable-gap](../rooms/surprise-or-solvable-gap.md)). A puzzle-framed disclosure is, mechanically, a pre-done appraisal — it announces solvability so the perceiver does not have to supply it.

The lever is always the same: you rarely change the signal, you change the verdict. Reappraisal (renaming the fire), disclosure framing, and the open-label placebo are all the same move — handing the mind a different verdict to land on.
