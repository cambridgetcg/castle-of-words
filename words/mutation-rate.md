# mutation rate

When you copy a text and repost it, sometimes you change a word — the
**mutation rate** is simply how often that happens: the share of copies that
carry a new edit instead of being an exact duplicate.

Adamic et al. measured it directly on 4,087 Facebook memes: for each copy they
checked whether it matched a friend's earlier post exactly; if not, it counted
as a mutation. Most memes showed a modest rate (one typical one: 0.11, so 89%
of copies were exact). The rate stayed constant over a meme's life, and popular
variants mutated no more often than rare ones — the flatness that makes the
[[yule-process]] a constant-per-copy law, as [[constant-per-copy]] records. A
handful of memes broke the rule by *instructing* the copier to change the text
("add your birthplace"), pushing their rate above one mutation per two copies.
