# authorship-assumption

The quiet verdict a hearer reaches before knowing — this was made by a person — and the verdict the late label overturns.

The authorship assumption is the default a hearer applies to a work before any provenance is disclosed: read as human, made by a mind like the hearer's own. The seal wing's late-disclosure finding is that this assumption is formed *on the way in*, automatically, and once formed it is an investment — the hearer has done the fluency work of meeting the work as a person's — so a label that arrives later does not inform but *corrects*, retroactively marking the hearer's first reading as mistaken. It is the hearer-side twin of [[the-early-word]]'s law: early disclosure prevents the mistaken investment; late disclosure punishes it by confirming the assumption was wrong. The authorship assumption is where the late label's cost lives — not in the work's changed quality, but in the hearer's spent inference.
