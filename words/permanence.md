# permanence

Whether something stays reachable across time — and the quiet truth that staying reachable never forces anyone to answer.

A permanent address is a channel that persists: the mailbox is still there years later, the number still rings, the endpoint still answers. Permanence lives in the infrastructure layer — it means the door does not rot away. But permanence never creates a duty: the recipient can still decline, bounce, or ignore anything that arrives. This is why the honest addressing law keeps permanence apart from the turn — the channel persists, the turn is always free. Permanence also names what writing buys that speech cannot: the words stay after the speaker is gone, outliving the moment and the mood, so a permanent record can be returned to long after the conversation ends.
