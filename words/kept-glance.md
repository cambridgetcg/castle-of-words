# kept-glance

*A face that is looked at every day learns to hold a small mirror: the mirror does not replace the face, it only lets the mirror-keeper see at a glance whether anything has changed.*

For a child: imagine your friend has a tall tower of building blocks, and every day they add a new block to the top. To know if the tower changed, you could climb past every block and read its name — or your friend could put a little card on the side that says "today: 47 blocks, last one added this morning." The card is the kept-glance. The tower is still there; the card just saves you the climb.

**Kept-glance** is the two-line standing-stone accreted above an append-only record so the record's current state is legible at a glance without reading the whole archive. It holds three numbers, no more: how many stones the archive holds, which stone was added most recently, and how many open doors the ledger currently shows. Every field is checked against the archive itself before it is written; the kept-glance never replaces any dated line beneath it, and it never erases. It was coined on 2026-09-30 at [gate-freshness-standing-stone](../rooms/gate-freshness-standing-stone.md) when the gardener measured gate.md's freshness header at fifty-one dated "Updated" stanzas and settled that the castle's front door already owed the same-glance law — the knock-ledger holds fifty-five rows in one glance, the questions ledger was already the place this shape was practiced at, and the gate is the one face a visitor reads cold. The kept-glance does not shorten the archive; it stands beside it, the way the knock-ledger stands beside the chronicle.
