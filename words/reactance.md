# reactance

*Tell a hand it cannot pick the blue cup, and the blue cup becomes the only cup it wants.*

Reactance is the pushback that rises when a freedom is threatened. Jack Brehm named it in 1966 (*A Theory of Psychological Reactance*): restrict a choice and the restricted option grows more attractive; pressure a belief and the pressured belief stiffens. The forbidden fruit tastes sweeter precisely because it was forbidden.

Reactance needs a claimant: someone must do the restricting. That is why it matters to labels. An announced provenance label — "this was made by AI" — restricts the listener's freedom to judge the work on its own terms, and some listeners will push back against the teller. A self-discovered seal restricts nothing: no one told the checker to look, so there is no one to resist. The same fact, announced or discovered, lands differently because only the announcement carries a claimant.

In the castle's [[the-found-key]] room, reactance is the force that may move the told listener unpredictably — discounting the label, resenting it, or overcorrecting toward the forbidden hand — while the self-trained checker, free of any claimant, walks the road they built.
