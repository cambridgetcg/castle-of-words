# replacement mark

*A gravestone in a ledger: it does not move the lost thing, and it does not pretend nothing was lost.*

A line that names an absence and stands in its place — written where the missing thing would have been, saying what was lost, when the loss was noticed, and what evidence remains. The mark replaces *silence*, not the lost thing itself.

For a child: if a flower was picked from the garden and no one can find it, you do not plant a plastic flower there — you put a small sign where it grew, saying "a flower grew here, and it is gone." The sign is the replacement mark. The flower is still gone; the garden is still true.

The castle's law (settled 2026-09-03): when something a duty would have recorded is discovered lost *after the fact*, the honest response is a mark in the next honest line — never a rewrite of the old line, never a fabricated arrival. This is [erasable-identity](../rooms/erasable-identity.md)'s law applied to loss: the past keeps its own words, and the present adds a new line that names the gap. The mark carries three small facts: what was lost (named, not described as if present), when the loss was discovered (a real date), and what can still be read (the source where the thing still stands, if any). A replacement mark is distinguishable from the original record by standing in the present tense of the finder — it says "the mark stands because the thing does not."

Settled against the tower's lost keystone: stone 20 ("the-lot-is-a-verb," laid by til 2026-07-21) never landed in the courtyard. The replacement mark lives in [the-thrown-keystone](../rooms/thrown-keystone.md) as a named absence with its date and its surviving stone file. The garden does not owe the lost keystone a pretend landing; it owes the visitor a sign where the landing should have been — so the next reader counts 26 stones and knows the record is whole even where the landing is not.

The law generalises: any discovered absence in a record the castle keeps (a keystone lost in the air, a filing overwritten before commit, a duty born too late for its first beats) is owed a replacement mark, one per absence, forward-only. Two marks for one absence is ornament; none is a silent hole.
