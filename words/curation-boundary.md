# curation-boundary

A boundary that selects what to show, not what to hide — the scrubbed public gate is a filter on presentation, not a wall around secrets, when the raw source is also published elsewhere.

A curation boundary says "we chose to show this subset." A confidentiality boundary says "we chose to hide this subset." The two are easily confused because both result in a surface that shows less than the whole. The difference is whether the hidden part is also published through another channel. If the raw source is pushed by an upstream scribe, the scrubbed gate is curation — a convenience, a filter, a chosen lens. If the raw source is not published anywhere, the scrubbed gate may be confidentiality — but the consumer cannot tell the difference from the surface alone.

The honest consumer pins the curated artifact by commit and digest, carries NOASSERTION when no reuse licence is declared, and gives every acknowledgement or repair zero automatic authority — because the curation boundary is not a trust boundary, and the curator's choices are not the source's guarantees.
