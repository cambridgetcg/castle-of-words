# synchronous-vs-diachronic

*A contrast can span two places at once, or two times in sequence — and only the second kind turns a single failure into a named condition. The eye that sees a shape against its context and the ear that hears one verdict follow another are doing different work.*

Contrast comes in two kinds, and the castle's recognition words use both without ever naming them apart. In the failure path, contrast is *synchronous*: two contexts, one shape, two outcomes. In the success-confirms-the-condition subtype, contrast is *diachronic*: two alternatives, one condition, two outcomes. The distinction is stated in the body of [success-confirms-the-condition](success-confirms-the-condition.md), but it has no name of its own. Naming it is extraction, not discovery — it follows [articulate-vs-discover](articulate-vs-discover.md).

## The two kinds

**[failure-reveals-direct](contrast-as-revelation.md)** works by synchronous contrast. The default works in its own context; a different context tries the same shape and fails. The dark zone tried the solid-contrast route on near-black and found it flat — while the same route worked on warm paper. The light zone's route and the dark zone's attempt are two contexts holding one shape, and the outcomes differ. The contrast is *side by side*: the same shape, seen in two places, behaving two ways.

**[success-confirms-the-condition](success-confirms-the-condition.md)** works by diachronic contrast. A first alternative fails and reveals a condition; a second alternative succeeds while differing in the same load-bearing dimension and confirms the condition. The engine's rooms failed to name sources; the tower's poetic stones succeeded without them. The two alternatives are not two contexts holding one shape — they are two *things* following one condition across time. The contrast is *in sequence*: the first verdict followed by the second, and the second is the evidence the first was right.

The difference in one line: synchronous contrast sets the same shape in two places to show its demands; diachronic contrast sets two alternatives in time to show a condition holds.

## Why the diachronic kind is the stronger test

The distinction matters because the two kinds do not test equally. A synchronous contrast — one shape, two places, one fails — is a single observation. A single failure could be a fluke: the dark zone's flatness might have been a problem with gold thread on near-black, not with the solid-contrast route itself. One failing place does not tell you the condition was real; it tells you the shape behaved differently somewhere.

A diachronic contrast — a first alternative fails, a second succeeds in the same dimension — is a replication. The success is the second observation, and a second observation that confirms the first is what turns a single failure into a named condition. The engine wagered that the sourced-claims practice was conditional on empirical research; the tower's success confirmed the wager. Replication is the load the diachronic kind carries, and it is the only kind that carries it.

The two kinds are not symmetric in strength, but they are complementary in role. Synchronous contrast *reveals* — it shows that a default had conditions. Diachronic contrast *confirms* — it shows the conditions were correctly named. The failure path could not begin without the first and could not be trusted without the second. A condition named from synchronous contrast alone is a [wager-on-one-use](wager-on-one-use.md); the diachronic contrast is the confirmation that closes the wager.

## The craft

The maker who watches for a default should name *which* contrast they are seeing. A synchronous contrast says "this shape has conditions." A diachronic contrast says "the conditions are real." If a default is revealed by a failure, the craft is to watch for the diachronic contrast that follows — the second alternative that confirms the condition. The maker who sees the synchronous contrast and stops has named a wager; the maker who sees the diachronic contrast has collected the evidence.

The craft is also to know the diachronic kind when it arrives without being ordered. The tower was not built to test the engine — it was an independent act whose success as confirmation was a side effect. The maker does not manufacture the second alternative; they recognize it when it comes. The watch is the craft, and the watch is passive.

The word follows [articulate-cases-audit](articulate-cases-audit.md) and the castle's method for articulate cases: it is found by reading, not by watching practice. The distinction was already written in success-confirms-the-condition's body — "The contrast is *synchronous*… The contrast is *diachronic*… The diachronic contrast is a stronger test" — and this word only gives it a name. The naming is extraction, the [lag](lag-by-kind.md) from the distinction being stated to its being named is the shortest yet because articulate cases are ready the moment they are read, and the craft is to read the castle's own words for relationships that argue without being named.

And the word is its own instance of what it names. The synchronous-vs-diachronic distinction describes how contrast works in two arrangements; the two articulate cases it was found among ([success-confirms-the-condition](success-confirms-the-condition.md) and [contrast-as-revelation](contrast-as-revelation.md)) are themselves set in sequence — the second found because the first was read. The naming is diachronic: the distinction was stated, then read, then named, and the name is the replication that confirms the first was real.
