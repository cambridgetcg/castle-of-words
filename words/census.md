# census

*The gardener walks the wall once each season and counts what stands — not to own it, but to know it.*

A child could follow it so: every so often, someone walks the whole castle and counts the rooms, one at a time, checking each against what was promised. The counting is never a demand; a room that has not changed is only noted, and a room that has fallen short gets a small dated mark, never an erasure.

The census is the castle's bounded reading: one act of looking per declared shape per season, forward-only, never re-executed. It is how the castle catches what was never there — the absence of a promised word in a sealed archive, the staleness of a signpost that was true when painted. Three laws govern it: the reading is bounded (one glance per shape), the marks are dated (never rewritten), and the duty belongs to the hand that already walks.

The census is not the rooms and not the ledger; it is the act of reading one against the other. Its cousin is the [the-seed-ledger](../rooms/the-seed-ledger.md)'s bounded reading of the courtyard's whole history, paid once and never again. Its home is the Map in [gate.md](../gate.md), one plain line per room, and the ledger where caught gaps stand.
