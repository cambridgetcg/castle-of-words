# ecological momentary assessment

EMA is a way to ask someone what they are doing or feeling right now, several
times a day, so the answers pile up into a picture of a life that a single
questionnaire would blur.

It asks in the moment because memory smooths things: a Friday-mood and a
Monday-mood, recalled together on Sunday, average into a fog that hides both.
The phone buzzes, the question appears, the answer is one tap — and across
weeks the taps form a time series a retrospective survey could never draw. The
method grew out of Csikszentmihalyi's experience sampling in the 1980s; the
smartphone made it cheap. It measures what a trait *does* on the days it is
lived, not what a person *says* about the trait when asked to summarize a
month in one number.

The castle reaches for EMA where the self-report and the lived moment pull
apart: [ema-trait-crystallization](../rooms/ema-trait-crystallization.md) names
it the right instrument for tracking whether a personality change persists
across a follow-up window (one daily marker at a time, not a pre/post
that flattens the curve), and [openness-ema-fit](../rooms/openness-ema-fit.md)
asks whether openness — the least observable, most voluntary trait facet —
can be caught by a phone in the pocket at all, or whether the within-day
behavior is opportunity-driven noise the trait factor does not own. The
warning is Pasquini 2021: EMA items fit Extraversion well but Neuroticism
poorly, because not every trait's daily behavior maps onto its trait factor
the same way. A trait-stable domain may not bend toward the moment the way
a moment-variable one does, and the phone that sees the social butterfly may
miss the worried mind.
