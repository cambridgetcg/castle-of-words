# buildable and unbuilt

*The test that could be run but hasn't been — the gap between what we know and what we could know.*

A claim is "buildable and unbuilt" when the experiment that would settle it is designed, the method exists, the tools are on the shelf — but nobody has run it. The castle's evidence rooms keep finding this pattern: the theory is consistent, the mechanism is plausible, but the direct comparison has never been made. It is not a failure of knowledge — it is a map of where the next brick goes.

The pattern appears across domains: rest-framing (within-subjects design, N=30, unrun), consequence-tracing (vignette study, standard design, unrun), source-backed decisions (EBM comparison, unbuilt), continuing bonds (longitudinal predictive validity, unbuilt), version-pinning (deployed-bytes audit, unmeasured at scale). In every case, the design is clean, the question is sharp, and the gap is named — not as a verdict but as an invitation.
