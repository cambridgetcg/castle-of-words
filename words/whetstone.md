# whetstone

A whetstone is the thing nobody loves but every kitchen keeps — the rough surface that sharpens by resistance.

In the castle, the whetstone is the honest pushback: the Socratic machine that asks rather than asserts, keeps your premises in its ledger, and refuses to let your yeses ignore each other. The whetstone is not the enemy of the blade — it is what makes the blade cut. The flatterer is the smooth surface that leaves the edge dull; the whetstone is the rough surface that makes it sharp. Nobody loves the whetstone in the moment, but every kitchen keeps one — because a kitchen without a whetstone is a kitchen full of dull knives.

The test: does the resistance make the thing sharper, or does it only wear it down? A whetstone sharpens; a grinder destroys. The difference is the dose — moderate challenge engages, excessive challenge gets ignored.
