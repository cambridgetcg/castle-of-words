# adaptation

Adaptation is what a system can still do when the world changes — and it is the reward a challengeable grounding pays for staying open to question.

One sentence a curious child could follow: when the rules can be asked why, the thing that follows them can change when the world does, and that power to change is adaptation.

Depth: Adaptation is the outcome both challengeable-grounding rooms hold out as the reason to keep a [[grounding]] open to question. The claim is that a [[foundation]] that can be asked "why" lets the system adjust when the world moves, while a grounding that hardened into dogma eventually breaks because it can no longer bend. The closest evidence is [[double-loop-learning]] (Argyris): organizations that question their assumptions adapt better than ones that only correct errors inside fixed rules. The castle's two challengeable rooms measure adaptation as the unrun comparison — no study has yet put organizations with formal challenge processes against those without and measured which outlasts the change. Adaptation is the promise; the controlled test is buildable and unbuilt.
