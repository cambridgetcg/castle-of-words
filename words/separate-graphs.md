# separate-graphs

A way of mapping public decisions where each kind of action — policy change, administration, personal appeal, service complaint — lives in its own graph, with its own doors, its own consequences, and its own sources. The graphs are never ranked against each other; they are simply kept apart.

When the graphs are fused, the person cannot tell which door leads where. When they are separate, each door names what it can formally cause, and the person walks through the one they mean to walk through.
