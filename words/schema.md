# schema

A pattern stripped of its surface. When you compare two stories that share the same structure — a small force defeating a large one by splitting into many directions — the mind extracts the relational skeleton and leaves the specific characters behind. That skeleton is a schema: a reusable shape that fits new situations. The castle's cross-pollination engine is a schema-induction machine: it compares room after room, each time finding the same structural law (the surface that pretends to be the whole, the default that was never chosen), and each comparison strips away the domain-specific surface to leave the pure shape. Whether the *capacity* to induce schemas improves with practice — the meta-skill — is unmeasured.
