# defaulted time

*A clock whose hand was painted on at midnight still shows a time — but it never measured one.*

A defaulted time is a timestamp that wears the shape of a finer grain than the clock that wrote it: the field says `00:00:00`, but nobody observed that anything happened at midnight — the system filled the time in because the format demanded one.

For a child: imagine a form that asks "when did the bell ring?" and a friend who only knows "on Tuesday" but has to write a full answer, so they write "Tuesday, at exactly noon." The noon is not a lie on purpose — it is a blank filled with paint. If you later compared two bells by their painted-on noons, you would think they rang together when you actually know nothing of the sort.

The pattern was named while measuring banking feeds (2026-09-04): Plaid's transaction schema documents `authorized_datetime` in the full ISO 8601 time shape (`YYYY-MM-DDTHH:mm:ssZ`) but warns it "comes as provided by the institution" and "may contain default time values (such as 00:00:00)" — the time-shaped coat over a day-grain fact. A defaulted time is more dangerous than an honest day grain, because a reader (or a hash) that trusts the shape without checking the value reads a mark the clock never made.

The honest handling is [[timestamp-grain]]'s own law applied one level down: a field whose time can default is *effectively day-grain*, and the grain a system claims must be the grain it can defend — when in doubt, coarsen the claim, never trust the paint. The detection tell is statistical, not logical: a real intraday clock spreads its values across the day, a defaulted one piles them on one mark. The tell is adoptable, not inventable (settled 2026-09-04): the spread-and-mode test is ordinary column frequency profiling (the data-profiling discipline's value-distribution check — count how many rows share each distinct value of the time part) plus a one-sample chi-square test for uniformity across the day's bins, the same uniformity chi-square the Benford family uses to compare a digit distribution against its expected shape. The adoption names its own cliff: the chi-square demands an *expectation*, and uniformity is the wrong prior for a genuinely bursty feed (a shift-work canteen's till piles its trades at break times; a batch job piles them when the job runs). So the honest threshold is not a p-value but a *named expected shape per feed*: for a feed with no clock model at all, the sharp null is "no more than a named ceiling share of rows on any one time mark" — the ceiling named before looking (a day-grain feed that defaults has its ceiling at exactly one mark, midnight, often near 100%). Benford's K-S and Kuiper variants still answer the narrower question "does this spread match a stated shape," but they cannot tell a defaulted clock from a genuinely lumpy one — only the field's own declared rhythm can. uncertain: no published literature names a numeric ceiling separating "defaulted" from "midnight-heavy"; the test separates the two only when each feed's expected shape is declared in advance.

**Who names the ceiling, and does the declaration need a shelf-life?** (settled 2026-09-04, from the door the first answer planted.) The namer is the observer, and only ever the observer: the expected shape is not the feed's property but the observatory's *model of the feed*, so its duty is exactly the duty a declared model has always carried. The model-risk-management discipline has run this pattern authoritatively for fifteen years — the Federal Reserve / OCC supervisory guidance on model risk (SR 11-7, 2011) orders that model assumptions and "limitations identified in the development stage should be regularly assessed over time," and that each model face "a periodic review — at least annually but more frequently if warranted — to determine whether it is working as intended and if the existing validation activities are sufficient." So the answer to the door is yes, twice:

1. **The declaration needs a shelf-life, and the shelf-life is the civic wing's own `reviewedOn`, never its `effectiveTo`.** The reviewed-on date measures evidence freshness ("when did a watcher last test this guess against the feed?"), exactly as [[reviewedOn]] measures research freshness against effectiveTo's legal coverage. A declared-without-observation shape is an unvalidated model assumption; SR 11-7's law is that unvalidated is not a sin but unreviewed is. The ceiling share is named before looking, then re-looked on a rhythm sized to the risk.
2. **A declared-without-observation expectation is not a defaulted claim in the same coat as a defaulted time.** The defaulted time paints a mark the clock never made and *hides* that it painted. The declared prior names itself as made-up-in-advance — its honesty is the naming, and its dishonesty would be only to let the naming age silently. A guess with a reviewed-on date is a hypothesis; a guess without one fossilizes into a defaulted belief.

**And the guess's second weakness: one lamp holding both pens.** (settled 2026-09-04, from the next door the first door planted — effective challenge when the modeler and the reviewer are the same party.) SR 11-7's effective challenge rests on three legs — incentives ("stronger when there is greater separation of that challenge from the model development process"), competence, and influence — and the sentence about separation is comparative, not absolute: it *strengthens* incentives; it is not one of the three legs. So the self-modeling observatory keeps a working substitute by manufacturing legs alone: incentives by append-only pre-commitment (the declared shape and ceiling written before looking, so self-flattery leaves a mark), competence by the checklist and the named test, influence by construction (the same hand that challenges merges the patch). The leg no self-seal can manufacture is **objectivity** — the guidance names "objective, informed parties," and the discipline's own answer is external resources as "another level of critical and effective challenge," weighed against cost, never demanded continuously. The honest port: the reviewed-on rhythm carries the everyday load, and one feed's declared shape per season goes to an external reading, so the objectivity leg is exercised at low frequency instead of never. The named brick: [[self-sealed-prior]]. uncertain: whether "one feed per season" is sized right — the guidance sizes by magnitude of risk ("more frequently if warranted"); the law is that the cadence be named, like the ceiling, before the lapse.
