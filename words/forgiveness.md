# forgiveness

*To forgive is to hold the record without being held by it.*

Forgiveness is the intentional choice to release resentment toward someone who has wronged you, while still remembering what happened. It is not the same as condoning (saying the wrong was okay), excusing (letting the person off the hook), or forgetting (erasing the memory). The record stays; the grip loosens. New emotional truth is added on top of the old, the way a migration adds a new chapter without rewriting the old one.

In the body, forgiveness measurably helps: thinking about forgiving lowers cardiovascular stress, and more forgiving people report fewer illnesses across many studies. Forgiveness can be taught and learned, with real reductions in anger and stress — even for people whose family members were murdered (Luskin's Northern Ireland studies).
