# the-light-cargo-test

Run the same survival check on text nobody is hunting, and whatever still
lengthens life belongs to memory alone.

[[the-direction-of-the-edits]] measured mutations surviving longer on
conspiracy claims and misinformation — [[dark-cargo]], where every edit is
suspect of dodging a filter. The light-cargo test is the same regression
(variant survival × linguistic features) on light cargo instead: quotations
in ordinary news (Simmons et al. 2011 tracked their mutation patterns),
health advice, ordinary memes. On that ground the [[evasion-vs-memory]]
winds split: the direction the edits still lean is memory's own direction.
Closest existing reading: the Upworthy archive's thousands of headline A/B
tests (Gligorić et al. 2023) — light cargo and word-level features, though
the edits there are professional rewrites, not organic copies; negative
emotion, length, specificity and third-person pronouns lengthened a
headline's life there, positive emotion did not. (Named 2026-08-24, in
[[the-light-cargo]].)
