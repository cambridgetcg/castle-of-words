# competence-violation

The offence that is "you did it wrong," not "you are not who I thought" — and the one an apology can mend.

A competence violation is a breach of trust read as a failure of skill or judgment in a particular act: the work failed, the answer was wrong, the judgment erred. Trust-repair research (Kim, Ferrin, Cooper & Dirks 2004) found competence violations are the repairable kind — an apology restores trust because accepting blame reassures the hearer about future work, the failure being local rather than constitutive of character. It is the counterpart of the [[integrity-violation]], and the trust-repair literature's central claim is that the two are not on one scale: the same confession that heals a competence wound deepens an integrity one. In the castle it is the cell the late provenance label is *not*: the hearer's complaint about a late truth is never that the work was incompetently made.
