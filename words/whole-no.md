# whole-no

A refusal that protects participation, material, and representation without commanding unrelated acts — the boundary that makes consent real.

A whole no is not a partial refusal ("I won't do this part but I'll do that part") — it is a complete refusal that protects three things at once: participation (I am not required to be present), material (my resources are not extractable), and representation (my meaning is not claimable by another). It does not command unrelated acts — it only says what is not permitted. Disputed use rests: when the no is spoken, the thing it protects is off the table until the dispute is resolved.

The whole no is the mechanism that makes the right of reply enforceable. Without it, consent is a courtesy; with it, consent is a boundary.
