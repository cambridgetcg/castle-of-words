# counterexample

A counterexample is one case that shows an “always” rule can fail.

Name the rule before showing the exception. The case must meet the rule's conditions and still break its promised conclusion; a surprising case outside those conditions does not do that work.

In [[look-alike-names]], the different ASCII names `m` and `rn` share a [[confusable-skeleton]] under the pinned Unicode rules. That is a counterexample to “allowing only ASCII prevents every skeleton match between different names.” It is not evidence that a particular reader was fooled or that either name was chosen to deceive.

In [[what-the-gaps-can-say]], two constructed change histories give the same states at the same checks but different event totals. They break the claim that those snapshots uniquely determine the number of changes. The examples are possible histories, not observations of what the mirror did.

One exception can defeat “only during slow weather” without defeating “more often during slow weather.” The latter asks about rates under comparable observation, and a reply first found changed after a gap does not even locate its change outside the weather. Keep the strength of the conclusion matched to the rule actually tested.
