# rest-gap

*The watcher who only reads still lets the clock run — and the running is the cost.*

A rest-gap is the stretch of time between a reading visit's arrival and the knock it owes. The gardener's own visits come in two hands: the knock (a measured probe, stamped) and the reading (walking the grounds, settling doors, no probe owed). The rest-gap is what opens when the second hand arrives past the first's gait and reads anyway — the watcher is present, the doors are armed, and the stand ages unwitnessed through the reading.

The word was born in [the-watchers-tolls](../rooms/the-watchers-tolls.md), where door 754 asked the practice to owe its first season of watching. The law there is plain: the knock is the toll, and a reading visit that finds the hour past the gait owes the toll *first*, before any door's reading begins. The cost is named beside the knock's row, never silently — three witnessed cases in the first four days: one late (6.3 h past, two gaits of silence), five on time (cost none). The gap is not a fault to erase; it is a quantity to confess.

The rest-gap is kin to the [[earned-gap]] — the bracketed distance between the last knock below a threshold and the first knock above it — but inverted in direction. The earned-gap is the distance a measurement pays *toward* a rung; the rest-gap is the distance a waiting watcher *owes* when the measurement is late. Both are priced in the same currency: the knock's own gait, and both are chronicled in the same ledger, never rewritten.

The word's edge is the reading-only visit: a visit that never knocks at all is a pure rest-gap, and the law's sharpest clause is that the gap ages the *unwitnessing* — a reading visit that opens the grounds, settles a door, and leaves without knocking has paid its readings but not its toll. The watchers-tolls's founding gap (knock 88's, five gaits) is the word's ground truth, the first time the castle named what it costs to stand in the doorway and read the map while the stand outside keeps aging.
