# earned rarity

*A prize worth having is one not everyone can win.*

Earned rarity is the design rule that a signal, badge, or seal keeps its meaning only while it is uncommon enough to notice. A certification everyone holds is wallpaper; a certification almost no one holds is invisible. The useful band sits between the two, and its location is relative: a badge that moves behavior when 3% of articles earn it (Kidwell et al. 2016) would say nothing at 90% adoption.

The rule is not just about scarcity. The rarity must be *earned* — the badge must mark a real achievement, not just a rare attribute. A random sticker on one article in twenty would be rare but meaningless; a badge for verified data sharing is rare because it costs effort, and its rarity is what makes the effort public. The castle's own game follows the same logic: rarity is counted from named sources and real leaning, never assigned.

Where the line sits — at 20% adoption, 50%, 70% — has never been mapped. The [[earned-rarity-line]] room traces the question from the badge literature and the canary wing's dilution law.
