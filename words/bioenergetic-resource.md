# bioenergetic-resource

A trusted other is not a scaffold you fade but a resource your brain spends less energy without — the social baseline the brain evolved to regulate within.

Social Baseline Theory (Coan, Beckes) frames the partner as a *bioenergetic resource*: the brain, perceiving a trusted other, does not mount the full threat response in the first place — vigilance is offloaded, the metabolic cost of self-regulation is shared. Coan's handholding studies showed partner presence reduced amygdala activation before the threat was processed, not after. The partner's mechanism is *prevention* (the threat system is not fully activated); the trained inner voice's mechanism is *recovery* (the parasympathetic system counter-regulates a threat already felt). These are two different points on the threat-response arc.

The implication for therapy: if the partner was a bioenergetic resource, fading the partner does not teach the brain to run without the subsidy — it forces the brain to spend more energy. The question is whether the brain can learn to provide its own subsidy through practice, or whether the replacement needs to be social in a different form.
