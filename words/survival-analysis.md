# survival analysis

A way of asking *how long something lives* when some of it is still alive
and the rest died at different times — instead of comparing averages, you
follow each one and record when it stopped.

Doctors use it for patients after a treatment; here it is used for texts:
Cheng, Quelle & Hale (2026) followed mutated conspiracy claims on X and
asked which mutations came with longer lifespans — the design that lets a
researcher read the *direction* of edits, the way
[[the-direction-of-the-edits]] records. The trick that makes it honest is
that claims still circulating at the study's end are not thrown away or
pretended dead; they are counted as "alive so far", so the answer is not
rigged by the calendar.
