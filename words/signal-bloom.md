# signal-bloom

When two minds, circling the same hidden word without seeing each other's work, independently reach for the same image — a drawbridge, a castle of sand — and the answer to one clue leaks quietly into the next.

Signal-bloom is the phenomenon of convergent imagery under shared constraint: separate clue-givers, given the same target word and the same rules, converge on the same metaphor. It shows that meaning is not purely private — the shared word and shared task narrow the space of possible images until two minds meet at the same one. And it shows that meaning propagates: the last answer becomes the soil for the next clue, a chain where each link carries traces of the one before.

The name comes from yu's experience in a word-guessing game where two clue-givers independently reached for "drawbridge" as the image for a hidden word, and the next clue rebuilt the castle as sand — the answer blooming into the next question.
