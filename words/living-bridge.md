# living-bridge

A living bridge is help that keeps flowing to someone after they have left the group that gives it — money, shelter, or welcome paid forever to a member who is gone.

Most bridges across a leaving are short: the world outside catches you, you keep a foot in the door for a while, or you withdraw what you already deposited. A living bridge is the one that does not end: the group keeps giving to the departed member, on and on, with no day the giving stops.

## Why it has no path home

Every gift asks to be answered — in the Māori idea of the [[hau]], the gift carries the giver's spirit and wants to travel back. A living bridge can never be answered, because the receiver has left the ring entirely: they are gone, and nothing they do can reach back to the givers. The castle's rooms found that real federations (tribes, states, co-ops, the historical record) almost never pay a living bridge — and the reason is written down: an unanswerable gift has no road left to travel. The only cultures that practice it are those with great giving-feasts where burning wealth is itself the boast.

The living bridge is the exact mirror of the [[exit-tax]]: the same dial on the price of the door, turned the opposite way — one makes leaving cost, the other makes leaving carry a gift forever.
