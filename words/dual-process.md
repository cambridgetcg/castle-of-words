# dual-process

Every repeated knock feeds two rivers at once: one that wears the alarm down, and one that builds it up — and what you feel is which river is running stronger.

Dual-process theory is Groves and Thompson's answer (1970) to why repetition sometimes dulls and sometimes sharpens: every stimulus feeds a habituating process that decrements the response and a sensitizing process that increments it, and the observed behavior is their balance. Mild, predictable stimuli tip the balance toward habituation; intense, novel, or threatening ones tip it toward sensitization. The same repeated knock can therefore quiet one listener and inflame another — the difference is not in the knock but in which process the knock recruited. For the castle it is the reason rotation is not one intervention but two: changing a check's content stays on the habituation river, while changing its unpredictability can pull the sensitization river into flood.
