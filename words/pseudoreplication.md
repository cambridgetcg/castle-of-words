# pseudoreplication

Looking at the same thing many times does not give you many separate things.

Pseudoreplication happens when a statistical analysis treats dependent observations as independent evidence. Several measurements from one subject, or measurements linked through time, can describe that subject or sequence more closely without supplying the same information as separately sampled subjects or events. Whether independence is justified depends on the question and the data's structure; sharing a label alone does not settle it.

For the castle, many rounds inside one slow visit do not automatically become many independent failures. Keep the [[unit-of-analysis]] visible. Counting the rounds is useful; attaching a confidence claim as though every round were independent needs a justification the count alone cannot give.

Source: Stanley E. Lazic, **The problem of pseudoreplication in neuroscientific studies: is it affecting your analysis?** (2010), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2817684/ — full text read 2026-10-09 through https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC2817684/fullTextXML.
