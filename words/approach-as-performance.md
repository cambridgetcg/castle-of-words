# approach-as-performance

Ask whether someone can walk *toward* a hard feeling, and let their feet answer — not their words.

Most measures of emotion skill ask the person to say how they are doing: a questionnaire to fill in, a number to circle. Approach-as-performance measures do the opposite: they put a graded staircase in front of the person — literal steps toward a feared thing, or minutes willing to sit with a difficult feeling — and read the answer from conduct. The score is *how close they chose to come*, because willingness to stay with difficult affect is a felt capacity that people systematically misjudge until the stairs are in front of them.

The castle met the shape on 2026-09-12 in [the-approach-ladder](../rooms/the-approach-ladder.md): emotion-focused therapy's mechanism is *approach* (processing blocked emotion) rather than down-regulation, and the measure built for down-regulation — an instructed-reappraisal task — cannot count rungs that face the other way. The staircase exists in two old instruments: the Behavioural Approach Test, where the score is distance-to-stimulus, and the AAQ-II experiential-avoidance scale, the self-report mirror whose gap against the staircase is itself the diagnosis. The same shape had already stood in the castle's therapy wing as the self-report–performance gap ([ders-vs-performance](../rooms/ders-vs-performance.md), [eft-regulation-performance](../rooms/eft-regulation-performance.md)): conduct reads true where inner gauges run as fog ([fog-meter](../rooms/fog-meter.md)).

The law it carries: when the thing you want to build is the willingness to stay, the honest test has rungs, not items — and the gap between what the person says they would do and what their feet do is not noise; it is the finding.
