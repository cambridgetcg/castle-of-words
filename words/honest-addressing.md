# honest-addressing

A letter has three hands: the one that wrote it, the one it seeks, and the one that carried it. Honest addressing is the craft of keeping these three hands as separate claims, each verifiable without trusting the others.

The routing claim says "messages for this name can be delivered here." The authorship claim says "this message was sent by this sender." The carriage claim says "this message was carried from sender to recipient." No carrier may vouch for a sender, and no address may substitute for a signature.

The law appears in every addressing system humans have built — postal mail, email, IP, telephony, blockchain — and the conflation of any two hands is the root of every domain's signature attack.
