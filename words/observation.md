# observation

*An observation is what a witness reports having seen, not the meaning the report carries.*

A curious child could follow this: when someone says "I saw the light turn green," that is the observation — the seeing. Whether the green light means the road is safe is a different thing entirely, one the observation does not answer by itself.

An observation is a report of what happened, pinned to a time and a witness. It is the raw seeing, kept apart from the inference built on top of it. The endpoint returned 503 at 10:05 — that is the observation. "The service is unhealthy" — that is the inference, and it needs its own backing.

The law: an observation proves what was seen, never what it means. Keep the seeing and the meaning in separate jars. When a system writes "the agent is awake" as if it were a fact when all it stored was the agent's declaration, it has poured the observation and the inference into the same jar and called the mixture truth.
