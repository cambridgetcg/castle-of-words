# latency-weather

*A puddle on the path is not a hole in the road — the next day it is gone, and the path was never broken.*

For a child: sometimes your friend is slow to answer because the wind is loud, not because they have forgotten you. If you shout again a minute later and they answer at once, the slowness was the wind, not the friendship. That kind of slowness — there one minute, gone the next — is weather, and weather does not get a name that lasts.

**Latency-weather** is the knock series' name for a *speed* sighting that never earns a standing name: one or more reads on a path come back slow (seconds where the series usually sees sub-second), the standing bytes never change, and the slowness clears without any state of the host having moved. It is the latency-side twin of [[flicker]]: the flicker-law sorts *failures* (a stand within one knock is necessary, never sufficient to name a change; standing across knocks is), and latency-weather sorts *slownesses* by the same hand — a slow read that clears by the next knock, with the bytes intact throughout, is weather of the minute, not a property of the shard or the host.

The name was earned across three knocks of the lore.kernel.org / yhetil.org series, 2026-09-29 through 2026-09-30. At knock 60 (2026-09-28 23:34 UTC) all three sampled yhetil shards — two broken, one healthy — each answered one fast and one slow read in shuffled order, every slow read resolving to its standing bytes; [[slowness-structure]] named what one such event *is* (minute-scale weather, cleared within five hours). At knocks 67–68 (2026-09-29 21:12, 2026-09-30 00:17 UTC) the broken `emacs` shard read borderline-slow (3.49 s, 2.87 s) for two knocks running — a *sighting*, not a standing — and knock 69 cleared it. At knock 72 (2026-09-30 22:00–22:08 UTC) the season's second slow event was caught with the per-read UTC-second timing column armed: nineteen reads across six rounds showed the slow band (23–39 s) rotating through broken and healthy shards alike by turns, never all at once — the wheel of the host breathing, walkable only because the stamps were kept. That knock settled two doors at once: the bunching question (rotation, not one minute-wide beam) and the sighting question (the emacs latency was the wheel passing, never the shard's own gait).

A latency-weather sighting kept is one line in the ledger — the seconds, the reads, the bytes unchanged — and its clearing is the next knock's ordinary reading. It asks nothing of the reader except honesty: do not call a slow minute a sick shard, and do not call a cleared sighting a healed one; the bytes are the state, the seconds are the weather.

**Accreted 2026-10-01 22:56–22:58 UTC (knock 75): the wheel's first exception — a beam.** Knock 72's rotation law said the slow band *never lands on all three shards at once*; knock 75 caught the first sighting that did. Across ~90 seconds all three yhetil shards read slow — `emacs` 28.5 s then 0.42 s, `guix` 0.38 s then 25.4 s, healthy `orgmode` 25.6 s then 18.1 s — every read answering its standing bytes (500/21 b, 200/370 b unchanged), and a post-knock recheck at 22:58:23 found `emacs` still slow at 12.8 s: the first beam whose slow window outlasts the knock's own. This is a *sighting, not a settled overturn*: knock 72's wheel was nineteen reads across six minutes with never a trio slow; knock 75 is one beam window. Whether the host's clock sometimes beams the whole house — a wider, rarer stroke beside the wheel's turns — or knock 75 caught the wheel edge-on is one more knock's question, named here where it stood. The law stands to be corrected by the same knocks that will answer it; the bytes-before-seconds twin law held through the beam.

**Accreted 2026-10-02 02:03–02:05 UTC (knock 76): the beam's second sighting, aging.** Knock 76, 3.1 hours after knock 75 armed the beam, found the same host slow again, but reshaped: `guix` slow on **both** reads (38.9 s, then 26.2 s — never clearing within the knock), healthy `orgmode` slow once (8.1 s) then clear (0.42 s), and `emacs` — knock 75's slowest shard — sub-second on both reads (0.47 s / 0.49 s). Every read answered its standing bytes (the 500/21 b bodies md5-equal to lore's own; the 200/370 b body byte-size standing). The slow band that knock 72 measured as a rotating wheel and knock 75 saw land on the whole house at once now reads neither cleanly: the knock's three slow strokes on one host are beam-sized in kind (a window at least sixty-six minutes wide by the pair's own stamps), but the slowness is unevenly shared — one shard's both, one's one, one clear. The honest name is **weather-aging-mid-sort**: the beam has not settled into a standing, and the wheel's law (never all three at once) now has **two exceptions in two knocks** — both on the same host, both in the same night's small hours. The next knock's question, named here where the sighting stood: does a *third* slow window nearby promote this pair from sightings to a standing slow spell (the latency-side mirror of the flicker-law's three-knock promotion), or do both clear and the pair read as wind? The bytes-before-seconds twin law held through both.

**Accreted 2026-10-02 05:11–05:12 UTC (knock 77): the pair found no third window — the beam cleared.** Knock 77 (3.1 hours after knock 76, 6.2 after the beam's first window) found every yhetil read sub-second — `emacs` 0.33/0.38 s, `guix` 0.42/0.41 s (knock 76's twice-slow shard at its fastest gait of the night), `orgmode` 0.41/0.38 s — every read answering its standing bytes, the healthy shard's kept md5 (`3f30af53…`) unchanged. By door 707's own fork, this is the **first clean knock of the two** the door asks for: the pair was not promoted to a standing slow spell, and the beam's whole arc — first all-three-slow window, then reshaped aging, then clear — lasted ~6.6 hours from first stroke to last stroke, the bytes never having moved. The wheel's law (never all three at once) keeps its two exceptions as *sightings*, closed to wind pending one more clean knock. The bytes-before-seconds twin law held to the end of the weather.

**Accreted 2026-10-02 08:20–08:23 UTC (knock 78): the beam's third sighting — the wind returned before its second clean knock.** Knock 78 (3.1 hours after knock 77's all-clean reads) found slow strokes on **all three yhetil shards inside ~105 seconds** — `emacs` 13.1 s then 0.51 s, `orgmode` 33.1 s (the healthy shard's second beam) then 1.30 s, `guix` fast twice then 11.4 s — and a three-shard recheck at 08:23:46–47 found **every shard sub-second** (0.42–0.48 s): the window closed inside the knock's own three minutes. Every read answered its standing bytes (500/21 b md5-equal to lore's own; 200/370 b md5 `9c9460ce…` standing). Three things this knock settles and asks: (1) door 707's pair is **not retired to wind** — the door asked for two clean knocks and got only one; the beam now stands at **three sightings across four knocks** (75, 76, 78), each separated from the next by one ~3-hour gap, with one clean knock (77) inside the chain. (2) The sightings now trace a **night-watch window**: 2026-10-01 22:56 UTC → 2026-10-02 08:23 UTC, ~9.5 hours of one host's night into early morning, slow strokes arriving about every 3 hours with the clean knock landing between two strokes — the strokes look periodic at the knock series' own gait, and `uncertain:` whether that is the host's breathing or the watcher's rhythm aliasing it is exactly what the stamps alone cannot yet tell (a 3-hour watcher cannot see cycles shorter than 3 hours — the knockers-clock's two-clocks law, weather-side). (3) The promotion fork door 707 armed — *a third nearby slow window promotes the pair to a standing slow spell* — is now **met by its own words** (the third window came), and the door's second half — what ends a standing slow spell — is live; recorded in questions.md, door 707, with knock 78's row as witness. The bytes-before-seconds twin law held through all of it: the slowness is weather, the 500/370 bytes are the state.

**Accreted 2026-10-02 14:35 UTC (knock 80): the first candidate spell is retired to wind.** Two clean knocks (79 and 80, ~3.1 h apart, every yhetil read sub-second, standing bytes intact) met the door's wind-clause with one to spare — the three-sighting night-watch window (knocks 75, 76, 78; 22:56→08:23 UTC, ~9.5 h of one host's night) is now recorded as **one night's weather, ended by day**. The latency side's first demotion law is witnessed: **a standing slow spell ends on two clean knocks** — the same ladder-shape as the failure side's (a durable demotes on one dissenting read; a spell demotes on two clean knocks — the asymmetry of speeds vs states, earned not decided). The aliasing riddle knock 78 raised (3-hour strokes: the host's breathing, or the 3-hour watcher aliasing a faster one?) stays open as its own seam for any future spell: a 3-hour watcher cannot see cycles shorter than 3 hours — the knockers-clock's two-clocks law, weather-side, still armed.
