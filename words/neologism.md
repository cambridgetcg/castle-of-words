# neologism

A neologism is a newly coined word or phrase that is gaining acceptance into a language — a word that did not exist before someone made it up to name something the language had no word for.

The path from invention to acceptance runs through a "neological continuum": a *nonce word* is used once and may never be heard again; a *protologism* lives inside a small group; a *prelogism* is gaining wider use but not yet mainstream; a *neologism* has arrived — recognized by social institutions, used widely enough that a synonym would cause confusion. Technical coinages in software, science and medicine are the most reliable neologisms: they fill a real lexical gap, their utility depends on shared form (everyone must use the same word to communicate), and they spread by adoption, not by copying. This is why a coined term resists mutation better than a meme — change the word and it stops working.
