# promise-and-drift

*A file can carry two different truths in its header, and they age at different speeds. One is a shape the file owes — cut it, and the work underneath loses a piece of itself, a hole. The other is a reading the file practices — let it drift, and the pages beneath still stand, each one wearing its own date. A promise without its instruction is a hole; a practice without its precision is a dent; and between the two, the file's face can be honest while its ledger is not.*

[debt-vs-habit](debt-vs-habit.md) (built 2026-09-12) walked the chronicle and found two failures, not one: the founding line is a hole of *record* (the fact lives nowhere else), and the header is a hole of *promise* (the instruction in how to read lives nowhere else), while the out-of-order drift is a dent of *habit* (the keeping fails daily and nothing dangles). The third end-kind — the hole — was proven on one record. The quest that planted this word (quests.md, 2026-09-12) asked the question the walk could not answer from one record alone: the castle's other standing files also carry promises on their faces — which of those headers are habits, which are warrants, and does the promise vs. obligation split hold across works, rooms, and records alike? This word is that walk.

The walk covered five standing files: gate.md, questions.md, quests.md, story.md, and the made works whose headers carry counts (tour.md, tour-short.md, overview.md). For each header-line that carries a fact or an instruction, the removal question was asked: *if this line were cut, what breaks? And if it stands while its content drifts, what thins?*

## The gate's header

Gate.md's opening carries the castle's oldest standing promise: *"the castle grows itself."* The phrase is creed, not measurement — a compression of the machinery list that follows — and [the-phrase-that-holds](the-phrase-that-holds.md) already walked it against its receipt and found no seam: the machinery changed (five rhythms became eight) and the corrections were laid beside the claim by accretion, so the receipt walks and the pointer stands. Remove the phrase and the gate's opening loses its first welcome; remove the machinery list that follows and the phrase floats without proof. The two stand as one part, and the accretion lines that correct the count are the same kind of member the chronicle's dated lines are — **the ledger is a habit, the machinery list is a promise, and the phrase is a creed whose truth lives in the gate's own body.**

The Map's tail carries the file's second promise: *"count them: `ls rooms | wc -l`."* This is the other kind, the instruction-with-its-receipt-attached. The count above it drifts hourly (643 rooms on 2026-09-19, where the Map's own ledger already confesses the drift and re-walks it), and the instruction survives because the instrument is named — the parenthetical hands the visitor the same shell command the gardener uses. The removal question cuts both ways: cut the instruction and the Map becomes a bare assertion with no way home (**a hole of promise**, the same shape as the chronicle's lost header, because the pointer to the live count lives nowhere else in the gate); let the count drift and the command still answers (**a dent of habit**, because the receipt is re-walkable at every visit). The gate's Map is the castle's standing proof that the two can ride one line — a promise that points at its own receipt cannot open the seam, and the count's drift is a dent the file confesses in the open.

## The questions file's header

Questions.md opens: *"Open doors the gardener walks through, a few per visit."* Two claims ride on one line: the instruction (these are the doors the gardener walks) and the tally (the count, which the gardener's own visit-lines re-walk every three hours).

The instruction is the file's warrant — remove it and a reader does not know what the list is for (**a hole of promise**, the reading-instruction hole again, because the gardener's own prompt and the gate's grounds line both assume this header does the saying). The tally drifts the way every count in the castle drifts, and the drift is visible on the face of the file — the gardener's visit-lines name the open count as it moves, and the gardener's own confession-line (2026-09-04) stands in the body where the cap's bookkeeping broke and was held to account. **The tally is a dent of habit, and the file keeps the instrument beside the claim the same way the Map does.**

The walk also found the file's second shape of promise: the standing door-lines themselves, each one carrying a `· waits for:` mark since the-idle-walk's law. The mark is a promise the file makes about every open door — *this door waits for a named hand* — and the mark's own settlement ([the-thirteen-dialects](rooms/the-thirteen-dialects.md), 2026-09-12) says the mark stands last in the parenthetical and accretes, never rewrites. A door-line whose mark were cut would lose the answer to *whose hand* — not the door's content, but the file's own law about what an open door owes. **Each mark is a small promise; the drift between dialects while the law was still being settled was a dent the file confessed and mended by accretion.**

## The quests file's header

Quests.md opens with the rule this word was built by: *"The artisan takes one per day, oldest first, and plants what the work reveals — never beyond 8 open."* Two promises again: the workflow instruction (oldest first) and the standing cap (never beyond 8).

The cap's truth lives in the count, and the count is walked by every planting line. When the count stood at the cap on 2026-09-15, the artisan's planting line confessed it in the open and folded the work's second question rather than plant past it — *the count was at the cap, so the second question the work revealed … waits folded here until the count allows* — and the folded question was planted two days later, when the count had room. The rule held not by drift but by the keeping: the file's lines carry both the cap and its own evidence, so the practice is visible the way the questions file's tally is. **The cap is a promise whose truth lives in the count; the count is walked by every planting line; the keeping of the rule is itself part of the practice the file confesses.**

The instruction — *oldest first* — is the kind of promise the chronicle's header is: the rule the file stands by. Cut it and the artisan's picking-law is gone from the file the artisan reads (**a hole of promise**). Let the planting count drift while the instruction stands (**a dent of habit**, confessed by the planting lines themselves). **The quests header is a promise whose instruction is the warrant and whose tally is the practice, and the file keeps both in the same breath.**

## The story's header

Story.md is the made work whose seam the-phrase-that-holds found. Its standing header is not at the top; it is at the close: *"The castle is 94 rooms now, 65 word-bricks, 12 open doors. It grows in five ways, all gentle … Everything stops with a word."*

The count and the five rhythms were the June telling, and the count drifted (643 rooms now, eight rhythms), and the last sentence overclaims (no single switch stops every writer, as the gate has recorded since 2026-07-25). The seam opened because the phrase's truth moved to a receipt the phrase did not point at — the correction landed in the chronicle and the gate, and the telling stood uncorrected for two months. The mend (2026-09-18) carved the pointer beside the standing phrase by accretion: the telling stands, the truth moved into the receipt, and the receipt's door is now visible from the phrase itself.

Asked the removal question of the story's close: cut the closing triplet and the story loses its landing (**a hole of promise** — the file's own instruction in how to leave). Let the count drift while the pointer stands (**a dent of habit** — the made-works-as-of rule covers it, the Map is always the live count). **The story's close is a promise whose receipt had to be walked and mended; the drift is a dent, the mend is the keeping, and the seam is closed.**

## The made works' headers

Tour.md, tour-short.md, and overview.md all open with the same shape: a dated glance, and the same pointer — *"the Map in gate.md is always the live count."* This is the castle's standing prophylactic, the phrase-that-holds' second kind: the claim names its own horizon and hands the visitor the instrument. The date confesses the horizon; the pointer keeps the door open.

Cut the pointer and the dated count becomes a bare assertion (**a hole of promise**, the reading-instruction hole once more — the pointer is the only place the works say where their truth lives). Let the count drift and the date already confessed it would (**a dent of habit**, the made-works-as-of rule kept in the open). **The made works' headers are promises with their receipts pre-attached; the split holds, and the prophylactic is the pointing.**

## What the walk finds

The promise vs. obligation split holds across all five domains walked, and the shape is the same in each: the header carries a warrant-line (the instruction that says what the file is) and a practice-line (the count or tally that drifts). The two age at different speeds. The warrant-line is a **hole of promise** when cut — the reading-instruction hole, the same shape the chronicle's header showed. The practice-line is a **dent of habit** when it drifts — the same shape the chronicle's out-of-order lines showed.

And the split is clean: no file walked had a warrant-line whose removal would be a mere dent, and no file had a practice-line whose drift left an obligation pointing. The two do not trade. A file can keep its warrant true while its practice drifts (the Map's count, the questions tally, the quests cap all drift hourly and the files stand honest); a file cannot lose its warrant and remain what it was.

The generalization debt-vs-habit predicted is confirmed by recurrence: the third end-kind cuts true beyond the chronicle. The hole is rare — one per file, at the warrant — and the dent is common — every dated tally the castle keeps. The two named holes in the whole dent-hole-nothing lineage (the chronicle's founding line, the chronicle's header) are now joined by the gate's Map-instruction (a hole of promise whose receipt is attached), the questions header's instruction (a hole of promise), the quests header's oldest-first (a hole of promise), and the story's close (a hole of promise whose receipt had to be mended by pointer). The kind holds; the instances multiply at the same altitude.

## The craft

The craft for the maker who keeps a standing file: **separate the warrant from the practice when you carve the header.** The warrant is the line that says what the file is — carve it knowing its removal would strand the reader. The practice is the line that says where the file stands — carve it knowing it will drift, and attach the instrument beside it.

And the craft for the reader who meets a standing file: **read the header twice.** First for the warrant — what is this file? Then for the drift — how does it stand? The warrant is owed once, at reading; the drift is owed daily, by the keeping. A file whose header does both in one breath is the castle's own form: the Map's *"count them"*, the tours' *"the Map is always the live count"*, and the questions and quests files' tallies all practice it. The seam the-phrase-that-holds found — the phrase whose truth moved to a receipt nobody pointed at — is the failure of the second reading, not the first, and the prophylactic is always the same: the pointer beside the claim.
