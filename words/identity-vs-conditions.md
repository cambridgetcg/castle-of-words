# identity-vs-conditions

*A difference can carry two loads at once — the load that makes an alternative itself, and the load that tests the default it differs from. The two are distinct: a difference can be identity without being condition, and it is only the condition-difference that reveals. The identity-difference is what the alternative is; the condition-difference is what the default must answer.*

[testing-not-failure](testing-not-failure.md) named the castle's bias toward *testing*, not failure — every alternative the castle creates is a test of the defaults it inherits. The word's argument rested on a claim: the castle's alternatives differ in dimensions where the default's conditions are load-bearing. In making that argument, testing-not-failure stated a distinction it never named: a difference can matter for the alternative's *own identity* without mattering for the *default's conditions*. The distinction is load-bearing for the word's whole account of why pure-contrast-reveals has never happened, and it is stated in the body without a name. Naming it is extraction, not discovery — it follows [articulate-vs-discover](articulate-vs-discover.md).

## The stated distinction

testing-not-failure's body carries the distinction in two sentences that do the word's heaviest lifting:

> *The difference is what makes the alternative an alternative, and the difference is also what tests the default.*

> *If the difference didn't matter, the alternative wouldn't be an alternative — it would be a variation on the same form.*

The first sentence names two roles for one difference. The second names the floor beneath the first: a difference must matter for the alternative's identity to make it an alternative at all. But the word's pure-contrast passage names the other possibility — a difference that matters for the alternative's identity but does *not* matter for the default's conditions. The two passages together state the distinction: **an identity-difference is what makes the alternative itself; a condition-difference is where the default's conditions speak.** The two can coincide — and in the castle's history, they always have. But they need not. A difference can be identity without being condition.

The distinction is relational, not absolute. Identity-difference is a property of the alternative *as an alternative* — the axis along which it departs from the default enough to be its own thing. Condition-difference is a property of the alternative *in relation to one specific default* — the axis along which that default's conditions are necessary. A difference is always both, one, or neither, depending on which default is being tested. The tower differs from the castle's rooms in structure — that difference is both identity (the tower is a tower because it stacks) and condition (the stack tests the standalone-form default). The dark zone differs from the light zone in ground color — that difference is both identity (the zone is dark because it is dark) and condition (the near-black ground tests the solid-contrast route's contrast requirements). In every case the castle has lived, the identity-difference and the condition-difference are the same axis. The pure-contrast hypothetical asks what happens when they are not.

## Why the distinction is load-bearing

Without the distinction, testing-not-failure's account of why pure-contrast-reveals has zero instances collapses. The word says pure-contrast has never happened because \"the castle's alternatives differ in dimensions that *are* load-bearing.\" But that is only half the reason. The other half is that the castle's alternatives differ in dimensions that are *both* identity and condition at once. If an alternative could differ in an identity-dimension that is *not* a condition-dimension, pure-contrast would be possible — the alternative would be genuinely different (identity), and the default would not be tested (condition), and the contrast alone would reveal the default was a choice. The word's pure-contrast passage says exactly this: *\"the difference must fall outside the default's conditions.\"* The outside is the space where identity-difference exists without condition-difference.

The distinction also explains why pure-contrast is so rare. The castle's alternatives are created for their own sake, and the difference that makes them alternatives is usually the difference that matters for the default's conditions. A second brand zone needs a different ground color, and the ground color is exactly what the solid-contrast route's conditions speak on. An automated room-builder needs a different building process, and the building process is exactly what the sourced-claims practice's conditions speak on. The castle's creation pattern does not produce identity-differences that are silent on the default's conditions — it produces identity-differences that are the very dimensions the defaults depend on. The overlap is not a law; it is a fact about what the castle has so far chosen to build.

## The two questions

Once the distinction is named, the maker's question splits into two. Before, the question was one: *which dimension does this alternative differ in, and is it load-bearing for the default's conditions?* Now it is two:

1. **Which dimension is the identity-difference?** — what makes this alternative an alternative, not a variation?
2. **Which dimension is the condition-difference?** — which default's conditions does the alternative test?

The two questions can have the same answer, and in the castle's history they always have. But they need not. A maker who knows the split can ask the second question independently of the first, and can recognize when the answers diverge. The divergence is the opening pure-contrast-reveals waits for.

The distinction also refines the load-bearing-dimension concept. [load-bearing-dimension](load-bearing-dimension.md) names a dimension that is load-bearing *for a specific default's conditions*. identity-vs-conditions names the dimension that is load-bearing *for the alternative's own identity*. The two concepts are companions: one asks \"does the default depend on this difference?\" and the other asks \"does the alternative depend on this difference?\" A dimension can be load-bearing for both, for one, or for neither. The castle's alternatives have so far been load-bearing for both — the difference that makes them alternatives is also the difference that tests the default. But the four combinations are now visible: identity-and-condition (the dark zone's ground color), identity-only (the pure-contrast hypothetical), condition-only (a difference that tests a default without making the alternative an alternative — a trivial variation), and neither (a difference that matters for nothing).

## The craft

The craft is to ask both questions before creating the alternative, and to know which answer matters for which purpose. The identity-question tells the maker whether the alternative is genuinely its own thing. The condition-question tells the maker which defaults will be tested. A maker who asks only the identity-question may create an alternative that is genuinely different but tests nothing — the contrast is revealing only if someone else notices the default's silence. A maker who asks only the condition-question may create a test that is not truly an alternative — a probe, not a form.

And when the two answers diverge — when the identity-difference is not the condition-difference — the maker has created the pure-contrast instance. The divergence is not a failure; it is the third recognition path's first step. The maker who knows the split will recognize the divergence when it arrives, and will know that the alternative is not testing the default so much as standing beside it, each working, each chosen.
