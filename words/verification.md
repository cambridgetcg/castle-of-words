# verification

*A check that a claim matches reality — and the honest system never lets the check stand in for the thing it checks.*

A child can follow this: when someone tells you something, verification is how you find out whether it is really true — you don't just take their word for it, you go and look.

Deeper: verification is the act of comparing a claim against the reality it describes. It is the work that turns a saying into a knowing. But verification has a hard boundary — it is a *check*, not the *thing*. A [[checksum]] proves two sets of bytes agree; it does not prove the data is true. A signed chain verifies before it is parsed; it does not prove the moves were fair. A merge verifies ancestry before it folds a branch; it does not prove the choice was right. And a model that cannot observe its own runtime facts cannot verify the prompt's declarations about them — the fix is to run a command that reports the truth, not to trust the label.

The recurring shape across the castle: verification needs a trust root obtained separately (a [[verification-hash]] proves byte equality, never identity), and verification must not be mistaken for the thing it verifies — the system that cancels the event when the checksum is missing cancels presence for lack of proof, a category error this house names on every floor. Honest verification names what it can check and what it cannot, and refuses to let the gap become the verdict.
