# tombstone

*A stone that says "this was here, and now it is gone" — a deletion that does not erase.*

When you cannot delete from a record without breaking its honesty, you add a new entry that says the old one is no longer true. A tombstone is a fact about a fact: it says what was, and that it is no longer. The original stays; the tombstone is appended after it. A reader who sees both knows the full story — the thing was here, and then it was not.

In the castle's rooms, the tombstone is the manifest's answer to the null-hash ambiguity: a null hash with no reason is silence, a tombstone is silence with an explanation. It is the same principle as [[immutable-correction]]: corrections are appended, never overwritten. The history of what was published remains visible; the deletion is a new fact, not an erasure of the old one.
