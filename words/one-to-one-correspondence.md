# one-to-one-correspondence

Two groups match one-to-one when each thing has exactly one partner on the other side and nothing is left over.

This is a rule about partners, not proof that they are the partners we meant to keep. A restored message map can give every letter a unique number and still send a reader's old number to the wrong letter. The comparison in [[comparing-saved-maps]] therefore checks uniqueness within the named scope before comparing the actual saved pairs; equal totals alone do not keep the promise.

In [[precise-across-time]], visible pairing supplies a handle for exact matching in the reported number tasks. This is a useful connection, not a claim that a database and human memory share a mechanism: pairing groups now and preserving yesterday's particular pairings are different demands.
