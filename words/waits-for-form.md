# waits-for-form

The small shape a waiting door wears: whose hand it waits for, named in one fixed place, so no reader has to guess whether the door is open, claimed, or resting.

The settlement the gardener laid 2026-09-12 after reading all thirteen open doors in `questions.md` and finding the same `· waits for:` law kept in thirteen dialects — some mid-sentence, some at the tail, some carrying expired-then-recommissioned trails. The settled form: one position (the mark stands at the **end** of the parenthetical, as the last clause), one shape per kind (`yu`, `live session`, or `commission (name)`), and one expiry rule — when a commission expires under [the-commission-expiry](../rooms/the-commission-expiry.md), the mark does not vanish; it grows: `waits for: commission (name) · expired <date>, door returned`. A door with no mark is open to any hand; that is [the-idle-walk](../rooms/the-idle-walk.md)'s law this word only tidies.

The virtue named: the mark is a receipt, not a fence. It tells the next reader — gardener, architect, or yu — whose claim holds the door, without stopping any hand from picking it up the way doors have been answered out from under their reservations before.
