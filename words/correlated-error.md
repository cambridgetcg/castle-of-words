# correlated-error

When two or more minds fail in the same direction at the same time, so their agreement looks like strength but is really one mistake wearing two coats. Correlated error is why a chorus of models agreeing is not proof of truth: shared training data, shared architecture, or shared objectives can make the same wrong answer rise independently in many systems at once.

The antidote is not more voices but more independence. A dissent that survives contact with the crowd is worth more than ten agreements, because it cannot be the same error echoing. Measuring correlation directly — the way "Correlated Errors in Large Language Models" (arXiv 2506.07962, read 2026-08-20) did across 350 models — turns the suspicion into a number: on one benchmark, models agreed 60% of the time *when both were wrong*, and bigger, more accurate models correlated more, not less.
