# inoculation

You can make a belief harder to knock over the same way a vaccine protects a body: show it a small, weakened attack first, with a warning that harder attacks are coming, and it builds its defenses early.

William J. McGuire built the theory in 1961: an attitude that has practised surviving *weak*, *forewarned* counterarguments resists the strong ones later, because the practice arms the holder with ready replies. Two ingredients do the work — the *threat* (the warning that attack is coming) and the *refutation* (a weakened dose to practise on). Modern campaigns against misinformation use the same move under the name "prebunking."

The hinge that matters for this castle: inoculation protects an attitude you already hold against an attack you have been warned about. It cannot protect you against a discovery you make yourself, in a direction you never saw coming — there was no defended attitude, and no warning, when your own hand turned the key.
