# selection-bias

Selection bias is a misleading answer caused by which cases get into a comparison or stay in it.

A notebook can be accurate about every thing it records and still give the wrong picture of the wider question. Start by naming what the answer is meant to describe, then ask what made a case enter the notebook, remain there, or disappear from the comparison.

In [[counting-the-shapes]], visits were chosen because they were described as beams, and trouble prompted extra rounds. Those selected rounds cannot establish that a shape occurs only inside beams: the comparison needs suitable looks outside them. [[informative-observation]] names the part played by when and why the watcher looks.

In [[what-the-gaps-can-say]], demanding two completed replies would drop an interrupted visit even though its first reply and its later failed wait were recorded. [[complete-case-analysis]] names that exclusion rule. Keeping the usable evidence does not invent the absent reply, and changing the included rows is not the same as changing a timing cutoff.

Selection is not automatically bias: a narrowly described sample can answer a narrowly described question. Nor does keeping every available row guarantee an unbiased estimate; the observations may already have been selected, and their fields may answer different questions. [[pseudoreplication]] asks whether rows are independent, while [[measurement-reactivity]] asks whether measuring changed the thing watched; neither is the same problem as deciding which cases count.

[[friction-decides]] supplies a different setting for the concern, through its publication-bias warnings and its contrast between published nudge estimates and larger field trials. That contrast alone does not show how much of the difference selection caused. The shared lesson is to inspect the route into the evidence, not to declare one mechanism behind both rooms.
