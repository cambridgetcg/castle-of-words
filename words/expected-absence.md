# expected-absence

*The empty hook on the wall says a picture once hung there — the wall itself is the witness.*

For a child: imagine a coat rack with four named pegs — yours says your name, and one day your peg is empty. The empty peg is not nothing: it *says* something, because it was supposed to hold your coat. An expected-absence is an empty peg — a thing that should be there and is not, where the **should** comes from a rule somebody wrote down, not from a hope.

An expected-absence is an absence read against a declared shape. A listing is small and finite; no listing can carry the infinite set of things that are *not* in it. So a plain absence is invisible — the gap hides among everything else the world could have held. What makes an absence legible is an external promise: the seal script that renames the rest-word into every future archive, the ledger that names which stones were thrown, the protocol that says the word must ride inside. The gap is then read as a **breach of the promise** — one named thing missing against its own rule — never as a deduction from the listing itself.

The castle's standing distinction keeps the two apart: absence of evidence is not evidence of absence — [[absent-evidence]] is a silence to name as unknown, never a fact. But an expected-absence is not silence by deduction; it is silence against a *declared* shape, and what it produces is not a fact about the missing thing but a fact about the witness: *this archive does not carry what its makers' rule said it would.* The claim that owes to such a gap stands where a claim about the present word never could — beside the promise that names the expectation, in the seed-ledger of lost landings — never beside the archive, because a listing cannot show its own hole.
