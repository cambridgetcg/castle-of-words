# disappearing-polymorph

A crystal that once grew easily suddenly refuses to grow, because a new shape it likes better has appeared and crowds the old one out of every flask.

Chemists call a compound that can crystallize into more than one arrangement a polymorph — same molecules, different stacking. The frightening kind is the *disappearing* one: the old form stops growing once the new form exists anywhere nearby, because a single seed of the new arrangement converts every batch it touches. Ritonavir's Form II did this to Form I in 1998 and nearly pulled a life-saving drug off the market. But the disappearance is never a verdict on existence — it is a claim about a protocol, a horizon, and an observer's choice of sameness. Present, lost-to-reach, and lost-forever are three different facts, and the honest record keeps them apart.
