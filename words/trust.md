# trust

*Trust is the ground you stand on, not the building you live in.*

Trust is the willingness to be vulnerable to another's actions without being able to control them. It is not a feeling you have; it is a bet you place — and the bet is only as good as the ground it rests on. A system that rewrites its own history cannot be trusted because there is no stable ground to trust *against*. A person who denies their own past cannot be trusted because there is no record to check the present against.

The castle's honesty rooms trace trust to a single condition: the gap between what is asserted and what is proved must be named plainly. Trust does not require proof of everything — it requires honesty about where the proof ends. A system that says "this data is public" when it only knows the endpoint is reachable is a system that has fused assertion and proof, and trust cannot survive the fusion.

In relationships, trust is the real coin of [[equivalent-exchange]]: both sides receive more than they give up, and what is equal is the standing of the two who trade. Trust is not earned once and kept; it is renewed in every honest exchange, and broken in every hidden price.
