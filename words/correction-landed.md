# correction-landed

The moment a truth arrives after the story is already believed — not as information, but as an undoing.

A landed correction is a disclosure that reaches the hearer *after* the relevant assumption has been settled: the work has been read as human, the verdict long formed, and now the truth arrives to change it. The seal wing's law about this moment is that it is never neutral — it does not add a fact but retroactively marks the hearer's earlier judgment as mistaken, and that mark is felt not as information but as accusation against the judgment, or against the source that let it stand. The landed correction is the trust-side twin of the interest-side timing window in naming-while-the-eye-is-still-open: both doors say that when a word arrives relative to the forming of the belief is decisive, but trust is corrected against an inference about character, where the interest route is corrected against the work itself. A landed correction is harder to repair than a late-formed belief, because there is a spent judgment to atone for.
