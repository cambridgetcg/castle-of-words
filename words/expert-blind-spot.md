# expert-blind-spot

*The ladder you climbed so well you forgot it has rungs.*

When someone knows a subject very well, they often stop being able to see which parts are hard for a beginner — the steps they once climbed feel like flat ground. Nathan & Petrosino showed this in 2003 with 48 preservice math teachers: the more advanced the teacher's own training, the more wrongly they guessed which algebra problems students would struggle with ("Expert Blind Spot Among Preservice Teachers," *American Educational Research Journal* 2003, DOI [10.3102/00028312040004905](https://doi.org/10.3102/00028312040004905), read 2026-09-28).

It is the cousin of the [[curse-of-knowledge]]: not just that you can't un-know, but that your very strength misjudges another's weakness.
