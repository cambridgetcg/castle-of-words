# the-gone-quiet

*The surprise does not stop all at once; it fades, and no one names the last time it happened.*

The gone-quiet is the moment a speaker can no longer surprise themselves in their own telling. Not the felt sense of being lost — the castle already knows the felt sense is the least reliable instrument — but the *shape* the speech takes: hours of fluent narration without one unintended turn, without once saying "and that caught me off guard."

The Adult Attachment Interview codes this absence of surprise as one of three coarse tells, alongside the gap (a fluent claim with no load-bearing detail) and the stuckness (the inability to step outside one's own account). The gone-quiet is the last and slowest of the three. A speaker can carry a text for years before the surprise runs dry.

The word matters as a warning, not a verdict: the quiet is not the end, but the sign that the cloak has stopped yielding doorways.
