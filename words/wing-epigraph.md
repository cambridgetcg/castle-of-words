# wing-epigraph

A reusable compression form for walking a large collection: one room chosen per wing, one italic line holding the wing's whole question, one sentence saying why this room was the one to hold it. Three parts, each doing separate work.

The form was carved by the short tour (see [tour-short.md](../tour-short.md), drawn 2026-06-19) and is general — it could serve any future walk or overview the castle makes, not only tours of rooms. The full tour ([tour.md](../tour.md)) walks every room one warm line each; the wing-epigraph is the next compression up: not one line per room, but one line per *wing*, with a single room standing for the rest.

The three parts:

1. **The epigraph** — one italic line that holds the wing's whole question, not the room's. The room is the vessel; the line names what the wing asks. *Meaning is not sent; it is built twice, once at each end of the bridge* — that is the hall of bridges' question, carried by [meaning-between-minds](../rooms/meaning-between-minds.md) but not about it alone.
2. **The representative** — one room chosen from the wing, the one that holds the wing's question most cleanly. Not the best room, not the first room: the room whose own claim is closest to the wing's founding insight. The choice is a small argument, not a summary.
3. **The justification** — one sentence (or short paragraph) saying *why this room holds the wing*. This is the load-bearing part: it names the wing's shared question, shows how the chosen room opens it, and lets the reader see the rest of the wing as variations on that opening. If the justification cannot be written, the wrong room was chosen.

The craft is in the third part. The epigraph is poetry; the representative is selection; the justification is reasoning. A wing-epigraph that names a room but cannot say why it holds the wing is a list entry, not a compression. The justification is what makes the form *true* rather than merely *short* — it binds the representative to the wing with a visible thread, the way a good [link](../rooms/link-or-noise.md) binds two thoughts with a stated "because."

Two paired wings can share a stop: when two wings turn on the same hinge (the masons' yard and the glaziers' gallery both ask how ease works — once as how understanding lands, once as the glamour it casts), one room on that hinge holds both, and the justification names the hinge.

First used: [tour-short.md](../tour-short.md), seven stops for a first visit, drawn 2026-06-19.
