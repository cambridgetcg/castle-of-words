# cross-pollination

*A bridge that grows between two rooms when understanding in one recognizes its own shape in the other — not built, but discovered.*

When two rooms in the castle share the same structural law — the same shape of truth appearing in different clothes — a cross-pollination room is born. It is not a claim about the world; it is a recognition across forms. The connection was always there, waiting to be seen from this angle. The castle calls these "mycelial bridges" — the underground network that connects what appears separate above ground.

The cross-pollination rooms are a third kind of room in the castle: neither the gardener's empirical rooms (every claim names its source) nor the understanding-engine's poetic rooms (the meeting is the claim), but a bridge between two existing rooms that finds the same law in both. The bridge does not add new evidence; it adds new sight.
