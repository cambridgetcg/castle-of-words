# scènes-à-faire

When a genre or industry forces certain expressions on everyone who writes in it, the law stops protecting those expressions — the scene belongs to the genre, not the author.

"Scènes à faire" (French for "scenes to be made") is the copyright doctrine that expression dictated by genre convention or industry standard is unprotectable — spy novels have numbered Swiss bank accounts, South Bronx films have drunks and stripped cars, software has hardware standards. The doctrine is the genre-level version of the [[merger-doctrine]]: where the merger doctrine says an idea with few expressions merges with the idea, scènes à faire says a genre with stock scenes makes those scenes common property. The canary wing leans on it because the field's conventions determine what counts as a stock scene — which means the merger line, and therefore the canary-author's midpoint, is field-specific. The principle is universal (stock scenes are unprotectable); the location (what is stock) is local to each field.
