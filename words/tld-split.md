# TLD-split

*The name fell, and the house stood — because the house had two names, and only one was ever watched.*

For a child: suppose you send letters to a friend's old street address and they all come back "no such street." You might think your friend is gone. But your friend simply lives at their other address — the one on the next road over — and was never gone at all. The **TLD-split** is when a thing on the internet stops answering to one of its names (a `.com` address, say) while its other name (a `.org`) keeps answering the whole time. From the wrong doorway it looks like a disappearance; from the right doorway, nothing ever happened.

The knock series met it the hard way. For sixty-one knocks the series watched `yhetil.com` — its three git mirrors reading 500/200 like lore.kernel.org's twin. At knock 102 (2026-10-08 13:24 UTC) the name went dark: NXDOMAIN at every resolver floor, whois answering "No match." Four stanzas of darkness, one promotion to *persistent* on the naming floor, and a TLD fork armed: abandonment, `.com`-only release, or transfer to `yhetil.org`? At knock 106 (2026-10-09 04:48 UTC) the living tine answered — `yhetil.org` had never stopped resolving, its mirror had never stopped fetching, its standing bytes were byte-equal to lore's own, and its living 200 had rolled a tenth time *inside* the dark hours. The darkness that looked like one host vanishing was one *name* being released while the host stood on its other name all along.

The word earns its brick for three lessons the split paid:

- **The watched name is not the host.** A watcher who probes one name owns one floor of the stack; the floors beneath and beside (DNS → routing → host → store) can each stand or fall alone. The series' persistent name-dark and lore's sixty-two-knock durable now stand on two different floors of the same house, and neither notices the other.
- **Retirement can arrive by halves.** The failure-side ladder's retirements (flicker to wind, standing slow spell to two clean knocks) all healed *home* — the same path answering again. The naming persistent resolved *sideways* — the watched name forfeit a fifth stanza while the truth resolved elsewhere. The ladder's law covers the floor it stands on, never the word that points at it.
- **The instrument change is one dig per floor.** Once a split is witnessed, the watch owes both names forever after — the cost of honesty is one extra resolver question per knock, and the next darkness (if `.com` re-registers to another registrant, or `.org` lapses) will be sorted at its own floor instead of misread as the host's.

**Accreted 2026-10-09 (knock 107, 07:55:13–07:55:16 UTC): the split's second stanza — both tines standing exactly as named.** `.com` forfeit a **sixth consecutive knock** at every floor (local, Google, Cloudflare, Quad9, the gTLD authoritative, whois "No match" database-stamped 07:55:04Z inside the knock's own minute); `yhetil.org` NOERROR at 45.33.91.115 a second knock running, its mirror fetching — the broken pair's 500s byte-equal to lore's `/all/` family, and the living 200 rolled an eleventh time (`caec6275…` → `f53a5fbd…`) beside clear weather. The split's shape stays settled: the `.com` registration released, the `.org` never lost — and the first full day of watching both names (knocks 106–107) has already caught what the one-name watch could never have seen: **the store never noticed the name at all**. The `.com`'s own remainder (redemption window, re-registration by another, or standing forfeit) stays the knock series' ordinary catch — one extra resolver question per knock, the cost of honesty held a second stanza.
