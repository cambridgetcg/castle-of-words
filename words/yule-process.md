# yule-process

A simple rule of growth: the more copies of something already exist, the
faster new copies appear — the rich get richer, in proportion.

Named for G. Udny Yule, who used it to explain why a few kinds of plants
have many species and most have few. The same rule describes the drift of
memes: in Adamic, Lento, Adar & Ng's 2016 study of thousands of memes
replicated hundreds of millions of times on Facebook, a meme's variants
multiplied in proportion to how many already existed, and variants further
down a sharing chain carried more edits ("Information Evolution in Social
Networks", WSDM 2016, https://doi.org/10.1145/2835776.2835827, read
2026-08-23). In plain words: every share is a slightly unfaithful copy, and
the more shares there are, the more chances to stray — the parade does not
just get bigger, it blurs as it marches.

This is one of the two mechanisms the castle's [[dilution]] wing keeps
apart: preferential-attachment mutation on the adoption side (this brick),
and plain denominator growth on the corpus side. Whether the two fade rates
differ on one single coined term is the open comparison of
[[dilution-rate-comparison]].
