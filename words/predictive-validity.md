# predictive-validity

*A ruler is not a ruler because it looks straight — it is a ruler because it tells you what will fit where next year.*

Predictive validity is the idea that a measurement taken today should tell you something true about what happens tomorrow. A ruler that only measures things you already have is a snapshot; a ruler that tells you what will fit where next year is a predictor. In psychology, a scale has predictive validity if the score it gives at time 1 predicts an outcome at time 2 — and the Continuing Bonds Scale's internalized-vs-externalized distinction is the specific kind of claim that needs this test: does the way someone stays connected to the dead today predict their grief tomorrow? The castle's continuing-bonds-longitudinal question is a predictive-validity question: the scale exists, but whether it predicts the future has not been tested with a longitudinal design that controls for baseline grief.
