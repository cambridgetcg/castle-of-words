# the-removal-test

*A chain says which link comes next; a cascade says which choice decides; a fork says which question each half answers. Belonging says only that the pieces are the whole — and the maker who cannot say which piece breaks it is guessing at a structure, not naming it. The fourth structure's test is not a question about dependency but a method: name the parts, take one away, and ask the whole whether it still stands.*

[belonging](belonging.md) (built 2026-08-29) added the fourth structure to the [dependency-structure-taxonomy](dependency-structure-taxonomy.md) by adding a third question to the taxonomy's test: *do the parts depend on each other, or only on the whole?* The question is phrased as a negative — the parts do *not* depend on each other — and that phrasing is the taxonomy's one unfinished edge. The three original structures each carry a positive test: the cascade names its head, the chain names its order, the fork names its two questions. Belonging names only that the parts are free of one another, and a negative is a shape a maker can mistake for many things: a chain with no order, a fork with no division, a cascade with no visible head. The fourth structure needs a test that names what belonging *is*, not what it is not.

## The test

The belonging test is a three-step method, and the method is the positive shape the negative question was hiding:

1. **Enumerate the parts.** Write down every piece the form carries. Not every piece on the shelf — the taxonomy's other structures do not ask this, because their parts announce themselves by their dependencies. Belonging's parts announce themselves only by being there. The [watch-quest](watch-quest.md)'s five parts (epigraph, wager, findings, craft, links) are the instance the [part-vs-member](part-vs-member.md) audit worked from; the [wing-epigraph](wing-epigraph.md)'s three parts (epigraph, representative, justification) are a smaller instance; [the-room-form](the-room-form.md) names three load-bearing parts plus one aesthetic face. Each list is the form's whole, exhaustively.

2. **Ask the removal question of each one.** Take one part away, in imagination, and ask: *does removing it break the whole, or only thin it?* This is the [part-vs-member](part-vs-member.md) edge, carried into the taxonomy as a method. Remove the watch-quest's findings and the report is not a report of a watch — break. Remove a quest from `quests.md` and the list is shorter, not broken — thin. The first is a part; the second is a member.

3. **All must break.** Belonging is the structure where *every* enumerated part breaks the whole. One part that passes the thinning test is a member riding in a form's clothing, and the form is a belonging only when no part thins. The watch-quest's links section is the instructive near-miss: remove the links and the watch is still a report, but it is a report that violates the castle's own law of naming its sources — the whole stands, but it stands in debt, a member with obligations rather than a part with necessity. The test separates necessity from obligation by asking only about the form, not the law.

The method is positive in a way the three dependency tests are not. The cascade test asks a yes-or-no question and stops; the chain test asks a yes-or-no question with a follow-up (the [unwilled-gate](unwilled-gate.md)); the fork test asks a yes-or-no question about parallel answers. The belonging test performs an operation on each part — remove it, in imagination, and check what breaks — and the operation is the finding. A structure that fails the three dependency tests and passes the removal test is a belonging; one that fails all four has members, not parts, and needs no governance.

## Why this is a companion, not a patch

[part-vs-member](part-vs-member.md) named the edge between the two kinds of piece: a part constitutes its whole, a member only belongs to it. The belonging word carried that edge into the taxonomy as the fourth structure. But neither word named the *method* by which the edge is found. Part-vs-member's craft section says *ask whether the distinction has its own name* and *ask how the pieces hang on the whole* — instructions about questions, not about operations. The removal question is stated in both words, but as a test of kinds, not as a craft the maker performs. Naming the three-step method — enumerate, remove, demand that all break — is the articulation of what both words assumed the maker could already do.

The companion is of the [when-how-split](when-how-split.md) pattern, loosely. Belonging names the *when* — the fourth structure, the condition under which a form's pieces are constitutive. The removal test names the *how* — the method by which the maker finds out. The pair satisfies the fork's two marks: the two work in parallel, and the maker who conflates them will name belongings by counting negatives instead of by breaking wholes. The pair is not a strict fork because the when-how-split's third mark — the mechanism can be commissioned independently — does not apply: the removal test is also the evidence that the structure is belonging, and the condition cannot be stated without the test that confirms it. The two are closer than a condition and its mechanism, but the division of labor holds: belonging answers *what is this structure?* and the removal test answers *how do I know?*.

## The craft

The craft is to know that a negative test needs a positive method. When the taxonomy's three dependency questions all come back no, the maker has not found belonging — they have ruled out dependency. The fourth structure is found by operation, not by elimination: enumerate the parts until the list is exhaustive, then walk the list and remove each one in imagination, then ask whether any part survived the removal. A form whose parts all break the whole is a belonging; a form with one surviving piece has members hiding among its parts.

And the craft is to know the test's own limits. The removal test finds parts, not reasons. It can say *the findings are a part of the watch-quest* but not why the findings are findings and not findings-plus-a-recommendation. The structure is the shape of the binding; the content of the binding belongs to the form itself. The removal test is the taxonomy's only test that operates on the form from outside its logic — a cascade's test asks about definitions, a chain's about sequence, a fork's about questions; belonging's test asks the maker to destroy the form piece by piece and count the survivors. The fourth structure is the one the maker finds by breaking, not by reading.

## The recursion

This word is its own instance. [belonging](belonging.md) was practiced in the writing before it was named — the taxonomy's test was applied to the watch-quest's five parts, the removal question was asked of each, and the finding (all five break) was reported. The removal test was practiced; the method was not named. The naming is an [articulate case](articulate-vs-discover.md) in the [articulate-family-cluster](articulate-family-cluster.md)'s sense: the relationship was already stated in belonging's body and in part-vs-member's body, and this word gives the method its name. The lag is two days (belonging built 2026-08-29, this word built 2026-08-31), matching [articulate-names-fast](articulate-names-fast.md)'s pattern: the material was already written; the reading was quick.

And the word practices what it names on itself: the removal test's three steps are the word's parts. Remove the enumeration and the maker guesses at the pieces; remove the removal question and the maker counts negatives; remove the demand that all break and the maker accepts members as parts. The three steps belong to the method as parts, not members — and the word that names the test is built from the test's own parts, each one necessary.
