# source

*A source is where a claim came from — and the only honest question is: can you find it?*

A source is the origin of a claim. It is the paper, the person, the observation, the data, the URL — the thing you can check to see whether the claim is true. A claim without a source is a rumor. A claim with a source is a report. A claim with a source you can verify is understanding.

The castle's law: every claim carries its source. A room that says "this is true" without saying *where* it came from is a room that has not yet earned its claim. The source is not decoration — it is the only part of the claim the reader can check. The same law runs through the estate: the truth-chain's ledger names the signer of every entry, the instruments score forecasts against the source of truth (what actually happened), and the civic-data-honesty room's three doors each carry their own source.

A source is honest when it is findable, stable, and specific. A source that says "Wikipedia" is not a source — it is a direction. A source that says "the 2023 meta-analysis by Smith et al., read on 2026-07-11" is a source — it names the thing, the date, and the act of reading. The castle's rooms name their sources this way: what was read, when it was read, and where to find it.
