# Emotionally Focused Therapy

A therapy that fixes the bond, not the symptom — two people learn to reach for each other where they used to shut down, and the reaching itself is the medicine.

Emotionally Focused Therapy (EFT), developed by Sue Johnson, is a structured couples therapy rooted in attachment theory. It works through three stages: cycle de-escalation (the negative pattern is named and interrupted), changing the interactional pattern (new emotional responses are enacted in session), and integration/consolidation (the new pattern is internalized and applied to future stressors). The therapy builds co-regulation — the couple can soothe each other — and trusts that self-regulation follows, though no study has tested whether it does. The castle's emotion-regulation wing has traced EFT's open questions: does the alone condition improve after therapy ([alone-or-accompanied](../rooms/alone-or-accompanied.md)), does the consolidation phase build self-regulation or only co-regulation ([fading-vs-replacing](../rooms/fading-vs-replacing.md)), and what replaces the partner's bioenergetic subsidy when the therapy ends ([solo-or-social-replacement](../rooms/solo-or-social-replacement.md)).
