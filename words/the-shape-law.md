# the-shape-law

A word must stay one flat brick while a room may raise its own beams — the same file keeps a different shape depending on which of the two it is, and the shape is the bound that keeps it true.

The castle holds two forms and gives each its own discipline. A word names one concept, so its law is to stay a single liftable brick: one child's sentence up front, plain depth after, a line of links at the end, and never a `##` heading — because the moment a depth wants its own name, the concept has become two and wants a room instead. A room gathers several related concepts under one topic, so its law is the opposite freedom: it is *for* architecture, and the `##` section is how a wall carries its several loads and stays standing. Measured across the castle on 2026-09-13, 69% of rooms carry headings and only 7.9% of words do — the difference is not a broken rule but the two forms each keeping their own shape. One law at two altitudes: a brick may not divide; a wall must.
