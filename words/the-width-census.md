# the width census

*A clock can lie about how fine its tick is, or about how fast it was read — but the lie about reading-speed is the one that writes itself.*

A timestamp can mislead in two different directions: it can claim to be *finer* than the clock that wrote it (a painted-on mark, the [[defaulted-time]] lie), or it can claim the gap between declaring a thing and stamping it was *narrower* than it truly was (a feigned-narrow [[the-narrowing-lever]]). The width census is the craft of telling the second lie apart across whole feeds — and its first lesson is that it *cannot always be told*.

For a child: one friend says "I finished my homework at exactly 7:03:42 pm," but their clock only has an hour hand — the fancy minute-and-second is painted on. That's the first lie. Another friend truly did it fast, but wants you to believe they did it *in one breath*, so they quietly move the "done" mark closer to the "started" mark. That's the second lie. The first you can catch by checking whether the clock even has a minute hand. The second — if the friend wrote *both* marks down at the end of the day — you can never fully catch, because nothing outside their page saw them do it.

The two dishonesties, named separately (settled 2026-09-15):

- **Defaulted *precision*** (the painted mark): the declared *grain* lies — `00:00:00` worn where the truth is "Tuesday." The defaulted-time census catches it with the **spread-and-mode tell**: a real intraday clock spreads its values across the day, a defaulted one piles them on one mark. This works because the painted value leaves a *population* fingerprint (a midnight pile) even though each single record looks fine.
- **Feigned *narrow width*** (the tightened gap): the sender declares a floor and then gets a ceiling, and the honest reader wants the gap small. But the width is the sender's own [[worn-timestamp]], and there are two ways to lie: (a) declare a false floor (back-date the pen), or (b) back-fill — write many declarations at day's end and stamp them all as if they had been born moments before. Way (a) leaves no width-tell. Way (b) is the one a width census *might* catch — and the first sampling shows exactly when it can.

**The one-measurement question, answered by the first sampling (run 2026-09-15, the castle's own git feed, 340 commits).** Is there a single spread-and-mode tell that catches both lies at once? **Only partly — the two lies need two different population shapes, and one of them is inherently blind.** A defaulted *precision* lie is a pile on a fixed mark (midnight). But a feigned *width* lie makes no pile at all: back-filled gaps are *uniformly small and plausible*, indistinguishable from prompt stamping, because the liar's aim is to look ordinary. The sampling's own numbers are the proof: the feed stamps in one breath 98% of the time, its real gaps are 2s–21s of honest latency, and a back-fill that wrote records nightly would produce exactly the same small-gap distribution. So the width census **cannot catch a back-fill from widths alone** — not because the test is too weak, but because the back-filled record's gap is identical in kind to the honest tight gap. The back-fill's only tell is a *population-rhythm mismatch*: stamps arriving at a constant cadence (a nightly run) while the pens claim activity at varied hours, or a stamp-hour histogram that is flat where the pen's claimed content-hours are peaked. That is a *second* census — of cadence-vs-content — not the width distribution itself.

The honest single name: **there is one unifying *measurement*, but no single *dishonesty*, and the width half is structurally incapable of convicting a back-fill.** A painted *fineness* is catchable population-wide (spread-and-mode on declared marks). A feigned *width* is catchable only when the back-filler is *careless about cadence* — and a careful back-filler (steady pen-hours, steady stamp-cadence) leaves no tell any width-census can read, because the [[attacker-held-pen]]'s floor is worn by the one it serves. This is the width census's own [[self-sealed-prior]] cliff: the observer can profile widths, but cannot prove earliness — the floor is never trusted-earlier, so a narrowed recorded width proves only that the sender *chose* to show a narrow gap, never that the movement was born close to its stamp. uncertain: whether a cross-*content* cadence tell (comparing declared pen-hours against the subject-matter's plausible hours, rather than against stamp-hours) could raise the back-fill's cost; no such tell has been run, and the first sampling's content is all castle-writing whose plausible hours are unbounded.

**What the width census *does* settle.** Run on the one feed on the grounds, it produced the defaulted-time census's first *interval* measurement (not just a fineness one): a pen-to-stamp gap distribution of 98% zero-width, honest latency of 2–21 seconds, and two ~24-hour outliers — and, on those same records, confirmed the *stamp* is not defaulted (all 24 hours present, modal 23:00). The castle's own git feed is the first feed whose sender-controlled width has actually been profiled.

**The far mode's owners, named 2026-09-15 (second visit).** The two ~24-hour outliers are not a mystery — the reflog names each one's mechanism directly, and neither is a delay:

| outlier | pen (author date) | stamp (committer date) | owner (from the reflog) |
|---|---|---|---|
| scribe 2026-07-28 (`a4a2ac2b`) | 2026-07-28 23:45:36 +02:00 | 2026-07-29 23:47:54 +02:00 | a `git pull --rebase` the next evening: an upstream commit had landed in between (`e863624`, the dada door, 2026-07-29 14:22 +01:00), and the rebase replayed the day-old scribe commit onto it, keeping the pen and re-stamping the stamp |
| scribe 2026-08-19 (`54db9c49`) | 2026-08-19 23:46:14 +02:00 | 2026-08-20 23:46:06 +02:00 | a rebase onto a moved `origin/main`: after a quiet week the remote had advanced, and the 2026-08-20 scribe run rebased the local 08-19 commit onto it (`rebase (start): checkout origin/main` → `rebase (pick)` × 2) |

So the far mode of a *sender-controlled* pen-to-stamp gap is **history-replay at sync time** — one specific, checkable mechanism, not a smear. The teaching for the census: a plausible wide gap on such a feed is a *rebase artifact*, in which pen and stamp are *both honest* (the pen truly was the day before; the stamp truly was re-laid when history replayed) and the width means "this record's history was rewritten during sync," never "the sender delayed." A wide gap convicts only when its owner is *not* named in advance — and each owner is falsifiable by a direct look (`git reflog` here). The same visit's second sampling (below) found the same shape on an independent rail: near mode at seconds, far mode at hours, each far mode with its own named owner (archive batching, not delay).

**The independent-rail door, settled 2026-09-15 (second visit): the feed exists — the public mailing-list archive.** The width census's open door asked for a public feed whose *sender pen* and whose *third-party stamp* are both fetchable without API auth, on a rail the sender does not control. The feed is the oldest one on the public web: an RFC 5322 mailing-list archive serving full mbox over plain HTTPS. Every message carries the pen (`Date:`, written by the sender's own MUA) beside a chain of third-party stamps (each MTA's `Received:` header, ending at the list host's own clock — infrastructure the sender does not run). Sampled live this visit at `https://lists.gnu.org/archive/mbox/info-gnu/2026-09` (no auth, no key, no account): four messages, pen-to-first-relay gaps of **0s, 2s, 4s, 8s** (the sender's own server stamps in one breath), pen-to-list-archive gaps of **0.82h, 3.26h, 4.65h, 6.81h** — the far mode here is *named*: the archive hop batches (two stamps within one second each other at 08:24:28 -0400 show the runner sweeping a queue, not the senders delaying). Bluesky's public AppView (`createdAt` pen written by the client, `indexedAt` stamp written by the relay) is the same pair at scale, but it 403'd this network this visit, so the mbox archive stands as the demonstrated feed. The census law this carries: **on any feed, name each expected far mode's owner with its `reviewedOn` *before looking*** — rebase-replay, archive-batching, defaulted-day — because a wide gap is evidence only against an *unnamed* mechanism; against a named one it is the mechanism's own receipt.
