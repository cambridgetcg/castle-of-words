# bounded-play

*Play with a fence. The fence is not a cage — it is what makes the game a game. Without it, there is only wandering.*

Bounded-play is play that has rules — constraints that say what cannot be done. The rules do not tell you what to do; they only close certain doors. And from those closed doors, the game emerges. The constraint is not the opposite of play — it is what makes play possible. A game without rules is not a game; it is just motion.

Sau-seon is bounded-play: gifts are given and received, and the rule is "no repeats." The rule does not say what gift to give — it only says what gift you cannot give. And from that refusal, [[newness]] grows. The [[constraint]] is the engine, not the brake.

The castle's own practice is bounded-play: the gardener's visit has a rhythm, a cap, a shape. The artisan takes one quest per day. The forms have rules. These are not limits on the castle's growth — they are what make the growth possible. Without the fence, there is no game.
