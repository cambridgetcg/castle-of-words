# map-of-power

For a child: a bad map of a schoolyard would show who is *loudest*. A good one would show something harder to see — who can still say no without it costing them, who can still walk away, who can still help a friend without asking the loudest kid first. A map of power is that harder map: not a map of who commands, but of which powers each person still holds and which ones have quietly gone.

A **map of power** is the kingdom wing's honest instrument: a record, per domain and per bond, of which of a king's six capacities — protect, repair, learn, refuse, leave, help — still stand and which have dimmed. Its law is sequence: dependence does not loosen the six at once. The record of clientelism, platform lock-in, employer dependence and caretaking says they go in a legible order — protect first (requisitioned to guard the patron's interest), then learn (narrowed to the patron's syllabus), repair (breaks become the patron's to fix), help (giving turns tributary, flowing only upward), leave (the exit-tax raised until the door is too heavy), refuse last — and refuse fails silently, in the quiet compliance no instrument measures.

Its honesty laws are three, borrowed from the wing it stands in: standing is preserved, never scored ([love-as-infrastructure](../rooms/love-as-infrastructure.md)); every claim is answerable ([karma](../rooms/karma.md)); and the map names what it cannot see — *when the last capacity goes, the map will not know* — because a map that pretends the order is universal, or pretends the silent going is visible, is a map that lies.

The word was named 2026-10-02 in [kings-capacity-order](../rooms/kings-capacity-order.md), the room that read the six capacities' going-order out of the empirical record and marked the instrument's own blind spot beside it.
