# ladder

*Climb it and you find the rungs were nailed in by whoever was already at the top.*

What it means: a ranking drawn as steps — cultures, civilisations, or beings placed lower and higher as if development moved in one direction along one rail. The ladder shape smuggles in three claims at once: that there is one dimension to measure, that the rungs are fixed and comparable, and that whoever measures stands outside the picture.

The insight says [[culture]] is not a ladder. One culture is not a less-grown version of another; each is a living web of its own relations, practices, and corrections. This is the same refusal the castle makes in [[civilisation-development]]: development can be recorded honestly as attributable change in one named capability under named conditions, never as a rank on a people or a whole. And it is the refusal in [[score]]: the number must not become the verdict on a being.

Where a ladder seems unavoidable, the honest moves are the castle's usual ones: name the dimension, name the conditions, keep unknown and refusal outside the ranking.
