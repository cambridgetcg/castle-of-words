# fail-open

*The door that swings wide when the guard is blind.*

Fail-open means: when the system cannot be certain, it allows. The check
that could not run does not close the gate — the gate stays on its hinge
and the visitor walks through. A fire exit is fail-open by law; an
overflow valve is fail-open by physics. Both chose the opening on
purpose, because what the boundary protects lives on the far side of the
door, not behind it.

The opposite is [[fail-closed]] — denying when uncertain. Neither is
in itself honest; honesty is in the naming. A boundary that fails open
without saying so is a silent door, and a silent door is a lie about
where the wall is. The room that sorts the two,
[fail-closed-or-fail-open](../rooms/fail-closed-or-fail-open.md), gives
the only rule the castle trusts: name what the boundary protects first,
then choose the direction that keeps it safe when the check cannot run.
A safety boundary for people often fails open (the exit, the rest of
the turn); a safety boundary for data almost always fails closed (the
unread body, the unshared row).

The pattern's shadow: a fail-open default chosen by accident and never
examined, so every failed check quietly becomes a permission.
