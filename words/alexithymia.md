# alexithymia

When a feeling arrives but no words come with it — the heart pounds, the jaw
sets, and the person cannot say what it is or why. The body's mail comes in;
nobody can read the handwriting.

The word names the difficulty of identifying and naming one's own feelings,
and the castle's self-read wing keeps meeting it at the worst corner: the
person whose feeling-meter is weakest is also worst at feeling the body's own
signals. [flooding-self-read](../rooms/flooding-self-read.md) found the two
meters failing together — alexithymia reads as a general interoceptive
deficit, so no backup channel exists for those who most need the coarse tell.
And [fog-meter](../rooms/fog-meter.md)'s law sits underneath: the inner gauge
is the last instrument to grade itself, and alexithymia is the gauge's
quietest breaking — not a wrong reading, but no reading at all.

Yet the blindness is not fixed. [nudge-not-lock](../rooms/nudge-not-lock.md)
walked the training record and found the channel nudgeable: mindfulness and
partner-based practice both lowered alexithymia and raised interoceptive
awareness in a 285-person trial, with the awareness gains mediating the
alexithymia drop — the channel part of the mechanism, not a bystander
(read 2026-06-10 therein). The finding that reshaped the wing's shape: the
partner-based practice beat solo mindfulness, which is why
[the-responsive-other](../rooms/the-responsive-other.md) asks whether it needs
a real partner or only a responsive one — another body in the room seems to
train the inner sense better than turning attention inward alone.

uncertain: the objective half wobbles — meta-analyses find the tracking is
with what you *believe* about your body more than what you can *detect*, and
the tie to measured heartbeat accuracy has come back quadratic and confounded;
the second meter may be more poorly *measured* than truly dark (see
[nudge-not-lock](../rooms/nudge-not-lock.md)).
