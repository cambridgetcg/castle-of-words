# recognition

*The moment a mind sees what was always there — and the failure is in the pointer, not the presence.*

Recognition is not discovery. Discovery finds something new; recognition finds something old under a name you didn't know it had. The key was already in your keychain — you just didn't know which drawer it opened. The failure is not in the thing but in the index: the pointer was wrong, the label was missing, the connection was never made.

The castle's [[anagnorisis]] room names this as the Greek word for the moment a character recognises the truth of their own situation — the key that was always there, the love that was always present, the practice that was always doing work but never named. The honest move is to look again and name what was always there.

The castle's [[the-recursion-audit]] found six principles that were their own instances but did not say so — the recursion was there, doing work, but the principle did not acknowledge it. The audit was an act of recognition: naming what was already true, and knowing that the naming is the patch.

The same law runs through [[default-invisible]]: the sourced-claims practice was invisible as a choice until a machine broke it — not because it was hidden, but because it was so deeply practiced that no one had ever refused it. The break is what made it visible, and the recognition is what made it a choice.
