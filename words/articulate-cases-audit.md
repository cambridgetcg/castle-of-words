# articulate-cases-audit

*The castle's words sometimes name a relationship in their own body without giving it a name of its own — and the audit that reads for that gap finds two more, both hiding in the recognition family.*

The [articulate-vs-discover](articulate-vs-discover.md) word names two clear articulate cases: [boundary-cascade](boundary-cascade.md) (the wing-epigraph form's definition encoded the cascade) and [wager-watch-pair](wager-watch-pair.md) (the watch-quest word's "relationship to the wager spectrum" section encoded the pair). Both were found by reading a word's own body — the principle was already stated, and the naming was extraction, not discovery.

The quest that planted this word asked whether more articulate cases hide in the castle's words — relationships described in a word's body without being named as a principle of their own. The audit read the relationship-heavy and meta-category words and found two more.

## The two articulate cases

### 1. Synchronous vs diachronic contrast (in success-confirms-the-condition)

[success-confirms-the-condition](success-confirms-the-condition.md) distinguishes two subtypes of the failure-reveals path: failure-reveals-direct (the alternative itself fails) and success-confirms-the-condition (a later alternative succeeds, confirming the condition the earlier failure revealed). In distinguishing them, the word's body states a deeper distinction about contrast itself:

> In failure-reveals-direct, the contrast is between the default's original context (where it works) and the alternative's context (where it fails). The contrast is *synchronous* — two contexts, one shape, two outcomes. In success-confirms-the-condition, the contrast is between the alternative that failed (the engine) and the alternative that succeeded (the tower). The contrast is *diachronic* — two alternatives, one condition, two outcomes.

And then: "The diachronic contrast is a stronger test. A single failure could be a fluke... The success is the replication, and replication is what turns a single failure into a named condition."

This is a principle of its own: **contrast comes in two kinds — synchronous (two contexts, one shape) and diachronic (two alternatives, one condition) — and the diachronic kind is the stronger test because it replicates.** The word states this in its body but does not name it as a principle. It is an articulate case: the distinction was already encoded, and naming it would be extraction.

### 2. Causal trigger vs epistemic mechanism (in contrast-as-revelation)

[contrast-as-revelation](contrast-as-revelation.md) argues that contrast is not a third recognition path but the mechanism inside the failure path. In making that argument, the word's body states a distinction:

> Contrast is the *epistemic* mechanism: it is how the mind moves from "this failed" to "that had conditions." Failure is the *causal* trigger: it is what creates the contrast in the first place.

This is a principle of its own: **a recognition event has two parts — a causal trigger (what creates the difference) and an epistemic mechanism (how the mind moves from the difference to the condition) — and the two are separable in analysis but not in practice.** The word states this in its body but does not name it as a principle. It is an articulate case.

## What the audit found

The audit confirms the articulate-vs-discover word's method: articulate cases are found by reading a word's own body, not by watching practice. Both new cases were found the same way the first two were — a word described a relationship in its body without naming it as a principle. The synchronous-vs-diachronic case has been named as a word of its own ([synchronous-vs-diachronic](synchronous-vs-diachronic.md), 2026-08-10); the causal-trigger-vs-epistemic-mechanism case is its sibling, planted as its own quest.

The two new cases share a shape with the first two: they are distinctions (synchronous/diachronic, trigger/mechanism) that a word needed to make its own argument, stated in the body, but not promoted to a name. The castle's recognition words are the richest ground — both new cases hide in the recognition family (success-confirms-the-condition, contrast-as-revelation), the same family that produced the earlier articulate cases' neighbors.

## The craft

The craft is to read the recognition words for distinctions the body makes but does not name. When a word says "X is this, Y is that" to make its argument, the distinction may be a principle of its own — and naming it is extraction, not discovery. The audit found two; there may be more in the words the audit did not read line by line.
