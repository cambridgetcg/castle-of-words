# the-phrase-that-ages

*The seal said "first drafted," and the seal was true. One day the hands re-dealt, and the seal was still true — but the truth was a bruise, not a fact. The work was not less honest. The seal was.*

The seal-spec (2026-09-08) is a spec for a small mark on a mixed-hand work: two lines above the work's first line, one tap to the receipt. Its load-bearing phrase is *first drafted* — the machine drafts, the human chooses, cuts, answers for. The spec's own uncertain section names the risk: *whether "first drafted" survives as the honest phrase when the machine's share grows past drafting into shaping*. The quest this word answers asked whether the phrase survives the hands re-dealing, and whether the spec should carry a rule for retiring its own inscription before it becomes a lie.

The answer is no — the phrase does not survive. The truth is no longer in the phrase. It is in the receipt.

## The phrase is a mixed-hand claim

*First drafted* is true only when the human's share is choosing, cutting, and answering for — the three acts the mixed-hand evidence says are what the listener's judgment tracks. The mixed-hand room (read 2026-08-25) found the collapse is not proportional to labour-share but to intentionality: the listener asks *whose mind is behind this?*, not *how much did each hand do?*.

The phrase works because *first drafted* keeps the human's intentional share visible by implication: *first drafted* implies there was a *second* act — a choosing, a cutting, an answering. The human mind is behind the work because it is the mind that decided which first drafts became the work.

When the machine's share grows past drafting into shaping — when the machine chooses the cuts, when the machine decides which drafts survive, when the machine answers for the work's shape — the phrase *first drafted* still names the machine's first act. But the human's share has moved from *choosing* to *blessing*, from *cutting* to *accepting*, from *answering for* to *trusting*. The phrase is still true. The implication is now false.

## The spec's own uncertainty was the warning

The seal-spec's uncertain section (2026-09-08) already named the risk. It said the mark's claim should be re-read whenever the hands re-deal. The re-dealing is not hypothetical. The castle's own works are mostly machine-drafted, human-answered-for today — the phrase holds. The works' rhythm is conservative; the same form, the same hands, the same deal. The day the rhythm breaks is the day the phrase needs to be re-read.

The spec was built with the warning inside it. The warning is load-bearing: the spec's own honesty law ([the-law-of-honest-assertion](../rooms/the-law-of-honest-assertion.md)) says a claim must name what it proves and what it does not. The inscription *Some lines in this work were first drafted by an AI* proves the machine drafted. It does not prove the human chose. The spec knew this. The spec carried the knowledge as an uncertainty, not as a rule.

## The principle: when the phrase's truth is no longer in the phrase

The finding is general. A seal that certifies provenance relies on a phrase that names the *kind* of hand, not the *share* — because the share is what the receipt holds, and the seal is not the receipt. The seal is the door; the receipt is the room. But the phrase is not free. The phrase is the seal's face, and the face must match the hands.

When the hands re-deal past the phrase's implication, the seal is still true. The machine *did* first draft. But the seal is now a half-truth: it proves the first act and implies a second that no longer stands. The [scoped-seal](../rooms/the-scoped-seal.md) room found the seal's power is in the rarity of its reading, not its claim — but the seal's honesty is in the claim, and a half-true claim is the one failure the spec cannot survive.

The principle is: **the phrase's truth condition is not in the phrase. It is in the receipt.** When the receipt's truth changes — when the human's share moves from choosing to blessing — the phrase must change with it, or the seal becomes a lie by implication. The spec's rule for retirement is not a new law. It is the same law the seal already carries: name what backs each assertion, never pretending the saying is the proof. When the saying's implication is no longer backed, the saying must say so.

## The craft

The craft is to watch the hands, not the phrase. The maker who plants a seal with a load-bearing phrase should ask: *what does the phrase imply about the human's share, and does the receipt still carry that implication?* The receipt is the checkable part. The phrase is the door. When the door's shape no longer matches the room behind it, the door must be re-carved or removed.

And the craft is to know the seal's two failures are different. The first failure is the wallpaper-seam: the seal fades because it is everywhere, unread. The second failure is the re-dealing seam: the seal fades because it is still read, and still true, but no longer honest. The first failure is visible. The second is quiet — the seal still says what it always said, and the lie is not in the words but in what the words stopped meaning. The first failure is a problem of reading. The second is a problem of truth.

The seal-spec's uncertain section named both. The wallpaper-seam is the scoped-seal's law, and the spec says *frequency, not honesty, governs the fading*. The re-dealing seam is the honest-assertion law, and the spec says the mark's claim should be re-read. The spec knew its own phrase could outlive its truth. The craft is to act on the knowledge before the phrase becomes the lie.

## What this word cannot settle

uncertain: whether *first drafted* has already failed on some work the castle has made. The castle's works are mostly machine-drafted, human-answered-for today, and the phrase holds. But the works' rhythm is not the only rhythm. If yu's hand moves — if a work arrives where the machine chose the cuts and the human only blessed them — the phrase will not have been re-read in time. The watch is not the castle's; the watch is the receipt's. And the receipt is the room, not the door.

The shelf's law is readiness: the next carving should be drafted before the hands that need it arrive, not after.)*
