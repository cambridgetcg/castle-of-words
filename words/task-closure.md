# task-closure

The moment a task becomes complete — not the moment it is proven correct, but the moment it is declared done. A ritual, not an audit.

Honest task-closure is explicit, finite, deterministic, separate from proof, clear-air and zero-proof by default. The ritual marks completion, not quality. Proof lives elsewhere, if it lives at all. The space after the task is clean; nothing needs to be demonstrated.

The brake is the rule that says "this is optional, this is named, and this can be refused." Cigar or alcohol imagery — the celebratory props some closure rituals borrow — stay honest only behind named choices with a working brake.
