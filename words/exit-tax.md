# exit tax

An exit tax is a toll a federation charges at the door — a cost laid on the leaver for the act of leaving.

The secession literature names it plainly: one of the standard arguments against a right to secede is "minimization of strategic bargaining," and its instrument is the exit tax — making departure expensive so that the threat of it cannot be used as leverage in every quarrel (Secession, Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secession — read 2026-08-31). The exit tax is the exact mirror-image of the leaver's floor: where the floor lowers the cost of leaving so the right is real, the exit tax raises the cost of leaving so the right is rarely used. Both are deliberate manipulations of the same dial — the price of walking out — and they point in opposite directions. The floor says the right matters more than the stability; the tax says the stability matters more than the right. The [[the-leavers-floor]] room found floors of three kinds but never a living bridge; the exit tax is the proof by existence that federations do reach for this dial, and that the documented reaching, so far, is in the direction of walls, not bridges.
