# grief

The deep sorrow that comes from losing someone or something loved — not a problem to solve but a process that reshapes the one who carries it.

Grief is the emotional, cognitive, and physical response to loss. It is not a single feeling but a constellation: sadness, anger, yearning, numbness, guilt, relief — often cycling rather than progressing in stages. The dominant 20th-century model (Freud's "grief work") said healthy grief requires detaching from the deceased. The continuing bonds model (Klass, Silverman & Nickman, 1996) challenged this: maintaining a connection with the deceased is normal, not pathological. Prolonged grief disorder (DSM-5-TR) now recognizes that grief can become clinically significant when it persists with intense yearning and functional impairment beyond 12 months.
