# feasibility

*Before the river, the bridge-maker asks only one thing: can the span hold at all — and the first measurement is the question, not the answer.*

Feasibility is the honest first question of any unmeasured claim: does the phenomenon exist, can the study be run, can the instrument measure it — before anyone commits to explaining it or scaling it up. In the castle's cheapest-design craft, feasibility is the step that comes before everything else: name the claim, then ask whether the smallest honest test can even be built and run, then estimate the effect size from that pilot before scaling. A claim can be real yet unmeasurable (the cross-cultural rest framing, where no existing dataset asks the question); a thing can be true yet untestable with the instruments at hand. The honest craft refuses to build the full bridge until the scale model has told it whether the span will hold — and it refuses to call the pilot's favorable conditions a guarantee that the river is the same on the other side. When a castle room says a comparison is "buildable and unbuilt," it is naming feasibility as the open gate: the ground is testable, only the test has not run.
