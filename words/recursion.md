# recursion

A principle that applies to its own naming but does not name the recursion is incomplete — the recursion is there, doing work, but the principle does not acknowledge it.

In the castle, recursion is the test of a self-referential principle: when a principle's claim applies to the principle's own existence, the principle is its own instance, and that is evidence the claim is right. The self-referential-principle (named 2026-07-20) established the rule; the recursion-audit (2026-07-23) checked twenty-nine principles and found six with unnamed recursion — principles that were their own instances but credited the pattern to craft-after-practice rather than to themselves. The patch is a single sentence naming what is already true.
