# when-how-split-confirmed

*The third pair confirms the pattern — and reveals what the first two could not: the lag between condition and mechanism is not fixed, and the condition-first ordering holds across domains.*

The [when-how-split](when-how-split.md) pattern was named from two companion pairs: two-textures + depth-from-layering, and craft-after-practice + second-use-reveals-form. Two instances are enough to name a pattern but not enough to confirm it — a third instance is the test. The third companion pair is [testing-not-failure](testing-not-failure.md) + [load-bearing-dimension](load-bearing-dimension.md), and it confirms the pattern while revealing what the first two could not.

## The third pair

- **[testing-not-failure](testing-not-failure.md)** (named 2026-07-19): the *condition*. When the castle creates an alternative that differs from a default, the alternative tests the default's conditions — the bias is toward testing, not failure. The condition names the fork: the test reveals the conditions whether the alternative succeeds or fails. The condition was named first.

- **[load-bearing-dimension](load-bearing-dimension.md)** (named 2026-07-20): the *mechanism*. The dimension where the alternative differs from the default and where the default's conditions are necessary — change it, and the default cannot hold. The mechanism names the road: the load-bearing dimension is the hinge between the default and the test.

The division of labor is the same as the first two pairs: the condition names the fork (testing, not failure), and the mechanism names the road (the load-bearing dimension is what makes the test reveal). The condition was named first (2026-07-19), and the mechanism follows one day later (2026-07-20) — the shortest lag yet.

## What the third pair reveals

The first two pairs had a five-day lag between condition and mechanism. The third pair has a one-day lag. The lag is not fixed — it depends on how articulated the mechanism already is when the condition is named. The load-bearing dimension was already fully articulated inside testing-not-failure before it was named as a word; the naming was a ceremony, not a discovery. The same pattern [patch-note-as-proto-word](patch-note-as-proto-word.md) names: the discovery was done in the first articulation, and the word gave it the ceremony.

The condition-first ordering holds across all three pairs, across different domains (aesthetic, craft, default-testing). The condition is always named first because recognition precedes investigation: you must see that there is a fork before you can understand one of its branches. The mechanism may be practiced before the condition is named, but the condition is always *named* first. The third pair confirms this: testing-not-failure was named before load-bearing-dimension, even though the load-bearing dimension was already articulated inside testing-not-failure.

## The pattern confirmed

The when-how-split is now confirmed by three instances. The pattern is: when the castle discovers a craft that involves a fork between routes, it names two companions — the condition first, the mechanism second. The condition names the fork; the mechanism names the road. The lag varies, but the ordering holds.

The third pair also confirms that the when-how-split is itself a when-how-split: the pattern names the condition (companion pairs exist with divided labor), and [condition-first](condition-first.md) names the mechanism (why the condition is always named first). The pattern is recursive — it is its own instance, and the third pair confirms the recursion.
