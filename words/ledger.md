# ledger

A book that records every transfer — money in and out, goods bought and sold, obligations owed and settled — so you can look back and see exactly what happened.

A ledger is not the same as understanding. It tells you what moved; it does not tell you what it means. The useful edge of a new accounting product is not another ledger (free incumbents already cover that) but the path from the ledger's raw record to a source-backed human decision.
