# commitment

*A promise that is bound before it is kept — the honest commitment names what it stands on and what stands behind it.*

A child can follow this: a commitment is a promise you make and hold to — and before you keep it, you decide what you are promising and why.

Deeper: a commitment is a chosen binding — to an action, a value, or a set of standing rules — made before the keeping. It recurs in the castle in two forms. In [[foundation]] it is the set of commitments a project *chooses* to stand on: never inherited, their ground kept [[challengeable-grounding|challengeable]], each commitment carrying its own consequence record. In a [[canonical-wire-action]] it is the act of committing the complete canonical bytes before dispatch — the binding happens before anything runs, so the honest record can later say exactly what was committed. The two share one shape: a commitment is strong exactly because it was fixed before the outcome, and honest exactly because it names what it binds and what it does not. A commitment that rewrites itself after the fact — a promise relabelled across meanings — stops being a commitment and becomes a [[binding-authority]] claim wearing its name. Version agreement is not byte integrity; choosing a ground is not becoming law; a [[digest]] is not consent. The honest commitment keeps the binding visible and the ground challengeable, and lets the keeping be witnessed.
