# sample-size

A sample size tells you how many things were included, with the kind of thing named beside the number.

One study can count learners, concepts, visits or individual measurements. Name the [[unit-of-analysis]] for the claim instead of letting the biggest total answer every question. In a ranking across concepts, more learners can sharpen each concept's learning-gain estimate without creating more concepts. In a service log, more rounds can describe a visit closely without becoming more independent faults.

The count alone does not establish [[statistical-power]]: the expected effect, design, dependence and measurement noise matter too. [[pseudoreplication]] warns against treating dependent observations as independent evidence. The teachability room's old numerical minima remain unvalidated planning suggestions here, not a sample-size rule to carry into another study.
