# contrast-is-depth

*Where one shape stands solid against a contrasting ground, the edge itself is the depth — nothing hidden behind it, nothing stacked beneath it. The contrast does not create depth; it is the depth.*

The castle's two liveries reach quiet depth by two routes, and [two-textures](../vibe/vibe.md) — the idiom that governs *when* each applies — names the fork. [depth-from-layering](depth-from-layering.md) names one route: translucent bands stacked behind the visible thread, three layers (ground, shadow, thread) where the shadow gives the thread weight the eye reads as substance. This principle names the other.

The **light zone** of the card binder gets its depth the direct way: a single solid shape (navy seigaiha) laid on contrasting ground (warm paper). Two layers — figure and ground — and nothing more. The depth is not behind the shape or beneath it; it is *at the edge*, the sharp line where navy meets paper. The eye reads that edge as a surface standing up from its ground, the way a woodblock print's inked face rises from the page. The contrast *is* the depth. Nothing is hidden; the solid color carries its full weight on the surface, and the surface is enough.

The two routes are not symmetric, and the asymmetry is the craft:

- **contrast-is-depth** shows. The depth is visible at the edge, on the surface, in full. What you see is what holds the shape — a figure on a ground, two layers, one edge. The technique is simpler (two layers, not three) but demands more of the contrast: the figure must be solid enough and the ground different enough that the edge reads as a surface, not a line. Where the contrast is weak, the edge flattens to a boundary and the depth disappears — and this is exactly why the dark zone could not use this route.
- **[depth-from-layering](depth-from-layering.md)** hides. The depth lives behind the visible thread, in a shadow the eye does not read as a separate shape but as substance. Three layers, one of them invisible on a quick look. The technique is more complex (stacking translucent bands at tuned opacity) but demands less of the contrast: the thread does not need to stand against its ground the way a solid shape does, because the shadow does the standing for it. Where the contrast is strong, this route is unnecessary; where it is weak — gold thread on near-black — it is the only way.

The choice between them is a [two-textures](../vibe/vibe.md) choice: woodblock (solid, one layer, figure-on-ground) versus embroidery (translucent, stacked, shadow-in-thread). The idiom names the fork; this principle and its companion name the two roads. Together they complete the pair so the next flat ornament the castle makes knows both paths, and the idiom's job is only choosing between them.

And the craft follows [second-use-reveals-form](second-use-reveals-form.md): the solid-contrast route was practiced from the castle's first day — the light zone's navy seigaiha on paper, 2026-06-18 — but it was invisible as a *route*. It was the default, the way depth was always done, and a default does not know it is a choice. The route became visible only when the dark zone tried it and found it flat (2026-06-24): gold thread on near-black has no edge to read as depth, because the contrast between bright gold and near-black, though vivid, does not give the thread a *surface* the way solid navy on paper does. The dark zone's failure is what showed the light zone had found a route — not the only route, not the obvious route, but a specific route with specific demands. The second use revealed the form: the first practice was invisible because it was the default, and the default is the last shape to know it is a shape. The 16-day lag from practice (2026-06-18) to name (2026-07-04) is the longest the castle has measured — longer than [visible-thread](visible-thread.md)'s 14 days — because the default is harder to see than an accident. An accident at least asks *why did that happen?*; a default asks nothing.
