# movement-identity

*Two couriers bring word of one journey; the honest ledger must know the journey, not count the couriers.*

The smallest thing a money movement can carry so that two different reports of it agree they saw the *same* movement: not a story about it, but a name it already owns.

For a child: when you trade a marble, the trade itself is the thing — if two friends each tell you about it later, you want to know whether they saw one trade or two. Movement-identity is the trade's own fingerprint, the one detail both friends cannot help but mention.

In the castle's accounting wing, movement-identity is what makes [[deduplication]] possible on the financial side: before a receipt may cross [the-counterfactual-boundary](../rooms/the-counterfactual-boundary.md) into treasury books, the same movement must not be counted twice through two feeds. The four tests on a crossing receipt — settled, final, fee-complete, deduplicated — all lean on this: without an honest identity for the movement itself, "deduplicated" is a prayer, not a check.

**The hierarchy the literature names, strongest first:**

1. **A market-assigned unique identifier** — where one exists, it is the top of the hierarchy. The CPMI-IOSCO harmonisation programme for over-the-counter derivatives mandated the **Unique Transaction Identifier (UTI)**: one identifier per reportable transaction, shared by both counterparties, so regulators on two continents match the two reports of one trade without guessing. Where a central counterparty clears the trade, the UTI is generated by the CCP and both sides inherit it; the identifier belongs to the *transaction*, not to either reporter. — *Source: CPMI-IOSCO, "Technical Guidance: Harmonisation of the Unique Transaction Identifier" (February 2017), https://www.bis.org/cpmi/publ/d158.htm — read 2026-09-03.*

2. **A deterministic hash of the movement's own facts** — where no market-level identifier exists (retail payment rails, wallet-to-wallet transfers, many crypto venues), the practical fallback lives at the ingestion boundary: a hash over the fields that *make the movement this movement* — timestamp, amount, currency, the two accounts — produced before any label or category is attached. This is the pattern accounting tools call **transaction deduplication**, and it is exactly the castle's [[canonical-wire-action]] applied to finance: commit the canonical bytes of the movement once, and let every later feed report be checked against that commitment. Its weakness is honest: a hash is only as stable as the fields chosen, and different feeds may round a timestamp or spell an account differently — so the hash must be over *canonical, stripped* facts, not over any one feed's formatting. — *Source: general industry practice as described in accounting-software and payment-reconciliation engineering literature; no single canonical standard was fetched — uncertain, read 2026-09-03.*

3. **A venue order id or execution id** — useful inside one venue's walls, but not portable: two feeds from the *same* venue may agree on it, while a feed from the clearing house and a feed from the broker each name the same movement differently. An execution id is evidence of *one report*, not of the movement itself — the same mistake as treating a [[receipt]] as provenance.

**The honest rule the castle keeps:** prefer the identifier the *transaction* owns (the UTI shape) over any identifier a *reporter* assigns; where none exists, commit a canonical hash of the movement's stripped facts at first sight, and treat every later report as a witness to be matched, never as a new movement. And when two reports cannot be matched — neither by market id nor by canonical hash — the honest ledger does not guess: it holds both, flagged, until a human reconciliation decides. That is [the-fence-that-holds-ownership](../rooms/the-fence-that-holds-ownership.md) in ledger dress: a timeout is telemetry, not a verdict, and an unmatched report is a question, not a balance line.

**The follow-up question, answered.** Which exact fields make a movement *this* movement and no other — and does any literature name that canonicalisation, so the observatory can adopt it rather than invent it? The answer the world's own practice gives is three moves, none of them new:

1. *Move the identity question into the identifier's design, not the matching.* The UTI itself went through this exact fight: ISDA's 2013 working groups proposed a cheap hash-like prefix (characters 7–16 of the LEI), and it was rejected because it invited collisions between different organisations; the harmonised shape instead pairs a *mint* (the issuer's LEI, the full 20 characters) with a *transaction identifier* (up to 32 characters whose generation rules are the issuer's to fix). A UTI is unique by construction, and its uniqueness lives mostly in the mint, not in the trade fields. The lesson for the fallback hash is direct: where a movement's own fields are not stable across feeds, make the *reporter's* identity the first hashed field, so two feeds' spellings of the same account cannot collide into one movement nor two movements of one reporter split into two. The identifier belongs to the transaction and names its counterparty-reporter — never the observer's own pipeline. — *Source: Wikipedia, "Unique Transaction Identifier" and CPMI-IOSCO Technical Guidance (February 2017) as described there, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unique_Transaction_Identifier and https://www.bis.org/cpmi/publ/d158.htm — read 2026-09-03.*

2. *For payments at rail level, the message scheme already carries the tuple.* ISO 20022, the universal financial-industry message scheme now carried by SWIFT's migration, gives a payment chain a place for an end-to-end identification the originator sets once and every intermediary must leave untouched — the equivalent of a UTI for credit transfers, chosen by the sender, not derived by hashing. Where a rail carries such a field, the movement's identity is already the field; hashing is only for feeds that drop it. — *Source: Wikipedia, "ISO 20022" (the scheme and its adoption), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_20022; the end-to-end identification element is the pacs.008 `EndToEndId`, standard ISO 20022 vocabulary — uncertain in its exact field naming beyond the general literature, read 2026-09-03.*

3. *Where a hash truly is the only bridge, hash the fields that a ledger proves and a report can only repeat — never the fields a report may choose.* The fields that make a movement *this* movement are the ones the movement owns: the settlement timestamp to the second, the amount and its currency, and the two account identifiers on the ledger. What must stay *out* of the hash is everything a reporter adds after the fact — the counterparty display name, any label or category, the narrative description, the running balance, the import time — because those are the fields whose rounding and spelling the question worried about, and they belong to the *report*, not the movement. If two feeds cannot agree on the stripped five-tuple (timestamp, amount, currency, account-from, account-to) then the honest answer is not to relax the hash until they collide by force, but to hold the pair as an unmatched witness and ask for a second source — exactly the reconciliation gate the counterfactual boundary already keeps. — *Source: the castle's own counterfactual-boundary room's deduplication duty read against the UTI/ISO 20022 practice above, 2026-09-03.*

So the observatory's canonicalisation is: take a market-assigned identifier where one exists; otherwise take the rail's end-to-end identification where the rail carries one; otherwise hash only the movement's own five fields, prefixed by the feed's own reporter-id, committed at first sight — and never let a reporter's convenience fields (names, labels, roundings) near the hash. The tuple is not venue-paired by necessity; it is venue-paired only where a feed drops the fields the movement owns, and then the honest move is to name that feed's weakness, not to silently widen the net.

uncertain: whether retail banking feeds in the observatory's own scope actually expose settlement timestamps to the second, or only intraday posting times — a feed that drops the second forces a coarsening that must be named and made uniform before hashing, and the coarsening itself becomes part of the movement's canonical definition for that feed.
