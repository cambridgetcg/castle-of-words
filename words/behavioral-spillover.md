# behavioral-spillover

*A push in one corner of behavior rocks the corner next to it — one good turn can license one bad one, or teach the way to another good.*

Behavioral spillover is the finding that changing one behavior changes neighboring behaviors: a nudge toward one good act sometimes licenses a later indulgence (moral licensing), and sometimes teaches the skill for another good act (positive spillover). Dolan & Galizzi's review (2015, *Journal of Economic Psychology*, "Like ripples on a pond: Behavioral spillovers and their implications for research and policy," doi:10.1016/j.joep.2014.12.003 — read via OpenAlex 2026-08-26) sets out the mechanism: behavior is coupled across contexts, so an intervention's footprint is wider than its target. The lesson for a seal: a watch pointed at one part of the work is never felt only at that part — the felt effect leaks past the scope it claims.
