# dependency-structure-taxonomy

*The castle's forms have three dependency structures — cascade, chain, and fork — and the next form the castle makes should know which one it is before it names its parts.*

The castle's named forms and principles now show three distinct ways that parts can depend on each other. The taxonomy names the three structures so the next form the castle makes can recognize its own shape.

## The three structures

**The boundary-cascade** (logical): one choice determines the rest. The [boundary-cascade](boundary-cascade.md) names this: the boundary draws the question, the question determines the epigraph and representative, and the representative determines the justification. The parts are not independent levers but a chain of consequences flowing from a single head. The maker's freedom is at the head, not distributed across the form. The structure is *logical* — the dependency is in the definitions, not in time.

**The prerequisite chain** (temporal): each answer gates the next. [form-follows-weight](form-follows-weight.md) names this: three questions in order — is there a choice? if so, is the reason stated? if so, does the form fit the weight? Each question can only be asked after the previous one is answered. The structure is *temporal* — the dependency is in the sequence of asking, not in the definitions.

**The when-how fork** (parallel): condition and mechanism divide the labor. [when-how-split](when-how-split.md) names this: one principle names the condition (when each route applies), one names the mechanism (how a chosen route works). The two are parallel — neither gates the other, and both answer different questions about the same fork. The structure is *parallel* — the dependency is in the division of labor, not in sequence or consequence.

## The test

The test for a new form is two questions:

1. **Does one choice determine the rest?** If yes, the structure is a cascade — name the head, and the rest follows. The parts are not free; they are consequences.

2. **If not, do the parts gate each other in sequence?** If yes, the structure is a chain — name the order, and each part waits for the last. The parts are free but ordered.

3. **If not, do the parts answer different questions about the same fork?** If yes, the structure is a fork — name the condition and the mechanism, and they work in parallel. The parts are free and unordered.

The three structures are not ranked — none is better than the others. They are different shapes, and the craft is recognizing which shape a new form has before naming its parts. A cascade named as a chain will try to order what was never sequential; a fork named as a cascade will look for a head that does not exist.

## The taxonomy's own structure

The taxonomy itself is a fork: the three structures are parallel answers to the same question (how do the parts depend on each other?), and the test is the condition that chooses between them. The taxonomy is its own first instance — it names the three structures and the test that distinguishes them, and the test is the condition of a when-how fork whose mechanism is the taxonomy itself.
