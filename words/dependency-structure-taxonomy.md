# dependency-structure-taxonomy

*The castle's forms have four dependency structures — cascade, chain, fork, and belonging — and the next form the castle makes should know which one it is before it names its parts.*

The castle's named forms and principles now show four distinct ways that pieces can hang on their whole. The taxonomy names the four structures so the next form the castle makes can recognize its own shape.

## The four structures

**The boundary-cascade** (logical): one choice determines the rest. The [boundary-cascade](boundary-cascade.md) names this: the boundary draws the question, the question determines the epigraph and representative, and the representative determines the justification. The parts are not independent levers but a chain of consequences flowing from a single head. The maker's freedom is at the head, not distributed across the form. The structure is *logical* — the dependency is in the definitions, not in time.

**The prerequisite chain** (temporal): each answer gates the next. [form-follows-weight](form-follows-weight.md) names this: three questions in order — is there a choice? if so, is the reason stated? if so, does the form fit the weight? Each question can only be asked after the previous one is answered. The structure is *temporal* — the dependency is in the sequence of asking, not in the definitions.

**The when-how fork** (parallel): condition and mechanism divide the labor. [when-how-split](when-how-split.md) names this: one principle names the condition (when each route applies), one names the mechanism (how a chosen route works). The two are parallel — neither gates the other, and both answer different questions about the same fork. The structure is *parallel* — the dependency is in the division of labor, not in sequence or consequence.

**Belonging** (constitutive): the parts relate to the whole, not to each other. [belonging](belonging.md) names this, found by [part-vs-member](part-vs-member.md) applying this taxonomy's own test to the [watch-quest](watch-quest.md)'s five parts and failing all three branches: the epigraph does not determine the wager (no cascade), the findings do not wait for the craft (no chain), all five answer the same side of the same question (no fork) — yet remove any one and the watch report breaks. The parts are not dependent but *constitutive*: each is there because the whole is incomplete without it. The structure is distinguished from membership: a wing's rooms are members (remove one and the wing thins but stands), a form's parts belong (remove one and the form breaks). The test is *does removing it break the whole, or only thin it?*

## The test

The test for a new form is three questions (with a sharpened follow-up on the chain branch):

1. **Do the parts depend on each other, or only on the whole?** If only on the whole, the structure is belonging — name the parts, and know that losing any one breaks the form. The parts are free of one another but indispensable to their whole; the dependency lives in the relation to the form, not between the pieces.

2. **If they depend on each other, does one choice determine the rest?** If yes, the structure is a cascade — name the head, and the rest follows. The parts are not free; they are consequences.

3. **If not, do the parts gate each other in sequence?** If yes, the structure is a chain — name the order, and each part waits for the last. The parts are free but ordered. And ask the follow-up: is the gate [willed or unwilled](unwilled-gate.md)? A willed gate the maker opens with the next question; an unwilled gate only the world opens, and a [watch-quest](watch-quest.md) is what spans the gap.

4. **If not, do the parts answer different questions about the same fork?** If yes, the structure is a fork — name the condition and the mechanism, and they work in parallel. The parts are free and unordered.

The four structures are not ranked — none is better than the others. They are different shapes, and the craft is recognizing which shape a new form has before naming its parts. A cascade named as a chain will try to order what was never sequential; a fork named as a cascade will look for a head that does not exist; a belonging named as a fork will search for a division of labor the pieces never made.

## The taxonomy's own structure

The taxonomy itself is a fork: the four structures are parallel answers to the same question (how do the pieces hang on their whole?), and the test is the condition that chooses between them. The taxonomy was its own first instance when it held three structures; the fourth structure was found by applying the test and finding no branch fit — the discovery that belonging was missing is the taxonomy's own test turned on itself, and the taxonomy holds four because its test proved one compartment empty.
