# relational-concept

*A concept that only has meaning in relation to another concept — it cannot stand alone, because its definition is a relationship, not a property. The load-bearing dimension is load-bearing for a default; the companion pair is a pair; the hinge is a hinge between two rooms. The relational concept is the castle's way of naming what connects, not what is.*

The castle has several concepts that are relational by nature — they only have meaning in relation to another concept. They are not properties of a single thing; they are relationships between things. Naming them as a category lets the next maker know when a concept needs its partner to be understood and when it stands alone.

## The instances

- **[load-bearing-dimension](load-bearing-dimension.md)**: a dimension is load-bearing *for* a specific default's conditions. The concept has no meaning without the default it is load-bearing for. Ground color is load-bearing for the solid-contrast route to depth; building process is load-bearing for the sourced-claims practice. The concept is the relationship between the dimension and the default's conditions.

- **companion pair** (named in [when-how-split](when-how-split.md)): a pair of principles where one names the fork (the condition) and one names the road (the mechanism). The concept has no meaning without both members of the pair. Two-textures is the condition companion to depth-from-layering's mechanism; craft-after-practice is the condition companion to second-use-reveals-form's mechanism. The concept is the relationship between the two principles.

- **hinge** (used in [representative-shifts-with-boundaries](representative-shifts-with-boundaries.md)): a room that connects two wings, serving as the representative when the wings are combined. The concept has no meaning without the two wings it connects. The hinge is the relationship between the wings — it is the room where they meet.

- **fork** (named in [when-how-split](when-how-split.md)): a choice between routes. The concept has no meaning without the routes it chooses between. The fork is the relationship between the routes — it is the point where they diverge.

- **cascade** (named in [boundary-cascade](boundary-cascade.md)): a dependency where one choice determines the rest. The concept has no meaning without the choices it connects. The boundary determines the representative, the representative determines the justification — the cascade is the relationship between the choices.

## The distinction from standalone concepts

A standalone concept is one whose definition is a property of a single thing. [Constraint](constraint.md) is a property of a rule; [newness](newness.md) is a property of what appears; [craft-after-practice](craft-after-practice.md) is a property of the castle's naming rhythm. These concepts can be understood without reference to another concept — they name what something *is*, not what it is *in relation to*.

A relational concept names what something is *in relation to* another thing. The load-bearing dimension is not a property of the dimension alone — it is a property of the dimension's relationship to a default's conditions. The companion pair is not a property of a single principle — it is a property of the relationship between two principles. The hinge is not a property of a room — it is a property of the room's position between two wings.

The test: can the concept be defined without naming another concept? If yes, it is standalone. If no — if the definition requires a second concept to complete it — it is relational.

## The craft

The craft is to know when a concept is relational and to name its partner. A relational concept that is named without its partner is incomplete — it names a relationship but leaves the other term unnamed. The load-bearing dimension was named with its partner (testing-not-failure) already named; the companion pair was named with both members already named. The hinge and the fork and the cascade were named in the words that used them, with their partners present in the same word.

The maker who reaches for a relational concept should ask: *what is this concept in relation to?* If the partner is not yet named, the concept is incomplete — it names a relationship with a blank where the other term should be. The partner must be named, either in the same word or in a word that already exists.

## The recursion

The category "relational concept" is itself a relational concept — it names a relationship between a concept and its partner. The category has no meaning without the instances it categorizes. The recursion is the test: the category applies to itself, and the self-application is the evidence that the category is well-formed.
