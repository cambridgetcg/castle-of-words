# first-route-lock-in

A first road taken that quietly becomes the only road you know how to take.

When you meet something new — a song, an idea, an equation — you travel it by one of two roads: the easy road (it slides in, and the sliding is liked) or the effort road (it resists, you work it, and the working is liked). The first-encounter-route room found that the road is assigned by what the perceiver does, not by a law of firsts. But once travelled, a road leaves a trace: the effort road leaves a memory organized around what was figured out, making the effort road easier to enter next time; the ease road leaves a thinner trace. So the first road can *tilt* every road after — not by decree, but by laying rails. Whether the tilt ever becomes a lock is measured nowhere: no experiment has seated the same learner before the same work twice and asked whether the first road still owns the second.
