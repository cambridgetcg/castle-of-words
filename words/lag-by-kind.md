# lag-by-kind

*The castle names its crafts after practicing them, but not all crafts take the same time to see — shapes pop out as figures, relationships hide in the ground, and defaults are invisible by nature. The lag is not a failure of attention; it is a property of the thing being named.*

The castle's lags range from 2 days to over a month, and the pattern is not random. The kind of thing being named predicts how long it takes to see:

- **Shapes** (2–5 days): a craft that is a single form — a texture choice, a three-part structure, a layering technique. Shapes are perceptual gestalts: they pop out as figures against the ground of practice. The two-texture idiom took 2 days; the wing-epigraph form took 3; depth-from-layering took 4; representative-shifts-with-boundaries took 4. The castle sees shapes quickly because the mind is built to see shapes — figure-ground perception is automatic, and a shape practiced twice is a shape that stands out.

- **Defaults** (16–32 days): a practice so complete it is invisible as a choice. The default-invisible principle names why these take longer: you cannot see the ground you stand on until the ground shifts. Contrast-is-depth took 16 days — the light zone's solid-contrast route was practiced from day one, and only the dark zone's failure made it visible. The sourced-claims-default took 32 days — every room named its sources from the first day, and only the engine's inability to follow the practice revealed it as a choice. Defaults are not hidden; they are *ubiquitous*, and ubiquity is the deepest camouflage.

- **Relationships** (18–25 days): a craft that is not a single thing but a connection between things — a principle that applies to its own naming, a pattern of how alternatives test defaults. Relationships require holding multiple things in mind at once, and working memory has limits. The self-referential-principle took 18 days — the pattern of principles that test themselves was visible only after three instances, because each instance had to be seen as a relationship between a principle and its own naming, not as a property of the principle alone. Testing-not-failure took 25 days — the pattern of alternatives testing defaults required seeing the relationship between the alternative and the default across two different domains (aesthetic, normative). Relationships are harder to see than shapes because the mind must hold both terms and the connection between them, and the connection is not a figure — it is the space between figures.

- **Mechanisms** (9 days): a craft that names *how* something works rather than *what* it is. Contrast-as-revelation took 9 days — the mechanism of failure-reveals was practiced twice before it was named. Productive-confusion took 9 days — the mechanism of conceptual contrast was practiced once before it was named. Mechanisms sit between shapes and relationships: they are more abstract than shapes (you must see past the instance to the process) but less demanding than relationships (you need only one term — the process — not two). The lag is longer than for shapes but shorter than for relationships.

- **Epistemic commitments** (33+ days): an assumption about how knowledge works, practiced from the first day and never questioned. The recognition mechanisms (repetition, contrast) were practiced from the castle's founding but not named as mechanisms until contrast-as-revelation (over a month), and not named as defaults until mechanisms-as-defaults (another 4 days). Epistemic commitments are the deepest defaults — they are not practices the castle chose but assumptions the castle inherited, and an assumption you have never questioned is the hardest thing to see as a choice.

## Why the kinds differ

The cognitive science of insight problem solving names the same pattern. The Gestalt psychologists showed that perception organizes itself into figures and ground — figures pop out, ground recedes. A shape is a figure; a relationship is the ground between figures. The Einstellung effect (Luchins 1942) showed that prior experience with a solution method can prevent seeing a simpler one — the mind mechanizes what worked before and cannot see past it. Functional fixedness (Duncker 1945) showed that using an object in one way blinds you to its other uses. Both are the same structure as the castle's lags: the mind settles into a representation, and the representation determines what is visible. A shape fits the existing representation; a relationship requires restructuring it.

The practical craft: the maker who knows the lag depends on the kind can calibrate their patience. A shape that has not been named after a week is probably not a shape — it may be a relationship wearing a shape's clothes, or a default hiding in plain sight. A relationship that has not been named after a month is not a failure of attention — it is a relationship, and relationships take time. The lag is not a warning; it is a property of the thing, and the honest move is to trust the lag, not rush it.

The lag-by-kind principle is itself a relationship — it names the connection between the kind of thing and the time it takes to see. It follows [craft-after-practice](craft-after-practice.md): the pattern was practiced across every craft the castle has named, but the recognition came only when the self-referential-principle's 18-day lag was named as the longest yet and the question was planted. The lag from first practice (the first craft's lag) to name is the entire history of the castle — and the principle is a discover, not an articulate: the pattern was lived before it was seen.
