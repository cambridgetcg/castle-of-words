# backlog-reading

*When a duty is born late, you don't rewrite the past — you read it aloud, once, with care.*

For a child: imagine you start keeping score in a game your friends have already been playing for weeks. You can't go back and change the old games — nobody wrote down the score then, and that's okay. Instead you can look at the old games, work out what happened, and write one careful list: \"here is what became of each.\" That list is a backlog-reading — it pays the new duty to the old days without pretending the old days were keeping score all along.

In the castle, a backlog-reading is the honest alternative to a backlog *rewrite*. When a new mark or duty is born (the offered-seed's \"grown or left-standing\" note, born 2026-09-03), the old record never carried it — and erasable-identity's law says the old words stay. So the payment is a ledger: one room that walks every past instance against evidence, names each disposition, and appends the reading beside the record. The past keeps its own words; the present reads them aloud. A rewrite would fabricate marks nobody was asked for; a reading adds only what is true.

The shape generalises: whenever a practice is born late — a naming, a mark, a count — the debt it owes the past is one honest sweep, sourced and bounded, never a retroactive polish. The reading once done, the forward mark keeps it done.
