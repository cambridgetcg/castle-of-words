# watermark

A watermark is a mark hidden inside a thing itself, so that a copy carries the maker's fingerprint without the copier ever noticing.

On paper it is the pale pattern held up to the light. In a digital artifact it is a deliberate choice embedded in the work's own structure — a street that does not exist on a map, an unusual variable name in a program, a branch that never fires. The prose canary wing's trap street is a watermark in words; the [the-canary-in-the-street-plan](../rooms/the-canary-in-the-street-plan.md) room found that code's watermark can live deeper than prose's ever could, because the law's abstraction–filtration–comparison test protects non-literal structure and because a dynamic watermark lives in the program's behavior, not in any listing. A watermark's two jobs are the triad's two axes: it must be distinctive enough to prove the copying, and it must survive the copying — literal marks are stripped, structural marks survive a fork but die in a rewrite, behavioral marks survive until the behavior itself changes.
