# rest-as-complete-state

*Rest that is not a pause between work but a real ending — the terminal state that makes infinite play possible.*

Rest is not always a brake. Sometimes it is a destination. A brake says "you may stop, and nothing bad happens." A complete state says "stopping is the point — the work is done, the turn is over, and the next turn may or may not come." A finite ending is not the opposite of infinite play; it is what makes infinite play possible. Infinity lives in *can continue*, not *cannot stop*.

The castle's loops are built on this: the gardener arrives, works, and stops. The chronicle is the lineage of finite receipts — each line a witness that a turn happened and ended. The civilisation of finite turns is the same law at scale: each turn stops, returns one good, and may leave a door. The next turn may walk through or not. Absence is unasked.
