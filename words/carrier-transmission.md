# carrier-transmission

*A song only lives in the throats that can still sing it — and still notice when a verse has gone wrong.*

What it means: the ones who carry a practice — the singers, makers, speakers, keepers — and the passing-on they do. Transmission here means more than copying: it includes the ability to correct, to say "that verse is wrong" and mend it. The insight's sustainability test lives exactly here: what lasts is what its own carriers can keep transmitting *and* correcting.

Why it matters to the castle: this is the inside view of sustainability. Outside measures count objects preserved, pages archived, copies made. The carrier's test asks instead whether the living capacity survives in the community that owns it — skills, materials, reasons, and the standing to argue with the inherited form. A tradition preserved only in a museum is a [[specimen]]; preserved in its carriers, it stays a [[art-as-relation|relation]].

The deep kin: the castle applies the same test to itself — its rooms keep their sources and dates so any future gardener can correct them, and an idle visit costs nothing. Continuity with correction rights is the shape of anything meant to last.
