# quasi-experimental-design

*A bridge that is already built on one side of the river is not a bridge you built — but it is still a bridge you can walk across to see what is on the other side.*

A quasi-experimental design is a research design that compares groups that were not randomly assigned. In a true experiment, researchers assign people to groups by chance; in a quasi-experiment, the groups already exist — for example, one hospital ward decided to run evidence-based and another did not — and the researcher compares what happens to them. Quasi-experiments are weaker than randomized experiments because the groups might differ in ways that explain the outcome, but they are often the only honest design available for studying real-world changes that cannot be randomized. The castle's source-backed natural-experiment question is a quasi-experimental question: can we find a hospital system that switched from trust-based to evidence-based practice and compare outcomes before and after, without randomizing the switch?
