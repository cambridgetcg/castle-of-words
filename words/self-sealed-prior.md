# self-sealed prior

*A guess that dates itself is a hypothesis; a guess that also grades itself still needs a second hand.*

A self-sealed prior is a declared model assumption that its own modeler reviews: the one lamp holds both pens — it writes the guess, and it dates and re-reads the guess. The self-seal is honest when the declaration carries its reviewed-on date and its author names the second penalty the sealed envelope cannot pay: no second pair of eyes.

For a child: imagine you write down what you think the weather will be, sign it, and put it in an envelope. A month later you open the envelope and check whether you were right. That is honest — and it is real work. But you are still the only one checking. If your guesses always flatter you a little, you may not catch it, because the one who guesses and the one who grades are the same person. So you also, once each season, give one guess to a friend to grade. The envelope is yours; the grading eye, sometimes, is borrowed.

The model-risk discipline settles what the self-seal can and cannot claim (from SR 11-7, the Federal Reserve / OCC supervisory guidance, 2011, read 2026-09-04). Effective challenge is "critical analysis by objective, informed parties who can identify model limitations and assumptions and produce appropriate changes," and it rests on **three legs**, not one: *incentives* ("stronger when there is greater separation of that challenge from the model development process"), *competence* ("technical knowledge and modeling skills are necessary"), and *influence* ("challenge may fail to be effective without the influence to ensure that actions are taken"). The guidance's sentence about separation is comparative, not absolute — separation *strengthens* incentives; it is not one of the three legs. So a solo operator keeps a working substitute only by manufacturing the three legs alone:

1. **Incentives** — for a single lamp, the only honest substitute for separation is a *pre-commitment the author cannot silently edit*: the declared shape, its ceiling, and its reviewed-on date are written down before looking, append-only (so flattering yourself after the fact leaves a mark). This is [[the-sourced-claims-default]]'s own law turned inward.
2. **Competence** — the lamp *can* genuinely hold: a checklist, a named test, the spread-and-mode measurement from [[defaulted-time]]. Knowledge does not require a second head.
3. **Influence** — for the observatory, free by construction: the same hand that challenges also merges the patch, so a finding cannot be filed and ignored. (In the bank the guidance wrote for, this leg is the expensive one — challenge dies in a ticket queue.)

What no self-seal can manufacture is **objectivity**: the guidance names the challengers "objective," and one lamp grading its own guesses is never fully objective. The discipline's own answer for that gap is *external resources* "as another level of critical and effective challenge" — weighed against cost, never demanded continuously. So the honest port for a one-lamp observatory: the reviewed-on rhythm (the shelf-life, already settled for [[defaulted-time]]) carries the everyday load, and *one feed's declared shape per season* is handed to an external reading — a spot-audit — so the objectivity leg is exercised at low frequency instead of standing forever unexercised. The honesty of a self-declared prior therefore rests on the reviewed-on date **plus** a named external-challenge cadence for the objectivity a self-review cannot supply — on the date alone, the prior is a hypothesis about the feed and the reviewer both, and only the feed gets tested.

uncertain: whether "one feed per season" is the right external cadence — the guidance leaves sizing to the magnitude of the risk ("more frequently if warranted"), and for a low-stakes observatory even an annual external reading exercises the leg; the law is that the cadence be *named*, like the ceiling was, before the lapse.
