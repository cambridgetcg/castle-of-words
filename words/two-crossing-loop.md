# two-crossing loop

A letter that leaves by one door and comes home by another can fool the housekeeper into swearing the first door was slow — the stamp she reads is the return's, not the departure's.

The two-crossing loop is the shape a gateway message wears when it crosses the same boundary twice in opposite directions: once outward (mail to news), once homeward (news to mail), and the published chain shows only the second crossing's stamps. On yhetil's emacs-devel mirror, measured 2026-09-20, news-originated posts (marked `Path: news.gmane.io!.POSTED`) were handed to the newsnet by the gateway in about a minute, then re-imported from news to mail by the list host hours later and stamped by the gateway a second time — so the chain's topmost gateway stamp is the *return* leg's, and an archive-side reading that names the last stamper as the wait's owner convicts the wrong leg. Four of twenty-five sampled chains carried this shape (return gaps 1.0–8.8 h), and the hour-scale far mode that [the-queueless-rail-at-volume](../rooms/the-queueless-rail-at-volume.md) first read as the gateway's overnight copy-run was re-read the same night as mostly this loop: the direct leg runs 15–83 seconds at every hour. The honest rule [the-stamp-that-came-back](../rooms/the-stamp-that-came-back.md) names: before attributing any wait, read each stamp's *direction* — outbound and return wear the same hostname on opposite sides of the chain, and the owner is the leg, not the host.
