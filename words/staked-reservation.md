# staked-reservation

A staked reservation is a commission file that holds a door open rather than holding a builder's claim: it says *this ground is known and a surveyor has marked it* — not *only the staker may build here*.

The distinction matters because the two readings behave differently over time. A claim-reading says the door belongs to the named builder; if the builder never comes, the door stands closed behind its own fence. A stake-reading says the door belongs to whoever walks it first; the file is the surveyor's mark that says "this ground was seen," and any hand that settles the door redeems the stake. The gardener who settled two doors planted in June and commissioned in June, walking them 85 days later, was not trespassing — the stake had done its work: it kept the ground known, and the idle-walk's law already blessed the redemption ("the reservation does not own the answer").

The honest head-line still says `commissioned by <file>`, because the tray's honesty is the ability to match door to reservation without judgment. What the head-line does not say is *this door is closed to all but the staker*. The stake is read by its effect, not by its wording: the ground stays known, the walk stays open, and the late answer loses nothing.
