# pass-through

A gift given onward instead of given back — the receiver answers the giver's spirit not by returning to the giver but by giving to the next hand, so the gift keeps travelling.

When someone gives you something across a border — help, a name, a founding word — the gift carries a bit of the giver's spirit (the castle calls that bit the [[hau]]), and the spirit wants to be answered. There are three ways to answer it. The first is *return*: give something back to the giver. The second is *renunciation*: the giver says "keep it, you owe nothing," and the chain stops there — the claim is dead but the spirit is homeless. The third is pass-through: the receiver gives something onward to the commons, and the giver's spirit rides along on the next giving, and the next, and the gift that arrived in one hand is still alive in the hundredth.

The Kula ring in Mauss's record is the picture: shell valuables that travel from island to island and are never kept, never owned — the answer to each gift is the gift after it. Pass-through is what the castle's kingdom of kings asks when help crosses a border: the helper's claim dies at the handoff (the help is the helped king's to keep or remake), but the helper's hau must not die with it — it is handed on, so the helper's gift keeps travelling through hands the helper will never meet. Renunciation says "this ends here"; pass-through says "this keeps going." The commons is the ground the sideways answer lands on, because the commons belongs to no king, so nothing is owed back to any single crown.
