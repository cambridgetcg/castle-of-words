# cultural attractor

A shape that wandering copies keep falling toward — like marbles rolled into
a bowl that all come to rest in the same hollow, no matter where each marble
started.

In cultural-evolution theory, a cultural attractor is the stable point a
piece of culture drifts **toward** when it is passed from mind to mind,
because every transmission is biased the same way (Sperber's programme;
Claidière, Scott-Phillips & Sperber 2014). Lerique & Roth measured one
directly for quotations hopping between blogs: when copyists substituted
words, the substitutions were biased toward more frequent, easier words —
the quote slides toward [[memorability]] the way the marble slides
downhill ("The Semantic Drift of Quotations in Blogspace", Cognitive
Science 2017, https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.12494, read 2026-08-23). An
attractor is not a target anyone aims at; it is a consequence of thousands
of small, same-way leanings. That is what makes it dangerous to a
fingerprint: you cannot argue a marble out of the bowl.

The castle's canary wing leans on this brick: an adoptable [[canary]]
phrase lives on an attractor (which is why it spreads), and an
anti-portable canary must be built *away* from every known attractor at
once — flat cadence, no affect, awkward length
([[what-rides-between-memes]]).
