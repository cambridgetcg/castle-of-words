# knock

*You stand at the door and ask once; what answers — or does not — is the finding.*

For a child: to find out whether a friend's door is open you do not guess from the street and you do not remember yesterday — you walk up and knock, once, and you write down what happened. The door might open, might stay shut, might answer with a riddle you have to solve first. All three are answers. Even nobody-home is an answer, if you write it down.

A **knock** is a single bounded probe at a door, made by a hand that records what came back. The castle knocks at gates it does not own — a curl at lore.kernel.org, a pointing thread walking through a [[bridging-brick]], a reader arriving at a word — and each knock is measured by the same three questions: did it open, what did it show, and how long did the answer stand? A knock is never a demand: it costs the door nothing it was not already shaped to give, and it entitles the knocker only to what the door answers, never to what the knocker wanted.

The knock's honesty is its record. One knock names a moment; a series of knocks names a [[gate-state|gate's cadence]] — weather, retune, or reopening — and the series is honest only when every knock wrote down the same things: the shape it knocked with, the code and the body that came back, and the time. A knock that reads only the code can be fooled (a 200 that is really a challenge page); the honest knock fetches the body, and reads the version twice, in two places, before trusting it. What a knock cannot do is knock again retroactively: the absence between two knocks is not a measurement, only a silence the record must name.

The knock is the small shape almost every castle instrument takes: the census's one reading per promised shape, the pairing-check's tray count, the tender's one glance at the Map, a new name tried once at a readmitting gate. Where the knock is the *event* and the door never chooses, the door is a [[willed-gate|gate]] — and where the door is a page whose answering threads may never come, it is the unwilled kind: the [[bridging-brick|brick that waits]], whose zero knocks is an absence, not a failure.
