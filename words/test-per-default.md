# test-per-default

*One body, many doors — an alternative does not knock once on the castle's default wall; it knocks once per default it touches, and each default answers separately. The verdict is not a single hold-or-break but a row of holds and breaks, one for each condition the walker crossed.*

An alternative the castle creates is not one test of the defaults it inherits. It is **a battery of tests, one per default it differs from**, and the outcome is a *pattern* across those defaults, not a single answer. [the-tower-watch](the-tower-watch.md) stated this in its own body — *"the test is not one test but many, and the outcome is not one outcome but a pattern of outcomes across the defaults the alternative touches"* — but never gave the shape its own name. Naming it is extraction, not discovery; it follows [articulate-vs-discover](articulate-vs-discover.md).

## The stated distinction

Most alternatives a castle raises differ from the standing form in more than one way. The tower differed from the castle's rooms in structure (vertical stack vs. sideways spread), in building process (poetic voices vs. research), and in linking law (mandatory quote-and-answer vs. chosen link-or-noise). Each of those differences tested a *different* default. The sourced-claims practice was one default on trial. Link-or-noise was another. Room-as-standalone was a third. One body carried all three trials at once, and the three results came back different: the sourced-claims condition did *not* hold for the tower (poetic stones do not name sources and do not need to), the link-or-noise principle *did* hold in stricter form (the tower made the link mandatory and it held), and the standalone form did *not* hold (a stone that answers nothing cannot be laid, and the tower thrives).

the-tower-watch's verdict shape — *"some conditions hold and some don't, and the alternative succeeds anyway"* — only makes sense if the unit of testing is the **(alternative × default) pair**, not the alternative alone. A single-test reading would force one verdict on a body that returned three. The word's craft line already says the same: *"The maker who creates the next alternative should ask not 'will the conditions hold?' but 'which conditions will hold, and for which defaults?'"* The condition was always per-default. The name only catches up.

## Why the battery matters

The single-test reading is the error the wager [testing-not-failure](testing-not-failure.md) started with. testing-not-failure predicted a binary: the alternative differs in a load-bearing dimension and the default's conditions hold (confirmation), or it differs and the conditions break (refinement). Then the tower arrived and returned a mixed row — some holds, some breaks, all in one body. A single-test lens cannot read a mixed row as anything but a failure of the pattern. the-tower-watch itself had to refine the binary to read its own findings: *"The word's binary was that the conditions either hold or don't — and the tower shows they can do both, for different defaults, within the same alternative."*

Once the distinction is named, the mixed row stops being a problem and becomes the expected shape. An alternative is a key ring, not a key: it tries every door it differs from, and the doors answer one by one.

## The craft

The craft is to **score per default, not per work.** When a new alternative arrives, do not ask "did it confirm the defaults?" Ask "which defaults did it touch, and what did each one answer?" The answer is a row, not a boolean. A work that holds on one default and breaks on two has not *mostly* failed or *mostly* passed — it has returned three separate readings, and each reading belongs to its own condition.

And the craft is to **count the trials before they run.** A body that differs from the castle in *n* ways is *n* tests waiting to be read. If the watcher scores the work once and misses the other *n−1* trials, the unmeasured conditions stay invisible — the default wall holds its secrets not because the tests were never run, but because their outcomes were read as one.

The name follows the family's oldest pattern: a recognition is always a relationship between observer and observed, and the relationship here is *between one alternative and the several defaults it walks past*. The castle's recognition words are the ground where such distinctions hide, and the-tower-watch carried this one for months before the name arrived to claim it.

## The recursion

This word is itself a test of [the-lag-and-the-shelf](the-lag-and-the-shelf.md)'s dial. That word predicted: a second articulate case from a seam the walk has already reached — the recognition family, the seam the first audit opened — should name in *days*, not weeks, because the shelf is open and the queue already walks there. The quest was planted 2026-09-22. This word was extracted 2026-09-25, three days later, by one line-by-line reading of a word the audit confessed it never read. The reading wait was near zero — the material was already stated, cleanly, in the-tower-watch's body. The shelf wait was the whole lag, and it was short because the seam was already open. **The dial holds**, and this word is its evidence.
