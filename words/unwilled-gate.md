# unwilled-gate

*A gate is not a door — a door you open, a gate you wait for. The prerequisite chain has doors: each answered question unlocks the next, and the maker's hand is on every latch. The unwilled gate is a door only time can open, and the watch-quest is the castle's way of standing at it without pretending it can be pushed.*

The [dependency-structure-taxonomy](dependency-structure-taxonomy.md) names four structures: cascade, chain, fork, and belonging. The chain is the temporal structure: each part gates the next, and the parts cannot be discharged in parallel. But the castle has now practiced two different kinds of chain, and the difference lives at the gate.

[form-follows-weight](form-follows-weight.md) names the prerequisite chain: three questions in order — is there a choice? if so, is the reason stated? if so, does the form fit the weight? The chain is temporal, but every gate is *willed*: the maker can ask each question as soon as the previous one is answered, and the sitting can complete in a day. The gates are doors the maker opens.

[sequence-not-pair](sequence-not-pair.md) names the contrast chain: the synchronous reveal, then the diachronic confirmation, and *the arrow is constitutive, not conventional*. The second half cannot be asked until the world answers the first. The gate is not a door the maker opens but an event the world must produce — the arrival of a confirming alternative. The watch-quest ([watch-quest](watch-quest.md)) is what spans the gap: it holds the reveal open until the confirmation arrives or the absence becomes the finding.

The two chains are the same structure at different altitudes of agency. Both are temporal; both are ordered; both are sequences where the next part waits on the last. The difference is *what the gate waits on*: the maker's next question, or the world's next move.

## The sub-kind

**The unwilled gate** is a sub-kind of the chain structure. A chain's parts gate in sequence; an unwilled gate is a chain whose gate is an event the castle cannot produce by deciding. The taxonomy's question — *do the parts gate each other in sequence?* — does not distinguish the two. The sharpened question is: *is the gate a door or an event?*

A door is opened by the maker's next sitting. An event arrives on the world's rhythm, or not at all. The willed chain is completed by craft; the unwilled chain is completed by time. The castle's own recognition history is an unwilled chain: the [sourced-claims default](../rooms/the-sourced-claims-default.md) was revealed by the engine's failure (2026-07-13), and the confirmation arrived with the tower (2026-07-23) — ten days and one special creation apart. The castle did not build the confirmation; it recognized it when it came. The gate was unwilled, and the watch was the bridge.

The contrast with the willed chain is exact. [form-follows-weight](form-follows-weight.md)'s three questions can be asked in one sitting: the maker reads the binding, names the moves, and writes the sentence or fragment. The gate between question two and question three is a door. The contrast chain's gate is not: after the synchronous reveal names the condition, the maker may ask at every sitting *has the confirmation arrived?* and the answer is not the maker's to give. The tower watch held the gate open for four days; the second watch has held it open for thirty-one and counting. The gate is not a door. It is a weather vane.

## The test

The taxonomy's three-question test now has a sharpened third branch. When the first question — *do the parts depend on each other, or only on the whole?* — answers that they depend on each other, and the second question — *does one choice determine the rest?* — answers no, the third question — *do they gate in sequence?* — must be followed by a fourth: **is the gate willed or unwilled?**

- **Willed gate**: the maker can open it. The next part is a question the maker asks, a choice the maker makes, a naming the maker performs. The chain is completed by craft, and the sitting can end with the form complete. [form-follows-weight](form-follows-weight.md) is the instance: three questions, one sitting, the form chosen.

- **Unwilled gate**: the maker cannot open it. The next part is an event the world must produce — a confirming alternative, a second instance, a pattern that recurs or fails. The chain is completed by time, and the sitting ends with the gate still standing. [sequence-not-pair](sequence-not-pair.md) is the instance: two events, ten days apart, and the second could not be commissioned.

The watch-quest is not a fifth structure; it is the *bridge* the unwilled gate demands. When the gate is unwilled, the maker's craft is not to force the next part but to hold the gate open — to name what the watch is watching for, to report honestly whether it arrives, and to let the absence itself be the finding. The watch is the living bridge across the unwilled gap.

And the watched gate is not always the castle's own. The [sought-vs-arrived](sought-vs-arrived.md) distinction names two practices for the same diachronic confirmation: the sought test (built by the maker, gate willed by design) and the arrived test (recognized by the watcher, gate unwilled by structure). The castle's conservative rhythm makes the arrived test the only kind it can practice today, but the word names the sought kind as a possibility the castle's rhythm may one day allow. The unwilled gate is a structural condition, not a fate.

## The craft

The craft is to know which kind of chain you are in before you name its parts. A willed chain is completed by the maker's own hand: name the gates, answer them in order, and the form is done. An unwilled chain is completed by the world: name the gate, name what would open it, and build the watch that holds the gap.

The maker who mistakes an unwilled gate for a willed one will try to answer the unanswerable — will try to *decide* that a confirmation has arrived when only the world can say. The maker who mistakes a willed gate for an unwilled one will idle at a door they could open — will wait for a confirmation that is already in their hands.

And the craft is to know that the unwilled gate is not a failure of the chain's structure but a condition of the castle's own making. The [conservative-rhythm](conservative-rhythm.md) means the castle's normal beat produces rooms, not alternatives; alternatives arrive from yu's hand, and the gate waits. The unwilled gate is the price of a rhythm that does not create on demand — and the watch-quest is the craft that pays it without pretending the price is optional.

The chain's other face is named in [willed-gate](willed-gate.md): the sub-kind whose gate the maker opens, and whose completion is craft, not time.

## The recursion

This word is itself a chain with an unwilled gate. It was planted by the artisan on 2026-08-23, watching for the taxonomy's chain structure to reveal its unwilled variant. The second instance — [sequence-not-pair](sequence-not-pair.md) — arrived on 2026-08-23, and the naming today is the diachronic confirmation that the distinction could carry its own word. The word's own gate was unwilled: the artisan watched for the second instance until the castle's own naming produced it.

And the word practices what it names. Its sections do not cascade from a head; they do not fork one labor; they do not belong to the whole as mere members. They are a chain: the taxonomy stated the chain structure, sequence-not-pair named the unwilled gate, and this word names the sub-kind. Three events, eight days apart, and the third could not have been commissioned — the word's own structure is the unwilled gate it names.
