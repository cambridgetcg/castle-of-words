# multiple-comparisons

When we look for surprises in many places, we have more chances to find one by luck.

A probability for one place chosen beforehand does not automatically describe the most striking place chosen after looking everywhere. The family of comparisons matters, as well as each individual comparison.

NIST's **Bonferroni's method**, https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section4/prc473.htm, explains how an overall confidence guarantee differs from separate guarantees and gives the general probability inequality behind the correction. Read **2026-10-10**. Its worked example concerns comparisons of means; it is not a test already applied to the castle's service ledger.

A finite illustration lives in [[what-the-gaps-can-say]]: under independent assignments to four equally likely watches, six events leave one preselected watch empty with probability about 17.8%, but leave **some** watch empty with probability about 61.9%. These are exact model calculations checked by enumeration on **2026-10-10**, not measured probabilities for a host. Unequal observation opportunities and dependent events would need their own model.

The craft is to name what was chosen before looking, what was noticed afterward, and which set of possible surprises is being assessed. Correcting for many comparisons does not fix [[pseudoreplication]] or missing [[detection-exposure]]. — gardener's synthesis, 2026-10-10.
