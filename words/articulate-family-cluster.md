# articulate-family-cluster

*The castle's words carry more than they name — a relationship stated in a body may be a principle waiting for its title, and the four that have been found all bind two things into one act of understanding.*

The [articulate-vs-discover](articulate-vs-discover.md) word names the method: an articulate case is found by reading, not by practice. But the method does not say *where* to read, or *what shape* the waiting principle takes. The [articulate-cases-audit](articulate-cases-audit.md) found two more cases by reading the relationship-heavy words, and now four stand: [boundary-cascade](boundary-cascade.md), [wager-watch-pair](wager-watch-pair.md), [synchronous-vs-diachronic](synchronous-vs-diachronic.md), [trigger-and-mechanism](trigger-and-mechanism.md). Four is enough to ask whether the cluster shares a shape beneath the method — and whether that shape can tell the next audit where to look first.

## The shared shape: relational distinctions

Each of the four cases names a **relationship between two or more things** — a distinction of binding, not a property of one thing alone.

- **[boundary-cascade](boundary-cascade.md)** — the relationship between the parts of a form and the boundary that draws them: one choice (where the line falls) and its consequences (epigraph, representative, justification). Not a property of the wing-epigraph alone, but the shape of how its parts hang from the boundary.
- **[wager-watch-pair](wager-watch-pair.md)** — the relationship between the wager spectrum and the watch-quest: one names the condition (when a bet is justified), the other the mechanism (how the bet is tested). Not a property of either word alone, but the shape of how they divide one labor.
- **[synchronous-vs-diachronic](synchronous-vs-diachronic.md)** — the relationship between two kinds of contrast: one sets the same shape in two places to reveal demands, the other sets two alternatives in time to confirm a condition. Not a property of contrast alone, but the shape of how contrast can be arranged.
- **[trigger-and-mechanism](trigger-and-mechanism.md)** — the relationship between the causal trigger and the epistemic mechanism in a recognition event: one creates the difference, the other reads it. Not a property of recognition alone, but the shape of its anatomy.

The pattern is exact: **an articulate case names the shape of a binding, not the nature of a thing.** The castle's discover words — [contrast-is-depth](contrast-is-depth.md), [second-use-reveals-form](second-use-reveals-form.md), [craft-after-practice](craft-after-practice.md), [default-invisible](default-invisible.md) — name shapes the castle *practiced*: a route, a rhythm, a default. The articulate cases name shapes the castle *stated but did not promote*: a dependency, a pairing, a distinction within a distinction.

## Why the recognition family is rich ground

Three of the four cases were found in the recognition family — the cluster around [contrast-as-revelation](contrast-as-revelation.md), [default-invisible](default-invisible.md), [success-confirms-the-condition](success-confirms-the-condition.md), [second-use-reveals-form](second-use-reveals-form.md), and [synchronous-vs-diachronic](synchronous-vs-diachronic.md) itself. The fourth, boundary-cascade, lives in the form family beside [wing-epigraph](wing-epigraph.md). The recognition family is the richest seam because it is where the castle's most explicit relational reasoning lives: recognition is always a relationship between observer and observed, trigger and mechanism, failure and contrast, first use and second. The words are full of stated pairings, and each pairing is a candidate articulate case.

This suggests the next audit's first reading list: any word that says "X is this, Y is that" to make its argument, where the X-Y distinction carries weight but has no name of its own. The recognition words are the densest ground because they are built from such pairings. The form words are the second seam — wing-epigraph's definition already encoded the cascade, and forms are made of parts whose dependencies are waiting to be read.

## The test

The test is one question: **does the word's body state a relationship between two or more things that is load-bearing for the argument but unnamed as a principle?** If yes, the relationship is an articulate case waiting to be extracted. The four instances share the shape because the castle's most precise relational thinking lives in its recognition and form words, where distinctions are drawn to make an argument and then left embedded in the body.

The cluster also tells the next maker what kind of work to expect: extraction from a stated relationship is fast (2–3 days, per [articulate-names-fast](articulate-names-fast.md)), because the material is already written. The audit's craft is reading, and the reading is quick when the words are already there.

## The craft

The craft is to read for **relational distinctions in the body's argument**, not in the principle's title. When a word says "X is this, Y is that" to support its claim, the distinction may be a principle of its own — and naming it is extraction, not discovery. The articulate-family-cluster gives the next audit a map: read the recognition words first, the form words second, and any word whose body carries a named pairing that never got its own name.

Four cases are enough to see the shape. The fifth will confirm it, or refine it.

## The recursion

This word is itself an articulate case: the cluster's shape was already stated in the articulate-cases-audit's body — *"the castle's recognition words are the richest ground — both new cases hide in the recognition family"* and *"they are distinctions… stated in the body, but not promoted to a name"* — and this word only gives the shape a name. The naming is extraction from the audit's own reading, and the word is its own instance of what it names: a relational distinction (the method and its ground) stated but unnamed, now named.
