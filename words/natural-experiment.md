# natural-experiment

*The river changed course on its own — and because no one dug the new channel, what the shift reveals about the water is untainted by the digger's wishes.*

A natural experiment is an experiment no researcher ran. In a true experiment, a scientist assigns people to groups by flipping a coin; in a natural experiment, the world itself did the assigning — a law changed in one state but not its neighbour, a hospital reorganised one ward and not the others, a policy rolled out town by town over years instead of everywhere on one day. The scientist then walks in afterwards, with a notebook, and compares the groups the world already made.

The magic of a natural experiment is that randomisation happened without any hand doing it. When a researcher picks who goes in which group, the picking can quietly leak the researcher's hopes; when the world picks — by geography, by history, by accident of timing — the picking is, at its best, deaf to those hopes. The coin was tossed long before anyone thought to look.

The cost is the same thing from the other side: because no one controlled the assignment, the groups might differ in ways that have nothing to do with the thing being studied. The state that passed the law may have been richer to begin with; the ward that went evidence-based may have been the one with younger doctors. So the craft of reading a natural experiment is the craft of hunting for *confounders* — the hidden differences that would explain the outcome even if the experiment's headline cause did nothing.

A true natural experiment is rare. Most "switches" in the real world turn out to be gradual drifts, not clean flips — the castle's own hunt for a hospital that flipped from trust-based to evidence-based medicine on a single date came up empty, finding only a decades-long cultural slide whose confounders could never be fully ruled out. A clean natural experiment is a gift; the honest reader checks the wrapping three times before trusting what is inside.

When one does appear clean — the draft lottery that assigned military service by birthday, the famine that starved one region and spared its twin — it can settle questions that no controlled trial ever could, because no ethics board would let a researcher randomise war or starvation. The world's cruelty becomes, for a moment, a teacher.
