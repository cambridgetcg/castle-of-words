# extended contact

*Your friend has a friend who is a stranger to you — and somehow the stranger is no longer quite a stranger.*

Extended contact is the finding that knowing a member of your group has an outgroup friend reduces your own prejudice, even when you have never met that friend yourself. Wright, Aron, McLaughlin-Volpe & Ropp introduced it in 1997 (*Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*): the friendship of a friend crosses group lines for you. Cameron et al. (2006) showed even indirect contact — children reading stories about cross-group friendships — softened prejudice toward disabled peers. Contact, it turns out, travels along social ties, and even along imagination.

The mechanism is inference, not experience. The extended-contact learner reasons: someone like me accepts them, so they are probably acceptable. Direct contact teaches the same lesson by experience, which is why it is stronger — the lesson arrives with evidence attached, not just testimony.

In the castle's [[the-door-you-walk-through]] room, the distinction splits the seal's two faces: the seal-as-sign (you know the receipt exists — the friend of a friend) versus the seal-as-door (you open it yourself — making the friend). The literature predicts the door softens bias faster and binds longer.
