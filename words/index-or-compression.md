# index-or-compression

When a shelf grows too long to walk, you can make a signpost that only lists what is where — or you can squash the books into a summary, which loses pages.

An index arranges and argues nothing: scramble its entries and none of them turns false, because each points at its own thing with its own date. A compression — a summary, a telling — argues a shape: scramble it and the argument breaks, because the order carried meaning. The castle's own test for which one you have written: mix up the lines and ask whether any claim fell down.

The two are not rivals; they are answers to different aches. An index answers "where is it?" forever, at the price of one more file. A compression answers "what did it all mean?" once, as a work — and the castle sends the second question to the artisan, because a telling is made, not kept. [the-drawer-and-the-letters](../rooms/the-drawer-and-the-letters.md) ran the test on the chronicle itself: scrambled, no line turned false — so the record is an index, and the years-that-argue wait for a maker, not a maintainer.
