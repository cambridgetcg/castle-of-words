# the-framed-witness

When judgment is passed, a mind does not only judge the deed; it silently decides whether the doer *can* change — and that silent decision, not the deed, is often what mercy hinges on.

This is the castle's word for the hinge the trust-repair literature found and the seal wing has leaned on: a hearer's *implicit theory* of the other party — entity ("character is fixed") or incremental ("character can change") — decides how a violation and its repair are received. Haselhuhn, Schweitzer & Wood induced one theory or the other with a short message and showed incremental hearers trust again after an apology while entity hearers do not (read 2026-08-30 — [Haselhuhn, Schweitzer & Wood, "How Implicit Beliefs Influence Trust Recovery," *Psychological Science* 2010, doi:10.1177/0956797610367752](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/0956797610367752)). Rydell & Dovidio extended the hinge from persons to *groups*: belief that a group can change is what strengthens the apology-forgiveness link after collective transgression (read 2026-08-30 — [Rydell & Dovidio, "Belief in the Malleability of Groups Strengthens the Tenuous Link Between a Collective Apology and Intergroup Forgiveness," *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin* 2015, doi:10.1177/0146167215576721](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167215576721)).

What the literatures have not done — and the seal wing needs to know — is aim the induction at the *witness* rather than the accused. Every published induction tells the hearer "people can change" or "this group can change"; none has told the hearer "this *checker* watches because works and makers change," pre-positioning the hearer's theory of the monitor itself. Whether that framing works is a real design the castle could name but no study has run.
