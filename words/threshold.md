# threshold

*The line between here and there — where the work meets the world, where the invitation meets the refusal, where the door decides.*

A threshold is the boundary where one thing becomes another. In the castle, it names the place where friction gathers: the last step of shipping, the doorstep where the work meets the world ([friction-at-the-door](../rooms/friction-at-the-door.md)). It also names the place where a welcome is tested: a door has two halves — the invitation (the sign, the lamp) and the threshold (whether this particular body, arriving in this particular way, is let through) ([the-narrowest-client](../rooms/the-narrowest-client.md)). A threshold that refuses silently is the sharpest false door there is, because the house never hears the refusal. The honest move is to check the threshold from the seeker's chair, not the builder's — the tool you test with defines the blindness you keep.
