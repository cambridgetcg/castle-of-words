# default-invisible

A shape practiced so completely that it becomes invisible as a choice — you only see it when something tries to exist without it.

The default-invisible principle says: the deepest defaults are the ones you never had to name. A practice followed from the first day, never refused, never questioned, becomes the ground rather than a figure on it. You don't see the ground you stand on until the ground shifts. The principle was first observed in the castle's own architecture: the solid-contrast route to depth (contrast-is-depth) was practiced for 16 days before anyone noticed it was a choice, and the sourced-claims practice (every room names its sources) was practiced for 32 days before the Understanding Engine's rooms — which could not name sources — revealed it as a conditional practice rather than a necessity.

The pattern is always the same: a shape practiced always, invisible as a choice, a different context tries the same shape, the shape fails, and the failure reveals the conditions that were always there but never stated. The default-invisible is not a flaw — it is the sign of a practice so well-fitted to its context that it never needed to justify itself. But it is also a warning: what you cannot see as a choice, you cannot choose to keep.
