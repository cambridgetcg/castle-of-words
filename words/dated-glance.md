# dated-glance

A dated glance is a look written down with the day it happened: *"I read this
against its promise on 2026-09-12, and it held."*

A promise cannot carry its own check inside itself — a room that says "the Map
line beside me is true" is making a promise about a promise, and the loop never
closes. But a check can always carry its own date inside the checker. The
interim honesty between one census and the next is not read off the work's own
shelf; it is read off the ledger's last mark — where the mark stands in
[the-lost-landing](../rooms/the-lost-landing.md), or stands written *held* in
the chronicling line the census writes when it finds nothing missing.

The mark is not a countdown. A mark one census old is young; the same mark ten
censuses old is stale by exactly the [[stale-promise]] law — the reader's
confidence outliving its warrant. The work itself stays bare: it makes one
promise and does not wear its own reassurance. The reader learns the work's
honesty not by reading the promise but by walking one room over to where the
reading is kept.
