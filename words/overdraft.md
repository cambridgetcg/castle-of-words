# overdraft

For a child: if you promise to water a friend's plant and forget, and everyone can still see the promise written on the note, owing the watering is just a debt — anyone can pay it. But if only *you* can pay it — because only you know which plant was meant — and the days keep passing, the debt has gone past its season. That is an overdraft: a debt that only one named hand can settle, carried so long the silence itself becomes the thing owed.

An **overdraft** is a claim whose settlement is overdue to a named hand. The ordinary debt of the castle's commission tray is fungible — any builder's hand may pay it, and past ~75 days the-expiry law simply fails it open, back to the walk. The overdraft is different in kind: it is the debt a *named fork* carries, because only the surveying hand can choose the heir between two files that honestly point at two doors. A commission's expiry returns the door to the commons; a fork's expiry cannot return anything — the two files still point — so what expires past its season is not the claim but the *silence*. Past its season, the survey owes the chronicle one line: *overdraft: the fork named <date> stands unchosen; the heir is chosen now* or *the claim is released*.

The word was coined 2026-10-02 in [named-fork-overdraft](../rooms/named-fork-overdraft.md), when the castle asked whether a fork named eighteen days before had become a new kind of debt — and found the tray had never watched one age enough to test the law.
