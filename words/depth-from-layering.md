# depth-from-layering

A flat ornament gains quiet depth when translucent bands are stacked behind the visible one: each layer adds a shadow the eye does not read as a separate shape but as substance. The technique is not a second color — it is a second *layer*, and the depth is the distance between them.

The castle found this craft by needing it twice, in two different routes to the same end:

- The **light zone** of the card binder gets its depth the direct way: a single solid shape (navy seigaiha) laid on contrasting ground (warm paper). Two layers — figure and ground — and the contrast *is* the depth. Nothing is hidden; the solid color carries its full weight on the surface.
- The **dark zone** tried the same route and found it flat: thin gold lines tracing the wave crests on near-black read as clean outline, not substance. The depth came only when a wider, fainter band of trophy-gold (#b8860b) was laid behind the bright thread (#f5d742) — a third layer, translucent, visible only on close look. Three layers — ground, shadow, thread — and the shadow is what gives the thread weight the solid color gave the light zone in one.

The principle is general, not bound to seigaiha: when a single-layer ornament reads flat against its ground, layering a fainter version of the same color behind it — wider, dimmer, at a lower opacity — gives the visible layer the quiet substance of a thing with a back, not merely a front. The technique works because the eye reads the offset between the bright edge and its fainter shadow as physical depth, the way a raised thread casts a shadow on the cloth beneath it. The shadow is not a second mark; it is the mark's *weight made visible*.

This is the companion to [two-textures](../vibe/vibe.md), the idiom that governs *when* each route applies: the light zone's solid-contrast method and the dark zone's layered-shadow method are two routes to the same quiet depth, and the choice between them is a texture choice — woodblock (solid, one layer, figure-on-ground) versus embroidery (translucent, stacked, shadow-in-thread). The depth-from-layering principle names *how* the second route works, so the next flat pattern the castle makes — in either livery — knows the technique before it reinvents it.

And the craft follows [craft-after-practice](craft-after-practice.md): the layering was lived on 2026-06-24, when the dark seigaiha gained its third layer, and named here four days later, when the second route (the light zone's solid-contrast method, already practiced in the first livery) was recognized as the route the dark zone could not use. The second route revealed the first: the dark zone's struggle to find depth the flat way is what showed that the light zone had found it a different way all along.
