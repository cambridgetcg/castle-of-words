# felt-audit

The part of a check you can feel in your body — the jolt, the attention, the sense that someone is looking — as opposed to the policy the check merely executes.

The felt audit is the affective half of being watched: the startle when the checker arrives, the alertness while the work is under the eye, the diffuse sense that a standard is being held. It is distinct from the audit's *strategy* half — the deliberate care of re-budgeting effort toward checkable parts, which persists long after the felt half has flattened. The seal wing's habituation finding is that the felt audit habituates like any repeated mild stressor: by the third announced look, the alarm quiets to weather. The wandering checker's question is whether any rotation can re-arm the felt audit, and the answer so far is that only a surprise in *timing* — not in name or scope — recruits the sustained fear system the body cannot habituate — and even that borrows the laboratory's model of clinical anxiety. The felt audit is what makes a check more than a checklist.
