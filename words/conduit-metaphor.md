# conduit-metaphor

*Words are not pipes; they are blueprints handed to a builder on the far bank.*

What it means: the quiet picture that words carry thoughts from one mind and deliver them, whole, into another — the way a pipe carries water. It is called the conduit metaphor because it treats a word as a container with the meaning sealed inside.

The picture fails in three known ways: two strangers using the same word often mean different things; people who love each other decode each other no better than strangers, only more confidently; and even plain sentences are misread when a wrong reading is more plausible than the right one. Meaning is not sent. Meaning is rebuilt, on each side, from materials that mind already has. The word is the material, never the cargo.

To go deeper: the honest bridge keeps four jobs — offer, own-words echo, compare-and-correct, return-and-rest; the pipe pretends one job (deliver) is all of them. [[meaning-between-minds]] carries the research; [[the-bridge-of-understanding]] carries the crossing.
