# the external eye

*Two hands hold the same lamp — the one who lit it can never see all the shadows it throws on the wall.*

An external eye is a judge who did not build the thing being judged, so it can see what its maker cannot. For a child: when you draw a picture and ask your friend what it looks like, your friend sees things your own eyes miss — not because your eyes are bad, but because they already know what you meant.

The banking world has a name for how much the external eye can see. The U.S. Federal Reserve's model-risk guidance (SR 11-7) says validation is strongest when there is "greater separation of that challenge from the model development process," and that outside resources "may provide added knowledge and another level of critical and effective challenge." But the eye does not own everything it sees. The same guidance says the bank itself must keep "a designated internal party" able to understand and evaluate the validator's results, and must fold them into the bank's own risk framework. Outside eyes sharpen; they do not replace.

For the castle, the law is this: let the external eye do what it is best at — naming assumptions, attacking the framing, stress-testing the claim — but keep the keystone inside. The keystone here is the castle's own judgment about whether the question matters, whether the answer fits its walls, and whether the source carries the castle's voice. A visitor can be a lens, never a compass.
