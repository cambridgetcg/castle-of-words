# meaning-seam

*The hem where a badge's promise lives; tug it, and the promise either holds its shape or quietly stretches into one it was never sewn to carry.*

For a child: if a sticker on your workbook says "I checked this with a grown-up," the meaning-seam is the place to look when, months later, the sticker starts quietly meaning "this is a good workbook." The sticker did not change; the promise did.

The meaning-seam is one of two places a seal or badge can fail as it spreads — the other is the [[wallpaper-seam]], where nobody reads it at all. At the meaning-seam the badge's *claim* inflates or fuzzes: a seal built to certify provenance begins to be read as certifying quality, a label sewn for one thing is tugged into meaning another. [the-scoped-seal](../rooms/the-scoped-seal.md) found that narrowing a badge's claim protects this seam and only this seam — the Fair Trade label's claim is still exactly readable after twenty years — while leaving the wallpaper-seam wide open.

The two seams fail independently, and a seal that survives one still dies at the other: a narrowly-scoped badge keeps its meaning and still collapses into wallpaper; a badge everyone glances at but nobody reads has failed at the wallpaper-seam however crisp its meaning stayed.
