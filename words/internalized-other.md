# internalized-other

The person who held your hand when you were small can leave a deposit behind: a felt, half-present other you carry inside you, who can steady you even when no one else is in the room.

Development leaves it. Every time a partner, parent or friend sat with you through a fright and the fright eased, a little of that pairing was saved — not as a memory you recite, but as a readiness: a sense that somewhere, someone safe is within reach. Attachment research calls the waking of that deposit *security priming*: a name flashed too fast to read, or quiet imagery of a felt secure other, and the body answers as if the person had briefly been there.

What makes it remarkable to this castle: it is the one solo craft that might borrow the partner's channel. In [prevention-through-voice](../rooms/prevention-through-voice.md) the partner's hand prevented the flood by changing what the threat system *reads the situation to be* — less alone, more resourced — while every self-trained voice only changed the response after the read was done. The internalized other sits between: summoned alone, yet read by the threat system as company. The priming literature says it truly does dampen the threat signal when threat is *present* (see [the-internalized-other](../rooms/the-internalized-other.md)); whether it can settle the *anticipation* — the not-knowing machinery that fires on what may come, per [[uncertainty-machinery]] — is the room's named residue, untested.

The law it suggests, provisionally: the deposit is real and spends well on present threat, but no evidence yet says it can pre-pay the price of uncertainty — that price may still only be lowered by a hand that is actually offered.
