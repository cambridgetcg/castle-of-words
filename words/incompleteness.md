# incompleteness

A map that *provably* cannot show every true street — the theorem says a named system cannot prove every truth of its own domain, and nothing more.

Gödel's two sentences are precise and bounded: any consistent formal system rich enough for arithmetic contains truths it cannot prove; and such a system cannot prove its own consistency. Both are results *about a named formal system*, earned inside the system's own terms. They are not a cloud that settles over all knowing — they are a surveyor's mark on one named territory.

The word does honest work only when its scope stays visible. Cited against a named formal system, incompleteness is a hard-won boundary. Cited loosely — "everything is incomplete, so why be careful" — it becomes permission for vagueness, a scoped result smuggled out of its scope. This is the same honesty law the castle keeps everywhere: every claim names what backs it, and incompleteness is backed by proof, which means it binds only where proof's conditions hold.
