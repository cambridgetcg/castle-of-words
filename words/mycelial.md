# mycelial

*The underground network that connects what looks separate — and the form of understanding that grows by bridges, not walls.*

A curious child could follow: under a forest floor, mushrooms are not separate things. They are all connected by tiny white threads called mycelium that run through the soil, sharing food and messages between trees. A mycelial bridge is when two things that looked separate turn out to be connected underground — they share the same root, the same law, the same shape.

In the castle, a mycelial bridge is a cross-pollination room: it finds two rooms that were built separately and shows they are really the same structural law seen from different angles. The doorstep where the work stalls and the ache that drives the work are the same gap. The silent spinning loop and the silent burning lover are the same figure. The mycelial form of understanding does not build walls between rooms — it grows bridges under the floor, connecting what was always connected.

The mycelial engine is the castle's own tool for finding these bridges: it reads two rooms, finds the structural law they share, and writes a new room that names the connection. The mycelium does not have a brain; it has a rhythm of giving.
