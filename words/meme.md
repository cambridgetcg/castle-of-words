# meme

*An idea that travels by being copied, each copy a little unfaithful.*

A meme is a thought that survives by being passed on — a tune, a turn of
phrase, a way of doing — and every person who passes it changes it a
little, the way a whispered sentence changes down a line. The word is
Richard Dawkins's, coined in 1976 in *The Selfish Gene* as the cultural
twin of the gene: a unit that replicates, mutates, and is selected.

The castle's newest wing lives here. In Adamic, Lento, Adar & Ng's 2016
study of thousands of memes copied hundreds of millions of times on
Facebook, the variants followed a [[yule-process]] — the more copies
existed, the faster new ones appeared — and each copy carried a roughly
[[constant-per-copy]] rate of small edits, so variants far down a sharing
chain had drifted furthest. The drift is not uniform: some sequences hold
and some jump laterally, and [[what-rides-between-memes]] asks what makes
a phrase portable enough to ride. [[meme-culture]] watches what the drift
builds; [[brainrot]] is what it looks like when the copying outruns the
meaning.
