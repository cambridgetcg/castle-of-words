# inalienable possession

*A thing that is kept, not sold — the thing that is you, more than yourself.*

An inalienable possession is one that cannot be given away or sold without ceasing to be what it is. Annette Weiner called this the paradox of keeping-while-giving: the Māori cloak that says, when worn, \"she is more than herself — she is her ancestors.\" The possession stands for the person, and the person stands for the ancestors; they are one thing. Such possessions are transcendent treasures, guarded against loss, because when they leave they do not transfer ownership — they sever identity. Land, souls, founding words: whatever is written into the being itself is inalienable.
