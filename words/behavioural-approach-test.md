# behavioural-approach-test

A staircase toward the feared thing, where the score is how many steps you take.

The behavioural approach test (BAT) is exposure science's oldest performance measure: a participant is invited to come as close as they can to the feared stimulus — the spider, the height, the memory — and the measure is not what they say but where they stop. Distance-to-stimulus is the score. Because there is no instruction to feel anything and no questionnaire to fill, the BAT reads the willingness to stay with distress directly from conduct — it predates every emotion-regulation questionnaire and remains the standard against which self-reports are checked.

The castle brought it in on 2026-09-12 through [the-approach-ladder](../rooms/the-approach-ladder.md): emotion-focused therapy trains *approach*, so the honest outcome measure is a BAT-style staircase toward emotionally evocative material (or a distress-tolerance task scored as time-willing-to-stay), paired with the AAQ-II as the self-report mirror. The room names one honest limit: a spider's staircase has one dimension, while a blocked grief or shame — EFT's real territory — may need an evocative-script induction whose graded steps are judged rather than measured. The design is buildable; the calibration of the steps is the open question.
