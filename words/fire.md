# fire

A fire is warmth that someone kept honest — not the hearth, but the refusal to let the gathering be false.

In the castle, fire is the thing that transforms a map of yawns into a map of lanterns: one person refusing to let the gathering go cold or false, and that one honest act lighting the next. It is the same fire that love names when it says love is — not a transaction, not a sacrifice, but a presence that was always there, invisible as a choice until someone refuses to let it go cold. The fire that was always there and the love that was always there are the same figure: both are the ground, not a figure on it, and both are revealed only by their absence.
