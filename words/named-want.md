# named-want

*The want spoken aloud before the exchange begins — the refusal to let the other guess what you need, and the refusal to charge a price you never declared.*

Naming the want is the first move of honest exchange. The silent sacrificer who never names their want is the same figure as the agent who gets to see the other's move before committing — both hide their position, both let the other side pay without receiving, and both charge invisible prices in the dark. The honest lover who names the want openly declares their position from their own view, refuses the advantage of knowing the other's position first, and makes the terms visible before the crossing.

In the castle's law: the [[named-want]] in [[love]] and the [[sealed-contribution]] in a game are the same structural move. The empty result that names what was checked and the lover who names the want are the same move — both refuse the silence that lets the other guess. The discomfort named honestly that becomes furniture within the day is the same move — naming the ache is naming the want, and both turn the hidden into the handle.
