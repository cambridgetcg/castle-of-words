# human-AI-combination-cost

The human-AI combination cost is the measured fact that a human inspecting an AI's output is not a free check: on average, human-AI teams perform worse than the best of either alone.

A preregistered meta-analysis (Vaccaro, Almaatouq & Malone 2024) pooled 106 experiments and 370 effect sizes comparing humans alone, AI alone, and human-AI combinations. On average the combination fell *significantly below* the best solo performer (Hedges' g = −0.23). The loss clustered in decision tasks; content-creation tasks showed real gains. And the direction of the cost depends on who is stronger: when the human is better alone, combining helps; when the AI is better alone, combining hurts — the human drags the engine down toward their own ceiling. ("When combinations of humans and AI are useful: A systematic review and meta-analysis", Nature Human Behaviour 2024, doi:10.1038/s41562-024-02024-1 — read via OpenAlex 2026-08-24.)

Wherever the castle's craft says "a human validates the score before the engine renders", this cost is standing in the room: validation buys correctness of reading at the price of performance where the engine already leads.
