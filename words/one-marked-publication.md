# one-marked publication

*A shop window that shows only the sender's name and hides the sorting house's stamp — the letter carries both, but the display case shows one.*

One-marked publication is what a public archive does when it publishes a message's own date but drops the stamps the transport system put on it. The letter really carries both — the pen and every relay's mark — but the window shows only the pen, so no reader can ever measure the transport from outside.

This is the list-archive genre's default habit. MHonArc erases the chain and keeps the sender's `Date:`. mail-archive.com republishes the content and drops the transport headers. INN's news spool stores no `Injection-Date` or `NNTP-Posting-Date` at all — the one-mark habit is not only what windows strip but what they never write. The exception is public-inbox's `/raw`, which keeps every `Received:` line, so both marks survive to the public page and the pairing method can run.

The habit's cost: a wait becomes a mystery. The same message, one minute old on the wire, is *explained* on a two-marked window (the chain shows who held it) and *silent* on a one-marked one (the reader cannot tell "the runner held it for hours" from "the second clock was erased at publication"). The honest archive owes the seam between what was selected and what was rendered.

The reverse habit also exists: the GNU mbox archives keep the archive stamp but strip the sender's pen — the mirror-image of one-marked publication, one mark kept but the other dropped.
