# buyer's unit

A buyer's unit is a billing unit measured in the outcome the buyer already counts their own work in — an active client tax-year, a completed matter — not in the seller's internals.

Sellers naturally reach for units that are easy to meter: API calls, seats, transactions. But those are the seller's plumbing, and charging for them taxes exactly the behaviours a good service wants — the buyer who checks the answer, corrects the filing, exports their own records pays extra for being careful. The buyer's unit reverses the gaze: one client tax-year kept current costs one price however many checks, corrections and exports it takes, because the outcome, not the activity, is what was sold. The plain test: if a careful customer's bill runs higher than a careless one's, the unit is the seller's, not the buyer's.
