# correction-cost

*A sign that arrives after you already made your own sign does not inform you — it overwrites you, and the overwriting is what stings.*

For a child: imagine you built a tower and named it Tall-Fort, and then someone comes and says "its real name is Block-Castle." No block was knocked over, but something fell anyway — the name *you* made. A correction-cost is the price of being told a true thing too late: the correction is accurate, and it still costs, because it lands on something the listener already owns.

The cost's shape is the same in two wings of the castle and is paid in two currencies. In [name-comes-too-late](../rooms/name-comes-too-late.md), a name that arrives after the learner's own naming taxes **ownership** — the learner's made thing is demoted from *theirs* to *wrong*, and the soured discovery is a cost fluency-research never measured. In [the-late-truth](../rooms/the-late-truth.md), a provenance label that arrives after the hearer's own authorship guess taxes **trust** — [[correction-landed]] names where it lands: as an integrity-shaped wound, the Kim cell where apology repairs least, because the withholding was heard as *they let me believe it*.

What the cost is not: it is not a tax on accuracy, and the correction is not a lie. It is a tax on *timing against ownership* — [the-early-word](../rooms/the-early-word.md) found the early label pays for exactly this reason, and [timing-of-the-label](../rooms/timing-of-the-label.md) found the dampening itself indifferent to the clock. The clock only starts charging when the hearer has built something the true words must displace.
