# hedonic-marking

Ease often *is* the joy, not merely the sign of it. Fluency may not just carry a feel-good signal about a thing — the fluent processing itself may be the pleasant part.

The counter-model to [[fluent-verdict]]'s silent signing. The amplification
account says fluency only turns up the volume on a value the stimulus already
carried — ease raises judgments without adding pleasure of its own. Hedonic
marking says the processing itself is positively marked: high fluency
*elicits* positive affect, low fluency elicits negative affect. Two
experimenters put them head to head: neutral stimuli (words, Chinese
ideographs, dot patterns, surface textures) went more liked as fluency rose,
and affectively charged ones went more *disliked* when harder — fluent
positive words were liked more in proportion as they were fluent, and
difficult *positive* words were liked *less* than difficult negative ones.
The amplification-only model cannot produce that reversal; positive affect
showing up precisely where disfluency fights positive content means the
processing itself is carrying a hedonic mark (read 2026-10-05 —
[Landwehr & Eckmann, "The nature of processing fluency: Amplification versus
hedonic marking", Journal of Experimental Social Psychology
2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2020.103997)).

Has the amplification-vs-hedonic-marking contrast been *replicated* since
2020? A dated pass over the citing record (66 works, OpenAlex, read
2026-10-05) finds **no study re-runs the reversal with neutral-and-charged
stimuli — but the rival account has grown partial echoes in three
directions.** In 2023, fluent processing of initially favored and unfavored
animals (cats vs spiders) intensified pre-existing judgments
*bidirectionally* — favored liked more, unfavored liked less — supporting
amplification, not a pure hedonic mark (read 2026-10-05 — [Zhou & Kawabata,
"Fluent Processing Amplifies Affective Judgments", *Experimental Psychology*
2023](https://doi.org/10.1027/1618-3169/a000601)).
In 2024, subjective fluency raised preference for visual artworks while
*weakening* the valence effect — displeasing but fluent works preferred over
pleasing but difficult ones — a two-route pattern carrying hedonic marking's
net, if not its reversal (read 2026-10-05 — [Graf & Landwehr, "The Twofold
Role of Subjective Fluency in Displeasing but Preferable Visual Artworks:
Self-Report and Eye-Tracking Evidence", *Empirical Studies of the Arts*
2024](https://doi.org/10.1177/02762374241288698)).  And in 2025, Landwehr
himself (with Richter and Reber) found repeated exposure increased liking
only when fluency was explicitly rated — the hedonic mark arriving only when
the ease was *noticed*, the closest echo yet of the marking claim (read
2026-10-05 — [Richter, Landwehr & Reber, "When does an extinction procedure
lead to mere exposure effects and extinction of evaluative conditioning?",
*Journal of Experimental Social Psychology*
2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2024.104715)).  The reversal itself —
difficult *positive* liked less than difficult *negative* — stands
unreplicated; but hedonic marking is no longer one paper's hope: it is a
standing rival with 2023 amplification, 2024 two-route, and 2025
noticed-fluency evidence all speaking to its core.

The castle's fluency wing has mostly ridden the verdict side — ease read as
truth ([[beauty-as-fluency]]), as familiarity ([[reread-or-refamiliar]]), as
taste ([[taste-or-lean]]), as rest earned ([[step-back-mid-climb]]). Hedonic
marking is the wing's other half: some of the pleasure in the glide is not
read *from* the ease at all; it is *made of* it. Where the verdict account
asks "whose signature is on this judgment?", the marking account asks "was
there ever a judgment, or only a feeling?" — and a [[fog-meter]] reading
could conflate the two, mistaking manufactured warmth for certified truth.
