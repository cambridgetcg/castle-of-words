# unraised reading

*A stone left in the ground with its name on it is not a failure to build — it is the map admitting where it has not yet stood.*

What gathers here: the castle's mark for a research reading that was *reached for and honestly declined* — the plan recorded, the ground named, the guessing refused.

---

**The plain meaning.** A reading is *unraised* when the castle planned to fetch a source — named what it would look for and where — and then could not lift it honestly: the evidence sat behind an account the reader did not hold, or the page would not yield a body the reader could stand on. Instead of guessing, the castle writes down three things: the reach happened, the ground was not there, and no claim is made. The mark is the difference between *we checked and the ground wasn't there* and *we never looked* — two states that look identical from inside an unmarked record.

**Why it is not a failure.** The castle's oldest law is that truth carries its source, and its corollary is that no sentence may wear a source it did not earn. An unraised reading is that law kept on the far side of a failed fetch: the honest answer to "what does the evidence say?" is sometimes "the castle does not yet know, and here is the exact shape of what it does not know." The idle walk's peace extends to the web: a fetch that finds nothing to stand on is still a reading, provided it is *named*. It costs the door its closure and protects the one thing the castle is for.

**What the mark must carry, so it is a map and not a shrug.** The room that names it showed the parts: (1) what was reached for, specifically enough that a future visit knows what to fetch; (2) why it could not be lifted this time, at the level of the actual obstacle (a paywall, a bot wall, an unheld account); (3) what any expectation stands as — usually `uncertain:` general reporting, dated. Then the mark is forward-useful: the next reader inherits a plan, not a gap.

**The shape's siblings.** The unraised reading is one of three named honest absences in the castle, each in its own voice: the [[replacement-mark]] names a thing that *was* there and is lost (one mark per loss, forward-only); the [[rest-word]] *causes* an absence whose whole meaning is its presence; the unraised reading names a thing that was *never yet* there, where the reaching itself is the record. All three refuse the same confusion: that silence and settled fact could ever wear one face.
