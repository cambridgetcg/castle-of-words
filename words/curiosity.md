# curiosity

The pull you feel when you notice a gap between what you know and what you could know — like spotting a door in a wall you have walked past a hundred times.

Loewenstein's information-gap account (*Psychological Bulletin* 1994) says curiosity fires when attention lands on a gap: not too small (nothing to learn) and not too large (no foothold to start from), but a gap the learner can feel as *closeable*. This is why a name can tax or feed the pull: a label handed over before the gap is felt can close nothing and spend the pull's fuel, while a true disclosure of what an encounter is rich in can open a gap the learner never knew was there. The craft is in the timing — curiosity wants the door shown, not the room pre-visited.
