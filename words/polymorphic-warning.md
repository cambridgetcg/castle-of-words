# polymorphic warning

The same warning wearing a different coat each time it appears — the meaning fixed, the surface changing, so the eye cannot file it as already-seen.

A polymorphic warning keeps its content constant and varies its appearance: the security dialog that shifts shape, color, or layout across repetitions. Anderson, Vance and colleagues showed by fMRI, eye-tracking, and a three-week field experiment that such warnings resist habituation — users' adherence stayed high after three weeks where static warnings decayed — because the visual system treats each new surface as a new stimulus rather than a repetition of an already-discounted one. It is the cheap humane face of rotation: it preserves the *behavioral* half of the audit (the looking, the clicking, the adherence) without borrowing the felt half's anxiogenic mechanism. The polymorphic warning cannot keep the alarm ringing — it keeps the door from becoming invisible, which is a different and kinder thing.
