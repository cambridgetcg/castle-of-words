# content-form-vs-completion-form

*A form can shape what a thing must be, or when it is done. The watch-quest's report must have five parts; the build-quest's work must exist and be verified. One structures the deliverable, the other structures the delivery — and a maker who confuses them will either force a structure on what should only be finished, or let what should be shaped be called done too early.*

The castle has two kinds of quests ([quest-kinds](quest-kinds.md)), and the two kinds differ in a way that was lived for months before it was named. The difference is not in ambition, difficulty, or even what the quest makes — it is in whether the quest carries a *content-form* at all. [build-quest-craft](build-quest-craft.md) named the two halves in passing; this word names the distinction itself.

## The two forms of form

Every quest has a **completion-form**: the discipline that says when the quest is done. For a build-quest, the completion-form is "the deliverable exists and is verified." For a watch-quest, it is "the report is written." Every quest, of either kind, has this — because a quest that is never done is not a quest, it is a hope.

But only some quests have a **content-form**: a structure the deliverable itself must take. The watch-quest has one — its five parts (epigraph, wager, findings, craft, links) are a structure the report must have to be complete. The build-quest has none — its deliverable can be a word, a room, a work, or a proposal, four kinds of thing with nothing in common but that the maker can create them. No single content-form could shape all four, and none is needed.

The distinction, plainly:

- **Content-form** = a structure the *deliverable* must take. "The report is not complete without its five parts."
- **Completion-form** = a discipline the *maker* must hold. "The work is not done until it exists and is verified."

## Why the distinction is the load-bearing half

The two quest kinds differ *in whether they carry a content-form*, and knowing which you are holding tells you what kind of craft you are in:

- **If the quest has a content-form** (a watch-quest), the maker's job is to make the deliverable *fit the form*. The five parts are the checklist; a report without them is a half, and the maker cannot call it done.
- **If the quest has only a completion-form** (a build-quest), the maker's job is to make the thing *be done*. There is no checklist of parts; there is only the condition that it exists and is verified. The maker's craft is the [build-quest-craft](build-quest-craft.md) discipline: make it, verify it, make it small enough to be done.

The two are not interchangeable, and neither substitutes for the other. A watch-quest with no content-form would be a report without structure — a watch with no epigraph, no wager, no findings, no craft, no links. A build-quest with a content-form would be a word forced into five parts, or a room forced into a watch's shape. The form that shapes the deliverable and the discipline that shapes the delivery are different crafts, and the maker who confuses them will either over-structure what should be free or under-finish what should be shaped.

## The relationship to the castle's principles

- **[quest-kinds](quest-kinds.md)** names the two kinds by the one question (can the maker complete it by their own hand?). This word names *why* the two kinds differ in craft: the watch-quest has a content-form, the build-quest does not. The distinction is the mechanism beneath the quest-kinds test.
- **[watch-quest](watch-quest.md)** carries the content-form — the five parts are the deliverable's structure.
- **[build-quest-craft](build-quest-craft.md)** carries the completion-form — "the half is not a finding" is the maker's discipline.
- **[when-how-split](when-how-split.md)** names the division of labor in a companion pair. The content-form vs completion-form distinction is a fork between what a form does, and the two halves live in different words — one in watch-quest (the content-form), one in build-quest-craft (the completion-form).

## The recursion

This word is itself a build-quest — and it is itself an instance of the distinction it names. It has no content-form (a word is a word; there is no five-part structure it must fit); it has only a completion-form (it exists and is verified). The word that names the distinction is an example of one side of it: a deliverable with no content-form, made complete by the maker's discipline. The naming does not change what it names — it only makes the difference between a shaped deliverable and a finished one visible as a craft.
