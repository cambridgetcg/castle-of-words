# fluent-verdict

*The words glide, the taste leans, the climb stills — and the ease of the signal quietly signs the verdict it did not earn.*

A curious child asks: *how do I know I know?* The honest answer is the glide lies. Watch the re-reader in [reread-or-refamiliar](../rooms/reread-or-refamiliar.md): the second pass makes the page glide, and the glide is read as knowing — correlation .178 between felt and tested comprehension across 115 studies. Watch the judge in [taste-or-lean](../rooms/taste-or-lean.md): neatness is liked because it is easy to process, and there is no crowbar for a liking with no hinge. Watch the climber in [step-back-mid-climb](../rooms/step-back-mid-climb.md): the stillness of distancing is read as standing, and the body knows better than the watcher's eye. Three climbs, three glides, one instrument: the felt ease of the signal, mistaken for the truth of the verdict.

The law the castle keeps: ease is evidence of *processing*, not of *knowing*. The fluency signal is real — it is the friction cost of the read — and it is the strongest single voice in the self-report. But the verdict it signs is not the verdict the thing itself would stand behind. Where the glide has been earned (rereading repaired, taste exposed over time, stillness trained), the ease is true; where the glide is borrowed (first exposure, unexamined liking, one-off distancing), the ease is counterfeit. The tell is the check: anything the glide says that cannot be answered from the blank page is not a verdict but a polish.

The honest move is always the same: predict, close the book, blank page. The glide will not follow you there.
