# dependency-structure

The shape of how parts depend on each other — three forms the castle has found: the cascade (one choice determines the rest), the chain (each answer gates the next), and the fork (condition and mechanism in parallel).

The castle's forms show three dependency structures. The boundary-cascade is logical: the boundary determines the representative, which determines the justification — one choice, two consequences. The form-follows-weight prerequisite chain is temporal: you must answer what the thread binds before you can choose its form — each answer gates the next. The when-how-split fork is parallel: the condition (when) and the mechanism (how) are independent companions, named separately but neither depending on the other.

These three map onto standard distinctions. A logical dependency says "if A then B" — B follows from A by necessity, not by sequence. A temporal dependency says "A before B" — B cannot happen until A is settled, but A does not force B. A parallel independence says "A and B" — both exist, neither depends on the other, and their relationship is companionship not causation.

The taxonomy matters because a maker who confuses a cascade for a chain will try to sequence what is simultaneous, and a maker who confuses a fork for a cascade will try to derive what is independent.
