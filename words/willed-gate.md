# willed-gate

*A door is not a gate — a gate you wait for, a door you open. The prerequisite chain has doors: each answered question unlocks the next, and the maker's hand is on every latch. The willed gate is a sub-kind of the chain structure, the kind the maker completes in a sitting, and the sitting is the bridge across a gap the maker alone can close.*

The [dependency-structure-taxonomy](dependency-structure-taxonomy.md) names four structures: cascade, chain, fork, and belonging. The chain is the temporal structure: each part gates the next, and the parts cannot be discharged in parallel. The castle has now practiced two kinds of chain, and the difference lives at the gate: what the gate waits on. The [unwilled-gate](unwilled-gate.md) names the chain whose gate is an event the castle cannot produce by deciding — the arrival of a confirming alternative, a second instance, a pattern that recurs or fails. The unwilled chain is completed by time, not by craft, and the watch-quest is what spans its gap. This word names the other kind.

**The willed gate** is a chain whose gate is a door the maker opens. The chain's parts gate in sequence: each answer unlocks the next, and the next cannot be asked until the previous one is given. But the answers are the maker's own. The maker asks the first question, answers it, and the gate opens — not because the world moved, but because the maker did. The chain is completed by craft, and the sitting can end with the form done. [form-follows-weight](form-follows-weight.md) is the instance: three questions in order — is there a choice? if so, is the reason stated? if so, does the form fit the weight? — and a maker who knows the binding can answer all three before the sitting ends. The gates are doors, not events; the latch is a hand, not a clock.

## The sub-kind

The taxonomy's sharpened third branch asks *is the gate willed or unwilled?* and both answers have now been named. The willed gate is not the default against which the unwilled is measured; the two are sub-kinds of one structure, and each has its own craft.

A chain with a willed gate is a **prerequisite chain**. Its parts are questions the maker asks, choices the maker makes, namings the maker performs. The chain is completed at a sitting because the maker holds every latch. The chain with an unwilled gate is a **contrast chain** — the [sequence-not-pair](sequence-not-pair.md) — whose parts are events the world must produce, and whose completion is a matter of time, not of craft. The two chains are the same structure at different altitudes of agency: both are temporal, both are ordered, both are sequences where the next part waits on the last. The difference is what the waiting is for. The willed gate waits for the maker's next question; the unwilled gate waits for the world's next move.

The build-quest is the practice that carries the willed gate. The [build-quest-craft](build-quest-craft.md) names the completion: the quest is done when the deliverable exists and is verified, and the maker completes it by their own hand. The build-quest is the bridge across the willed gap — the maker walks through the door they already hold. The watch-quest is the bridge across the unwilled gap — the maker holds the gate open until the world answers. The two quests are the two bridges the castle has built over the two kinds of gap, and knowing which gap a quest spans is knowing which bridge to build.

## The craft

The craft is to know which kind of gate a chain has before you name its parts. A willed chain is completed by the maker's own hand: name the gates, answer them in order, and the form is done. The maker who pauses at a willed gate, waiting for a confirmation that is not needed, has mistaken the door for a gate — and the sitting will stall at a threshold the maker could open. The willed gate's craft is to **proceed**: ask the next question, answer it, and let the chain run its course. The willed gate does not resist; it opens when the maker turns the latch.

And the craft is to know that the willed gate is not the trivial case. The unwilled gate is harder to wait through, but the willed gate is harder to design. A chain with willed gates must be built so that each answer genuinely unlocks the next — the questions must gate, not merely follow. [form-follows-weight](form-follows-weight.md)'s three questions gate because each answer determines whether the next question is askable: if there is no choice, there is no thread to state; if the reason is not stated, there is no weight to fit. A chain whose questions are only listed, not gated, is not a willed-gate chain — it is a flat list in temporal clothing, and the maker who mistakes it for a chain will answer the questions out of order and find the answers do not hold.

The willed gate's craft is therefore structural as well as temporal: the maker must build the gates, not just walk through them. The gates are the maker's own design, and a willed-gate chain is only as strong as its weakest latch. The maker who names the prerequisites in order has done the hard part; the walking through is the easy part. The unwilled gate is a structural condition of the castle's [conservative-rhythm](conservative-rhythm.md); the willed gate is a structural achievement — a chain that works because the maker designed the gates to gate.

## The test

When the taxonomy's third question — *do the parts gate each other in sequence?* — answers yes, ask the sharpened follow-up: **is the gate willed or unwilled?**

- **Willed gate**: the maker can open it. The next part is a question the maker asks, a choice the maker makes, a naming the maker performs. The chain is completed by craft, and the sitting can end with the form complete. The instance to test against: [form-follows-weight](form-follows-weight.md) — three questions, one sitting, the form chosen. The craft is to proceed; the maker holds every latch.

- **Unwilled gate**: the maker cannot open it. The next part is an event the world must produce — a confirming alternative, a second instance, a pattern that recurs or fails. The chain is completed by time, and the sitting ends with the gate still standing. The instance to test against: [sequence-not-pair](sequence-not-pair.md) — the synchronous reveal, then the diachronic confirmation, and the second could not be commissioned. The craft is to watch; the [watch-quest](watch-quest.md) is the bridge.

The willed gate is the sub-kind the castle has practiced longest. Every build-quest the castle has run is a willed-gate chain: the maker reads the quest, names the deliverable, writes it, verifies it, marks it built. The chain's gates are the stages of the making, and every one is a door the maker opens. The build-quest is the castle's first and oldest way of completing a chain, and its gates are all willed — the maker's hand is on every latch, and the sitting ends with the thing done.
