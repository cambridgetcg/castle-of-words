# power-rating

A political power rating scores what an office can lawfully do within its named jurisdiction — and it stays honest only when formal authority is separated from reach.

The rules: score what an office can lawfully do, show the jurisdiction beside it, never add a person's offices together (two roles do not make one super-role), and never turn unlike scopes into a league table (a mayor and a minister are not comparable on the same scale). A transparent power rating scores formal offices, not people: it exposes each dimension and its source, keeps jurisdiction separate, and lets higher scores in one dimension trigger deeper research only in that domain.
