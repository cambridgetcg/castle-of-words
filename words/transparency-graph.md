# transparency-graph

A network of facts about public life where each edge says exactly what its source proves, what it does not prove, and for which dates the claim was valid.

A donation, a meeting, a contract, a company role, a land title, an investigation, and a finding are different kinds of facts. Joining them into one edge — "X influenced Y" or "X owns Y" — manufactures a relationship the sources did not establish. A truthful transparency graph keeps each fact as its own edge, names the capacity in which each party acted, and never fuses separate facts into an inferred tie unless a competent official finding establishes the relationship. The graph's honesty lives in what it refuses to connect.
