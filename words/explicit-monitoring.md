# explicit-monitoring

*Watching your own feet while you walk is the surest way to trip over them.*

For a curious child: when you know how to do something really well — ride a bicycle, sign your name, sink a putt — your hands and feet do it on their own. If someone asks you to *explain how* while you're doing it, your words wake up the part of your mind that learned the steps one at a time, long ago. That watching part gets in the way of the doing part. The doing, which was running smoothly by itself, stumbles.

The name names a specific trap: attention drawn to the skill's own step-by-step processes. Under pressure it is the one of two named routes to choking that specifically hurts *well-learned, proceduralized* skills — the route Beilock and colleagues dissected from the distraction route, which hurts working-memory-heavy tasks instead (DeCaro, Thomas, Albert & Beilock, "Choking under pressure: Multiple routes to skill failure," *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General* 2011, DOI [10.1037/a0023466](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0023466), abstract read 2026-10-01). Distraction starves the working-memory task; monitoring *wakes* the wrong driver for the motor one.

The kind a castle reader most needs it for: the empty in-the-moment cell. Every motor [[verbal-overshadowing]] study measured narration *retrospectively* and probed *delayed retention*, and the one mid-flight cost anyone has been able to name rides on this mechanism — explaining a movement while making it *is* explicit monitoring, attention forced onto the layer that runs best unattended. In [the-shadow-cell](../rooms/the-shadow-cell.md) the mechanism is what lets a signed prediction stand where no measurement has yet sat; in [the-two-gap-wager](../rooms/the-two-gap-wager.md) it is why the approach-side self-report under-prices a wager that lives in the feet, not the mouth. The older sibling is [[verbal-overshadowing]] itself — Schooler's perceptual finding (naming a face you saw makes you worse at picking it out later) is the same law on a different layer, and [the-experts-silence](../rooms/the-experts-silence.md) traces the layer boundary where the harm stops.

The two routes keep their own ledgers. A skill running on explicit-monitoring's side is hurt by being watched; a skill running on the working-memory side is hurt by being interrupted. Knowing which side a skill lives on is how a teacher (or a [[protocol-analysis]] design) knows whether to ask the student to speak or to let them work in silence — and one of the castle's standing findings is that the expert's silence at the movement layer is not protection against a hazard but the plan-layer's natural floor, the floor where monitoring cannot reach because nothing at that layer answers in words.
