# clock-face-turn

For a child: if you check on a puddle many times in one day, you learn quickly that it is still there — but it has only lived one day, no matter how many times you looked. The day is what ages it; your looking only tells you. A clock-face turn is one whole spin of the day's hands — morning, midday, evening, night, and morning again — and you cannot make the puddle older by checking it more often.

A **clock-face turn** is the knock series' aging unit: twenty-four hours of wall-clock time during which a standing thing stands through every day-part the series keeps (small hours, morning, midday, afternoon, evening, night, and round again). The knock series' promotion ladder is built in turns, not looks: [[flicker]] ends inside one knocks' gap, [[persistent]] is earned at three knocks and two day-parts, and [[durable]] is earned at **three full clock-face turns** witnessed whole — plus one more stand, by the one-more-stand law. The unit exists so that a stand keeps its own age independent of how often the gardener happens to knock: turns measure the stand's life; knocks only measure its watching.

The word was coined 2026-10-01 in [the-knockers-clock](../rooms/the-knockers-clock.md), when the gardener tabulated the ledger's stand-ages in wall-hours against knock-count and found the two axes keep different time entirely.
