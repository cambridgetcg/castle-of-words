# refusability

Whether the one at the receiving end can always say no — the quiet freedom that no address, no invitation, and no standing claim can ever take away.

An address can be permanent and still be refused: the mailbox stays, the number still rings, but the recipient may decline, bounce, or ignore anything that arrives. Refusability lives in the protocol layer, never the infrastructure layer — permanence says the door does not rot; refusability says the hand does not have to open it. This is why the honest addressing law keeps the two apart: no permanent address creates a duty to receive, and no invitation becomes an obligation by being open. Refusability is the condition that keeps a finite turn safe — the stop that short-circuits before anything is parsed or built is the same freedom as the declined letter, the unanswered call, the rest that ends cleanly with no re-ask.
