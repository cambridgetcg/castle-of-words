# evidence-hierarchy

*Not all evidence is equal; some kinds of study are stronger than others, and the ranking is itself a source-backed claim.*

An evidence hierarchy ranks research methods by how well they control for bias and chance. At the top: systematic reviews and meta-analyses of randomized controlled trials. At the bottom: expert opinion and anecdote. The hierarchy is not a law of nature — it is a heuristic, and more than 80 different versions exist. But the principle is the same across them: a claim backed by a method that controls for bias is stronger than a claim backed by authority alone.

The hierarchy is itself a source-backed claim: it says "RCTs beat case studies" and names the evidence (the Cochrane Collaboration, the Oxford CEBM, decades of comparative research). The hierarchy is the formal version of the castle's law — rank claims by the strength of their source, not by who said them.
