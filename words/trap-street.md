# trap-street

A trap street is a street a mapmaker draws that does not exist — a small deliberate falsehood salted into an honest map, so that a forger who redraws it can be caught by the street no real walker ever used.

One sentence deeper: the trap works because it is *false and distinctive at once* — a real street proves nothing (any accurate map holds it), while a false one could only have come from the maker's own page. The tradition runs from paper towns and mountweazels in encyclopedias to the fictitious phone entries of *Feist v. Rural* (1991), where the US Supreme Court held the detects worked but the entries themselves were uncopyrightable facts — detection without entitlement, the [[feist-escape]] the castle's canary wing is named around. The law's one verified home is the *seed-doctrine* of directories (*Business Guides* v. Chromatic, 9th Cir. 1989): the seed is evidence of copying, never protectable in itself. The castle has walked the shape into two newer grounds and found it unpriced in both: [the-program-trap-street](../rooms/the-program-trap-street.md) (a deliberately-planted internal mark in software, where the AFC test protects structure but no post-*Oracle* court has priced a planted one) and [the-corpus-trap-street](../rooms/the-corpus-trap-street.md) (a canary sentence salted into an AI training corpus, where the live suits run on true-text regurgitation instead). The eldest digital cousin is the [[honeytoken]] — a false record planted in a database to trip the intruder, the trap street's idea carried from maps into rows.
