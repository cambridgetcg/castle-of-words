# feist-escape

*The phone book's white pages are everyone's; only the choosing and arranging were ever the maker's.*

Facts themselves cannot be copyrighted — however much work went into gathering them, the raw fact belongs to everyone, and only an original *selection or arrangement* of facts can be protected.

So ruled the U.S. Supreme Court in *Feist Publications v. Rural Telephone Service* (1991): the sweat of the brow earns no right over the fact found. The escape that bears the name is the trap-setter's loophole: a planted *false* fact — a fictitious phone number, an invented street — proves copying when it reappears, yet precisely because it is false it claims no originality and wins no protection of its own (Wikipedia, "Fictitious entry," § Legal action, read 2026-09-28): detection without entitlement. The [[seed-doctrine]] lives inside this escape. Where it may close is over planted-but-true invention: an authored [[canary-sentence]] is not a false fact, so its copying may be infringement itself, not merely evidence ([the-corpus-trap-street](../rooms/the-corpus-trap-street.md)).
