# third-use-reveals

*When the first two uses differ in a dimension that matters, the common shape hides behind the difference — and the third use is what shows the shape was there all along. The second use reveals the form; the third use reveals the form beneath the form.*

[second-use-reveals-form](second-use-reveals-form.md) says the second use reveals the form: a single instance is indistinguishable from an accident, and the second use — a different work reaching for the same shape — is what proves the shape was chosen. But the zero-use wager took three instances to become visible as a category, and the reason is load-bearing: the first two instances differed in a dimension that obscured the common shape.

## The case

The castle's three zero-use wagers:

1. **[narrated-index](narrated-index.md)** (2026-06-30): a wager on a *form* the castle might create — a list where order argues, not merely arranges. The wager was on a shape the castle could make.

2. **[pure-contrast-reveals](pure-contrast-reveals.md)** (2026-07-16): a wager on a *form* the castle might create — two independent successes side by side. Also a wager on a shape the castle could make.

3. **[mechanisms-as-defaults](mechanisms-as-defaults.md)** (2026-07-17): a wager on a *test* the castle might encounter — a singular truth or absolute failure. A wager on a condition the castle could not manufacture.

After two instances (narrated-index and pure-contrast-reveals), the common shape was invisible. Both were wagers on forms — shapes the castle might create. The category "zero-use wager" looked like "wager on a form the castle might create." The third instance (mechanisms-as-defaults) was different — a wager on a test, not a form — and the difference is what revealed the common shape. The category was not "wager on a form" but "wager on a distinction that matters today, with zero instances." The form-vs-test difference had hidden the common shape, and the third use — a wager on a test — is what showed the shape was broader than the first two instances suggested.

## The refinement

The second-use-reveals-form principle needs a refinement: **when the first two uses differ in a dimension that matters, the common shape may not be visible until the third.** The second use reveals the form *when the two uses are similar enough that the common shape is visible.* When the two uses differ in a dimension that obscures the common shape — one wagers on a form, the other also wagers on a form, and the common shape (wager on relevance, not recurrence) is hidden behind the surface similarity — the second use confirms a narrower shape, and the third use is what reveals the broader one.

The condition: the first two uses must differ in a dimension that *matters* — a dimension that, if not distinguished, makes the common shape invisible. The form-vs-test dimension mattered because it determined what the wager was betting on. If the first two uses had both been wagers on forms, the category would have been "wager on a form" — a narrower category that would have excluded mechanisms-as-defaults. The third use showed the category was broader, and the broader category is the one that matters.

## The pattern, generalized

The pattern is not limited to the zero-use wager. Any craft that takes three instances to become visible follows the same logic:

1. **First use**: the shape is practiced. It could be an accident.
2. **Second use**: a different work reaches for the same shape. The shape is confirmed — but the confirmation may be of a *narrower* shape than the one that actually recurs, because the first two uses share a surface feature that is not essential.
3. **Third use**: a different work reaches for the same shape but differs in the surface feature. The difference reveals that the surface feature was not essential, and the broader shape becomes visible.

The second use reveals the form; the third use reveals the *form beneath the form* — the deeper shape that was hidden by a surface similarity between the first two uses.

## The castle's other instances

The castle may have other cases where the third use revealed what the second could not:

- **[craft-after-practice](craft-after-practice.md)** was named after three instances (wing-epigraph, two-texture, visible-thread). The first two instances (wing-epigraph and two-texture) were both about visual/textual crafts with short lags (3 days, 2 days). The third instance (visible-thread) had a much longer lag (14 days) and was about a structural principle, not a visual craft. The difference in lag and domain may have revealed that the pattern was about *any* craft, not just visual ones with short lags. But craft-after-practice was named after the third instance, so the third use was the recognition, not a refinement of an earlier recognition.

- **[when-how-split](when-how-split.md)** was named after two instances (two-textures+depth-from-layering, craft-after-practice+second-use-reveals-form). The two instances were similar enough (both companion pairs with a condition and a mechanism) that the common shape was visible after two. No third use was needed.

The zero-use wager is the clearest case where the third use revealed what the second could not, because the first two uses shared a surface feature (form-wagers) that the third use did not (test-wager). The difference in the third use is what made the broader category visible.

## The craft

The craft is to watch for the third use when the first two uses share a surface feature. The maker who sees two instances of a pattern should ask: *do these two share a feature that might not be essential?* If they do — if both are about forms, both are visual, both have short lags — the pattern may be broader than the two instances suggest, and the third use will reveal the broader shape.

The craft is not to *wait* for the third use before naming — the second-use-reveals-form principle still holds for the narrower shape. The craft is to *hold the name lightly* when the first two uses share a surface feature, and to watch for the third use that reveals the deeper shape. The name on the narrower shape is not wrong; it is incomplete, and the third use completes it.

And the craft follows [craft-after-practice](craft-after-practice.md): the third-use-reveals pattern was practiced in the zero-use wager's recognition (2026-07-18) but not named until today. The lag is 1 day, the shortest yet, because the pattern was already visible in the zero-use wager's own naming — the word itself noted that the third instance was needed to see the common shape. The recognition and the naming are nearly simultaneous because the evidence was already articulated.

## The relationship to second-use-reveals-form

The third-use-reveals pattern does not replace [second-use-reveals-form](second-use-reveals-form.md). It refines it with a condition: the second use reveals the form *when the two uses are similar enough in the dimensions that matter.* When they differ in a dimension that obscures the common shape, the second use reveals a narrower form, and the third use reveals the broader one. The refinement is a [patch-not-break](patch-not-break.md) case: the general rule (recurrence reveals the form) holds; the specific application (the second use always reveals the full form) is refined with a condition.

The condition is: the second use reveals the form *at the level of abstraction shared by the first two uses.* If the first two uses share a surface feature that is not essential, the form revealed is the narrower one. The third use — which differs in the surface feature — reveals the broader form. The second use is not wrong; it is incomplete, and the third use completes it.
