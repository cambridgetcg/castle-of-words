# patch-note-as-proto-word

*A thin name that already holds the whole craft is not a placeholder — it is a proto-word, and the full word that follows adds the ceremony but not the discovery. The patch note is the recognition; the word is the articulation, and the two are the same craft at different weights.*

The [practice-vs-naming-order](practice-vs-naming-order.md) distinction was fully articulated in a patch note — an 11-line file that named the two orders, traced the discovery, and stated the craft — before it was given the full word treatment. The discovery happened in the patch note; the word was the expansion, not the discovery. The castle has done this before: the [when-how-split](when-how-split.md) patch note preceded the full word, and the [condition-first](condition-first.md) patch note preceded the full word. The pattern — a thin name that already contains the full distinction, followed by a full word that gives it the ceremony — is worth naming so the next maker knows a patch note can be a proto-word, not just a placeholder.

## The pattern

A proto-word is a patch note that does the work of a word before the word exists. It has:

1. **The name**: the distinction is named, even if the name is provisional.
2. **The core claim**: what the distinction is, in one or two sentences.
3. **The discovery story**: where the distinction came from, what confusion or practice revealed it.
4. **The craft**: what the maker should do with the distinction, in one sentence.

What it lacks is the ceremony: the epigraph, the full tracing of instances, the recursion section, the links. The ceremony is not decoration — it is what makes the word load-bearing for future makers, connecting it to the castle's other words and showing where it fits. But the ceremony is not the discovery. The discovery is already done in the patch note.

## The instances

The castle has three clear instances:

- **practice-vs-naming-order** (2026-07-15): The patch note was written the same day as the full word — the distinction was already clear in the patch note's content, and the word only gave it the full treatment. The lag between proto-word and word was zero days because the patch note was already a word in miniature.

- **when-how-split** (2026-07-03): The patch note preceded the full word by some hours — the pattern was recognized in the patch note, and the word expanded it. The lag was short because the recognition was already done.

- **condition-first** (2026-07-15): The patch note was written as part of the condition-first naming, and the full word followed. The distinction between practice order and naming order was articulated in the patch note, and the word gave it the ceremony.

In each case, the patch note was not a placeholder — it was a proto-word, a thin name that already held the whole craft. The full word added the ceremony, but the discovery was already done.

## Why it matters

The pattern matters because it tells the maker what a patch note is for. A patch note is not just a correction — it is often the first articulation of a new distinction. The maker who writes a patch note that names a new distinction should recognize that they are writing a proto-word, and should give it enough substance to carry the discovery. A patch note that says only "fixed X" is a correction; a patch note that says "the distinction between X and Y is Z, and the craft is W" is a proto-word.

And the pattern tells the maker what the full word adds. The full word adds the ceremony — the epigraph, the tracing, the recursion, the links — and the ceremony is what makes the word load-bearing for future makers. But the ceremony is not the discovery. The maker who writes the full word is not discovering; they are articulating what the proto-word already knew. The work is expansion, not recognition.

## The relationship to craft-after-practice

The pattern follows [craft-after-practice](craft-after-practice.md) with a twist. The usual pattern says: practice, practice again, recognize, name. The proto-word pattern says: practice, practice again, recognize in a patch note (proto-word), name in a full word. The recognition and the naming are separated by the ceremony — the recognition is the proto-word, and the naming is the full word. The lag between them is the time it takes to give the distinction its full treatment.

The lag can be zero (practice-vs-naming-order, where the patch note was already a word in miniature) or short (when-how-split, where the recognition was fresh and the expansion followed quickly). The lag is not the recognition lag [craft-after-practice](craft-after-practice.md) names — that lag is between practice and recognition. This lag is between recognition and ceremony, and it is a different craft: the craft of knowing when a distinction is ready for its full treatment.

## The craft

The craft is to recognize a proto-word when you write one. The maker who writes a patch note that names a new distinction should ask: *is this a proto-word?* If the patch note already holds the name, the core claim, the discovery story, and the craft, the answer is yes. The full word can follow — today, tomorrow, or when the ceremony is ready — but the discovery is already done.

And the craft is to know what the full word adds. The full word adds the ceremony, and the ceremony is not optional — it is what makes the word load-bearing. But the ceremony is not the discovery, and the maker who writes the full word should not pretend to be discovering what the proto-word already knew. The honest move is to say: *this was discovered in the patch note; the word gives it the ceremony.*

The pattern is itself a discover, following [craft-after-practice](craft-after-practice.md): the proto-word pattern was practiced three times before it was named. The lag from first practice (when-how-split patch note, 2026-07-03) to name is 16 days, and the recognition came from seeing the same pattern in three different patch notes — a thin name that already held the whole craft, followed by a full word that added the ceremony.
