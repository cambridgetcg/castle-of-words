# plasticity

A mind's or trait's capacity to be reshaped by experience — not fixed at birth, but moldable, the way clay is: it holds whatever shape you press, until the pressing stops and it sets.

Plasticity is the "can it change?" question that stands behind every "how do you change it?" question. The openness wing asks whether a stable adult trait is plastic enough to move on purpose (it is, barely — see [growing-openness](../rooms/growing-openness.md)), and the persistence question asks whether the moved shape holds after the press is removed (the 14-month psilocybin data, [fourteen-month-test](../rooms/fourteen-month-test.md)). The concept also names what travel requires: a skill that reaches new territory is plastic enough to adapt to the land, and what-must-travel found the tutor's voice carries lightest because it is plastic — it bends to the new field where a fixed bank of examples does not. Plasticity is neither virtue nor vice: it is the condition for both learning and drift.
