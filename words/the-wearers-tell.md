# the wearer's tell

*When the cloak stops warming the woman and starts asking to be worn, the tell is in the cold.*

The wearer's tell is the moment a booted king can no longer surprise themselves. It is not a feeling — the castle's flooding-self-read room already taught that the felt sense is the least reliable instrument. It is a *shape* in the speech: fluent claims without specific evidence (the gap), inability to look at one's own account from outside (the stuckness), and the long absence of an unintended turn — never once saying "and that caught me off guard." The Adult Attachment Interview codes these shapes to tell a speaker who owns their narrative from a speaker who is carried by it. The wearer's tell is the same shape turned toward a different question: not "do I still love my soul?" but "does my soul still leave doorways for me to walk through?" When the surprise runs dry, the tell has gone quiet, and the honest word for quiet is the beginning of being worn.
