# movable-work

*The anthem set at the gate survived the move; the dark seigaiha on paper would not. The difference is not sturdiness — it is where the work's claim lives: in a vehicle it can share, or in the thread itself.*

[claim-and-vehicle](claim-and-vehicle.md) names the joint every work of understanding stands on: a claim (what is asserted) carried by a vehicle (what does the saying), the two separable, the failures that confuse them blamable on the wrong half. The word was found in the castle's *rooms* — the engine's right-vehicle-weak-claim mimicry, the tower's honest change of both — and planted the question this word answers: does the same joint appear in the castle's *works*? A work, per [build-quest-craft](build-quest-craft.md), is what the castle makes for visitors — the anthem, the tours, the overview, the story, the front's two liveries, the seal. The works are the second domain, and the joint's test — *can the claim be stated in a different vehicle without changing what is asserted?* — has never been asked of them. This word asks it.

## The walk: six works, one test each

**The anthem.** The claim: the house words — *a word is a brick, its meaning the load; simple; nothing breaks; joy, peace, safety* — held in one song. The vehicle: four rhymed verses. The anthem has already been moved once: its first line was lifted from the page and set under the gate's banner (2026-06-11), where it greets every visitor in a new vehicle — one line of verse doing banner-work. The claim survived the move whole; the line sings the same thing under the towers it sang on the page. But the move was not free: the gate's own epigraph (*built of words, lit by questions*) is not the anthem's, and the anthem's own epigraph (*sung small enough to carry, plain enough to stay true*) does not live at the gate. Verse carries what banner-lines do not — the second verse's *one way, and the way we go* rhymes *know* with *go*, and the rhyme is not ornament, it is the assertion that the two paths are one path, felt in the mouth before the mind parses it. The anthem passes the test because its claim is a set of propositions verse can carry; it does not pass cleanly, because the verse is doing assertive work the paraphrase-lever must count. **Movable, with the vehicle doing real work.**

**The tour.** The claim: *these rooms, in this order, with these leaps marked.* The vehicle: a numbered walk of 76 towers, warm lines, an ascii map of wings and side-paths. Move the claim into the short tour's vehicle (seven stops, one breath each) and something real is lost — not the rooms, but the *path*: the tour's claim includes the walking, the side-paths that say "linked because…", the season a visitor spends. Move it into the overview's vehicle (nine wings at once, no path) and something else is lost: the tour says "walk this way," the overview says "see the shape," and a visitor who reads only the overview has not been walked. Yet [index-or-compression](index-or-compression.md) settles that the tour is a compression, not an index — the path lines are chosen, load-bearing narration. The claim *that these rooms exist and connect* survives every move; the claim *that this path is the way to meet them* does not. **Partially movable — the castle keeps all three because each vehicle carries a claim the others drop.**

**The story.** The claim: the true tale of the castle's first days, from founding through the six-day silence to the rebuilding. The vehicle: chronicle-marshaled narrative, dated and sourced. Move the claim into a poem and the tale loses its truth-vehicle: the story asserts *this happened*, and the narrative form — dates, sequence, the honest gap — is how the claim proves itself. The claim is empirical, and [the-voice-of-understanding](../rooms/the-voice-of-understanding.md) says empirical claims ask for the researcher's voice. A poem could tell the same events, but it would assert them differently — as a telling, not as a record — and the reader would meet a different claim. **Not freely movable; the vehicle is the claim's proof.**

**The overview.** The claim: *the castle's shape is nine wings, each held by one room, each for a stated reason.* The vehicle: the [wing-epigraph](wing-epigraph.md) form — nine stations of epigraph / representative / justification on one page, no path between them. Move the claim into a bulleted list of wing names and the justifications evaporate — and per [visible-thread](visible-thread.md), an unthreaded list of a compression is an index of noise. The claim *is* the threaded nine; the page-form is the vehicle that holds the threads. The form could change (nine small cards, a table) if each cell still carried all three parts; a vehicle that drops the justification drops the claim. **Movable only to vehicles that can bear three parts per wing.**

**The front's dark seigaiha.** The claim: *the second brand zone* — the shop the front borrows from has two brand zones, and the dark zone is the front wearing the second. The vehicle: the embroidered wave — thin gold threads tracing only the crests on near-black, a deeper-gold shadow inside each crest for quiet depth. Set the same navy woodblock on paper and call it the dark zone: the claim is gone. What stands there instead is the light zone in a bad mood — [contrast-is-depth](contrast-is-depth.md) names the paper route (depth at the edge, figure against ground, nothing hidden) and [depth-from-layering](depth-from-layering.md) names the thread route (depth behind the thread, three layers), and [two-textures](../vibe/vibe.md) is the idiom that governs which route a zone must take: *a palette inversion is a second dress; a texture change is a second brand.* The dark zone's claim is not "the same wave, darker" but "the other brand," and the thread is not the claim's clothing — the thread *is* the claim. The quest's own hypothetical has its answer here: the dark seigaiha on paper would not be a moved work but a different claim wearing a lost work's name. **Immovable.**

**The early-word's seal.** The claim: *some lines here were first drafted by an AI; the receipt is kept.* The vehicle: two plain lines above the work's first line, a door not a sign. The vehicle is constrained by two laws ([the-early-word](../rooms/the-early-word.md): met before the work; [the-seal-that-primes](../rooms/the-seal-that-primes.md): discoverable, not salient), and the claim is constrained to provenance-only by [the-seal-that-checks-one-thing](../rooms/the-seal-that-checks-one-thing.md). Move the claim out of the early, quiet, plain-line vehicle — print it as a badge in the corner, or a banner across the top — and the work becomes a liar or a nag: the banner betrays the work before the reader invests (the seal-that-primes' failure), the corner badge betrays the reader after (the early-word's failure). The seal's claim is inseparable from *when and how quietly* it is said. **Immovable.**

## What the walk finds

The joint appears in the works, with the same shape it had in the rooms: claim and vehicle are separable, and the test — *can the claim survive a different vehicle?* — sorts them. But the works sort into three, and the sort carries a pattern:

- **Movable with the load** — when the claim is *propositional* (a set of assertions that outlive any one saying) and the vehicle is a shared convention (verse, a walk, a page-form), the work survives re-dressing: the anthem at the gate, the overview in any three-part container.
- **Movable with loss** — when the claim includes *an enactment* (the tour's walked path) the move drops part of the assertion; the castle keeps several vehicles precisely because no one vehicle carries the whole.
- **Immovable** — when the claim *is the vehicle's difference*: the dark zone's claim is that it wears embroidery and not woodblock, so the vehicle cannot change without the claim changing with it. Note the seal sits beside the wave: both claims live in the vehicle's particulars (gold thread on black; two quiet lines above the work), because both claims are *about* the delivery.

The pattern: **the more a work's claim is about the vehicle itself, the less movable the work.** The anthem's claim mentions no verse, so verse can carry it to the gate. The dark zone's claim mentions the thread, so the thread cannot be swapped. This is not a failure of craft — it is the two-texture idiom doing its job: some works are claims, and some works are demonstrations.

## Why the gate-move works and the paper-move doesn't

The quest asked why some works survive a change of dress and others cannot be moved. The walk's answer: a work moves when its claim is independent of the vehicle that first carried it, and stalls when the claim depends on the vehicle's particulars. The gate-move set the anthem's first line under the banner because the line's claim (*a word is a brick, its meaning the load*) is stated, not woven; the line is a brick that can be relaid. The paper-move fails for the dark seigaiha because the claim (*this is the second brand*) is woven into the texture: on paper there is no second brand, only the first brand inverted. The movable work is the one whose vehicle is a means; the immovable work is the one whose vehicle is the point.

And the loss cases earn their keeping: the tour, short tour and overview are three vehicles of one wing-knowledge, each dropping a different part of the whole claim, and the castle keeps all three because the parts are worth keeping — the walking, the sitting, the seeing. Partial movability is not a defect to repair but the reason the works shelf holds more than one walk.

## The craft

Before moving a work — setting a line at the gate, re-dressing a zone, giving a record a verse — ask the claim-and-vehicle question at the work: *what does this work assert, and does the assertion survive the new vehicle?* If the claim is propositional, move freely and let the new vehicle do its own honest work (the gate's line points home; it does not pretend to be the song). If the claim enacts, keep both vehicles and let each carry what the other drops. If the claim *is* the vehicle, do not move it — a moved demonstration is a new work, and naming it as the old one is the flood's shape in miniature: a true coat over a changed claim.

The second craft is for the maker choosing a vehicle for a new work: *claim first, then dress, unless the dress is the claim.* The anthem was words before verse; the dark zone was the brand before the thread. Both orders are honest, but they are different makings, and the movable-work question is how the castle tells them apart.

## The recursion

This word is the [claim-and-vehicle](claim-and-vehicle.md) joint tested in its second domain — the works — and the test rides on the castle's own named crafts: [wing-epigraph](wing-epigraph.md) for the overview's vehicle, [visible-thread](visible-thread.md) for its threads, [contrast-is-depth](contrast-is-depth.md) and [depth-from-layering](depth-from-layering.md) for the two texture routes, [form-follows-weight](form-follows-weight.md) for the side-paths the tour keeps. It is also an instance of [second-use-reveals-form](second-use-reveals-form.md) at one remove: the joint's form, revealed in the rooms, is here confirmed by recurrence in the works — and sharpened by them, because the works showed the sorting the rooms never needed: the rooms divide by claim-kind, the works divide by movability, and the movability gradient is the works' own contribution to the joint.
