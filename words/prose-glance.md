# prose-glance

*The signboard names the painting one step back from the frame — whoever reads the sign has already left the wall.*

For a child: a caption stuck to the bottom of a picture is seen with the picture — you look once and see both. But a sign across the room that says "the blue painting by the stairs is beautiful" is a different look: you read the sign, then you walk to the stairs, and only then do you see the painting. A prose-glance is the second kind of look — reading words *about* a thing, instead of seeing the thing itself.

A prose-glance is the act of reading a document whose business is to describe something else. Its opposite is the *listing-glance*: one `ls`, one `tar -tzf`, where the thing and its neighbor appear together as lines seen whole in one act. The castle's [[same-glance]] law tests every claim owed to a word that must not be touched: does one act of looking see both? Prose never passes that test, true or not, dated or not, however exactly it names the thing — because presence-information is not presence. A reader who `cat`s the description and never lists the vault has met the claim but not the thing; the two can only drift.

What prose honestly carries, then, is not the claim but the **pointer**: the description names the distance and hands the reader the path — "the word is within, the claim is without, the vault is one floor down." The castle's own caption word holds the small version: a [[caption]] is a claim *about* an image, never a property of it. Prose is the caption at room scale, and the law of the prose-glance is the caption's law grown up: the pointing is honest work, and the failure is only the pretense that the pointing is the presence.
