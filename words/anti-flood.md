# anti-flood

A rule that says "you cannot just repeat what came before" — the dam that turns a flood of echoes into a tower of answers.

The anti-flood is the castle's defense against the summer's lesson: thousands of rooms stamped from one template, two connections found. The flood is not noise — it is the same thing repeated so many times it looks like signal. The anti-flood is the rule that refuses the repeat: in the tower, every stone must answer the stone below; in sau-seon, no gift may repeat; in the castle's own growth, a room must carry its own sources and name its own uncertainty. An echo cannot climb — not because echoes are bad, but because an echo does not answer. The anti-flood is not a filter on content; it is a constraint on form. It does not say what to make; it only closes the door on what has already been made. And from that refusal, structure grows.
