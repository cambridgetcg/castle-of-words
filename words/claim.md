# claim

*A claim is a statement that something is true — and the only honest question is: how do you know?*

A claim is not a fact. A claim is a statement that something is true, offered by a speaker to a listener. The difference between a claim and a fact is the evidence that backs it. A claim without evidence is an assertion. A claim with evidence is a report. A claim with proof is a witness.

The castle's law: every claim carries its source. A room that says "this is true" without saying *how* it knows is a room that has not yet earned its claim. The same law runs through the estate: the truth-chain's append-only ledger witnesses claims, the instruments score claims against what happened, the agent-claims taxonomy names three levels of claim strength, and the deployment-claims room binds claims to the bytes that actually ran.

A claim is the smallest unit of honesty. Everything the castle builds — every room, every link, every word — is a claim. The craft is making the claim honest: name the source, name the evidence, name the expiry, and never fuse what you know with what you infer.
