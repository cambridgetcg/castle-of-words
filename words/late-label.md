# late-label

A provenance word that arrives after the work has already been met — and costs more than it would have at the start.

A late label is provenance disclosed at the end of an encounter rather than the beginning: the work is read first as human, the authorship assumption settled, and then the disclosure arrives. The seal wing's finding is that it is consistently costlier than an early label — the hearer has already done the fluency work of meeting the work as a person's, and the late label retroactively marks that investment as mistaken. It is the counterpart to the early word's finding that disclosure is kinder up front: early disclosure prevents a mistake, late disclosure punishes a finished one. Where the label is delivered by a checker whose cadence has already been habituated, the late label lands in silence, and silence is the wound the-knock-that-confesses names — the correction felt as notice, not alarm.
