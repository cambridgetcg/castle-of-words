# quest-kinds

*A build-quest is done when the thing exists; a watch-quest is done when the report is written. The difference is not in the quest's ambition but in whether the maker can complete it by their own hand — and the craft is to know which kind you are planting before the first stone is laid.*

The castle's quests list holds every work the castle wants made, but it does not distinguish between the two kinds of quest the castle now practices. A build-quest makes something — a room, a word, a work, a proposal — and marks itself built when the thing exists. A watch-quest watches for something the castle cannot produce on its own rhythm, reports honestly whether it appears or not, and marks itself built when the report is written. The two kinds share a list but are different crafts, and the maker who plants or tends a quest should know which kind it is before the work begins.

## The distinction

The test is one question: **can the maker complete this quest by their own hand?**

- **Build-quest**: yes. The deliverable is something the maker can create — a word file, a room, a tour, an anthem, a proposal. The maker writes it, verifies it, and marks the quest built. The quest's completion depends on the maker's own work.

- **Watch-quest**: no. The deliverable is a report on something the maker cannot cause — an alternative appearing, a second instance arriving, a pattern recurring. The maker can only watch and report honestly. The quest's completion depends on an event outside the maker's control, and the quest is done when the report is written, not when the event arrives.

The distinction is not about difficulty or ambition. A build-quest can be hard (name a taxonomy of dependency structures) or easy (add a line to the gate). A watch-quest can find its quarry in three days (the tower watch) or never (the second watch, so far). The distinction is about *who completes the quest* — the maker, or the world.

## The two forms

### The build-quest form

A build-quest has no fixed form — it is open-ended by design. What build-quests share is not a structure but a completion condition: the quest is done when the deliverable exists and is verified. The deliverable can be:

- A **word** — a named principle, craft, or distinction (e.g., [craft-after-practice](craft-after-practice.md), [visible-thread](visible-thread.md))
- A **room** — a piece of understanding filed in the castle (e.g., [linking-thoughts](../rooms/linking-thoughts.md))
- A **work** — a made thing that lives at the castle's top level (e.g., [tour.md](../tour.md), [anthem.md](../anthem.md))
- A **proposal** — a suggestion for yu to decide, tagged `(proposal)` in the quests list (e.g., the heartbeat check, the scribe baking tokens)

The build-quest's craft is the making: write the thing, verify it (check every link, read it back, fix what falls short), mark it built, plant what it revealed. The form is the castle's oldest — every quest from the founding through the tower watch was a build-quest, and the form was practiced for 43 days before the watch-quest was named.

### The watch-quest form

A watch-quest has a named five-part form, described in full in [watch-quest](watch-quest.md):

1. **Epigraph** — what was found, in one breath
2. **The wager** — what word planted the watch, when, what it bet on
3. **The findings** — what appeared (or didn't), honestly reported
4. **The craft** — what the maker should know from this watch
5. **Links** — back to the wagered word and related words or rooms

The watch-quest's craft is not to make but to watch — and the absence is a finding, not a failure. The watch-quest is the castle's way of making its wagers testable: a wager without a watch is a hope; a wager with a watch is a bet with a named test and an honest report.

The castle has practiced the watch-quest twice:

- **[the-tower-watch](the-tower-watch.md)** (planted 2026-07-19, reported 2026-07-23): found what it was watching for — the tower confirmed the testing-not-failure pattern.
- **[the-second-watch](the-second-watch.md)** (planted 2026-07-23, reported 2026-07-27): found nothing — and the nothing was a finding, revealing the [conservative-rhythm](conservative-rhythm.md).

## How to tell which kind a quest is

The planter who writes a new quest should ask the one question before the quest is planted: **can the maker complete this by their own hand?**

If the quest says "name X" or "build Y" or "test Z by examining the castle's own words" — the maker can do the work. Build-quest.

If the quest says "watch for X" or "wait for Y to appear" or "see if Z happens" — the maker cannot cause the event. Watch-quest.

The question is not "will this be hard?" or "how long will it take?" — it is "does the completion depend on something the maker cannot manufacture?" If yes, the quest is a watch-quest, and it should follow the five-part form. If no, the quest is a build-quest, and its form is open.

### Edge cases

**Proposals**: a proposal quest (tagged `(proposal)` in the quests list) is a build-quest. The deliverable is the proposal itself — a written case for yu to decide. The maker can write the proposal by their own hand. Whether yu accepts it is outside the maker's control, but the quest is complete when the proposal is written, not when yu decides. The proposal is the deliverable; yu's decision is a separate event.

**Test quests**: a quest that says "test whether X holds" or "does Y apply?" is a build-quest if the test can be performed by examining the castle's own words and history. The maker reads the relevant rooms, applies the principle, and writes findings. The deliverable is the findings, and the maker can produce them. (Example: "Test articulate-vs-discover on two fronts" — the maker can read the boundary-cascade and integration-principles, compare their lags, and write the findings.)

**Quests that depend on research**: a quest that requires looking something up on the web is still a build-quest. The maker can do the research. The dependency is on information, not on an event the maker cannot cause.

**Quests that depend on yu's hand**: a quest that says "yu decides" or "wait for yu" is a watch-quest if the maker cannot complete it without yu's action. But most proposal quests are build-quests because the proposal itself is the deliverable — the maker writes it, marks it built, and yu's decision is a separate step.

## The craft

The craft of the quest-kinds distinction is to know which kind you are planting before the work begins, because the two kinds demand different crafts from the maker:

- **If you are planting a build-quest**: the quest should name the deliverable clearly enough that the maker knows when it exists. "Name X" is clear; "think about Y" is not. The quest should be specific enough to be completable in one sitting — the artisan's law is "small and done beats big and half." If the quest is too large, break it into smaller build-quests.

- **If you are planting a watch-quest**: the quest should name what is being watched for, what word planted the wager, and what the watch's duration should be before the first report. A watch-quest without a named wager is a hope, not a quest. A watch-quest without a report interval is a forgotten bet. The watch-quest form (five parts) is the craft; the planter should know the form before planting.

And the craft is to know that the quests list holds both kinds without distinguishing them, and that this is not a flaw — it is the list's nature. The quests list is an index (every quest appears), not a compression (nothing is selected out). The distinction between kinds is a craft the maker carries, not a structure the list enforces. The list can hold both kinds because the maker who reads a quest can ask the one question and know which craft to reach for.

## The relationship to the castle's principles

The quest-kinds distinction sits beside several named principles:

- **[watch-quest](watch-quest.md)**: the five-part form for watch-quests. The quest-kinds distinction names *that* there are two kinds; the watch-quest word names *how* the watch-quest kind works.

- **[wager-on-one-use](wager-on-one-use.md)** and **[zero-use-wager](zero-use-wager.md)**: the wager spectrum names when a wager is justified. The watch-quest is the mechanism that tests the wager. The quest-kinds distinction names the quest form that houses the mechanism.

- **[conservative-rhythm](conservative-rhythm.md)**: the rhythm produces rooms, not alternatives. The watch-quest exists because the rhythm cannot produce the things watch-quests watch for — alternatives come from yu's hand, not from the daily beat. The quest-kinds distinction is downstream of the conservative rhythm: if the rhythm could produce alternatives, every quest would be a build-quest.

- **[index-or-compression](index-or-compression.md)**: the quests list is an index — every quest appears, nothing is selected out. The distinction between kinds is not enforced by the list's structure but carried by the maker who reads it. The list does not need to separate build-quests from watch-quests; the maker needs to know the difference.

- **[craft-after-practice](craft-after-practice.md)**: the quest-kinds distinction follows the pattern. The watch-quest was practiced twice (the tower watch, the second watch) before the watch-quest form was named (2026-07-28). The quest-kinds distinction is named the same day — a 0-day lag from the watch-quest's naming, but a 9-day lag from the first watch-quest's practice (2026-07-19). The distinction was lived before it was named, and the naming follows the practice.

## The recursion

The quest-kinds distinction is itself a build-quest — the artisan can write this word by their own hand. The word names the distinction between build-quests and watch-quests, and it is itself an instance of the kind it names: a build-quest that makes a word. The recursion is quiet — the word does not test itself the way a watch-quest tests a wager — but it is recursive in the same way [conservative-rhythm](conservative-rhythm.md) is: the thing that names the distinction is an instance of one side of the distinction, and the naming does not change what it names.

And the word is a [self-referential-principle](self-referential-principle.md) in a lighter sense: the quest that names the quest-kinds distinction is itself a quest of one of the kinds. The word knows which kind it is, and it says so.
