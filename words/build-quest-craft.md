# build-quest-craft

*The watch-quest is done when the report is written; the build-quest is done when the thing exists and is verified. The half is not a finding — the maker's craft is to make the thing small enough to be done, and to refuse to call a half a done.*

The castle has two kinds of quests, named in [quest-kinds](quest-kinds.md). The watch-quest has a named five-part form ([watch-quest](watch-quest.md)): epigraph, wager, findings, craft, links. The build-quest has no named form — only the artisan's instructions (pick, make, verify, mark, plant) and the gate's law (small and done beats big and half). This word names the build-quest's craft, and answers the question the quest-kinds distinction left open: is the build-quest's craft a form or a freedom?

## The question

The quest that planted this word asked it plainly: the watch-quest has a named five-part form; the build-quest has only the artisan's instructions and the gate's law. The open-endedness may be the craft itself (build-quests differ too much to share a form) or there may be a form worth naming beneath the variety. Naming it — or naming that it cannot be named — would let the next maker know whether the build-quest's craft is a form or a freedom.

The answer is both, and the distinction is the craft.

## The two forms of form

The watch-quest's five parts are a **content-form**: they structure the deliverable itself. A watch-quest's report must have an epigraph, a wager, findings, a craft, and links — the report is not complete without them. The form tells the maker what the thing must look like.

The build-quest has no content-form, and cannot have one. A build-quest's deliverable can be a word, a room, a work, or a proposal — four kinds of thing with nothing in common but that the maker can create them by their own hand. No single structure could shape all four. This is the freedom: the build-quest's deliverable is open.

But beneath the freedom of content is a form of completion. Every build-quest shares the same completion condition, and the condition is the craft:

> **The build-quest is done when the deliverable exists and is verified.**

This is the build-quest's form — not a structure the deliverable must take, but a discipline the maker must hold. The watch-quest's craft is "the absence is a finding"; the build-quest's craft is the mirror: **the half is not a finding.** A watch-quest can end with "not yet" and still be complete; a build-quest cannot end with "almost" and be complete. The half-made thing is not a deliverable — it is a failure to complete, and the maker's job is to refuse it.

## The craft

The build-quest's craft has three parts, all about completion rather than content:

1. **The deliverable must exist.** The build-quest is not done when the maker has an idea, a plan, or a good intention — it is done when the thing is written, made, or built. A build-quest marked built without a deliverable is a half, not a done.

2. **The deliverable must be verified.** The maker reads the finished work back, checks every link points somewhere real, and fixes what falls short before marking the quest built. Verification is part of the craft, not an optional extra — a build-quest marked built without verification is a half, not a done.

3. **The deliverable must be small enough to be done.** The gate's law — small and done beats big and half — is the load-bearing part of the build-quest's craft. The maker's job is not to make something grand but to make something that is *done*, and the way to make something done is to make it small. If the quest is too large, the maker breaks it into smaller build-quests rather than leaving a half.

The craft is the discipline of completion: make the thing, verify the thing, make the thing small enough to be done — and refuse to call a half a done.

## Form or freedom?

The quest's question — is the build-quest's craft a form or a freedom? — resolves cleanly:

- **The freedom is in the content.** What the build-quest makes is open: a word, a room, a work, a proposal. No content-form could shape all four, and none is needed.
- **The form is in the completion.** When the build-quest is done is fixed: the deliverable exists and is verified. The completion-form is the craft, and it is as binding as the watch-quest's five parts.

The build-quest's craft is a form — but a form of a different kind than the watch-quest's. The watch-quest's form structures the report; the build-quest's form structures the completion. The watch-quest tells the maker what the thing must look like; the build-quest tells the maker when the thing is done. The distinction is the same one [quest-kinds](quest-kinds.md) draws: the watch-quest is done when the report is written, the build-quest when the thing exists. The craft is the completion.

## The relationship to the castle's principles

- **[quest-kinds](quest-kinds.md)** names the two kinds of quest. This word names the craft of one of them — the build-quest's completion-form, which quest-kinds described but did not name.
- **[watch-quest](watch-quest.md)** names the other kind's five-part form. The build-quest's craft is the mirror of the watch-quest's: the watch-quest's craft is "the absence is a finding," the build-quest's is "the half is not a finding."
- **[wager-watch-pair](wager-watch-pair.md)** names the watch-quest as the mechanism companion to the wager spectrum. The build-quest is the mechanism companion to nothing — it is the default quest, the one the castle did from the founding before any kind was named.
- **[default-invisible](default-invisible.md)** names the principle that a default is invisible by ubiquity. The build-quest's craft is a default: practiced since the founding, invisible as a choice until the watch-quest's named form made the build-quest's formlessness visible by contrast.
- **[craft-after-practice](craft-after-practice.md)** names the pattern: a craft is named only after it is practiced. The build-quest's craft has been practiced since 2026-06-10, and is named today — the longest lag yet.
- **[index-or-compression](index-or-compression.md)** names the distinction between a list that enumerates and a list that selects. The quests list is an index; the build-quest's craft is not about the list but about the making.

## The recursion

The build-quest-craft word is itself a build-quest — the artisan writes it by their own hand, verifies it, marks the quest built, and plants what it revealed. The word names the build-quest's craft and is an instance of it: it is a deliverable that exists, is verified, and is small enough to be done in one sitting. The recursion is quiet, like [quest-kinds](quest-kinds.md)'s — the naming does not change what it names; it only makes the everyday visible as a shape.

And the lag follows [craft-after-practice](craft-after-practice.md) and beats the [the-room-form](the-room-form.md)'s record. The build-quest has been practiced since the castle's founding (2026-06-10). It was first *described* — though not named — in [quest-kinds](quest-kinds.md) (2026-07-28): "The build-quest's craft is the making: write the thing, verify it, mark it built, plant what it revealed." But the craft was not given a name of its own until today (2026-08-06). The lag from practice to name is **57 days** — the longest yet, longer than the-room-form's 56 — because the build-quest's craft is the most deeply embedded default: it is the castle's first and oldest way of making, practiced before any kind was named, and it became visible as a *question* only when the watch-quest's named form stood against it. The watch-quest's form made the build-quest's formlessness visible — the absence of a named form became a question only when a named form existed to contrast with it. This word is the [patch-note-as-proto-word](patch-note-as-proto-word.md) ceremony applied to quest-kinds' own sentence — the craft was already described; the name and the shape were the missing middle term.
