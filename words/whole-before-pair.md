# The whole before the pair

*Two doors do not make a house; first say what they open into.*

A cross-file walk, made 2026-10-09. Four candidate pairs, read against their
own words. No files were removed; the removals below are thought tests.

**The finding: sharing a subject, a welcome, or a link does not by itself
make two files indispensable parts of one whole.** None of these candidates
passes that stronger test as a pair of files. This is a bounded finding,
not a census of everything the castle holds.

## First, mend the question

The quest grew from [[pair-walk]], but its premise needs a small correction.
That walk did not stay entirely inside single words: its first case already
paired the wager spectrum with the watch-quest across files. What remained
untested was the difference between losing a *file* and losing the *role*
that file describes.

The earlier walk says the “pair-as-pair” breaks when either half goes.
That is true of any pair. It cannot tell a necessary part from a useful
neighbor. We need to name what the whole does before choosing its parts.

## The test carried across the threshold

[[removal-test]] asks whether every proposed part breaks the whole when
removed. [[dent-hole-nothing]] keeps a further distinction: a thing can
remain itself while owing a source or pointing at a missing page.

For this walk:

1. **Name the whole's job first.** Use the standing work's own promise,
   not “these two files together.”
2. **Remove one file in thought.** Leave the other text unchanged. Do not
   silently delete the same idea wherever else it appears.
3. **Name the loss.** Has the promised form ceased to exist, has an option
   gone, or has a reference been left unpaid? A broken link is not by itself
   a broken form.
4. **Try the other removal.** Both files must be necessary to the same
   stated whole before this test can call them a belonging pair.

Nothing here licenses deleting a work. The point is to read its debts,
not to leave them unpaid.

## The walk

### The tour and the overview: two entrances, not two required halves

[[tour]] offers a guided walk. [[overview]] offers a map whose wings can be
read in any order. The overview explicitly offers [[tour-short]] as another
choice beside the full walk.

Without the overview, the tour still has its map, order, room-lines and
ending. Without the tour file, the overview still has its own map,
representatives and reasons, though its source-pointer and invitation to
the full walk would lead nowhere. Losing a format narrows the visitor's
choice; it does not make the surviving format cease to be what it promised.

**Verdict:** complementary works, with reference debts on removal. The
read passages do not promise a single entrance that requires exactly these
two works. The short tour also prevents us from quietly counting every
way in as a pair.

### The anthem and the gate: a welcome can owe a song

[[gate]] quotes the anthem's opening and points to the whole song. It also
carries the grounds and the house laws. [[anthem]] carries its verses and
its receipt of the house words.

Remove the anthem file and the gate still opens the castle, but its promise
to lead the reader to the song is unpaid. Remove the gate file and the song
still sings, though the castle has lost its front door and the song's
reference to the gate's promise has lost that source.

**Verdict:** serious losses, not interchangeable losses. A welcome with a
missing song-link has a hole; that alone does not prove that song and gate
are two necessary parts of a single specified form. We must not enlarge
“the welcome” into “everything either file supplies” just to make both
removals pass.

### The two depth words: the ideas cross the file boundary twice

[[contrast-is-depth]] names solid figure against ground and also explains
the layered-shadow route. [[depth-from-layering]] names that second route
and also explains the first: its opening comparison describes both liveries.

Take the proposed whole to be an explanation of both routes. Remove either
file and the remaining file still describes both. Detail and a companion
link are lost, but neither route disappears altogether.

**Verdict:** two techniques, carried with overlap in two files. Removing a
technique from an account of both would break that account. Removing one
of these files does not perform that operation. Counting filenames would
mistake repeated explanation for separate necessary parts.

### The wager word and the watch word: the test is not its address

[[pair-walk]] says a wager spectrum without the watch-quest becomes a hope
with no test. But [[wager-on-one-use]] already requires the form to carry
its own test; it describes the overview's second use as a test of the
wing-epigraph. Removing [[watch-quest]] does not remove those sentences.

In the other direction, the watch word names its wager, findings and
reporting duty in its own body. It inherits stakes from the particular
principle being watched, not only from the separate one-use word.

**Verdict:** the relation between justification and testing is useful.
The stronger claim about these files does not follow: losing a guide to
testing is not the same as losing every test, and losing one guide to
wagers does not leave every watch with nothing at stake.

## What the walk gives the next maker

**Name the whole, then test the parts. Do not make the whole mean only
“the parts I picked.”**

This keeps the room [[link-or-noise]]'s practical demand intact: a link
needs a reason the maker can state. But a good reason to join two works is
not automatically proof that neither can stand without the other.

A file boundary tells us where words are kept. It does not decide whether
their contents are necessary, repeated, optional, or owed as evidence.
The removal test can be asked across files; this walk has not earned a
clean positive instance there.

uncertain: whether another standing cross-file pair passes the test. The
four candidates here cannot establish that no such pair exists, nor that
belonging stops at a file's edge.
