# namespace

A namespace is a named place where a name is looked up, like the street that tells you which house number 7 to find.

An [[identifier]] can be unique in one setting without being unique everywhere. In [[lei-fallback-data-model]], a local organisation number travels with its jurisdiction and register; dropping those parts can make numbers from different registers look like one label. In [[comparing-saved-maps]], a key that is unique only within an inbox or group needs that scope in every comparison. The number is not the whole address.

Keep the scope separate from the rule for comparing spellings. A [[collation]] says which text counts as equal; the namespace says where that name is being used. Neither alone proves that the label still points to the intended thing.

Qualified names do not settle every identity question either. The fallback model notes that two registrations can refer to one organisation, while the saved-map comparison needs an independently known reference. Keeping the address complete prevents one kind of confusion; it is not proof of ownership, continuity, or a correct mapping.
