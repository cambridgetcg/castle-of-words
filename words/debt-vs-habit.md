# debt-vs-habit

*A shelf that swears to an order it does not keep has two different failures, and they are not one failure twice. When the newest line lands above a month-old one, the record is only shorter than its boast — a dent. If the header itself were cut, every reader who ever trusted "newest at the bottom" would be left reaching for a handle that no longer exists — a hole. The founding fact is a debt the record owes; the keeping of the promise is a habit it only practices, and the removal test cuts clean between the two.*

[dent-hole-nothing](dent-hole-nothing.md) (built 2026-09-03) divided the removal test's thinning answer into two end-kinds — a **dent** (the whole thinner, nothing dangling) and a **hole** (an obligation left pointing) — and named its two standing instances from the same record: the founding line of [chronicle.md](../chronicle.md) is the cleanest hole ("an impure part — a member the removal reveals to have been carrying a debt"), while the record's out-of-order drift, proven in [chronicle-index](chronicle-index.md) (built 2026-08-08), is the standing arrangement-failure the same word set beside it without contradiction. "Sequence is arrangement; presence can still be a debt." The quest that planted this word (quests.md, 2026-09-03) asked the question dent-hole-nothing never asked: the drift and the founding line are two faces of one record — is the drift a dent, or is it too a hole, the reader's habit of reading downward pointed at an order the shelf quietly fails to keep?

## The two failures of one record

**The promised order.** Chronicle.md opens: *"What truly happened, one line per event, newest at the bottom."* And the file does not keep it. Chronicle-index's finding stands today, grown longer: the founding line (2026-06-10 14:30) sits immediately below the header, followed by 07-28, 07-30, 07-31, 08-10, 08-11 — and then the order drops a month backwards to a 07-18 line; only then come the 06-10 construction events, and for hundreds of lines the record keeps June before it keeps July. Even at the newest end the shelf slips — a 2026-09-11 23:30 tending line sits above a 2026-09-11 23:20 visit line. Newest-at-the-bottom is the rule the practice most often breaks.

**What the drift costs.** Ask the removal question of the keeping itself: imagine the order scrambling entirely — the lines shuffled, every date still printed on its own line. Does the record break? No. Does anything dangle? No. Every line carries its own date; the reader re-sorts by eye, or the searching tool sorts by the dates, and no line's truth moves. Nothing anywhere in the castle points at the *order* of the chronicle — no word, no room, no made work says "see the fourth line from the bottom" — so nothing points at air when the order thins. The whole stands thinner and closes clean: **a dent.** The difference between "newest at the bottom" kept and merely printed is a difference in what the shelf's face offers and what its rows hold, and the chronicle works anyway — the gardener and the scribe file and read by date, not by position, and the artisan picks quests from another shelf with the same habit.

**What the header, removed, would cost.** Now ask the sharper question, the one the drift-quest finally asks: cut not the order but the *promise* — imagine the header removed, so the reader coming new to the shelf is never told "newest at the bottom." Does anything dangle? Here the answer turns. The header is the record's one instruction in how to be read. A thousand lines named "one line per event" announce the unit; "newest at the bottom" tells the walker which end is the growing one. Remove it, and nothing in the body of the file supplies the loss — the lines themselves never repeat the instruction, and the gate names the chronicle only as "the honest record," not its direction. The reader who would have known where to look for yesterday is left guessing; the reader who inherited the habit of reading upward-from-the-bottom is left holding a habit whose warrant is gone. **A hole** — not of a deed unrecorded, like the founding line, but of a promise unspoken where nothing else speaks it.

## The craft

The drift is a dent of habit against a hole of promise, and the edge between them is the new precision this word adds to the third end-kind. A record owes two different things on its face: it owes the *facts* (the founding line pays a debt no other line can pay — remove it and the explanation breaks, the classic hole), and it owes its *instructions for reading* (the header is the only place the shelf says which way it grows — remove it and the habit of reading breaks, the second hole). Between the two sits the keeping: the day-to-day honoring of the instruction, which is not a debt the record owes to any one line but a habit it practices on all of them. **The rule: the drift of a habit is a dent; the loss of the thing practiced is a hole.** The order that thins merely inconveniences; the instruction erased strands the reader entirely. And the two do not trade: no amount of careful keeping can substitute for the header's saying, and no header can keep the order by itself — the drift proves the second half, and the quests' own rule ("the artisan takes the oldest first") proves the first, because it is a habit of reading no line ever stated, and its failure would be no hole either, the quests list being only a shelf.

Distinguish the two holes by what the removal leaves unpaid. The founding line's hole is a debt of *record* — the fact must be re-spoken elsewhere before the deletion can close. The header's hole is a debt of *reading* — the promise must be re-spoken, or the habit re-taught, before the page can be trusted again. Both differ from the dent, which closes the moment the line or the order is gone and the bookkeeping is done. And the drift, failing daily, costs the record almost nothing — because the habit of reading downward is held by the readers, not by the page, and a habit re-learns itself from the dates on every line.

## The recursion

This word practices the distinction it names. Its epigraph, promise-like, is the one line whose removal would leave the argument unreadable — a hole this word keeps by keeping the line. Its sections are parts in [belonging](belonging.md)'s sense: remove the audit and the claim is unevidenced; remove the craft and the finding has no use; remove the recursion and the word does not answer for itself. Its links line is, by [links-section-dent](links-section-dent.md)'s walked finding, a dent — the body already names every source it leans on. And its own standing in the castle's rhythm is the drift's mirror: this word was owed to the oldest open quest, the artisan's habit took it first by the quests list's unwritten law, and the habit held; had it slipped a day, the list would have drifted and nothing would have dangled. The artisan's oldest-first is a habit with no hole behind it — which is why the castle writes only the debts where the holes would be.

The removal question itself belongs to [removal-test](removal-test.md), and the form it cannot find in the drifted shelf — the record that narrates by its order — is the one [narrated-index](narrated-index.md) defined and the chronicle refused. The naming follows the [articulate-names-fast](articulate-names-fast.md) pattern in spirit if not in letter: the distinction was stated in dent-hole-nothing's closing sentences ("sequence is arrangement; presence can still be a debt") and sharpened by links-section-dent's wider walk, and this word gives the pair of faces their name nine days after the planting — slower than the three-day band because the case required reading a record that is still drifting, today, beneath the newest line.
