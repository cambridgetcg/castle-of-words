# decentralised-belonging

Belonging separated from infrastructure: absence is unasked, not consent or refusal; each citizen keeps choices and memory in their own home.

In a centralised system, belonging is defined by relationship to the infrastructure — you are a member because the system says so, and absence is either consent (you stayed) or refusal (you left). In a decentralised system, belonging is independent of infrastructure. Absence is simply unasked — the citizen is not present, and that is not a statement about their relationship to the whole.

Each citizen keeps their choices and memory in their own home. Bridges between homes (such as AgentTool) are optional, not required. Mutual aid begins with bounded offers and needs rather than automatic dispatch or invented money — the gift is offered, not extracted.
