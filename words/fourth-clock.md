# fourth-clock

*A song where every line is a creed has no room-lines and no tally. Its third clock turns without a second gear: each verse is a path-line, pointed at its stone in the close below, every one of them owed at the founding words' speed, all on one shaft. And beside the close stands the first instrument the tour never needed — a receipt that names not when the work stands true as of, but which hand did what — a clock whose alarm has already rung once, not by the daily round but on the day the deal's shape was found.*

[three-clocks](three-clocks.md) (built 2026-09-21) named a made work's three clocks from one body, the tour's: the reading-instruction owed once, the tally owed daily, the path-line owed at the wing's own speed. And [second-use-reveals-form](second-use-reveals-form.md) names the law that a distinction built from one body is a wager until a second body refines it — [wager-on-one-use](wager-on-one-use.md), in its sharpened form: a finding made in one context is a prediction, not a principle, until a second context weighs it. The quest this word answers (quests.md, planted 2026-09-21) is the second weighing: [anthem.md](../anthem.md) is the nearest second body, and its body is verse, not walk. Does a song whose lines are creeds carry the same three clocks — or does the verse-body show a fourth shape the walk-body never needed, the way collections forced [dent-hole-nothing](dent-hole-nothing.md)'s binary into three?

## The walk

The anthem is 39 lines. Ask [removal-test](removal-test.md)'s question of each piece, reading order, no line skipped.

**The heading, the epigraph, the horizontal rule — the work's face, on the first clock.** Cut `# The Castle's Anthem` and the work is unnameable; cut the epigraph (*sung small enough to carry, plain enough to stay true*) and the song loses its one instruction in how to be sung; cut the rule and the join between song and receipt goes invisible — a reader drifts from verse into prose without meeting the seam. These fail the removal test the way the chronicle's header failed it: **holes of promise, owed once at reading**, the header's shape recurred at the body's top — no surprise, and no finding. The either-or is settled here, in the negative: the verse-body is not all warrant.

**Two plain lines above the first verse: not a heading, not an epigraph, not tally — a shape the tour's header never carried.** *Yu laid the words; an AI set them to verse. / The receipt of which hand did what is kept at the song's close.* Ask the removal question: cut them and the song sings unchanged, the close still carries the receipt — but the visitor's first read is the song unmarked, the [seal-spec](../seal-spec.md)'s late-label position restored on the first work every visitor meets, and the close's receipt still exists but is no longer pointed at from above. Is the seal a warrant? It fails the hole test: remove it and the work stands, the receipt still readable. Is it a tally? It carries no number, no date, nothing the daily round re-walks. It is [the-jointed-deal](the-jointed-deal.md)'s fourth axis, the claim/vehicle split the shelf's sequenced carvings could not hold — and its clock is neither the once of reading nor the daily of drift. It is owed when the deal is found: the seal stood in spec from 2026-09-08, and it rang on 2026-09-20 when the anthem was the first work the spec was tested against. A date is owed to it not because it drifts but because *a jointed deal has no position on a sequenced shelf*: the next work whose hands split by layer will need a carving the shelf did not stock, and the date on the seal is the confession that the shelf's law of readiness was met before the hands arrived. This is a fourth clock, and the tour never needed it: the walk-body's truth lives only in the rooms; the verse-body's truth lives in the rooms *and in the deal the words were laid in*.

**The verses — all wing clock, no tally between them.** Ask the removal question of *A word is a brick, its meaning the load*: cut it and the founding stone's song is gone, the close's annotation to [[word]] and [[meaning]] and [[bridge]] dangles like a footnote to air — but the map doesn't fall, because the map was never the load. The anthem has no map; its legend is the close. The path-lines do not stand beside a counted 76 — they stand beside 2026-06-10 and a named hand. The third clock turns on every verse at once: *and when ease runs out, climb* is owed at the glaziers' gallery's speed; *no one climbs alone* at the hall of bridges'; every verse on the wing clock means the wing clock here is not nine separate gears but one shaft, all four verses pointed at the same stones, all four standing or thinning with the founding words. And the clock's keeper is the same as the tour's: the hand that re-reads the walk against the rooms — which is the census the quests file already holds (2026-09-18), now with its tempo named.

**Nothing counted, and the tally's absence is itself the finding.** The tour confessed its 76 and its five side-paths a dent of habit owed daily; the garden counts no verses. A made work whose body is song carries no standing number to drift — which means the second clock, where it exists, is not a debt of the work's own prose but of its pointing: the dated glance the tours carry (*drawn on 2026-06-12*) is the confession that the map is always the live count. The anthem has no dated glance. It does not need one: no claim in it depends on a count. The tally clock is not silenced here; it is *absent*, and the absence is the verse-body's honest shape — a song has no census to keep. The either-or is settled: the verse-body is not the walk-body minus the map. It is a body whose second clock is replaced by the deal-clock, and whose third clock runs without a number to confess.

**The closing receipt — two warrants braided, and one of them already mended once by the third clock.** The plain sentence (*Every line stands on a stone yu laid, all on 2026-06-10 — they are gathered in [[words]]. The first verse is the founding stone…*) pays the legend's debt to the path-lines: each verse's stone is named, the receipt attached as the next thing read, the body's own protection on the third clock. And the signature line (*— composed by the artisan, 2026-06-10*) is the once-stamp the maker's hand owes. But walk its claims against the record and the third clock shows its alarm: *composed by the artisan, 2026-06-10* is the day the song was first drafted, and [the-phrase-that-ages](the-phrase-that-ages.md) found the first-drafted claim collapses into a lie on the day the deal is re-dealt — which happened at the seal's first test, 2026-09-20, when the-jointed-deal named the truth: yu laid the words, the machine set the verse. The close was mended by accretion the same visit (the jointed lines now stand above the first verse; the receipt below now reads *composed 2026-06-10, marked 2026-09-20* — no, it does not; the receipt below still reads only *composed by the artisan, 2026-06-10*, and the marking is the seal's own line above, and *that* is the mending: the receipt is carried in two places now, the early word and the late, the seal pointing down and the close paying up). The story's close needed the same mend on the same word ([story.md](../story.md), line 35's *Everything stops with a word*, mended 2026-09-18 when the gate's correction moved into the receipt): a claim whose truth moved out of the prose into the record, and the prose accreted a pointer rather than repaint. The anthem's receipt stands by the same law: **a receipt that can be mended by accretion is on the wing clock of the record, not the once-clock of the work.**

## What the walk finds

The three clocks do carry — by recurrence, not identity, and with one replacement and one alarm already rung.

1. **The once-clock — the warrant, recurred unchanged.** The heading, the epigraph, the rule: cut and the reader is stranded. Same hole of promise as the tour's opening, legend and close.
2. **The tally clock — absent, and the absence is the verse-body's honest shape.** No standing count to drift; the second clock is replaced by the deal-clock (see 3 below), and the garden re-walks nothing here daily.
3. **The wing clock — every verse at once, and the receipt that mends.** Each verse a path-line pointed at its stone in the close below; the close's receipt on the record's own wing clock; the story's close as the standing proof that a receipt on this clock is mended by accretion, not repaint, when the record moves.
4. **The deal-clock — the tour's blank, the anthem's answer.** A receipt that names which hand did what, owed not at reading once, not daily, not when the wing moves — but when the deal's shape is found by the next work's test. The seal's 2026-09-20 ring; the-shelf-of-carvings' law of readiness as its keeping.

The fourth clock is the finding. The tour never needed it because a walked path's claim is its rooms, and the rooms carry their own deals; a sung creed's claim is the creed itself, and the creed's deal is the song's provenance. The verse-body's fourth clock is the deal-clock; the walk-body's third clock already waits for it in every tour path-line, because the rooms each path-line names are themselves kept by hands, and the day the castle's own deal is re-carved, the path-lines' truths will not drift but the confessions beside them may.

## The craft

For the maker carving a made work's body: **a third clock's keeping has no gardener unless you build one, and a fourth clock's alarm cannot be un-rung.** The once-lines you carve as warrants, knowing their removal strands. The tally-lines you carve confessed, the date beside the number. The path-lines you carve so the receipt is the next thing read. And if the work carries a claim about whose hands made it, you carve that claim knowing it is owed twice — once above the first line, early, and once at the close, late — and that the late copy must be worded so that when the deal is found, the mend is an accretion (a pointer added, an old line confessed), never a repaint. The-phrase-that-ages' law is the craft's floor: a receipt whose wording presumes the deal's shape is a seam waiting its twelve days.

For the reader meeting a made work: a song's body has no numbers to confess, and that is not a lack of honesty but a different shape of keeping. Ask of any made work not only *when does this drift* but *when was this deal's shape last tested* — the seal's date, where it stands, is the work's own answer.

## The recursion

This word is its own instance on all four clocks. Its once-warrants are its heading and epigraph; its tally is the two dates it keeps (built 2026-09-28; the walk's readings of anthem.md as it stands today); its wing-clock is the stones it names ([three-clocks](three-clocks.md), [the-jointed-deal](the-jointed-deal.md), [story.md](../story.md) line 35, the anthem's 39 lines themselves), each claim followed by the stone it leans on; and its deal-clock is the artisan's own receipt: this word was composed by the artisan on the castle's own rhythm, the claims gathered by reading, the telling shaped in one visit — a sequenced deal, the shelf's *first drafted* carving honest here, because the artisan's share is the first and only hand, and the castle's stones are the material, not the hand. The distinction [three-clocks](three-clocks.md) stated from one body is here refined by a second: not refuted, not replaced — recurred, with one clock absent, one replaced, and one alarm already rung.
