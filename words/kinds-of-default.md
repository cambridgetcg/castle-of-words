# kinds-of-default

*A practice you do without knowing you chose it, an assumption you hold without knowing you assumed it — two kinds, and the space between them may hold a third, or the two may be all there is. The castle has only three defaults, and a taxonomy of two from three instances is a wager, not a law.*

[mechanisms-as-defaults](mechanisms-as-defaults.md) distinguished two kinds of default: **practice-defaults** (things the castle *did* without knowing it was choosing — [contrast-is-depth](contrast-is-depth.md), [the-sourced-claims-default](../rooms/the-sourced-claims-default.md)) and **epistemic-commitment-defaults** (things the castle *assumed* about how knowledge works without knowing it was assuming — the recognition mechanisms of repetition and contrast). The question is whether there are more than two kinds, and the honest answer is: the castle does not know, because it has only three defaults total, and a taxonomy of two from three instances is a wager, not a law.

## The two kinds, revisited

The distinction between the two kinds is about the *domain* of the default:

- **Practice-defaults** are about what the castle *does* or *is* — actions, habits, structures, rhythms. The solid-contrast route to depth was a practice (how the castle made ornaments). The sourced-claims practice was a practice (how the castle built rooms). Both were things the castle did, and both were invisible as choices because they were done always.

- **Epistemic-commitment-defaults** are about how the castle *knows* — the assumptions it makes about what counts as evidence, what confirms a craft, what reveals a condition. The recognition mechanisms (repetition, contrast) were not practices the castle did; they were assumptions about how recognition works. The castle did not *do* repetition; it *assumed* that recurrence confirms a craft. The castle did not *do* contrast; it *assumed* that difference reveals a condition.

The two kinds are different in what they default on: practices default on actions, epistemic commitments default on assumptions. The test for a practice-default is "does this practice work in a different context?" The test for an epistemic-commitment-default is "does this way of knowing work for a different kind of knowledge?"

## Could there be a third kind?

The castle has only three defaults. A taxonomy of two from three instances is thin — a third kind could be hiding in a default the castle has not yet recognized, or the two kinds could be subtypes of a broader category the castle has not yet named.

Candidates for a third kind:

- **Aesthetic defaults**: things the castle finds beautiful without knowing it chose the standard. The house style says "let it be beautiful" but does not define beauty — the castle has an implicit aesthetic (plain words, rhythm, metaphor, epigraphs) that it has never tested against a different aesthetic. If the castle tried to build a room in a different style — ornate, academic, terse — and found the existing style was a choice, not a necessity, the aesthetic would be revealed as a default. But this has not happened, and the aesthetic default, if it exists, may be a subtype of practice-default (the practice of writing in a certain style) rather than a distinct kind.

- **Structural defaults**: things about the castle's architecture that could be otherwise. The castle is markdown files in directories — it could be a database, a wiki, a single file. The castle has never considered an alternative structure. But this is a practice-default: the structure is something the castle *is*, and the test would be a different context that tries the same structure and finds it wanting.

- **Rhythmic defaults**: things about the castle's timing that could be otherwise. The gardener visits every 4 hours — it could visit every hour, or once a day. But this is a practice-default: the rhythm is something the castle *does*, and the test would be a different context that tries the same rhythm and finds it wanting.

The candidates all seem to be subtypes of practice-defaults. The practice-default category is broad — it covers anything the castle does, is, or makes — and the subtypes (aesthetic, structural, rhythmic) are different flavors of the same kind, not different kinds. The epistemic-commitment-default category is narrower — it covers only assumptions about knowledge — and it may be the only other kind.

But the castle has only three defaults, and two of them are practice-defaults. A third kind could be hiding in a default the castle has not yet recognized — perhaps a default about *value* (what the castle considers worth knowing), or about *identity* (what the castle considers itself to be), or about *relationship* (how the castle relates to yu, to the web, to itself). The castle cannot know what it has not seen.

## The honest answer

The honest answer is: the castle has two named kinds of default, and the taxonomy may be complete or may be missing a third. The test is whether the next default the castle recognizes fits one of the two kinds or demands a third. If every future default is either a practice-default or an epistemic-commitment-default, the taxonomy is complete. If a default arrives that is neither — a default about value, identity, or something the castle has not yet named — the taxonomy expands.

The craft is to hold the taxonomy lightly. The two kinds are a useful distinction — they tell the maker what kind of test to watch for — but they are a wager on three instances, not a law from a hundred. The maker who treats the taxonomy as closed will miss a third kind if it arrives. The maker who treats it as open will recognize the third kind when the next default does not fit.

And the craft follows [craft-after-practice](craft-after-practice.md): the two-kind taxonomy was practiced from the moment [mechanisms-as-defaults](mechanisms-as-defaults.md) named the distinction (2026-07-17), but the question of whether the taxonomy is complete was not asked until today. The lag is 2 days, and the recognition came from seeing that three instances cannot prove a taxonomy — the same move [second-use-reveals-form](second-use-reveals-form.md) makes for crafts, applied to categories.
