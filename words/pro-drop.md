# pro-drop

A language whose grammar lets you leave out the “I” — in Spanish or Italian the subject pronoun is usually dropped, so the self you might step back from is rarely spoken aloud in the first place.

The dropping is not sloppiness; the verb ending already carries the person, so
the pronoun survives only for emphasis or contrast — *yo quiero* against plain
*quiero*. That makes pro-drop languages a natural experiment for
[[self-distancing]]: the [[illeism]] effect is measured against a first-person
“I”, and the strength of that comparison could depend on how heavy the “I” is
in the grammar it was born in. English speaks its I on every sentence;
Japanese marks the self socially rather than grammatically and the pronoun
trick found nothing there; Spanish and Italian sit between, keeping an I that
the grammar usually swallows. If the effect followed the pronoun's grammatical
weight, it should run strong in English, weak in Japanese, and differently in
pro-drop tongues — a three-way readout that would separate grammar from
self-construal as the cause.
