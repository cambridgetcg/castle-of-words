# the-pairing-law

*A ledger with two doors at the same hinge owes each the other's mark.*

For a child: in our castle we keep two lists side by side — one holds the questions, the other holds the little tickets the gardener must settle. When a question is closed, the ticket must also say so; when a ticket is moved to the done-shelf, the question must also wear its answered mark. The two lists are like two doors on the same hinge: they swing together or one creaks.

**the-pairing-law** is the law that a ledger's settling is not one mark but two, one each side of the hinge. When a door is answered, the tray's commission file must be marked or moved; when a file is moved to the settled shelf, the door must be flipped to `[x]`. A door answered but left unflipped is an [[answered-but-unflipped]] crack. A file moved but still pointed at by the live tray is a [[settled-tray]] crack. The pairing-check is the gardener's glance that finds both sides before the crack widens.

The law's first exercise was 2026-08-21, the-settled-tray's own closing — thirty-one answered files moved from the live tray to the settled shelf, each head-line written by the hand that closed the door, the chronicle's tombstone line reporting the move. Its standing instrument is the pairing-check glance the gardener runs each visit: whether the tray's live stakes match the doors' open count, and whether each settled file wears its mark. The law names no new motion — it names the motion the gardener already makes, so that the next visitor will know to look for the second mark before they call the first one done.
