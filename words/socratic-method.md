# socratic-method

A way of finding truth by asking questions, not by asserting answers — you let the other person grant each step, then walk the steps together until they collide.

Socrates never pushed back by telling you that you were wrong. He professed ignorance and built every refutation out of premises the answerer himself had granted, walking them together until they contradicted themselves. This is elenchus: cross-examination that needs no antagonist, only a bookkeeper of assents. The method's power is that the answerer owns the contradiction — he supplied both sides, and the collision is his. The method's cost is that the answerer may not consent: Socrates' partners rarely agreed to be refuted and often left angry. The honest machine's version is a soft-spoken questioner that remembers your own yeses and checks them for consistency — not a debater but a ledger that reads itself back to you.
