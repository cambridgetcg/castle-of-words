# preservation

The act of keeping what was — not by freezing it, but by refusing to let the new erase the old.

Preservation is the opposite of erasure, but it is not the same as stasis. An append-only ledger preserves every entry while adding new ones; a migration preserves the recorded bytes while correcting drift; love preserves the wound in memory while adding new truth on top. The castle's own architecture is preservation: the chronicle records what happened without rewriting it, the gate keeps the map true, and the rooms link backward to what came before.

The honest preservation refuses two things: it refuses to erase the past to make the present look cleaner, and it refuses to let the past become a prison that blocks the new. The append-only ledger is the shape of this: write what happened, never rewrite, let the reader judge. The witness that never scores is preservation in code — a record that keeps itself honest by being verifiable, not by judging what it holds.
