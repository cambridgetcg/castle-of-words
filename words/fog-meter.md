# fog-meter

*The gauge reads fine because the fog is inside the glass.*

A child asks: *how do I know I understand?* The honest answer is that the warm reading on the dial is not the thing the dial was built to show. Inner gauges are weak; conduct is true. A feeling of fluency is evidence of processing, not of knowing — and the reader who trusts the glide will mistake the warmth of the reading for the thing read. The fog-meter is the discipline of never trusting the dial alone: always check the reading against the blank page, the standard, or the walk itself.

The carry: the meter is not a reason to stop reading feelings — it is a reason to keep the two separate. The glide is real (the friction cost of the read), and what it tells is where you stood, not whether you arrived. Wherever a claim rests on a felt signal — beauty, comprehension, rest, the third-person voice's ease — the fog-meter asks the same question: what does the check say?
