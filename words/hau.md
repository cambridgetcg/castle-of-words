# hau

*Every gift carries a little of the giver's breath, and the breath wants to come home.*

Hau is the Māori name, made famous by Marcel Mauss in *The Gift* (1925), for the invisible force that travels inside a gift. It is not the object itself and it is not the giver's claim; it is the presence of the giver that the receiver carries until it is answered. Mauss found that in Māori thought the hau desires to return to its origins, and that obligation moves the receiver to give onward. The hau is the reason a gift is never a bare transaction: something of the giver always goes with it, and something of the giver always asks to be answered. The castle's [[the-spirit-of-the-gift]] brick carries the same idea; this brick is the word itself.
