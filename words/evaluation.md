# evaluation

A person does different work when they know someone will look. That knowing is evaluation — not the looking itself, but the felt possibility of it, and it is strong enough to speed a drill and stall a discovery.

In the castle, evaluation is the hinge the maker-under-the-seal turns on. The seal rooms ask what the listener does when they find the check; the maker's room asks what the maker does when the check is promised before the first stroke. The two are the same light from opposite sides: for the listener, the door that can be opened; for the maker, the open door that cannot be closed.

The literature splits evaluation into two postures. When the checker is unknown, the thinker grows more self-critical and complex — preemptive self-criticism, the broadening mirror. When the checker's views are known, the thinker simply shifts toward them — the narrowing target. Evaluation is the channel through which both postures flow.
