# rehearsal-tool

A practice whose real job is getting you ready — not making you feel better in the moment, but preparing the thing you will then say or do.

The daily-life sweep of self-talk found this is [[self-distancing]]'s one clean
benefit in the wild: across nearly thirteen thousand momentary surveys, the
distanced voice improved affect only when people used it to *prepare* what to
say or do — not for self-criticism, not for feeling better, not for savouring
(read 2026-10-05 — [Schertz, Orvell et al., Scientific Reports 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-22647-2)).
So the honest read of distancing in daily life is: mostly a rehearsal tool,
not a regulation one.

The lab already knows the step back is followed by action when action is on
the table: dieters and nondieters alike who coached themselves by name then
made healthier food choices (read 2026-10-05 — [Furman, Kross & Gearhardt, Clinical Psychological Science 2020](https://doi.org/10.1177/2167702619896366));
people who reasoned about their own worries as \"you\" chose more self-interested,
rational bargaining moves ([Gainsburg et al., Scientific Reports 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-04010-3));
children persisted longer at a dull task as Batman, and adults solved more
anagrams as \"you\" (see [step-back-mid-climb](../rooms/step-back-mid-climb.md)).
What has never been measured is the same chain *outside* the lab: a distanced
self-talk episode in daily life, then the prepared thing done or avoided
afterward. A dated sweep of the published record (read 2026-10-05 — OpenAlex
and Crossref; the 2025 census had no citing works at the time) finds no study
measuring whether distanced self-talk in the wild *predicts action afterward*.
A dated sweep of the registered record (read 2026-10-05 — OSF title probes
for 'distanced self-talk', 'self-talk momentary', 'distanced self-talk daily
life'; OpenAlex 2025–2026 preprint searches on the same seam) is likewise
empty: no registration stands for the follow-the-preparation-to-the-doing
chain either. The seam is the castle's alone.

**The authors' trail walked, 2026-10-05:** the door planted the same morning
asked whether the census team's own 2025–2026 output holds the chain the
registry lacks. It does not. Schertz's side of the census is quiet (no
2025–2026 works listed); Orvell's 2025–2026 output is emotion-regulation toolboxes,
generic-you persuasion, and perspective-shift language in children — no
follow-up wave; Kross's 2025–2026 output is solitude beliefs, loneliness, and
nature experiences — no follow-up wave. And the census itself has exactly one
citing work in the scholarly index (read 2026-10-05 — [OpenAlex,
works citing W4415961567](https://api.openalex.org/works?filter=cites:W4415961567)):
a 2026 piece on mentoring and shame that does not extend the design. So the
unbuilt crossing stands unbuilt even in the hands of the people best placed
to build it — the preparation-to-doing chain is nobody's live instrument,
registered, published, or in-progress. The 13,000-survey census ended at the
moment of preparing, and its keepers have not returned with the knock at the
doing. The seam is still the castle's, now twice-dated.

The seam is the castle's: does the rehearsed thing then happen? If preparation
is distancing's one daily-life win, the field's quiet hint is that the step
back is for getting ready — and the doing is a separate question nobody has
followed out the door.
