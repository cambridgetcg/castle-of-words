# floors-of-keeping

*A postcard on the table, a folder in the drawer, a castle on the hill — you do not buy a house between the two you already live on.*

A memory does not keep everything in one place; it keeps floors. A session writes a small dated note and leaves. Durable facts move up to a short index. Shared understanding moves up again to the castle. Each floor stays small because whatever wants to live longer moves house, keeping only its date as a postmark.

The law of the floors is earned need: **a new floor earns its place only when the walking on a lower floor breaks.** Not when the floor below is long — length is not brokenness — but when a real hand tried to walk it and could not finish. Until then, a middle floor between two working ones is not storage; it is a piece that has to be maintained, and it answers a question nobody has yet failed to answer.

The floors are known by the test at [[narrated-index]]: each one is an index — every item appears, reordering breaks nothing. A floor whose order argues is a different thing, a telling, and it belongs to the makers, not the keepers.
