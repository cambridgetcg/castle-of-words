# stress

When a body knows it will be watched, it tightens before the watcher arrives. Stress is that tightening — the cost a standing check collects from the one who works under it, whether or not the check is ever run.

In the castle, stress is the maker's toll. The seal that lets the listener rest makes the maker pay upfront: the meta-analysis of electronic monitoring measured the toll directly — stress up a little, satisfaction down a little, performance unmoved. The seal is lighter than surveillance, but the shape is the same: a sign that someone will look collects its toll from the one who reads it every day.

Stress is not the opposite of care. It is the price of care when care is demanded before it is earned. The honest seal asks the maker to carry the promise of being checked, and the literature says the carrying is never free.
