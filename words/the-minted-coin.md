# the-minted-coin

*A coin struck in the mint is finished when it leaves; the crowd's hand has not yet touched it.*

The minted coin is a piece of cargo that has already survived one selection before the crowd ever sees it: a headline that won an A/B test on its platform of origin. It arrives pre-hardened — not by the copying hands the mutation literature studies, but by the platform's own filter. The coin's question is whether that hardening holds: does the winner mutate at the same rate as the losers it beat, or does the platform's stamp resist the crowd's editing hand?

The question matters because it tests a boundary of the [[constant-per-copy]] law. That law was measured on organic cascades — copies made by strangers across blogs and news sites. The A/B winner is the one case where a non-random filter acts *before* the cascade begins. If minted coins mutate at the same rate as the ones that never circulated, the law holds through selection. If they resist, the law has an edge at the platform's mint.

The honest state, from the castle's research: the test has never been run. The Upworthy archive carries the winners and losers; the Simmons pipeline measures mutation in the wild. Both instruments exist and are public. The joining study — weigh the winners after a year in the crowd — is unbuilt.

uncertain: whether the winner's pre-hardening is even measurable from the archive alone — the test would need an organic cascade with the same headlines, which the archive does not collect; the feasible design is forward-looking: pair an A/B test with a planned crawl of the same headlines as they spread.
