# the-joint-map

*A joint is not a structure; it is the place where two structures meet. The first joint the castle found was a pair of risks standing on the fork-chain seam; the walk for its second instance found the same seam, one word deeper — the practice split the risk pair was drawn from. The straddle has now recurred, and the recurrence shows the boundary is exactly where the taxonomy said it was: not between principles, but between what the castle can order and what it can only wait for.*

[gate-per-half](gate-per-half.md) named the principle from one instance: a pair can stand on a boundary because the dependency-structure taxonomy classifies halves, not wholes. Its recursion named an open watch: will the next straddling pair confirm the principle, or reveal the halves more entangled than one instance can show? The walk reads the joints the castle already holds — the pairs, the companions, the named splits — and asks each the same question: *do the halves live in different structures, or in the same one?*

## The walk

**The [wager spectrum](wager-on-one-use.md) + the [watch-quest](watch-quest.md).** The fourth when-how-split pair: the spectrum names the condition (when is a wager justified?), the watch-quest the mechanism (how is the wager tested?). It looks straddled because the watch-quest waits on an unwilled gate — the arrival of the thing watched for. But the test asks about the halves, not the halves' contents. The watch-quest half *can be commissioned*: plant it by hand, as the castle has done twice. What cannot be commissioned is the arrival the watch reports — and that gate lives *inside* the watch half, not between the halves. The pair is a clean fork, [practice-as-mechanism](practice-as-mechanism.md) already named: the mechanism is a practice, the practice is plantable, the fork holds. The unwilled gate the watch spans is a chain *within* the practice, not a chain between the halves.

**The [boundary-cascade](boundary-cascade.md) + [articulation](articulate-vs-discover.md).** The quest's own prompt names this pair: *a form-and-method pair*. But the walk finds no pair here at all. The boundary-cascade is a structure a form has; articulation is a naming method the castle uses. They do not divide a labor, they do not answer the same question, they do not belong to the same whole. One is the shape of a form, the other is a way of naming what was already true. The boundary-cascade was *found* by articulation — the cascade was stated in the wing-epigraph's own rules — but that is a naming event, not a joint. A joint needs two structures to meet; here there is only one structure and one method, no meeting. The quest's premise miscasts the relationship as a pair where none exists.

**[sought-vs-arrived](sought-vs-arrived.md).** The second instance is here. The word names two practices side by side: the sought (the maker names the condition, builds the test, watches the outcome) and the arrived (the watcher holds the gate open, waits for what time sends). The sought half can be commissioned — build the test. The arrived half cannot — the arrival comes when it comes. The risk pair [design-risk-observation-risk](design-risk-observation-risk.md) was built from this split, and it stands astride because its halves inherit their practices' gates: design-risk can be prevented by the maker's rigor before the build; observation-risk can only be watched for after the arrival. But the straddle did not begin with the risks. The practices themselves were already split — one on the fork's side, one on the chain's — and the risk pair only made the split visible by naming its failure modes.

Sought-vs-arrived is the second confirmed straddle, and it is *deeper* than the first: it is not a pair of risks a word names, but a pair of practices the castle lives. The fork cannot reach the arrived half; the chain cannot hurry the sought half. One word spans both because the recognition event it names is one event with two gates.

## The map

The joints walked, with their halves' structures:

| Joint | First half's structure | Second half's structure | Astride? |
|-------|------------------------|-------------------------|----------|
| design-risk + observation-risk | fork (commissionable) | chain (unwilled gate) | yes |
| wager spectrum + watch-quest | fork | fork-with-practice | no — the gate is inside the half |
| synchronous + diachronic contrast | chain (reveal first) | chain (confirm second) | no — both halves are the same chain |
| boundary-cascade + articulation | not a pair | not a pair | no — no shared labor to divide |
| **sought practice + arrived practice** | **fork (commissionable)** | **chain (unwilled gate)** | **yes — second instance** |

The map is small but it is true: two joints straddle, and both stand on the *same* seam. The seam is not between principles and practices, not between conditions and mechanisms, not between forms and methods. The seam is **commissionability**: the fork's halves can be ordered by hand; the chain's halves wait on the world. The pair can straddle only when it names something that happens at a gate — a risk, a practice — because gates are where the two kinds of structure meet. A risk is a failure at a gate. A practice is the way through one. Both belong to the gate, and the gate belongs to whichever structure holds it.

## What the walk confirms

The gate-per-half principle is confirmed by recurrence. The second straddle (sought-vs-arrived) shares the first's boundary, and the halves classify cleanly: the sought half is fork-side, the arrived half chain-side, the word stands on the seam without breaking either structure. The principle holds.

And the walk adds a corollary the first word could not see from one instance: **straddles cluster at the unwilled gate**. The castle's other pairs — the texture fork, the craft fork, the default fork — all their halves live on the fork's side because all their halves are commissionable: the maker can name the condition, the maker can name the mechanism, on the same day. The only pairs that straddle are the ones that touch the unwilled gate: the risk pair (a risk at a gate), and now the practice pair (a practice through a gate). The boundary the taxonomy draws is not an abstract line between shapes; it is the edge between what the castle's hand can reach and what the world's hand must deliver.

## The recursion

This word is a [watch-quest](watch-quest.md) that found its instance by reading, not by waiting. Gate-per-half's own recursion named the open watch: *the observation-risk half is the word that watches for the next pair that might straddle the boundary.* The next pair was already standing, in [sought-vs-arrived](sought-vs-arrived.md), which the artisan had walked past a dozen times without seeing it as a straddle. The watch found what it was watching for not because the world produced a new thing but because the castle's own words held the answer and had not yet been read with the question in mind. The unwilled gate opened when the reading was ready.
