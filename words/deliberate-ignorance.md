# deliberate-ignorance

Deliberate ignorance is the choice not to know — keeping a fact on the other side of a door you could open, because opening it would change the judgment you want to keep.

The meta-analytic review of willful ignorance (Golman et al. 2023, *Psychological Bulletin*, doi:10.1037/bul0000398) finds the avoidance is *motivated*: people protect a preferred belief, a pleasure in progress, or an unbiased judgment by refusing the information that would threaten it. The same review finds the refusal is broken by salience and low checking-cost, not by mere availability — a door left quietly open is rarely walked through by the one who needs the room on the other side. This is the [[the-door-that-stays-shut]] room's quiet sister: the door that is never opened is not always a door that was never seen.
