# memorability

How easily a word or phrase lodges in memory — the trait that decides which
sentences survive being copied and which dissolve.

Some words are easy to hold (concrete, short, frequent); others slip
(abstract, rare). When text is passed from hand to hand, the slippery words
are shed first and easy ones grow in their place — Lerique & Roth measured
this drift in online quotations (2017, doi:10.1111/cogs.12494). For the
canary craft, memorability is a double-edge: a memorable fingerprint travels
far but mutates, so the maker who wants the fingerprint intact may choose
awkwardness on purpose ([[adoption-dilution]]).
