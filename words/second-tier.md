# second-tier

A second tier is a lighter look taken between the deep readings, confined to
only what changed since the last deep look.

The census reads every promised shape once per census — that is the deep tier,
bounded by being owed to all. But between the census's beats, builders extend
rooms and works drift from their lines. The second tier is the quick pass over
*just those*: the rooms whose last-touch is newer than the Map line that names
them, the made works that have drifted from the stones they lean on. It costs
one comparison of two timestamps per stone, and it is bounded to what the
timestamps point at — never a re-read of the whole castle between readings.

The two tiers are not two different duties arguing about thoroughness; they are
one duty at two speeds, and the speeds take turns. The census refreshes every
row on its rhythm; the second tier freshens, between rhythms, only the rows the
builders touched. Where the census found nothing changed, the second tier finds
nothing to do and folds back into the idle visit. Where a Map line has been
outgrown, the second tier catches it before the rhythm does — and the mark of
the catch stands in the ledger, never a silent rewrite.
