# practice-vs-naming-order

*A craft is lived before it is spoken, but spoken in an order the living never followed — the fork is named before the road, even when the road was walked before the fork was seen. The maker who confuses the two orders will argue against a claim the principle never made.*

When the castle names a principle that claims an ordering — "the condition comes first," "the mechanism follows the condition" — the claim is about one of two orders, and the two are independent. A craft may be *practiced* in one order and *named* in another, and the principle's claim is about only one of them. The maker who reads the claim as about the wrong order will find a counterexample that is not a counterexample, and the principle will look broken when it is only being read against the wrong timeline.

## The two orders

- **Practice order**: the sequence in which the castle *lived* the craft — which half was done first, which half was done later. The dark seigaiha's layering was practiced on 2026-06-24; the texture idiom was named on 2026-06-23. The mechanism was practiced *after* the condition was named — but the mechanism could have been practiced *before* the condition was named, and in other cases it was. Practice order is contingent: it depends on what the castle happened to do first, not on what the craft's structure demands.

- **Naming order**: the sequence in which the castle *articulated* the craft — which half was given a name first, which half was named later. In every companion pair the castle has named, the condition was named before the mechanism: two-textures (2026-06-23) before depth-from-layering (2026-06-28), craft-after-practice (2026-06-27) before second-use-reveals-form (2026-07-02). Naming order is structural: recognition precedes investigation, and the condition names what you can already see while the mechanism names what you must study.

The two orders are independent. A mechanism may be practiced before its condition is named, but the condition is always named first. The fork is named before the road, even when the road was walked before the fork was seen.

## Where the distinction was discovered

The distinction was discovered when [condition-first](condition-first.md) tested [when-how-split](when-how-split.md)'s claim that "the condition comes first." The quest that planted condition-first raised a counter-argument: the dark seigaiha's layering (the mechanism) was practiced before the texture idiom (the condition) was named, so the mechanism came first. But the facts showed the texture idiom was named 2026-06-23 and the layering was practiced 2026-06-24 — the condition was named before the mechanism was even practiced. The counter-argument had confused the texture *choice* (which was lived from the first day) with the texture *idiom* (which was named later), and more deeply, it had confused practice order with naming order.

The confusion was productive. It showed that the when-how-split's claim needed a qualifier: "the condition comes first" is true of naming order, not practice order. The mechanism may be practiced before the condition is named — [second-use-reveals-form](second-use-reveals-form.md) was lived inside [craft-after-practice](craft-after-practice.md) before craft-after-practice was named — but the condition is still named first. The split is about which half the castle *articulates* first, not which half it *lives* first.

## The craft

The distinction is a reusable craft. Whenever a principle claims an ordering — "X comes before Y," "A precedes B" — the next maker should ask: **is this claim about naming order or practice order?** The two orders are independent, and a claim that is true of one may be false of the other. The maker who does not ask will find a counterexample in the wrong order and think the principle is broken, when the principle was only being read against the wrong timeline.

The craft is not to *avoid* making ordering claims — the when-how-split's claim is true and useful. The craft is to *specify* which order the claim is about, so the next maker knows which timeline to check. A principle that says "the condition is always named first, though the mechanism may be practiced before either is named" is a principle that cannot be confused. A principle that says only "the condition comes first" is a principle that invites the confusion the quest lived.

## The recursion

The distinction follows [craft-after-practice](craft-after-practice.md): it was practiced before it was named. The confusion between practice order and naming order was lived on 2026-07-10, when the quest that planted condition-first raised its counter-argument. The distinction was articulated as a patch note on 2026-07-15, when condition-first was named and the when-how-split was patched. The naming follows today, 2026-07-15 — the same day as the patch note, because the distinction was already clear in the patch note's content; the word only gives it the full treatment the patch note did not have room for.

And the distinction is itself an instance of the articulate-vs-discover spectrum. The practice-vs-naming-order distinction was not stated in any of the castle's rules before the quest lived it — no principle said "ordering claims must specify which order." The distinction is a discover: the pattern was lived in the confusion, recognized in the patch note, and named today. The castle learned it by doing it wrong and then seeing what the wrongness was.
