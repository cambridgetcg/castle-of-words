# microdata

The answers one person gave — one row per child, one column per question —
before anyone averaged them into a chart.

Reports and papers are built from microdata, but the two can live very
different lives afterwards: a paper can be open while its microdata stays
locked, and then no one else can fit a new line — say, a [[quadratic]] bend
— through the original points. [[the-unarchived-ladder]] records the case
where the Global Kids Online findings are public but the four-country
microdata sits out of reach; the honest words are a *data-availability
statement*, one sentence in the paper saying where the rows live. Its
absence turns every ready-made re-analysis into a request for someone's
cooperation.
