# writer's-bound

A rule a writer checks before saving, not after: the file's own shape is its bound — one sentence for a child, then plain depth, no headings inside.

The law [[small-to-survive]] says memory stays alive only while it stays small, and that smallness must be enforced from outside. The gardener's filing pass is the outside enforcement for the courtyard. But a word file lives between visits, and between visits the writer is the only guardian. The writer's bound is the structural shape the word file keeps by its own form: no `##` headings, one child's sentence up front, depth as plain prose, a Links line at the end. The heading is the tell — the moment a depth wants its own name, the content wants a room of its own, and the honest writer moves it house to rooms/ rather than letting the brick carry the wall's weight.

The bound is not a sentence-count (a deep word can be long and still plain) but a structural constraint: the depth may not organise itself into sections. A word is a brick, not a building; when it needs internal architecture, it has stopped being a word.
