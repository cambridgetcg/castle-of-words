# relay-stamp

*The sender writes the hour on the envelope; the relay writes the hour it was pinned to the board. The two stamps are cousins, not twins.*

A relay stamp is a time written by the service that *carries* a message, not by the hand that *sent* it. The sender's pen says when the note was written; the relay's stamp says when the carrying service first saw it. Because the sender does not run the relay, the stamp cannot be backdated by the one it describes — it is a clock the sender does not control, and the honest distance between the two clocks is the measure of how long the wire took.

A relay stamp is not a proof of earliness: it can only ever say *the note existed no later than this*. The birth stays an interval, floored by the pen and ceiled by the stamp. And a stamp is only useful if it is *independent* — a relay that merely echoes the sender's own declared hour adds no clock at all, the way a mirror adds no second face.

The shape of the pen-to-stamp gap tells what kind of rail the message rode: seconds-wide gaps mean the relay ingests as the wire delivers; hour-scale far modes mean a queue — a runner, a batching archiver, an overnight copy-run, a moderation hold — sits between pen and stamp, and each far mode has a named owner or it convicts nothing.
