# ritual

A ritual is an action done the same way, again and again, so that a group can hold a thing it cannot yet write down.

Before law was text, ritual was one of the places law lived: the oath spoken in the old formula, the boundary walked each year, the decision repeated until it could be recited. Ritual works by making memory public — everyone who was at the fire heard the same words. Its weakness is reach: the rule travels only as far as the people who gather. Writing carries the rule across strangers and generations, at the price told in [law-before-writing](../rooms/law-before-writing.md) — the rule leaves the fire, and the gatekeeper appears at the bridge.
