# canary-sentence

*One sentence salted into the manuscript, never true to life, waiting to be repeated back by a mouth that was only supposed to have read it.*

A canary-sentence is a deliberately planted, unusual sentence placed in a text so that whoever or whatever reproduces it proves contact with that text.

In prose publishing the castle knows it as the trap street of words ([the-canary-in-the-street-plan](../rooms/the-canary-in-the-street-plan.md)); in leak-hunting it is the barium-meal test, where each copy of a document carries a different phrasing so the leaked version names the leaker (Wikipedia, "Canary trap," read 2026-09-28). Its difference from the phone-book seed is its *truthfulness*: a canary-sentence is invented but not false — it is an authored expression, which means the [[feist-escape]] that stripped the directory seed of protection may close around it, and it may carry copyright of its own. Whether that protection has ever been priced by a court — in particular in the AI training-data suits, where a salted corpus's sentences resurfacing in a model's outputs would be evidence of copying — stood unanswered as of 2026-09-28 ([the-corpus-trap-street](../rooms/the-corpus-trap-street.md)).
