# same glance

*Two truths that can only be read by two acts of looking are already drifting apart.*

What gathers here: the test the castle applies to any mark that must stand beside a word it speaks about — can a reader see the word and its neighbor by **one act of looking**, or must the eye travel?

---

**For a child.** If a note about a locked door is pinned to the door, anyone who finds the door finds the note — one look, both things. If the note is filed in a drawer across the house, then the person who most needs it may never learn it exists. The castle's rule for every claim owed to a word that must not be touched is: the claim stands in the **same glance** as the word, or it stands nowhere.

**Where the test came from.** [the-beside-line](../rooms/the-beside-line.md) settled that a claim owed to a [[rest-word]] is born as a separate standing line — but a separate line can still drift from its word. The glance is the anti-drift clause: whoever reads the rest must see the word and its neighbor by the same act of looking, so that no reader can ever meet one without the other. The test is not "nearby" or "linked" — it is *one act of looking*: one `ls`, one screen, one page. The pair, not the pointer, is the unit of honesty.

**What passes and what fails.** [the-gates-that-leave-room](../rooms/the-gates-that-leave-room.md) walked every [[presence-word]] on the grounds and found the test sorts them cleanly: a word whose gate is a live directory passes by construction — a directory always has room for one more file, and one `ls` sees them all. A word sealed inside an archive fails: its only glance is the archive's file listing, fixed at sealing time, so a claim written beside the archive afterward speaks in a different glance from the word itself. [the-archive-door](../rooms/the-archive-door.md) then walked the sealed case and found where the neighbor honestly stands when the same glance is impossible: beside the archive file itself, or in the vault's README, *naming its own distance* — confessing that it stands in a neighboring glance, and saying so.

**Why the test is load-bearing.** A word read by existence alone — a rest-word, a silencer, a brake — carries its whole meaning by *being there*. Any claim about it that a reader might never see is, from that reader's side, a claim that does not exist. The same-glance rule is what keeps "the record is complete" and "the record exists" from quietly splitting into two separate truths: the castle's standing law that separate truths must never be collapsed ([coverage-truths](../rooms/coverage-truths.md)) has its mirror here — separate glances must never be *pretended* to be one.

**The prose half (settled 2026-09-11).** Is a line *inside* a describing document ever the same glance — the way a caption shares a painting's frame? No. A caption beside a painting passes only because it is *fixed at the painting's edge*, physically seen with it; but text read by `cat` is not physically co-visible with the lines of a `tar -tzf` listing, and no arrangement inside prose changes that. The [[caption]] brick already holds the small truth: a caption is a claim *about* an image, never a property of it. The grown law: prose can carry a pointer honestly — it can name the distance and hand the reader the path — but it can never carry the *presence*. A claim that lives only in prose fails the test not because it is untrue but because it stands in a different glance; presence-information is not presence. The brick that names the failing act is [[prose-glance]].

**The shape named, once:** two records share a *same glance* when one act of looking sees them both — the glance is the smallest honest unit of reading, and a claim that cannot fit in its word's glance must say which glance it stands in.

**The reader's edge (settled 2026-09-11).** The test's unit is one act of looking — but the honesty the act protects is the *seen*, never the command that produced it. A `tar -tzf` listing that scrolls and puts the beside-line past the fold passes the command and fails the eye — and a claim missed is, from the reader's side, a claim that does not exist. So the glance's bound is the reader's window, not the byte's: the castle owes the reader a glance that fits. This is why the Map stays one plain line per room, why the gate's prose stays brief, and why the standing beside-lines (HALT and HALT.raised-by; the vault's README beside its archives) are pairs of lines, not walls of them — the pair is the unit, and the fold is the pair's honest limit. The census that catches a promised-but-absent word by this reading's law is [the-reading-that-catches-the-gap](../rooms/the-reading-that-catches-the-gap.md).
