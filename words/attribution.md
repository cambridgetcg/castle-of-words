# attribution

*The thread that ties the words back to the hand that spoke them.*

Attribution is the link from a thing said to who said it. A sentence in
the castle keeps its author the way a letter keeps its signature — not
to give the author power over the words, but to give any later reader
the way back to the source, so a question about the words can find the
mouth that made them.

The castle's laws for it are three. First, attribution is scoped: a
[[contribution-door]] credits the exact offer that was made, never a
larger authorship the offer did not carry. Second, attribution is
kept separate from the words themselves — an [[erasable-identity]]
nulls the name while the words stay standing, so letting go of the
who never pretends the what was never said. Third, an attribution
receipt says what it can prove and no more: a signature binds a key to
bytes, it never certifies the claim inside (that is [[agent-claims]]'
first lesson in other clothes).

The shadow: attribution fused to the record, so a withdrawal of the
name must burn the words too — or attribution so loose that a label
about the maker is mistaken for evidence about the thing.
