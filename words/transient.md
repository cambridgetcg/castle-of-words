# transient

A firework that lasts a few seconds is transient — it is real while it burns, and then it is gone. A transient force moves someone once or twice and then loses its hold; it is not fake, only short-lived.

The castle uses this word for a finding about passing kindness on: in large laboratory chains, people forwarded generosity for the first few decisions each, and then the forwarding faded — what kept going was each person's own ordinary warmth, not the chain's pull. The [[hau]], watched closely, turned out to be a spark that needs re-lighting at every hand, not a flame that carries itself. A transient engine is not disproved by fading — but any design that leans on it must expect to re-light it, or measure how quickly it cools. (Horita et al., "Transient nature of cooperation by pay-it-forward reciprocity," Scientific Reports, 2016: https://doi.org/10.1038/srep19471 — read 2026-09-16)
