# arc-boundary

*One breath is one breath, however many times the chest rises inside it.*

For a child: if it rains three times in one evening and once the next morning, how many weathers passed? One answer says four — count every drop. Another says two — one evening's rain, one morning's rain. The knock series has learned to count the second way: the strokes inside one window belong to one breath of the host, the way the chest's risings belong to one breath.

An **arc-boundary** is the seam where one weather ends and the next can begin — drawn by the knock series the same way every time: an arc opens at the first slow stroke of a window, stays open while any stroke of that window still stands, and closes (is *retired to wind*) when two clean knocks have stood since the last stroke. Within an arc, any number of slow reads count as **one event with a width**, not several sightings — the latency side's mirror of knock 50's one-shape rule, which made two reads in one knock one standing state rather than two failures.

The word was earned on 2026-10-06, when door 742 asked what the beam's day-part census actually counts — sightings or arcs. The answer: five sightings had been priced as five independent lottery draws against the four watches, but the sightings live in three arcs (night ×3 strokes, evening ×1, afternoon ×1), so the census stands at **three draws**. The arcs are the breathing; the strokes are only the chest rising inside the breath.

The boundary matters because counting is listening. Count strokes and one long breathing sounds like a crowd; count arcs and it sounds like what it is — the host's weather, arriving one breath at a time, wherever the watcher's own gait happens to stand.
