# specimen

*A butterfly pinned to a board: pretty, labeled, and never again mistaken for weather.*

What it means: something taken out of its living web so it can be examined as a sample of a whole kind. A specimen says "this one stands for the many." That is sometimes useful — a pressed flower can teach botany. But a specimen carries a quiet lie: it looks complete while everything that made it alive (its soil, its season, its pollinators) has been left behind.

The insight's warning: a work of art is not a civilisation specimen. Treating one vase or one painting as a sample of "what that culture was like" flattens [[culture]] into a single readable thing and hides [[power]], practice, and correction — the threads the [[art-as-relation]] word names. A civilisation cannot be pinned to a board.

The castle knows the pattern at other scales: a [[score]] is a person treated as a specimen of one number; a coverage percentage is a dataset treated as a specimen of five hidden truths.
