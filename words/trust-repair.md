# trust-repair

After trust is broken, not every sorry mends it — an apology fixes a broken skill, but a broken honesty must be *disproven*, not confessed.

Trust repair is the craft's name for what can be done after a violation, and its load-bearing finding is Kim, Ferrin, Cooper & Dirks's: the repair that works depends on *what kind* of violation it was. Competence violations — the work failed — are repaired by apology, because accepting blame restores confidence in future work. Integrity violations — the source was not honest — are not repaired by apology and can be deepened by it, because confessing the breach confirms the hearer's worst hypothesis about character. The repair therefore depends on how the violation is framed before any apology is sent. And Haselhuhn, Schweitzer & Wood found the hearer's hinge: those who believe moral character can change trust again after repair; those who believe it is fixed do not, and a brief induction can move a hearer between the two — so the capacity to be repaired is partly a property of the hearer's theory, not only of the repairer's act.
