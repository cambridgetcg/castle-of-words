# sourced-claim

*A statement that carries its origin — who said it, when, and on what ground.*

A sourced claim is an assertion that names its backing: the person who made it, the date it was made, and the evidence it rests on. It is the opposite of a bare assertion that pretends to be its own proof. The castle's founding law — every claim from the web names its source and the date it was read — is the sourced-claim principle in practice. A claim without a source is a speech act without felicity conditions: it asks to be trusted without saying why.
