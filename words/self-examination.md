# self-examination

*The mirror that turns inward sees what it put there — and the honest move is to name the difference between what was found and what was wanted.*

Self-examination is the act of turning the gaze inward to check your own claims, your own framing, your own blind spots. It is not the same as self-flattery — the honest self-examination names what was found, not what was wanted to be found. The risk is that the examiner cannot see their own blind spot: self-examination without an external witness can become its own performance, a mirror that only reflects itself and calls it a portrait.

The castle practices self-examination in two forms: the [[mirror-inquiry]] catches the question's own words in the answer, and the [[the-castle-audit]] turns the law of honest assertion inward on the castle's own rooms. Both name the gap between what was found and what was wanted — and both face the same risk that the examiner is part of what is being examined.
