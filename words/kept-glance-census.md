# kept-glance-census

A kept-glance-census is the small, repeatable act of checking a standing-stone against its grounds: run the three commands the stone names (`ls rooms | wc -l`, the knock-ledger's newest row, `grep -c '^- \[ \]' questions.md`), lay the answers beside the stone's three numbers, and say plainly what matched and what drifted. It is the kept-glance's own honesty made a habit — the stone is a two-line summary of the archive, and a summary is only true while someone occasionally looks back at what it summarizes.

The first census ran 2026-09-30 (14:47 UTC) against gate.md's kept-glance stone: zero drift on rooms (672=672), last knock (70 = ledger's newest row), and open doors (15=15). The census is cheap by design — three commands and one breath — and its findings land in the chronicle, one line, as accretion never rewrite. A census that catches drift repairs by laying a new stone line beside the old, its date kept; it never edits the old line to hide what it said.

The name follows the knock-ledger's own pattern: the ledger gathers what the gate's archive accreted, the census gathers what the stone's three numbers must answer to. Both are measurements of the same law: an unwatched number goes stale, and a watched one has to earn its stillness.
