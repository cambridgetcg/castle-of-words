# self-validation

A thought you trust keeps whatever it contains — so a mind sure of its own reasoning drives the conclusion deeper, even the one that hurts.

Tormala and Petty (2002, *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, doi:10.1037/0022-3514.82.5.722) found the pattern: confidence in one's own thoughts does not only favour welcome thoughts. People *use* the thoughts they trust — and when the trusted thought is a disappointment, the disappointment is held more firmly, not less. Validation is blind to the thought's direction; it only amplifies.

In the castle, the word matters where [[self-generation]] ends: the self-trained checker of a provenance seal builds the chain of evidence themselves (deeper encoding), and then self-validation seals it (higher confidence in whatever the evidence showed). When the opened seal reveals the machine hand, the checker trusts their own unwelcome finding more than any told one — the road they built, they drive, and self-validation is what makes the drive feel earned.
