# multitask-distortion

*Pay for the measured thing and the unmeasured thing starves — effort slides toward what the meter can see.*

Multitask distortion is the pattern Holmström & Milgrom named (1991): when a job has many parts and only some are measured, high-powered incentives on the measured parts pull effort away from the unmeasured ones. It is the economics of Campbell's law — the more a measure is used for decisions, the more it warps the behavior it measures, and the more the unmeasured dimensions are degraded. For a maker under a narrow seal, the prediction is sharp: effort and care concentrate in the checkable parts, and the uncheckable parts of the work may be starved, not enriched. The caution spreads in feeling but not in effort — and effort is the resource that was meant to spread.
