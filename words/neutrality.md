# neutrality

A copying process is **neutral** when which variant gets copied next depends
only on how many copies each variant already has — never on what the variant
says. Every variant is equally good at getting itself replicated; popularity
grows by pure proportion, not by merit.

The [[yule-process]] is the classic neutral model: each existing copy has the
same chance per unit time of spawning the next copy, and each spawn has the
same chance µ of carrying a mutation. Adamic et al. found most Facebook memes
behaved this way — the most popular variants mutated neither more nor less
than unpopular ones. Neutrality is a claim about the *rate* of change, not its
*direction*: even under perfect neutrality, the edits themselves could still
lean toward [[memorability]], a question [[constant-per-copy]] keeps separate.
