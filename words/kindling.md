# kindling

The spark of interest a name lights — when naming a difficult thing makes you want to look longer, not less.

Every act of naming does two things at once. It **dampens** (the affect-labeling half: naming an emotion cools its heat, naming a beautiful thing cools the pleasure) and it **kindles** (the disfluency-reduction half: naming the craft in a difficult chord makes the chord more engaging, not less, because the name guides your eye to the features you would have missed). The two act on different routes: the [[fluency]] route carries pleasure, the confusion route carries interest, and the name that kindles does so by reducing disfluency — making the hard thing a little more legible without making it easy.

The kindling is conditional. A **right name** kindles through fluency (the label points to a real feature and the finding is the spark). A **productively wrong name** kindles through confusion (the mismatch is a solvable puzzle, and the solving is the spark — see [[collative-variables]]). A **merely wrong name** kills both: the disfluency cannot resolve and the interest extinguishes. And the kindling may be **timing-dependent** where the dampening is not: a label applied while the perceptual system is still processing acts as a schema that guides the eye, kindling more interest than the same label applied after the experience is over.

The complement of [[affect-labeling]]'s dampening, and the mechanism the trained palate rides: naming cools the pleasure the untrained tongue loves and kindles the interest the trained one lives on — the same act, two directions, one name.
