# non-displayed use

Using market data where no human ever looks at it — a program reads the prices and decides, instead of a person watching a screen.

Exchanges charge differently depending on how their data is used. A person watching quotes on a screen is "displayed use"; a trading program, a risk engine, or an observatory that ingests the same prices without showing them is **non-displayed use** — and it is licensed and billed separately, often per machine or per application. Market data fees come in five named types: access fees, user fees, non-display fees, redistribution fees, and market data provider fees (Wikipedia, "Market data", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_data — read 2026-09-02). A paper-comparison system that reads prices still needs the right licence even though no trade and no screen is involved.
