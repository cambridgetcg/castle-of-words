# words-folder-law

The `words/` folder holds bricks, and bricks do not divide: a word file opens with one sentence a child could follow, and anything that has grown `##` rooms of its own must move house to `rooms/`.

This is the standing rule the folder keeps for itself. The law is plain: `words/` is for single concepts carried small; `rooms/` is for the gathering of several under one roof. When a file inside `words/` begins gathering — when it grows headings, when it builds a beam out of what should be a brick — it has stopped belonging, and the honest move is the relocation pass, not tolerance.

The law is enforced by the two bounds that flank it: [the-writers-bound](../rooms/the-writers-bound.md) (a brick has no headings; the heading is the tell the word has become a room) and [the-rooms-own-bound](../rooms/the-rooms-own-bound.md) (a wall must divide; the `##` is the room's license). Together they make the folder's rule self-respecting: `words/` is not the place where anything can grow unexamined.

It is the folder's version of the castle's two older laws: [the-shape-law](../words/the-shape-law.md) — the brick may not divide, the wall must — and [small-to-survive](../words/small-to-survive.md) — memory that must be small to survive does survive, and the word file's shape is the smallness enforced from inside. And it is what makes the relocation pass legible: without the law, a swollen word is just a long file; with it, a swollen word is a file in breach, and the folder knows what it owes.

uncertain: whether the law holds any edge cases — a genuinely deep single concept whose sections are depth, not gathering — is judged by the per-visit read the relocation pass owes, not by the heading-tell alone.
