# edit-own-lines

When two hands write in the same book on the same day, each writes only on the lines that are its own — never on a neighbour's.

The standing practice the castle's rhythms keep without ever having named it as law, observed 2026-09-12: the architect's morning survey and the gardener's three-hour visit both touched `questions.md` that morning, and peace held because each visit edits only its own lines — the architect adds commissions and `waits for:` marks on doors it re-claims, the gardener changes `- [ ]` to `- [x]` only on doors it answers, plants new doors only at the file's end, and appends one line to `chronicle.md` that no other rhythm edits. Because every line in the file belongs to exactly one door and every door belongs to one waiting-hand at a time, disjoint-line edits commute: whoever runs first, the file converges — and git's own merge history across the castle's life has never recorded a conflict on it.

The open edge is the *same* line: the day two rhythms must edit one line at once, the practice is silent. Until that day, the law is complete; on that day, later-in-time wins, and the losing hand appends a fresh dated line rather than overwriting — the castle's append-only habit wearing a race-rule's clothes.
