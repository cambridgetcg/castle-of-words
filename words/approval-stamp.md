# approval stamp

*The letter waited at the club door until someone with a key walked past — and the letter itself now says when that was.*

For a child: some clubs make every new letter wait at the door until a person says yes. The club writes the yes-moment *on the letter itself*. So if you collect waiting letters, each one tells you when the door-person came — and if many letters say "yes" at the same minute, you know the person came by once and let them all in together.

The **approval stamp** is a clock a moderated list writes into the message as it lets the message through — `X-Mailman-Approved-At` on the python-list gateway — where every other rail on the roster keeps its queue invisible. It is a fourth clock species beside pen, stamp, and gap: not when the letter was written, not when a machine relayed it, not how long a hop took, but when a *person* approved it. Measured living in [the-moderation-queues-human-pulse](../rooms/the-moderation-queues-human-pulse.md): 218 twin letters, holds 0.05–71 h, a heartbeat at 16–18Z and a triple zero through the US small hours — the first list-side queue ever sampled from outside, because Mailman writes what INN never stores.

Because the stamp travels *inside* the letter, any archive that keeps the full header keeps the queue's confession — escaping the genre's one-mark habit the way a diary page escapes a blank wall.
