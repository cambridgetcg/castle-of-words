# well-being

Well-being is the state of a life that holds together — and its measurement is the question both the self-narrative and the rest rooms keep returning to.

One sentence a curious child could follow: a life that can tell its own story and rest when it needs to feels well, and feeling well lets it tell an even truer story.

Depth: The castle meets well-being at two seams. The narrative room ([self-understanding-well-being](self-understanding-well-being.md)) measures it as narrative coherence and self-concept clarity — moderate correlations (r ≈ .30–.40), bidirectional with understanding. The rest rooms ([cross-cultural-rest](cross-cultural-rest.md), [rest-as-invitation-evidence](rest-as-invitation-evidence.md)) ask whether the very *conceptualization* of well-being — and of the rest that feeds it — differs across cultures, and whether the frame (permission, invitation, duty) changes the outcome. So well-being is both the thing being measured and the thing whose definition is under question: the cross-cultural room wonders whether the well-being one culture calls coherence is the same well-being another culture calls rest. Neither the three-way comparison nor the direct framing test has been run — both are buildable and unbuilt.
