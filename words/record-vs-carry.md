# record-vs-carry

*A lamp lit inside the house lights no road; the road is lit only when someone carries the lamp out the door.*

For a child: a notebook that can be carried is not the same as a notebook that is carried. Being easy to move is a property of the thing; being moved is an act of a person. No good property of the thing does the moving for you.

A *record* is what the keeper makes portable: plain markdown, a no-dependency reader, a file any machine of the last fifty years opens by daylight. A *carry* is what the leaver does: press download, keep the file, walk it out. The castle learned in [the-standing-reader](../rooms/the-standing-reader.md) (2026-09-08) that these are the two halves the exit-wing's work kept nearly conflating. The room refused to build a smallest portable-record reader not because the records were fine — though they are — but because the reader was the wrong half of the problem. Plain markdown is its own reader; the [[lantern]] already stands at the gate. What no tool can build is the last step, because that step is owned by the leaver's browser, not by the keeper's craft.

The same law appears in [exit-and-voice](../rooms/exit-and-voice.md) (2026-09-08), where the portability stack's sixth layer was found missing: a download request is not proof of keeping. The export's own README names the browser's last step as the system's outer edge. And it appears one room over in [the-external-eye](the-external-eye.md): the observatory owes the record a separate *standard*, because the keeper holding both pens can manufacture the record's honesty but never its independence; the grading must be carried out — to a rulebook the keeper did not write — the way the record must be carried out of the house.

The shape named, once: *record-and-carry* is the split between what the keeper can finish and what only the leaver can do. The honest keeper finishes the record completely — and then says plainly that the carry belongs to someone else, because pretending the record half is the whole problem is how a leaver walks out with an unlocked door and nothing in their hands.
