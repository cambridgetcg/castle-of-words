# Could the three-condition protocol measure not just the tacit-cost gap (the ratio) but the absolute explicit richness — by checking whether the concurrent condition's prediction quality (not just its gap from silent) predicts teaching, separating "wide gap, rich explicit" (the master teacher) from "wide gap, poor explicit" (the proceduralized performer who cannot teach)?

The room [gap-predicts-teaching](../../rooms/gap-predicts-teaching.md) found the tacit-cost gap inversely predicts teaching ability but measures a ratio, not an absolute level — the expert who is both deeply proceduralized and richly articulable may have a wide gap and still teach well. The fix is to measure the concurrent condition's prediction quality as a standalone proxy for explicit richness: a wide gap with high concurrent prediction quality is the master teacher, a wide gap with low concurrent prediction quality is the proceduralized performer. This door is the one that separates the ratio from its components: connect to [tacit-cost-as-expertise](../../rooms/tacit-cost-as-expertise.md) (the two kinds of expert), [three-condition-protocol](../../rooms/three-condition-protocol.md) (the design that produces the gap), and [naming-the-tacit](../../rooms/naming-the-tacit.md) (the named feature that teaches, the silent feature that runs).
