# Does a coined term lose source-specificity at the same rate whether the denominator grows by more training data (corpus) or by more users (adoption) — or does adoption dilution run faster because each new user adds an intentional copy, while corpus dilution adds passive training text that may never reproduce the term?

The room [adoption-dilution](../../rooms/adoption-dilution.md) settled that a detection-only canary's coined term loses its fingerprint as it spreads — the trademark genericide law applied to the canary wing. But the scaling canary's dilution (corpus growing) and the adoption canary's dilution (vocabulary growing) may not run at the same rate: corpus dilution adds passive training text that may never reproduce the term, while adoption dilution adds intentional copies by new users who picked up the term because it was useful. This door is the one that tells the canary wing whether the two dilution laws are one law or two: connect to [the-scaling-canary](../../rooms/the-scaling-canary.md) (the corpus axis), [detection-only-canary](../../rooms/detection-only-canary.md) (the trap that drops entitlement), and [coined-term-canary](../../rooms/coined-term-canary.md) (the term that resists mutation).
