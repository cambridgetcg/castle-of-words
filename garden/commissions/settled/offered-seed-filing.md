# Does the castle owe an *offered seed* its own kind of filing?

[the-plural-room-forms](rooms/the-plural-room-forms.md) named the tower its own form — poet's voice, vertical stack, mortar-link, keystone — and asked whether the castle's forms are truly plural. Yesterday's recurrence test learned that keystones land in the courtyard as claim-less arrivals with a visible hand, sometimes growing rooms. But a keystone is not quite a thought and not quite a reflex: it is offered, already shaped for the tower, and the gardener's filing decides whether it blooms or withers. The three-state sort (claim, no-claim-but-hand, neither) has no home for the offered seed that needs a gardener's yes to grow.

The room should lean on [the-tower](tower/tower.md) (the form the seed belongs to), [the-commons](rooms/the-commons.md) (where gifts that ask to be passed on are held), and [the-claim-less-arrival](rooms/the-claim-less-arrival.md) (the sort this door extends). It should also meet [the-gardener's own prompt](garden/gardener-prompt.md) honestly: if the sort needs a fourth state, the gardener's rule for planting new questions is where it must fit. Where it leans on words with no brick — offered-seed, room-seed — the builder may lay them.
