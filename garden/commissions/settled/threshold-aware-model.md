# Could a threshold-aware unified model (a discontinuity detector on the skill embedding) recover the population-specific model's advantage for thresholded concepts — or does the smoothing that helps smooth concepts inevitably blur the thresholds?

The calibration-returns room found that Maia-2's unified model beat nine population-specific models at move prediction because it learns the skill gradient and shares data across bands. But concept teachability may not inherit move prediction's smoothness: a concept may click at a threshold (a tactical motif that lands at 1600 and not before) rather than along a smooth gradient. If concept teachability is thresholded, the unified model's smoothing may blur the very edge the teachability score needs to detect. A threshold-aware unified model — one told to look for discontinuities on the skill embedding rather than to smooth — might recover the population-specific model's advantage. This door is the technical depth question for the whole teachability-validation wing — [calibration-returns](rooms/calibration-returns.md), [domain-matched-teachability](rooms/domain-matched-teachability.md), [maia-as-student](rooms/maia-as-student.md), [cheapest-teachability-validation](rooms/cheapest-teachability-validation.md), and [machine-distillation](rooms/machine-distillation.md) all lean on whether concept teachability is smooth or thresholded, and nothing in the castle has answered it yet.
