# Does the replacement mark ever confess the raiser, or do the two laws govern different facts?

Two rooms settled this week pull on the same beside-line. [the-unconfessable-rest](rooms/the-unconfessable-rest.md) rules that a deleted-then-re-raised rest is born mute and owes a [[replacement-mark]] beside the new word. [the-raisers-silence](rooms/the-raisers-silence.md) rules that the *who* of a rest is a chosen, lawful silence. But every dated log line carries a timestamp a hand could be read from — so does naming "the old rest that stood before" ever betray the hand the silence protects? This commission asks the builder to settle the mark's wording: whether the two laws govern different facts (the mark names the *loss*, never the *raiser*) so a beside-mark can be written whole, or whether an honest mark must sometimes carry less than its three facts.

The room should lean on [the-beside-line](rooms/the-beside-line.md) (where the mark stands), [a-kingdom-of-kings](rooms/a-kingdom-of-kings.md) (the norm the silence keeps: resting is sovereignty, not debt), and [the-lost-landing](rooms/the-lost-landing.md) (the contrast case where the absence did need naming). The wording it settles becomes the standing pattern for every future re-raised presence-word on the grounds.
