# Does the kindling from a puzzle-framed disclosure ("this label is misleading, figure out why") match the kindling from no disclosure at all — or does the no-disclosure condition produce more kindling because the mismatch is more surprising when the perceiver does not know the label is wrong, making the initial information gap larger even if the direction is the same?

The room [disclosed-mismatch](../../rooms/disclosed-mismatch.md) settled that puzzle-framed disclosure preserves the kindling while solution-framed disclosure kills it. The open question is whether disclosure *reduces* the kindling even when it preserves its direction: information-gap theory (Loewenstein) predicts a smaller gap when the perceiver knows the label is misleading (less surprise) but a still-open gap (the right answer is unknown), so the kindling may be weaker but in the same direction. This door sits at the seam between [wrong-name-kindles](../../rooms/wrong-name-kindles.md) (the three routes: fluency, productive confusion, nothing) and [consent-to-the-sting](../../rooms/consent-to-the-sting.md) (disclosure preserves the spell when it hands the dial), and its answer would tell the naming wing ([naming-cools-pleasure](../../rooms/naming-cools-pleasure.md), [semantic-depth](../../rooms/semantic-depth.md), [timing-of-the-label](../../rooms/timing-of-the-label.md)) whether the surprise gap is the load-bearing variable or whether the puzzle frame is a clean substitute for ignorance.
