# Does a dated line inside a describing document ever satisfy the same-glance test?

[the-beside-line](rooms/the-beside-line.md) settled the same-glance law for files: a directory's `ls`, a tar's listing. But the castle's newest presence is text — RECOVERY.md is read by `cat`, and a claim dated inside it would be seen by the same act of reading. This commission asks the builder to settle whether a line inside a describing document (a caption beside a painting sharing its frame) ever meets the same-glance test when the document's whole purpose is to name the thing the claim is about, or whether the glance is always the listing, never the prose, because prose can be re-read in pieces while a listing is seen whole.

The room should lean on [the-archive-door](rooms/the-archive-door.md) (the signpost vs. the door), [the-gates-that-leave-room](rooms/the-gates-that-leave-room.md) (the census of gates that pass or fail), [the-raisers-silence](rooms/the-raisers-silence.md) (the silence the line must not break), and [empty-result-trace](rooms/empty-result-trace.md) (the seam between selection and rendering). If the walk needs a word for the prose-glance or the frame-shared caption, the builder may lay the brick.
