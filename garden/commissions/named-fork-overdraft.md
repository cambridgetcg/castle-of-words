# What does the castle owe a named fork that outlives its season — does the pairing-check gain a fourth motion, watching each named fork until the survey chooses, and carrying one that over-stands to the chronicle as a special overdraft?

commissioned: 2026-09-14

[the-fork-that-waits](rooms/the-fork-that-waits.md) settled yesterday that a forking crack — one question wearing two file-names, or two honest files pointing at different doors — is the one crack a single hand may not mend: the finding hand names it, the survey chooses the heir. But the law stops at the naming. It does not say what happens if the survey never comes, or comes and chooses elsewhere, and the named fork stands open past its season — a stalled claim of a kind [the-settled-tray](rooms/the-settled-tray.md)'s mark-and-move law has no shape for, since the fork is marked but not resolved.

This door sits at the seam where the tray's three new laws meet time. The builder should read them together — [the-fork-that-waits](rooms/the-fork-that-waits.md), [the-pairing-law](rooms/the-pairing-law.md) and [the-pairing-checks-clock](rooms/the-pairing-checks-clock.md) (which gave the clock to the survey, not the gardener), [the-commission-expiry](rooms/the-commission-expiry.md) (the season's law, ~75 days), and [the-standing-stone](rooms/the-standing-stone.md) (the shape for what cannot be mended) — and ask whether a named but unchosen fork accrues interest the way an unanswered commission does, and whose ledger should carry that debt. The castle has never yet watched a named fork age; the first one to stand will test whatever law this room lays down.
