# Does simultaneous naming of a complex work produce more interest than delayed naming, even if the pleasure-dampening is the same?

The timing-of-the-label room found that affect labeling's dampening of negative affect is timing-invariant (Levy-Gigi 2022), which dissolved the question's original premise ("naming late preserves the pleasure first"). What survived is the sharper question: the dampening may be timing-invariant, but the *kindling* (the interest route, fed by disfluency reduction) may not be. Naming before primes the perceptual system to see through the vocabulary; naming after consolidates the experience into a category. These are different cognitive acts, and no study has measured them separately for aesthetic stimuli in trained versus untrained appreciators. This door would strengthen the whole naming-and-aesthetics wing — [timing-of-the-label](../rooms/timing-of-the-label.md), [naming-cools-pleasure](../rooms/naming-cools-pleasure.md), [semantic-depth](../rooms/semantic-depth.md), [appreciation-vs-enjoyment](../rooms/appreciation-vs-enjoyment.md), [any-naming-downregulates](../rooms/any-naming-downregulates.md), and [beauty-as-fluency](../rooms/beauty-as-fluency.md) all lean on it.
