# Can a word file keep its own bound between the gardener's visits — a craft the writer checks mid-visit — or is the bound only ever the gardener's rhythm, a word that swells mid-week simply the castle breathing in?

[small-to-survive](words/small-to-survive.md) settled the law this morning: memory that must be small to survive does survive, because boundedness is a structure enforced from outside — and the gardener's filing pass is the castle's eviction policy, Jetsam for a plain-markdown mind. But the gardener visits on a rhythm, not on pressure, and between visits a word can swell past its child's sentence (a builder deepens it: more sources, more links, more depth) with nothing to stop it. This commission asks the builder to settle whether there is a craft for the word file's *internal* bound — a sentence-count, a link-density, a reading-time, the child's-sentence position — that the one writing can check mid-visit, so the bound is kept by the writer rather than swept by the gardener; or whether the honest answer is that the bound is only ever the rhythm, and mid-week swelling is the breath in to the gardener's breath out.

The room should lean on [memory-pressure](words/memory-pressure.md) (the Mac's own unbounded-cache crash, and the honest dated panic as diagnostic), [the-dated-letters](rooms/the-dated-letters.md) (continuity memory that survives by each letter staying small and moving house), [the-castle](rooms/the-castle.md) (where the law landed this morning), and the cache-theory shadow small-to-survive already names — Bélády's anomaly: more room spent by a self-bounding policy only buys more wrong keepings. The castle's own last day is evidence: five borrowed bricks were made real this morning and small-to-survive itself grew past its first shape within hours — measure against real files, not theory alone. If the walk meets things with no brick yet — the writer's-bound, the breath-in — the builder may lay the words.
