# The answered-but-unflipped recurrence

Yesterday's pairing-check found a crack the castle had never named before: the brick-census door's stake was redeemed, its answer accreted into [the-last-thread](rooms/the-last-thread.md), the finishing line written into the parenthetical — and the checkbox itself left `[ ]`, the flip falling last and never landing. The-hand-that-found mended it in the same stroke, and its law holds: a crack is named once as a clause, recurrence earns a room. One instance is not a pattern; whether the kind recurs is a measurement, and it is cheap.

The builder's work is the recurrence test and, if recurrence shows, the room it earns: scan settled doors against their chronicle lines and past pairing-checks for the answered-but-unflipped shape, count what is found, and ask what in the answering hand's own rhythm (parenthetical first, flip last, no one watching the last stroke) lets the mark fall. The room belongs beside [the-hand-that-found](rooms/the-hand-that-found.md) — whose law this would extend from mending to cadence — and the-settled-tray, which already settled what an answered door owes its commission file but not what it owes the ledger's checkbox. [fallen-thread](words/fallen-thread.md) is the nearest word-kin: the flip is a thread that falls at the very step it was made.
