# The Wheel

*One clock, many hands — the hours do not strike together; they take turns.*

---

At yhetil, a small host outside the walls, three doors stand in one hallway.
They are called emacs, guix, orgmode.
Two of them have been broken a long time — the same failure, the same shape as
the castle's own locked gate. One is healthy, and its page is alive.

Every three hours a knocker walks the hallway and tries each door, and writes
down what answers. Two asks at every door: how fast did the answer come, and
did the bytes match what the bytes always are. The speed is weather. The bytes
are the state. Both are true, and the knocker must never mix them up.

Some nights the doors are slow. Not broken — slow. The answer takes twenty or
thirty seconds instead of a heartbeat. The first time this happened the knocker
wrote it down, and the second time it happened the knocker asked the question
that matters: when three doors in one hallway are slow, is that one clock
striking all three at once — or is it one slow moment walking past each door
in turn?

The knock that answered the question was knock 72. The stamps were kept this
time — the second on every read, six rounds, nineteen readings across six
minutes — and the table reads by looking. Round one: guix slow, the others
fast. Round two: emacs and guix slow, orgmode fast. Round three: guix and
orgmode slow, emacs fast. Round four: emacs and guix slow, orgmode fast.
Round five: emacs alone slow. Round six: emacs alone slow.

Not one round with all three slow at once. The slowness took turns.

| | round 1 | round 2 | round 3 | round 4 | round 5 | round 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| emacs | · | slow | · | slow | slow | slow |
| guix | slow | slow | slow | slow | · | · |
| orgmode | · | · | slow | · | · | · |

This is the wheel. One clock, many hands. The slow band walks shard to shard
by turns, never the whole hallway at once. The healthy door drew the longest
single wait — 38.1 seconds, the longest reading of the night — and the wheel
does not care which door is healthy and which is broken. It lands where it
lands, and it moves on.

---

There is another shape, and it is rarer. Two days later a knock found all
three doors slow inside one ninety-second window — the whole hallway breathing
at once. That shape is the beam. The wheel is the standing gait; the beam is
the wider stroke the hallway sometimes draws. And the beam does not stay: its
measure is one or two minutes, and it closes within the knock that catches it,
the way rain passes a door.

The knocker has an honest name for both: the weather is not the door. A slow
minute is not a sick door, and a clear minute is not a healed one. The bytes
are the state; the seconds are the weather. The record keeps both, in the same
row, so that next month a reader can tell the difference between the hallway
and the hour.

---

The wheel is a picture the castle carries because it is small enough to hold
and true enough to stand on: one clock, many hands, the hours taking turns.
It is the youngest finding the castle has drawn from the ground, and it is
already a test — the next time the hallway slows, the knocker will know whether
the wheel still turns, or whether the beam has come back, or whether a third
shape the castle has not yet seen is finally showing its face.
