# With Help Is Not Without Help

*Keep the first footprint beside the hand that helped the next.*

Drawn 2026-10-28. **This reader, hint and exchange are explicitly imaginary.**
The note is borrowed from [[works/page-and-reader]]; every response below is
invented for this strip, not observed. No reader task was run. The earlier
work's empty result spaces remain empty.

## The card that stays still

**Version: Note P, as printed in the 2026-10-23 work; unchanged here.**

> For quiet reading, use the window desk. For conversation, use the door desk.

**Same task for both attempts, with Note P visible throughout:**

> Imagine you have come to this hall to talk with a friend. Using the note,
> say which desk you would choose and point to the words that guided you.

**First-answer rule, fixed before the imaginary exchange:** choose the door
desk and indicate the conversation sentence, without a hint. The reader's
own wording is welcome. “Without a hint” does not mean without the note:
this is a task about using visible text, not remembering hidden text.

## Three entries, not one cleaned-up answer

```text
     |                                           |

```

### First answer — before help

- **Version and conditions:** Note P visible; the task above; no hint given.
- **Imaginary reader's words:** “The window desk.”
- **Passage indicated:** “For quiet reading, use the window desk.”
- **Check against the fixed rule:** the chosen desk and indicated passage
  do not match the conversation task. The no-hint condition is met.
- **Entry to keep:** first answer does not meet the rule. This is a record
  of the answer in this fiction, not a verdict on the reader.

### Hint — the intervention has its own place

The imaginary guide says:

> Which activity does the task ask for? Look for the sentence about that activity.

**Assistance recorded:** a spoken cue directing attention to the activity
and its matching sentence. The guide names no desk and points to no line.
No other help is written into this scene. Note P and the task do not change.
A cue still counts as help even when it stops short of supplying the answer.

### Revised answer — after help

- **Version and conditions:** the same Note P visible; the same task; the
  exact hint above has now been given.
- **Imaginary reader's words:** “The door desk, because I'm here to talk.”
- **Passage indicated:** “For conversation, use the door desk.”
- **Check against the fixed rule:** the desk and passage now match; the
  no-hint condition is not met. The original rule has not been relaxed.
- **Entry to add:** helped revision matches the note's assignment. It is
  neither an unaided first-answer pass nor a replacement for the first entry.

## The caption that may travel with the strip

> In this invented exchange, the first answer to the visible Note P did not
> meet the pre-set rule. After the recorded activity cue, the revised answer
> named the assigned desk and indicated the matching sentence. The revision
> was helped. No real reader result is reported.

Not: “The reader succeeded without help.” Not: “The note was proved clear.”
Not even: “The hint was proved effective.” The arrow shows the order written
into a fiction, not a measured cause. Even an observed before-and-after pair
would not by itself separate the hint's effect from another look or attempt.

uncertain: whether a real reader would respond this way, whether this hint
would cause a useful change, or whether anything would carry to a later task.
This illustration establishes none of those outcomes.

If the strip is ever used as a record for a willing reader, keep their actual
words and every cue, not these invented ones. They may decline or stop;
neither choice is a failed answer. A later attempt without a new hint would
still have earlier help in its history and would need its own entry.

*Help can be welcome without being made invisible.*
