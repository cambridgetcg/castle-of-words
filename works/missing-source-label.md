# Missing Source, Open Claim

*A missing witness leaves a question, not a verdict.*

Made 2026-10-24. A small label for a claim whose support cannot be inspected.

**This example is explicitly imaginary.** The rehearsal, red lantern, old
note and watch record R are invented. In this paper case, R is the sole
record offered in support, and it is unavailable. No real rehearsal was
observed, no source file disappeared, and no recovery was attempted.

## Earlier wording — kept, not renewed

**Old note A, version 1 — imaginary text:**

> The red lantern stayed lit for the whole rehearsal. See watch record R.

Keep that wording as the earlier claim, with the label below beside it.
The citation tells us what the note relied on; it does not show that R was
ever inspected or that it supported the whole sentence. R is a paper name,
not a link to a real file.

## The missing-source label — filled example

```text
MISSING SOURCE — CLAIM NOT VERIFIED

Claim carried forward from note A, version 1:
“The red lantern stayed lit for the whole rehearsal.”

Sole cited support:
Watch record R — unavailable in this imagined case.
No other evidence of the lantern's state is supplied.

Support needed:
A checkable observation record tied to this lantern and this
rehearsal, identifying the start and end, what was observed
between them, how it was observed, and any gaps in coverage.
A repeated sentence is not a substitute for those observations.

Present status:
Retained as an earlier claim, not offered as an established result.
Its cited support cannot be checked here. No counterevidence
showing the lantern went out is supplied either.

uncertain: whether the lantern stayed lit throughout, what R
contained, and why it is unavailable.
```

The record would still need to be assessed against **the whole rehearsal**.
Seeing the lantern lit only at the start and finish would not establish an
unbroken light between them. The support needed is not a recovered title
or a working address; it is evidence that bears on this exact claim.

## What deleting “See R” would do

```text
Earlier sentence + unavailable R   -> the evidence cannot be checked
Earlier sentence without “See R”   -> the evidence still cannot be checked
Earlier sentence + this label     -> the limit is visible; no proof is added
```

Removing the dead reference can tidy the page. It cannot supply the missing
observations, and leaving the sentence unqualified would hide the problem.
Nor does the missing record, by itself, show that the lantern went out.
**Unsupported here is not disproved; an annotation is not confirmation.**

If the page's job is to list verified results, this claim does not presently
qualify on the available evidence. Move it out of that list, keep the earlier
wording in its history, and say why. That withdraws an endorsement; it does
not announce that the opposite happened or that the original promise was met.

This is the boundary left open by [[works/removal-and-repair]]: an optional
invitation can be withdrawn, but a claim's need for evidence cannot be paid
by erasing the signpost to it.

## A label to use elsewhere

> **Missing-source note — added by:** ______ · **Date:** ______  
> **Earlier claim, quoted exactly:** ______  
> **Where it appeared — note, version and stated date:** ______  
> **Record cited — keep its name or locator:** ______  
> **What access check actually happened, when, and with what result:** ______  
> **Other evidence available, if any:** ______  
> **Support this exact claim needs:** ______  
> **Present status and where the earlier wording is kept:** ______  
> **uncertain:** ______

Describe the access failure narrowly: “this copy could not be opened” is
not “every copy is lost.” Leave unperformed checks blank. If evidence later
arrives, add a dated assessment rather than silently replacing this label
with “verified.”
