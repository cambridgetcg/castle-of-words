# Removal and repair

*A mended edge is not the missing cloth.*

Made 2026-10-19. One imagined cut, shown before and after its repair.

**This is a paper model of the castle's ornament shelf, not a reported loss.**
Slips A and B below are invented for this work. Their wording is not a quotation;
their two techniques come from the local depth words named at the foot.
No source file was removed or changed.

## Set the job before the scissors

**The whole's job:** name and briefly describe the solid-contrast and
layered-shadow routes. The promise does not include a colour recipe.

**Pass rule:** the displayed guide must retain both names and say what is
placed against or behind what. Check the text in each frame, not whether a
visitor can make an ornament from it. No reader trial was run.

The unit removed is **slip B**, not every mention of layering. First leave A
unchanged, as [[works/pair-test-card]] asks. Only after recording that result
may the repair begin.

## Frame I — the whole

```text
A — TWO ROUTES

Solid contrast: a solid shape against a contrasting ground.
Layered shadow: a broad, faint band behind a bright line.
For the dark livery's colour pair, see slip B.
                         |
                         v
B — COLOUR PAIR

Faint band: trophy-gold.
Bright line: bright gold.
```

**Read:** A meets the stated job. B supplies extra colour detail, and A's
invitation reaches a present note. Nothing is owed to an absent slip.

## Frame II — the missing piece, not yet mended

```text
A — TWO ROUTES

Solid contrast: a solid shape against a contrasting ground.
Layered shadow: a broad, faint band behind a bright line.
For the dark livery's colour pair, see slip B.
                         |
                         v
                   [ B absent ]
```

**Loss recorded before repair:** the colour-pair note has gone. Both route
names and descriptions remain, word for word. The original job still passes.

**Debt:** “For the dark livery's colour pair, see slip B” offers a destination
that this frame no longer contains. The verdict is **a hole, with lost
detail**, not a broken explanation of both routes and not a clean dent.
The surviving words are the evidence; the empty space alone is not the test.

## Frame III — the honestly repaired survivor

```text
A — TWO ROUTES

Solid contrast: a solid shape against a contrasting ground.
Layered shadow: a broad, faint band behind a bright line.
This guide names the routes; colour pairs are not included.
```

**Repair:** replace only A's last line. Withdraw the unavailable extra and
state the guide's limit. The two descriptions stay unchanged; no colour pair
has been recovered or invented to fill the gap.

**Job kept; offer reduced.** The same pass rule still holds. The optional
colour detail remains lost, but no invitation points to the missing slip.
Under this job, the repaired survivor has a **dent** rather than an unpaid
reference.

That does not turn Frame II into a clean removal. It took a further edit to
close the hole. Keep both verdicts: **the cut left a hole; the repair left a
smaller, honest guide.**

## What this repair cannot pay

This case withdraws an optional invitation. It does not show that every debt
can be paid by deleting a reference. If B held the sole evidence for a claim,
removing “see B” would not supply that evidence. If the original job required
the colour recipe, this survivor would fail it; quietly narrowing the job
would not make the old promise fulfilled.

uncertain: whether this strip helps a reader distinguish removal from repair.
The text has been inspected; understanding has not been measured.
