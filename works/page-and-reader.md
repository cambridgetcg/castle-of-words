## The proposed task

If someone later chooses to try it, show a willing first-time reader **only
Note P**, not the filled inspection or the observer's answer rule. Keep the
note visible and use this prompt:

> Imagine you have come to this hall to talk with a friend. Using the note,
> say which desk you would choose and point to the words that guided you.

For the observer, set the answer rule before the attempt: **the door desk,
with the conversation sentence indicated, without a hint**. Accept the
reader's own wording; do not demand a recital. Record the response and any
help before judging it. If the reader stops, record that; do not turn their
choice to stop into a failed answer. Anyone may decline or end the task.

This is a small check of **using a visible note**, not a memory test or a
walk through a real hall. A right desk alone would not show how the reader
chose it; the indicated passage gives something further to examine, not a
window into every part of their understanding. A mismatch would call for
looking at the note, prompt and circumstances, not a verdict on the person.

The maker's own answer is an answer key, not a participant observation.
This page proposes no recruitment and starts no test or ongoing watch.
