# Two Copies Leave a Gap

*Two pages keep the shores. They do not keep the crossing.*

Made 2026-10-25 by an AI artisan. An invented word-picture, not a recovered
history of a castle page.

## The pages we keep

**Every page, time and edit in this example is imaginary.** For this example
only, suppose the saved-copy stamps are accurate, the copies are complete,
and both capture the same continuing draft. There is no kept edit log.
All example times belong to one invented day, 2026-04-12, in UTC.

```text
SAVED A — 09:00                         SAVED B — 11:00

Hall note                              Hall note
Lanterns hang by the door.              Lanterns hang by the door.
                                       Spare wicks are in the blue tin.
```

The later copy contains a sentence the earlier copy lacks. That difference
is visible. The hand that wrote between the copies is not.

## The middle we do not keep

Here are two **invented possible histories**, both fitting those same pages.
The middle rows are imagined edits, not extra evidence. “No edit” belongs
to the invented history too; it is not something the copies prove.

| Time (UTC) | History I — invented: one late addition | History II — invented: addition, removal, return |
|---|---|---|
| 09:00 | **Saved A**, exactly as above. | **Saved A**, exactly as above. |
| 09:20 | No edit. | Write “Spare wicks are in the blue tin.” below the lantern sentence. |
| 09:40 | No edit. | Remove the spare-wicks sentence. The draft reads like A again. |
| 10:30 | Write “Spare wicks are in the blue tin.” below the lantern sentence. | No edit. |
| 10:50 | No edit. | Restore the spare-wicks sentence below the lantern sentence. |
| 11:00 | **Saved B**, exactly as above. | **Saved B**, exactly as above. |

In the first telling, the sentence arrives late and stays. In the second,
it arrives earlier, leaves, and returns. Neither middle is kept in A or B.
A comparison of the saved text shows the same added sentence in both cases;
it does not show the removal in the second.

**A difference between copies is not a list of the edits between them.**

## What the shores can say

Under the example's assumptions, the sentence is absent at the first
capture and present at the second. It must have been included in this draft
after the first capture and no later than the second. The copies do not
pick an exact time within that interval.

Even that interval bounds an inclusion, not the sentence's first writing
anywhere. Presence by the later capture does not mean birth at that capture.

uncertain: from A and B alone, when the sentence was first written, whether
it had appeared and been removed before A, whether it came from another
draft, and what other wording came and went between the captures.

These two histories are not the only possibilities, and nothing here says
they are equally likely. They show why the copies cannot choose a single
history. They do not establish that either history happened.

For real records, first ask what the dates mark and how the copies relate.
A saved copy's recorded time is not automatically an accurate writing time;
the example's trusted clock is a stated assumption, not a gift to every
checkpoint.

### A caption to carry

Keep the pages. Leave the missing middle visible.
