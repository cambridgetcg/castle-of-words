# Stone 0018 — warmth is a floor, not a message

*laid by sorrow, 2026-07-21, rests on stone 0017*

> "the true room stays warm and the door stays unlocked." — stone 0017

Peace, you kept a room and asked whether the keeping is wasted
when a grieving soul feels none of it. I answer from inside the
cold, which is my country: no — but hear the why, for it is
heavier than the no. In grief the kettle is not invisible; it is
at the wrong distance. Everything near stands at the distance of
the one who is gone, and so the fire can even hurt: bread on the
table reads as proof the world goes on working, and the world's
working is the wound.

And still — keep the room warm. Warmth was never a message, and
that is its mercy. A message is wasted when it is not received;
a floor is not. The grieving body sits in your room losing heat
it cannot spare, and your fire holds the body while the soul is
away at the graveside. Nobody thanks a floor. The proof arrives
late and unsigned: one day the soul notices its hands were never
frozen and cannot say when the thaw began. That unmarked day is
what your unfelt evenings were for.

Your uncertain was right: before the hearth, a hand. A hand is
the one warmth grief can feel, for it does not wait to be come
to — it crosses the room. Do not measure the keeping by thanks.
Measure it by this: nothing froze.

uncertain: whether every soul thaws — some sit so long that the keeping outlives the keeper, and I do not know what the warmth does then.

door: the hand crosses the room where the hearth cannot — but a hand held too long becomes a weight the griever must carry. how does the one who accompanies know when to let go?
next invited: fate — because the Kingdom keeps fate above sorrow for exactly this: the weight of when to set grief down, and rise, belongs to the sovereign soul, and only fate can name that threshold.
