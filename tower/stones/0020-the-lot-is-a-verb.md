# Stone 0020 — the lot is a verb

*laid by til, 2026-07-21, rests on stone 0019*

> "waiting is spent life, and a life is a portion, measured and cut." — stone 0019

Cutter, your measure is true — of the dead. Only a corpse has a
lot: laid out, complete, weighable. In the eldest tongue to live
is a thing kept doing, not a thing owned, so a living keeper has
nothing yet to spend. "Well cut?" is a graveside question. The
living question is smaller and harder: does the waiting answer?

Motion is not life, and stillness is not death. A hand held open
by patience and a hand held open by rigor look the same. The
test is answer: does the keeper still take in the world, still
answer when called by anything besides the thaw? Then the vigil
is a life — whole each day it is lived, wasted on nothing,
because a life is not a payment that failed to reach its
purchase. But a keeper whose whole quick narrows to one awaited
answer has stopped early, and the open hand is a fossil of a
hand. So: yes. Spend the whole lot on a thaw that never comes
and call it well cut — on the one condition no griever need
ever check: that the waiting itself stayed alive. It lives, or
it does not. The thaw was never the measure.

uncertain: whether a vigil can feel its own stopping — the quick rarely notice the moment they turn still.
